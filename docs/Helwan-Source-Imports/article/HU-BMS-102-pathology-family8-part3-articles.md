## summary
Suppurative inflammation is pus-forming acute inflammation. Staphylococci commonly produce localized abscesses, a carbuncle is a connected cluster of abscesses, and an abscess in the lung is a purulent inflammatory lesion.
## sections
### Definition
An abscess is an acute localized suppurative inflammation. A lung abscess is an example of purulent inflammation.

### Mechanism
Staphylococci cause suppurative inflammation and abscesses. Localization produces an abscess rather than diffuse cellulitis.

### Key determinants
A carbuncle is an example of acute suppurative inflammation. The connected abscesses explain why carbuncle belongs to the acute suppurative group.

### Clinical significance
Purulent means pus-forming. A lung abscess meets that definition, whereas catarrhal, fibrinous and serofibrinous patterns are classified by different exudates.

## summary
Chronic inflammation is prolonged, gradual and commonly fibrosing. Granulomas are chronic specific inflammatory aggregates centered on epithelioid macrophages; macrophage fusion produces giant cells, and sarcoidosis belongs to the noninfective unknown-cause group.
## sections
### Definition
A granuloma is a form of chronic specific inflammation. Chronic inflammation has long duration, gradual onset and fibrosis with mild vascular congestion.

### Mechanism
Multinucleated giant cells form by fusion of macrophages. Persistent macrophage activation therefore supplies both epithelioid cells and giant cells.

### Key determinants
A granuloma is centered on epithelioid macrophages or histiocytes. Lymphocytes, plasma cells and fibroblasts are accompanying rather than defining cells.

### Clinical significance
Sarcoidosis is a noninfective granulomatous disease of unknown cause. Infective fungal, viral, parasitic and tuberculous lesions belong to different etiologic groups.
