## sections
### Definition
First-pass metabolism reduces the fraction of an oral dose reaching systemic circulation. Apparent volume of distribution relates amount in the body to plasma concentration.

### Mechanism
Presystemic hepatic extraction lowers oral bioavailability. Distribution calculations use amount equals apparent volume multiplied by concentration, with careful unit conversion.

### Key determinants
Q63 converts 1 microgram/mL to 1 mg/L before division. Q66 assumes total body water equals 60% of body weight and uses 42 L times 5 mg/L.

### Clinical significance
These equations organise pharmacokinetic reasoning, but Q66 is an educational source calculation and not a patient-specific dosing instruction.

### Common misconceptions
Do not equate Vd with an anatomical compartment, infer zero-order elimination from first pass, or use the review-bank arithmetic as clinical prescribing guidance.

### Source-tested concept lines

Apparent volume of distribution

## sections
### Definition
Phase I commonly includes oxidation, reduction, and hydrolysis. Phase II commonly includes conjugation reactions such as acetylation, glucuronidation, methylation, and glutathione conjugation.

### Mechanism
Metabolism often increases polarity and water solubility, which can reduce tubular reabsorption and facilitate urinary elimination.

### Key determinants
Reaction class, substrate, enzyme system, and metabolite activity determine the actual outcome. Not every drug undergoes every phase.

### Clinical significance
The source tests reaction classification and the common direction toward faster urinary excretion, not an individual treatment plan.

### Common misconceptions
Reduction is not a Phase II conjugation reaction, and metabolism does not always inactivate a compound or increase efficacy.

### Source-tested concept lines
Acetylation is a phase II biotransformation reaction.
Phase I metabolic transformation includes oxidation, reduction, and hydrolysis
The liver is the main site of drug metabolism, but the gut wall, plasma, lung and kidney all metabolise drugs too

## sections
### Definition
Organophosphorus poisoning causes cholinergic excess through cholinesterase inhibition. Bronchospasm is an expected muscarinic airway manifestation.

### Mechanism
Excess acetylcholine stimulates bronchial muscarinic receptors, contracting airway smooth muscle and increasing secretions.

### Key determinants
Clinical findings vary with exposure, timing, severity, and mixed muscarinic, nicotinic, and central effects.

### Clinical significance
Recognition of bronchospasm supports urgent toxicology assessment. This Draft does not provide procedural management instructions.

### Common misconceptions
Dry mouth, mydriasis, and constipation are antimuscarinic patterns. Q68 is withheld because tachycardia is not a safely exclusive negative feature in all organophosphorus poisoning presentations.

### Source-tested concept lines
