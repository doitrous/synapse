## summary
Hyperaemia is active arteriolar inflow, whereas congestion is passive venous outflow failure. Chronic hepatic congestion produces nutmeg liver, and chronic pulmonary congestion produces haemosiderin-laden heart-failure cells.
## sections
### Definition
Hyperaemia is active whereas congestion is passive. Hyperaemia increases oxygenated inflow after arteriolar dilatation; congestion accumulates deoxygenated blood after venous outflow is impaired.

### Mechanism
Persistent congestion raises venous and capillary pressure, producing hypoxia, erythrocyte leakage, parenchymal injury and eventually fibrosis. The resulting morphology differs by organ but follows the same impaired-outflow principle.

### Key determinants
Chronic passive congestion of the liver produces a nutmeg appearance. Centrilobular congestion and necrosis alternate with midzonal fatty change to create a dark-red and yellow mottled cut surface. Chronic pulmonary congestion produces haemosiderin-laden alveolar macrophages called heart-failure cells. Alveolar macrophages acquire haemosiderin after ingesting extravasated erythrocytes.

### Clinical significance
Mitral stenosis causes chronic pulmonary venous congestion, so the lung finding is a siderophage rather than an acute inflammatory infiltrate. Chronic systemic venous congestion affects the liver and produces the nutmeg pattern before late congestive fibrosis develops.

## summary
Oedema develops when hydrostatic pressure rises, plasma oncotic pressure falls, vascular permeability increases or lymphatic drainage fails. Distribution and pitting distinguish systemic hydrostatic or oncotic fluid from local venous and chronic lymphatic swelling.
## sections
### Definition
Oedema is abnormal fluid accumulation in interstitial tissue or body cavities. A useful answer identifies both where the fluid is and which force or drainage pathway changed.

### Mechanism
Inflammatory oedema is driven primarily by increased vascular permeability. Congestive heart-failure oedema results from increased capillary hydrostatic pressure. Nephrotic oedema results from reduced plasma oncotic pressure. Cirrhotic ascites reflects increased hydrostatic pressure and reduced oncotic pressure. Hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid out of capillaries, plasma oncotic pressure draws it inward, permeability controls protein escape, and lymphatics return excess interstitial fluid and protein.

### Key determinants
Venous obstruction causes localised rather than generalised oedema. Congestive heart failure commonly produces pitting oedema. Filariasis causes lymphatic obstruction with non-pitting oedema. Local venous obstruction is not a generalised-oedema cause. Mobile hydrostatic or low-oncotic fluid usually pits, whereas chronic protein-rich lymphatic swelling becomes fibrotic and non-pitting.

### Clinical significance
Heart failure with crackles and dependent pitting oedema signals raised hydrostatic pressure. Nephrotic periorbital oedema signals reduced oncotic pressure. Cirrhotic ascites combines portal-hydrostatic and hypoalbuminaemic forces, so no single-force answer is sufficient for that vignette.

## sections
### Definition
A 5-mm subcutaneous haemorrhage is keyed as purpura. Petechiae, purpura and ecchymoses contain extravasated blood and do not blanch, whereas the source keys a blanching small red spot as an insect bite.

### Mechanism
The source relationships are retained exactly at the level required by the questions. They do not replace current clinical assessment, prevention or treatment guidance.

### Key determinants
A 5-mm subcutaneous haemorrhage is classified as purpura in the source
Polycythemia is the source-keyed exception to spontaneous-bleeding causes
A blanching 2-mm red spot is source-keyed as an insect bite rather than cutaneous haemorrhage
