## sections
### Definition
Renal drug handling depends on filtration, secretion, reabsorption, urine pH, and renal function. Family223 Q37-Q39 test pH-dependent ion trapping, while Q41 tests a renal-function effect on maintenance dosing.

### Mechanism
Alkaline urine ionises weak acids and acidic urine ionises weak bases, limiting passive tubular reabsorption. Reduced renal clearance removes less drug over time and may reduce the maintenance-dose requirement.

### Key determinants
Drug acid-base class, pKa, urine pH, protein binding, and renal clearance all matter. The review bank supplies qualitative comparisons rather than a complete clinical dosing model.

### Clinical significance
The source principles explain why renal impairment and urine pH can alter exposure. This Draft supplies no urine-pH manipulation or gentamicin regimen.

### Common misconceptions
Loading dose follows distribution, whereas maintenance dose follows clearance. Acidifying urine traps a weak base; alkalinising urine traps a weak acid.

### Source-tested concept lines
Ion trapping: a drug that ionises in a compartment cannot leave it, which is why urine pH is manipulated in overdose
Reduced renal function can require a lower gentamicin maintenance dose

## sections
### Definition
Bioavailability is the fraction of an administered dose that reaches systemic circulation. Intravenous administration is the 100% reference.

### Mechanism
An intravenous dose enters systemic circulation directly and therefore bypasses the absorption and presystemic-loss steps that can reduce availability by other routes.

### Key determinants
Route, absorption, formulation, and first-pass metabolism can alter bioavailability. Q40 tests route, not elimination speed or hepatic metabolic rate.

### Clinical significance
Complete bioavailability describes systemic entry, not the appropriateness or safety of an intravenous dose.

### Common misconceptions
Intravenous drug does not become 100% bioavailable by being rapidly absorbed; there is no absorption step from an administration site.

### Source-tested concept lines
Bioavailability is the fraction of an oral dose that reaches the systemic circulation, and first-pass metabolism is what removes the rest

## sections
### Definition
Biotransformation converts drugs into metabolites. Phase I includes oxidation, reduction, and hydrolysis; Phase II commonly conjugates a drug or metabolite with an endogenous substrate.

### Mechanism
Glucuronidation is a Phase II conjugation reaction. Metabolism usually increases polarity and water solubility, although a metabolite may be inactive, active, or toxic.

### Key determinants
Microsomal enzyme induction can accelerate metabolism of susceptible substrates. Its clinical effect depends on the drug, the pathway, and the metabolite, so the source carefully says some drugs may require an increased dose.

### Clinical significance
Reaction class and enzyme activity help predict exposure and interactions. This Draft makes no automatic dose recommendation.

### Common misconceptions
Protein binding is not metabolism, oxidation and reduction are not conjugation, and biotransformation does not generally make drugs more fat-soluble.

## summary

## sections
### Definition
A loading dose is an initial larger dose used to reach a target plasma concentration promptly. It avoids waiting through repeated maintenance doses.

### Mechanism
The amount needed to fill the apparent distribution volume follows from volume of distribution multiplied by target concentration. Clearance instead determines how much drug must be replaced over time.

### Key determinants
Volume of distribution and target concentration govern loading dose. Half-life explains how long untreated accumulation would take but is not the direct determinant printed in Q47.

### Clinical significance
Loading doses can be useful when prompt target exposure matters, but incorrect use can create a high early concentration. The source gives no patient-specific calculation.

### Common misconceptions
Do not substitute renal clearance or elimination-rate constant into the loading-dose relation, and do not assume every long-half-life drug automatically requires loading.

### Source-tested concept lines
A loading dose is an initial larger dose used to reach a target concentration promptly
Loading dose is governed by volume of distribution and target concentration
