## summary
Major receptor families differ in membrane topology, intracellular messenger pathways, response speed, duration, and representative agonists.
## sections
### Definition
A receptor converts ligand binding into a cellular response through an ion channel, G protein, enzyme-linked domain, or intracellular transcriptional mechanism.

### Mechanism
Ion channels alter ion flux directly. G proteins regulate second messengers, tyrosine kinases phosphorylate signalling proteins, and intracellular receptors alter gene transcription.

### Key determinants
- The source describes ion channel-linked receptors as typically containing five transmembrane subunits.
- Agonist binding to an ion channel-linked receptor opens the ion channel.
- Ion channel-linked receptors produce a fast, short response.
- Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the motor end plate open sodium-permeable channels.
- The source keys brain GABA receptors to opening chloride channels.
- Ion channel-linked receptors produce the fastest response among the listed receptor classes.
- G-protein-coupled receptors have seven transmembrane segments.
- G-protein-coupled receptors respond more slowly and for longer than ion channel-linked receptors.
- Stimulatory G protein increases adenylyl cyclase activity and intracellular cAMP.
- Increased cAMP activates cAMP-dependent protein kinases.
- Alpha-2 adrenergic receptors are coupled to inhibitory Gi protein.
- M2 muscarinic receptors are coupled to inhibitory Gi protein.
- Gq activation increases the second messengers IP3 and DAG.
- IP3 increases intracellular calcium.
- Alpha-1 adrenergic receptors are coupled to Gq protein.
- M1 and M3 muscarinic receptors are coupled to Gq protein.
- A tyrosine kinase-linked receptor has an intracellular domain that functions as a tyrosine kinase enzyme.
- Tyrosine kinase-linked receptors combine an extracellular hormone-binding domain with an intracellular tyrosine-kinase domain.
- The insulin receptor is a tyrosine kinase-linked receptor.
- Activation of tyrosine kinase-linked receptors activates intracellular signalling proteins.
- Intracellular receptors may be located in the cytoplasm or associated directly with DNA in the nucleus.
- Intracellular receptors regulate gene transcription.
- An agonist must enter the cell to act on an intracellular receptor.
- Intracellular receptors produce slow responses that require time for new protein synthesis rather than fast responses.
- Corticosteroids act as agonists at intracellular receptors.
- The source keys Thyroxin to action at an intracellular receptor.
- Sex hormone receptors are located inside the cell.
- Intracellular receptor effects can persist long after the agonist is removed.
- Ion channel-linked receptors act faster than intracellular receptors.

### Clinical significance
Receptor class helps predict onset, duration, messenger pathway, and the effects of agonists and antagonists.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse Gs, Gi, and Gq pathways; membrane receptors with intracellular receptors; or rapid ion flux with slow transcriptional responses.
