## summary
Question-led hand-hygiene teaching for hand-washing and alcohol hand rub with literal underlining provenance and current-guidance limitations.
## sections
### Definition
Hand washing uses mechanical rubbing, appropriate product, complete surface coverage, and adequate time. Alcohol-based hand rub offers point-of-care accessibility, and 70% is the suitable concentration in the source option set.

### Mechanism
Friction helps loosen material from the skin, while systematic rubbing reaches fingers, palms, nail beds, backs of hands, and wrists. Alcohol formulations act within a broader effective concentration range; exactly 70% is not the only effective formulation.

### Key determinants
Preserve the source distinction between friction, surface coverage, accessibility, and concentration. Do not convert the underlined wording into a universal rule that friction alone, antimicrobial soap alone, or exactly 70% alcohol determines effective hand hygiene.

### Clinical significance
Technique and access influence hand-hygiene performance. Every record remains Draft and requires current infection-control review before any protocol-facing use.

## sections
### Definition
Hand hygiene is indicated before patient contact and after glove removal. It is also performed when moving from wound dressing to medication administration for the same patient.

### Mechanism
Point-of-care availability permits cleaning when the indication occurs. Removing contaminated gloves and cleaning hands interrupts transfer from a dirty task to a cleaner task.

### Key determinants
Do not rub gloved hands, reuse gloves between patients, or carry wound-dressing gloves into medication administration. The ward-entry washing step belongs to the source scenario and does not replace indication-based hand hygiene.

### Clinical significance
Correct timing protects patients during direct contact and task transitions. The Draft preserves the source sequence without replacing local PPE, medication, or infection-control policy.

### Source-grounded claims
Point-of-care hand hygiene is performed after glove removal between patients.
Hand hygiene is performed before patient contact.
Gloves are removed and hand hygiene is performed before medication after wound dressing.

## summary

## sections
### Definition
Visible wound-drainage contamination calls for soap-and-water washing, and gloves do not replace indicated hand hygiene. The source also combines covered cuts, bare forearms, short clean nails, and no nail additions as preparation measures.

### Mechanism
Soap and water physically remove visible organic material. Hand hygiene remains necessary around glove use because contamination can occur during use or removal and unnoticed perforations may occur.

### Key determinants
Do not rely on alcohol hand cleaner alone for visibly soiled hands, paper-towel wiping, or gloves alone. Treat dress and nail requirements as setting-specific unless independently adopted by the institution.

### Clinical significance
Visible contamination and glove transitions are high-value infection-control moments. These tier-6 source answers remain needs-evidence teaching, not a published protocol.

### Source-grounded claims
The source combines all four listed preparation measures for effective hand hygiene.
