## sections
### Definition
First-pass extraction can reduce oral bioavailability, other drugs can alter metabolic enzymes, metabolism usually increases polarity, and the liver is the chief but not exclusive metabolic site.

### Mechanism
Route, lipid solubility, ionisation, binding, distribution barriers, enzyme activity, and body composition explain the source-tested relationships.

### Key determinants
First-pass extraction, free fraction, membrane permeability, metabolic capacity, lean mass, body water, and body fat determine relevant pharmacokinetic behaviour.

### Clinical significance
These are foundational teaching relationships and population tendencies, not individual dosing or treatment instructions.

### Common misconceptions
Do not treat the source’s bioavailability wording as a replacement for the formal systemic-circulation definition or passive diffusion as the only possible placental mechanism.

### Source-tested concept lines
Bioavailability is the fraction of an oral dose that reaches the systemic circulation, and first-pass metabolism is what removes the rest
Drug biotransformation can be altered by enzyme induction or inhibition by other drugs
Lipophilic drugs commonly proceed through Phase I then Phase II to become more water soluble
The liver is the main site of drug metabolism, but the gut wall, plasma, lung and kidney all metabolise drugs too
