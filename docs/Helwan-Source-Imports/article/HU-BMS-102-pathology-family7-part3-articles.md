## summary
Actinomycosis is a chronic suppurative granulomatous infection, most often cervicofacial, with grey-yellow sulphur granules in pus. Schistosomiasis forms parasitic granulomas and chronic urinary disease is associated with carcinoma of the bladder.
## sections
### Definition
Actinomycosis is a chronic suppurative granulomatous infection. Bilharziasis is associated with carcinoma of the urinary bladder.

### Mechanism
Actinomycotic bacterial colonies sit within abscesses and a surrounding chronic granulomatous response. Schistosomal ova provoke hypersensitivity-driven granulomas; prolonged urinary injury supplies a carcinogenic setting.

### Key determinants
Sulphur granules occur in actinomycosis. Actinomycosis most commonly affects the cervicofacial region.

### Clinical significance
Cervicofacial location plus draining suppuration and sulphur granules point toward actinomycosis. Bilharzial disease must be linked specifically to the urinary bladder when the question asks for the precancerous site.

## summary
Primary childhood pulmonary TB is represented by the Ghon complex, secondary TB causes marked local destruction through an accelerated hypersensitivity response, and miliary TB follows haematogenous dissemination.
## sections
### Definition
Childhood primary pulmonary tuberculosis is represented by a Ghon focus, lymphangitis and lymphadenitis without an apical cavity.

### Mechanism
Secondary tuberculosis causes extensive tissue destruction through an accelerated hypersensitivity response. Prior sensitisation accelerates the tissue reaction while acquired immunity limits lymph-node involvement.

### Key determinants
The primary complex contains Ghon focus, lymphangitis and lymphadenitis; bacteraemia is not a structural component, and an apical cavity indicates secondary disease. Miliary tuberculosis results from haematogenous spread.

### Clinical significance
Classify the phase before interpreting morphology: childhood primary disease forms a complex, secondary disease is locally destructive and often apical, and bloodstream seeding produces the miliary pattern.
