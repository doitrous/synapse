## summary
Elimination half-life and clearance determine concentration decline, dosing intervals, accumulation, and the time required to approach steady state.
## sections
### Definition
Drug elimination removes parent drug or metabolites, while elimination half-life describes the time required for an amount or concentration to fall by half.

### Mechanism
Clearance and distribution determine half-life. With repeated dosing or constant infusion, accumulation approaches a plateau over successive half-lives.

### Key determinants
- With a two-hour half-life, a 200 mg amount falls to 25 mg after six hours, or three half-lives.
- A continuously infused drug with a ten-hour half-life approaches steady state after about forty to fifty hours.
- When a dose is repeated every half-life, the accumulated amount immediately before the third dose is about seventy-five percent of steady state.
- Elimination half-life depends on distribution volume and clearance-related elimination, not on the time required for absorption.
- A concentration decline from 32 mg/L to 2 mg/L spans four half-lives, so a six-hour half-life requires twenty-four hours.
- The liver eliminates drugs and metabolites into bile.
- Therapeutic drug monitoring supports effective exposure while reducing toxicity risk.
- In zero-order elimination, the elimination rate is not proportional to plasma concentration.
- A loading dose is used to reach a target or steady-state concentration rapidly.
- When gentamicin clearance is one third of normal, reducing a 100 mg maintenance dose to about 33 mg at the same interval preserves proportional exposure.
- A drug’s elimination half-life helps determine the time required to reach steady state.
- After four half-lives of constant input, drug concentration reaches about ninety-four percent of steady state.
- A drug’s elimination half-life helps determine a suitable interdose interval.
- A 5 mg/L level at six hours with a three-hour half-life implies an initial 20 mg/L level; with a 10 L distribution volume, the IV dose was 200 mg.
- In first-order elimination, the amount eliminated per unit time varies directly with drug amount or concentration.
- After two half-lives, twenty-five percent of the original ibuprofen amount remains.
- Repeated dosing at half-life intervals reaches approximate steady state after four to five half-lives.
- With a forty-hour half-life and daily dosing, piroxicam approaches steady state around the eighth daily dose.

### Clinical significance
Half-life, clearance, distribution volume, loading dose, and therapeutic monitoring guide safe dosing and interpretation of drug levels.
