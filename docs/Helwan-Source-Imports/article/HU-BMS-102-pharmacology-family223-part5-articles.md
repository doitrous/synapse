## sections
### Definition
Biotransformation commonly changes lipophilic drugs into more polar products. Phase I uses oxidation, reduction, or hydrolysis, while Phase II commonly conjugates a drug or metabolite.

### Mechanism
Microsomal oxidation can increase ionisation and water solubility, and microsomal enzymes can be induced. Metabolism can also activate a prodrug rather than simply inactivate a drug.

### Key determinants
Reaction class, enzyme abundance, substrate specificity, and metabolite activity determine the outcome. Not every drug undergoes both phases.

### Clinical significance
Metabolism can alter exposure, activity, toxicity, and excretion. This Draft makes no automatic dose recommendation.

### Common misconceptions
Oxidation does not always inactivate or detoxify, and increased lipid solubility is not the usual direction that facilitates excretion.

## sections
### Definition
Apparent volume of distribution relates the amount of drug in the body to plasma concentration. Maintenance dosing replaces drug eliminated over time and therefore depends on clearance.

### Mechanism
Reduced renal clearance can reduce the amount that must be replaced over a dosing interval. Family223 Q52 applies a simplified proportional reduction to its stated gentamicin regimen.

### Key determinants
Actual gentamicin dosing depends on weight, renal function, indication, measured concentrations, interval, and local guidance. The carrier supplies only simplified review-bank arithmetic.

### Clinical significance
Vd and clearance answer different questions: distribution sets a loading amount, while clearance contributes to maintenance dosing.

### Common misconceptions
Do not convert Q52 into a clinical prescription, and do not define Vd by dose per body weight or by bioavailability.

## sections
### Definition
Absorption moves drug from an administration site toward systemic circulation; bioavailability is the fraction reaching systemic circulation. Lipid solubility favours passive membrane and blood-brain-barrier passage.

### Mechanism
Oral drug commonly enters portal blood and passes through the liver before systemic circulation. Source Q57 calculates 60%, while Q53 preserves the bank's simplified acidic-drug exception.

### Key determinants
Molecular size, lipid solubility, ionisation, surface area, perfusion, formulation, transit, and first-pass loss all affect absorption or availability.

### Clinical significance
The source comparisons explain route and barrier effects but do not recommend a route or dose.

### Common misconceptions
Unchanged gastrointestinal absorption is not always identical to systemic bioavailability, and pH partition alone does not fully predict total intestinal absorption.

### Source-tested concept lines

## sections
### Definition
Plasma-protein binding partitions circulating drug into bound and unbound fractions. Only unbound drug is immediately available for filtration, diffusion, and target interaction.

### Mechanism
Reversible binding can form a circulating reservoir that releases drug as free drug is removed, which may prolong apparent action.

### Key determinants
Binding affinity, protein concentration, competing drugs, tissue binding, metabolism, and renal clearance all shape duration.

### Clinical significance
High binding may limit filtration and distribution and may create displacement interactions. This Draft does not infer a dose adjustment.

### Common misconceptions
High protein binding does not necessarily increase Vd, facilitate glomerular filtration, or minimise interactions.

### Source-tested concept lines
High plasma protein binding can prolong drug action by providing a circulating reservoir
