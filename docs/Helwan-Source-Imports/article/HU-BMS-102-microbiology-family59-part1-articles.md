## sections
### Definition
The minimum inhibitory concentration is the lowest tested antimicrobial concentration that prevents visible growth of the organism under the specified conditions. In broth dilution, visible turbidity indicates growth and a clear tube indicates inhibition.

### Mechanism
A constant inoculum is added to serial antimicrobial dilutions and incubated with appropriate controls. Reading proceeds across the concentration series to locate the transition between visibly clear and turbid tubes.

### Key determinants
In broth dilution, the MIC is the lowest tested antibiotic concentration with no visible growth. In the Family-59 figure, 0.50 µg/ml is visibly turbid while the next higher tested concentration is clear, so the statement that the MIC is 0.50 µg/ml is false.

### Clinical significance
MIC interpretation requires a valid concentration series, inoculum, medium, incubation conditions and controls. A teaching image cannot substitute for a validated laboratory result or current organism-drug breakpoint interpretation.
