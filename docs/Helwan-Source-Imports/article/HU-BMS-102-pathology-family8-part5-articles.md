## summary
Increased permeability produces swelling and a cell-rich inflammatory exudate. Leukocytes then follow chemotactic signals such as C5a, while opsonins improve target recognition and phagocytosis.
## sections
### Definition
Inflammatory exudate contains more blood-derived cells than transudate. Chemotaxis is directed leukocyte movement toward an irritant.

### Mechanism
Increased vascular permeability produces inflammatory swelling. After emigration, leukocytes follow chemical gradients and recognize opsonized targets.

### Key determinants
Complement C5a acts as a leukocyte chemotactic agent. An opsonin enhances phagocytosis.

### Clinical significance
The cardinal swelling of acute inflammation reflects fluid escape, while cell recruitment and phagocytosis explain how the exudate becomes an active defensive response.

## summary
Exotoxin-driven pseudomembranous inflammation can produce systemic toxaemia. Serous inflammation forms burn blisters, Langhans giant cells show a peripheral horseshoe nuclear pattern, and coagulase helps localize a staphylococcal abscess.
## sections
### Definition
A skin burn produces serous inflammation. Langhans giant cells have peripherally arranged horseshoe-shaped nuclei.

### Mechanism
Pseudomembranous inflammation can cause serious systemic toxaemia. Coagulase promotes localization of a staphylococcal abscess.

### Key determinants
Toxaemia is toxin in the circulation, a Langhans cell is identified by nuclear arrangement, and coagulase localizes rather than spreads suppuration.

### Clinical significance
These distinctions separate four mechanisms that can all appear under the broad heading of inflammation: surface toxin injury, thin serous exudation, granulomatous macrophage fusion and localized pus formation.
