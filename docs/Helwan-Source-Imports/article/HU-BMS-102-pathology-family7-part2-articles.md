## summary
Primary tuberculosis means first infection in a previously uninfected host. Its site follows the portal of entry, while pulmonary disease is recognised clinically by chronic constitutional symptoms and productive cough, particularly when caseous material is present.
## sections
### Definition
Primary tuberculosis is infection occurring for the first time in a previously uninfected host.

### Mechanism
Primary tuberculosis may begin in lung, tonsil, intestine or skin according to the portal of entry; kidney is not a primary portal site. Inhalation produces a pulmonary focus, ingestion can involve tonsil or intestine, and inoculation can involve skin.

### Key determinants
The word primary refers to first infection, not to an unknown site or absent immune response. Kidney, bone, adrenal and brain disease can occur through spread but are not the listed primary portals in this source.

### Clinical significance
Pulmonary tuberculosis can present with low-grade fever, night sweats, cough and blood-stained caseous expectoration. The combination separates pulmonary TB from congestion, oedema, metastatic tumour and a routine pyogenic abscess in the keyed vignette.

## summary
The tubercle is a granulomatous macrophage response containing epithelioid cells, Langhans giant cells and lymphocytes. Central caseous necrosis is the characteristic tuberculosis pattern and is linked in this curriculum to hypersensitivity-mediated tissue injury.
## sections
### Definition
A caseating granuloma is characteristic of tuberculosis. Epithelioid cells in tuberculous granulomas are transformed macrophages or histiocytes.

### Mechanism
Macrophage activation produces epithelioid cells and multinucleated Langhans-type giant cells. Tuberculosis produces characteristic caseous necrosis through hypersensitivity-mediated tissue injury.

### Key determinants
The tubercle contains epithelioid cells, Langhans giant cells, histiocytes and lymphocytes rather than eosinophils or Mikulicz cells. Eosinophils and Mikulicz cells are distractors from other inflammatory patterns.

### Clinical significance
Caseation distinguishes the source-keyed TB granuloma from noncaseating sarcoidosis and suppurative actinomycosis. Necrosis type and cellular lineage should be identified independently before choosing an answer.
