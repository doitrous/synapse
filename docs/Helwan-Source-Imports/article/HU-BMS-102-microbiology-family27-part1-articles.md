## sections
### Definition
Bacteriophages infect bacteria. Temperate phage DNA may persist in a bacterial cell as a prophage, establishing lysogeny.

### Mechanism
In lysogeny the prophage is maintained without immediate productive lysis, so the phage is described as latent in the host. Prophage carriage can alter bacterial properties, including toxin production.

### Key determinants
Phages can mediate bacterial gene transfer. Differences in bacterial susceptibility to phages also create lysis patterns used in phage typing and epidemiologic source tracing.

### Clinical significance
The source’s property exception separates bacteriophages from viruses that usually infect humans, while the typing item applies bacterial host specificity to outbreak investigation.

### Common pitfalls
Do not confuse prophage with inclusion bodies, immediate lysis with lysogeny, or genetic elements such as plasmids and transposons with phage typing.

### Source-grounded claims
Bacteriophages infect bacteria rather than humans and can mediate gene transfer, toxin-related changes and typing.
Phage typing uses bacterial susceptibility patterns to support epidemiologic source tracing.
A lysogenic bacterial cell contains phage DNA maintained as a prophage.
A bacteriophage is latent in its bacterial host during lysogeny.

## sections
### Definition
Genotypic variation is heritable variation caused by an underlying genetic change. Plasmids and transposons are distinct bacterial genetic elements.

### Mechanism
Gene transfer can introduce new genetic material and contribute to genotypic variation. Plasmids replicate autonomously outside the chromosome, whereas transposons are mobile DNA segments that relocate within or between DNA molecules.

### Key determinants
Plasmids are generally double-stranded, smaller than the chromosome and useful as cloning vectors. Their independent replication makes the source’s chromosome-dependent statement the exception.

### Clinical significance
Mobile and extrachromosomal elements influence bacterial adaptation and dissemination of accessory traits, while remaining structurally and mechanistically distinct.

### Common pitfalls
Do not treat environmentally reversible phenotypic change as genotypic variation, read the source typo “dependably” as autonomous replication, or identify an entire plasmid when the stem asks for the mobile segment.

### Source-grounded claims
Gene transfer can produce heritable bacterial genotypic variation.
Plasmids replicate autonomously rather than dependently on the bacterial chromosome.
A transposon is a mobile bacterial DNA segment that can move between genomic locations.

## sections
### Definition
Transformation is uptake of free DNA by a recipient bacterial cell. Competence describes the recipient state that permits this uptake.

### Mechanism
Some bacteria transform naturally. In laboratory work, calcium chloride treatment followed by heat shock can enhance competence and facilitate entry of DNA.

### Key determinants
Transformation does not require F-plasmid mating or a temperate bacteriophage, and it is not limited to transfer of plasmids only.

### Clinical significance
Induced competence is foundational to bacterial genetic engineering and experimental introduction of recombinant plasmids.

### Common pitfalls
Do not substitute conjugation or transduction mechanisms when the tested statement specifically describes calcium-chloride treatment of recipient cells.

### Source-grounded claims
Calcium chloride treatment can enhance recipient-cell competence for bacterial transformation.
