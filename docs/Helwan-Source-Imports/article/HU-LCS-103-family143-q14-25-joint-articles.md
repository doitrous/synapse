## summary
Family-143 Q14–Q18 distinguish monosodium-urate gout and podagra from calcium-pyrophosphate pseudogout.

## sections
### Definition
Gout is monosodium-urate crystal arthritis, while pseudogout is calcium-pyrophosphate crystal arthritis. Family-143 tests the crystal identity and the great-toe term podagra.

### Mechanism
Gout follows hyperuricaemia with monosodium-urate deposition in and around joints. Pseudogout follows calcium-pyrophosphate deposition in the joint space and is presented in an older-patient inflammatory-arthritis vignette.

### Key determinants
Recurrent severe great-toe pain, monosodium urate and podagra point to gout. Calcium pyrophosphate points to pseudogout, not tophi, hyperuricaemia, great-toe involvement or monosodium urate.

### Clinical significance
These are local first-year pathology distinctions rather than a diagnostic or treatment protocol. All records remain Draft pending independent medical and named faculty review.

### Common misconceptions
Do not interchange the crystal types. Do not call calcium-pyrophosphate deposition gout or call podagra a wrist, knee, spine or hip disorder.

### Source-bound statements
The local joint sources present recurrent severe great-toe attacks, monosodium urate deposition and the term podagra as linked gout cues.
The local joint sources present pseudogout as calcium-pyrophosphate crystal arthritis rather than monosodium-urate gout.

## summary
Family-143 Q19–Q22 distinguish a clear-fluid dorsal-wrist ganglion from mechanically provoked prepatellar bursitis and its source-keyed histology.

## sections
### Definition
A ganglion is a small movable periarticular cyst, commonly on the dorsal wrist, containing clear mucinous fluid. Bursitis is inflammation of a synovial-lined bursa over a bony prominence.

### Mechanism
Ganglion formation may follow synovial herniation, displaced synovial tissue or connective-tissue degeneration; its wall is dense or oedematous connective tissue. Repeated mechanical pressure can inflame the prepatellar bursa in housemaid’s knee, with fibrous wall and inflammatory granulation tissue.

### Key determinants
A movable dorsal-wrist cyst with clear mucinous fluid and a dense connective-tissue wall points to ganglion. Painful prepatellar swelling after repeated kneeling points to bursitis.

### Clinical significance
These are local first-year pathology distinctions and not management guidance. Q22 retains an unqualified source stem and remains Draft pending independent medical and named faculty review.

### Common misconceptions
Do not call clear mucinous ganglion fluid a solid tumour. Do not generalise the Q22 chronic inflammatory-cell pattern to every acute bursal process.

### Source-bound statements
The local joint sources present ganglion as a movable dorsal-wrist cyst with clear mucinous fluid and a dense connective-tissue wall.
The local joint sources present repeated kneeling as a setting for prepatellar bursitis and print plasma cells, lymphocytes and macrophages as its histologic infiltrate.

## summary
Family-143 Q23–Q25 contrast a sacral notochordal chordoma with a calcified epiphyseal chondroblastoma in a young patient.

## sections
### Definition
Chordoma is a slow-growing malignant tumour arising from notochordal remnants in the axial skeleton. Chondroblastoma is a relatively rare benign epiphyseal tumour of young patients.

### Mechanism
Chordoma contains highly vacuolated cells in abundant mucoid matrix and is especially associated with sacral and spheno-occipital sites. Chondroblastoma arises near the epiphyseal cartilage plate and may show calcification, chondroblast-like cells and osteoclast-like giant cells.

### Key determinants
A slow-growing malignant sacral tumour from notochordal remnants points to chordoma; Q24 prints Physaliphorous cells. A calcified lytic proximal-tibial epiphyseal lesion in a 17-year-old with chondroblasts and osteoclast-like giant cells points to chondroblastoma.

### Clinical significance
These tumour descriptions are local curriculum claims, not diagnostic protocols. Q24 and Q25 remain explicit Draft review items pending independent medical and named faculty review.

### Common misconceptions
Do not silently respell Q24 or substitute cartilage, plasma or giant-cell distractors. Do not confuse a young epiphyseal chondroblastoma with a sacral notochordal chordoma.

### Source-bound statements
The local joint sources present chordoma as a slow-growing malignant sacral tumour from notochordal remnants with physaliphorous cells.
