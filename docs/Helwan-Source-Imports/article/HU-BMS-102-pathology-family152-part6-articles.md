## summary
A source-bounded guide to ischemia, infarction, end-arterial obstruction and red-versus-pale patterns.
## sections
### Definition
Infarction is ischemic tissue necrosis. The source cluster distinguishes acute complete end-arterial obstruction, anemic infarction in solid end-arterial organs, and hemorrhagic infarction in intestine. Several “always” formulations remain bounded to their exact option sets.

### Mechanism
Vascular obstruction reduces perfusion; the source then classifies infarcts by the involved organ and the red-or-pale pattern printed in each option set.

### Key determinants
Renal arterial occlusion produces an anemic infarction in the source option set
Thrombosis is the source-keyed commonest cause of ischemia
Infarction is necrosis of tissue caused by ischemia
Infarction follows acute complete obstruction of an end artery in the source model
Intestinal infarction is hemorrhagic or red in the source classification
Thrombocytopenia is the source-keyed exception among listed causes of ischemia
Splenic arterial embolization is source-keyed as a site where infarction may be red or pale
The heart is the source-keyed site of pale infarction

### Clinical significance
Use these records only to distinguish the literal source option sets.

### Common misconceptions
Do not promote “always” wording or tier-3 answers to official examination or clinical authority.

## summary
The source cluster separates abrupt ischaemic mechanisms and dry/infective gangrene features.
## sections
### Definition
Acute ischaemia follows abrupt arterial occlusion, embolic impaction or twisting. Dry gangrene is ischemic, mummified and demarcated; bubbles suggest an infective/gas-forming process rather than dry gangrene. Senile gangrene remains outside the infective category.

### Mechanism
The source relationships are retained exactly at the level required by the questions. They do not replace current clinical assessment, prevention or treatment guidance.

### Key determinants
Gradual arterial compression by an enlarged lymph node is the source-keyed exception to acute ischaemia causes
Dry gangrene shows demarcation, separation and mummification but not gas bubbles
Senile gangrene is not an infective-gangrene type

### Clinical significance
Use the records to discriminate terminology and mechanisms in the printed option sets.

### Common misconceptions
Do not promote competition reveals to official exam keys or infer facts beyond the printed alternatives.

### Family152 Part6 gangrene closure
Gangrene combines necrosis with putrefaction. The source cluster distinguishes dry, gradually ischemic and demarcated limb gangrene from wet, rapidly putrefactive gangrene associated with combined arterial and venous compromise, intestinal involvement and systemic toxicity.
Gangrene combines tissue necrosis with putrefactive digestion of dead tissue
Dry gangrene shows demarcation, separation and mummification but not gas bubbles
Wet gangrene is source-associated with intestinal involvement, combined arterial and venous occlusion, and severe toxemia
Dry gangrene occurs in limbs after gradual arterial obstruction in the source model
