## summary
Acute inflammation combines protective local events with systemic effects and possible harm. IL-1 drives hypothalamic fever, chemotaxis directs leukocytes, swelling can impair function, and incompletely evacuated suppuration can become chronic and eventually contribute to amyloidosis.
## sections
### Definition
Chemotaxis is directional leukocyte movement toward an inflammatory irritant. Interleukin-1 induces fever by acting on the hypothalamus.

### Mechanism
Protein-rich fluid escape precedes leukocyte emigration in acute inflammation. Fluid escape and emigration prepare leukocytes to follow chemotactic gradients into tissue.

### Key determinants
Tissue swelling can be a harmful effect of acute inflammation. Protective effects include dilution, fibrin, phagocytosis and immune stimulation.

### Clinical significance
Incomplete evacuation of an acute abscess can lead to chronic abscess formation and secondary amyloidosis. Chronicity, not the initial acute abscess, creates the prolonged inflammatory setting for secondary amyloidosis.

## summary
Suppurative inflammation produces pus through necrosis and abundant neutrophils. Localized pus forms an abscess, diffuse spread forms cellulitis, staphylococci favour abscesses, and a carbuncle is a clustered suppurative lesion; catarrhal inflammation instead produces superficial mucosal discharge.
## sections
### Definition
An abscess is an acute localized suppurative inflammation. Catarrhal inflammation produces discharge from superficial mucosal surfaces.

### Mechanism
Staphylococci cause suppurative inflammation. Coagulase helps localize the process, while necrosis and abundant neutrophils generate pus.

### Key determinants
Suppurative inflammation includes abscess and cellulitis with necrosis and abundant neutrophils. A carbuncle is an example of acute suppurative inflammation.

### Clinical significance
An abscess is localized, cellulitis is diffuse, and a carbuncle is a connected cluster of cutaneous abscesses. A superficial mucosal discharge points away from pus-forming lesions and toward catarrhal inflammation.
