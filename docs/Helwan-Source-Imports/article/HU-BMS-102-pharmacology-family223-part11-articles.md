## sections
### Definition
Ionisation and plasma binding determine how much drug is available for renal filtration or passive reabsorption. Alkaline urine favours excretion of weak acids, while only unbound drug is freely filtered.

### Mechanism
Ionisation, protein binding, enzyme location, first-pass extraction, and venous drainage explain the source-tested relationships.

### Key determinants
Drug acid-base character, free fraction, metabolic pathway, route, and enzyme capacity determine the relevant process.

### Clinical significance
These relationships support foundational pharmacology reasoning but do not supply dosing, poisoning-management, or individual prescribing instructions.

### Common misconceptions
Do not infer that every metabolite is inactive, every highly bound drug is long acting, or every already-absorbed sublingual effect stops immediately when the tablet is removed.

### Source-tested concept lines
Ion trapping: a drug that ionises in a compartment cannot leave it, which is why urine pH is manipulated in overdose
High plasma protein binding can prolong drug action by providing a circulating reservoir
Plasma protein binding limits the fraction of a drug available for glomerular filtration
