## sections
### Definition
GABAA is a ligand-gated chloride channel, while alpha-2, beta, M1, and M3 receptors signal through G proteins.

### Mechanism
Ion channels change membrane conductance directly; GPCRs signal through G proteins and intracellular effectors.

### Key determinants
Receptor family, coupling protein, ion selectivity, and downstream effector determine signalling.

### Clinical significance
The source tests receptor classification rather than treatment selection.

### Common misconceptions
Do not classify GABAA as a GPCR or generalise this item to every GABA receptor subtype.

### Source-tested concept lines
GABAA receptors are ligand-gated ion channels rather than G-protein-coupled receptors

## sections
### Definition
Drug action commonly terminates as metabolism and excretion reduce exposure. Tissue distribution depends on free-drug gradients, perfusion, affinity, concentration, and tissue capacity.

### Mechanism
Only unbound drug crosses membranes immediately; metabolism and renal excretion progressively remove active drug.

### Key determinants
Blood flow, binding, tissue solubility, concentration gradients, metabolism, and renal function influence disposition.

### Clinical significance
These relationships support pharmacokinetic interpretation, not dosing decisions.

### Common misconceptions
Drug need not always be fully excreted before action ends, and total plasma concentration alone does not define tissue transfer.

### Source-tested concept lines
Hepatic metabolism and renal excretion are major mechanisms terminating drug action
Tissue distribution depends on the unbound drug concentration gradient between blood and tissue
Blood flow, tissue solubility, blood concentration, and tissue volume can determine drug distribution

## sections
### Definition
Many orally administered drugs are absorbed efficiently from the duodenum, and solution formulations generally become available faster than listed solid or suspended forms.

### Mechanism
The proximal small intestine provides extensive surface area, while a dissolved drug bypasses disintegration and particle-dissolution steps.

### Key determinants
Surface area, perfusion, ionisation, gastric emptying, dissolution, vehicle, and release design affect absorption rate.

### Clinical significance
These are general formulation comparisons and not universal dosing rules.

### Common misconceptions
Do not infer that every drug is best absorbed in the duodenum or that every solution has identical bioavailability.

### Source-tested concept lines
Family223 prints duodenum as the best general absorption site after peroral administration
A solution generally provides the fastest bioavailability rate among the listed dosage forms
