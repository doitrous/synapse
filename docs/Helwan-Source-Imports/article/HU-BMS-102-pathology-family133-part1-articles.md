## summary
The source cluster distinguishes valve vegetations, coagulation pathways, thrombosis risks, clot morphology and travel-related stasis prevention.
## sections
### Definition
Thrombosis rests on endothelial injury, abnormal flow and hypercoagulability. Valve-surface thrombi are called vegetations; thrombi remain distinct from post-mortem clots. The travel item preserves its printed prevention exception but is not promoted to patient guidance.

### Mechanism
The source relationships are retained exactly at the level required by the questions. They do not replace current clinical assessment, prevention or treatment guidance.

### Key determinants
Cardiac-valve thrombi are termed vegetations
Factor VII belongs to the extrinsic rather than common coagulation pathway
Virchow-triad thrombosis mechanisms distinguish prothrombotic conditions from option-set exceptions
A clot is distinguished from a thrombus by composition, attachment, surface and timing
Movement breaks, hydration and risk reduction are source-listed travel-thromboembolism prevention measures

## summary
The source cluster separates abrupt ischaemic mechanisms and dry/infective gangrene features.
## sections
### Definition
Acute ischaemia follows abrupt arterial occlusion, embolic impaction or twisting. Dry gangrene is ischemic, mummified and demarcated; bubbles suggest an infective/gas-forming process rather than dry gangrene. Senile gangrene remains outside the infective category.

### Mechanism
The source relationships are retained exactly at the level required by the questions. They do not replace current clinical assessment, prevention or treatment guidance.

### Key determinants
Gradual arterial compression by an enlarged lymph node is the source-keyed exception to acute ischaemia causes
Dry gangrene shows demarcation, separation and mummification but not gas bubbles
Senile gangrene is not an infective-gangrene type

## sections

### Mechanism
The source relationships are retained exactly at the level required by the questions. They do not replace current clinical assessment, prevention or treatment guidance.
