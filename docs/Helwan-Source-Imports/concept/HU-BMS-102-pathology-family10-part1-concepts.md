Pathogenesis is the mechanism through which a cause produces disease manifestations
## id

pathology.general.pathogenesis-disease-mechanism-term
## aliases
Pathogenesis definition
Disease mechanism
Aetiology versus pathogenesis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases
[clear]
## definition
Pathogenesis is the chain of mechanisms through which an aetiologic factor produces structural, functional and clinical manifestations of disease. Aetiology names the cause, morphology describes structural change, and fate describes the outcome rather than the causal mechanism.
## explicit_objective
Distinguish pathogenesis from aetiology, morphologic change and disease outcome.
## pitfalls
Using aetiology and pathogenesis as synonyms, or choosing morphology when the stem asks how the cause produces the manifestations.

## definition
Increased microvascular permeability allows protein-rich fluid and inflammatory cells to escape into tissue, forming exudate. Increased hydrostatic pressure without endothelial leak more often produces a protein-poor transudate, while trauma may cause frank haemorrhage.
## explicit_objective
Identify increased capillary permeability as the mechanism producing inflammatory exudate.
## pitfalls
Choosing increased hydrostatic pressure, blood flow or vessel trauma without distinguishing exudate from transudate and haemorrhage.
