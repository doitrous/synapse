Triclabendazole, not praziquantel, is the drug of choice for Fasciola infection

## definition
Triclabendazole is the drug of choice for both Fasciola hepatica and Fasciola gigantica infection. Praziquantel, broadly effective against most other trematodes and cestodes, is ineffective against Fasciola because the adult fluke's tegument resists the drug's calcium-channel-mediated mechanism.

## explicit_objective
Name triclabendazole as the drug of choice for fascioliasis and state that praziquantel is not effective against it.

## pitfalls
Assuming praziquantel, being broadly anti-trematode, also covers Fasciola; Fasciola is the standard teaching exception to praziquantel's spectrum.

## arabic_label
أنواع السركاريا: مذنبة الذيل الطويل والمهدبة

## definition
Leptocercous (long-tailed) cercariae emerge from Lymnaea truncatula, Lymnaea caillaudi and the planorbid Segmentina snail, and develop into Fasciola hepatica, Fasciola gigantica and Fasciolopsis buski respectively. Match leptocercous and lophocercous cercarial morphology to their respective snail hosts and the flukes they produce.

## pitfalls
Assuming a single cercarial tail morphology applies across all the GI/hepatic trematodes; the leptocercous/lophocercous distinction tracks a real difference in snail host and resulting fluke.

## definition
Halzoon (pharyngeal fascioliasis) follows eating raw or undercooked liver from infected sheep, goats or cattle that still contains live adult Fasciola worms; the worms attach to the pharyngeal or laryngeal mucosa, causing oedema, dysphagia and potentially suffocation. It is caused by the migrating adult worm, not by an encysted metacercaria, and is treated by gargling with an astringent (vinegar or alcoholic) solution that detaches the worms.

## explicit_objective
Explain halzoon as adult-worm attachment to the pharynx from eating raw infected liver, and name its astringent-gargle treatment.

## pitfalls
Attributing halzoon to the encysted metacercaria stage; it is the adult worm, released alive from raw infected liver, that attaches to the pharynx and causes the syndrome.

## label
Eating liver from an infected animal can produce false (spurious) fascioliasis, with dead adult worms or transiting eggs passing through without true infection

## canonical_key
fasciola.spurious-and-false-infection.liver-ingestion

## aliases
Spurious fascioliasis
False fascioliasis
Pseudo-parasitism

## arabic_label
العدوى الكاذبة (الزائفة) بالفاشيولا

## arabic_aliases


## definition
False (spurious) fascioliasis results from eating liver from an infected animal that contains dead adult Fasciola worms or unabsorbed eggs, which then pass through the human gut and appear in stool without the person ever being truly infected. Fasciola gigantica is the species classically cited for this phenomenon, and it is distinguished from true infection by the absence of any established adult worm in the patient's own bile ducts.

## explicit_objective
Define false (spurious) fascioliasis as transit of liver-derived material through the gut rather than true established infection.

## pitfalls
Mistaking Fasciola material recovered from stool after a liver meal for true infection; without an adult worm established in the patient's own biliary tree, it is spurious passage, not genuine disease.

## definition
Immunodiagnosis (serological testing) is valuable for parasitic infections whose acute or larval phase precedes, or bypasses entirely, egg or larva shedding into stool — acute fascioliasis, acute clonorchiasis and visceral larva migrans (Toxocara) are all recognised uses. Trichostrongylus, an intestinal nematode whose adult stage and eggs are reliably found directly in stool, does not need serological diagnosis and is the exception among these options.

## explicit_objective
State which parasitic infections use serological (immunodiagnostic) testing and why Trichostrongylus does not need it.

## pitfalls
Assuming serology is used for every helminth infection; it is reserved for tissue-invasive or pre-patent infections, while a readily stool-diagnosed nematode like Trichostrongylus does not require it.
