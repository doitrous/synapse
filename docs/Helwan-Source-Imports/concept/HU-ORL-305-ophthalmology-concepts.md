## label
Aqueous humour is produced by the ciliary body into the posterior chamber and drains through the anterior chamber angle

## canonical_key
aqueous-humour.production-and-drainage

## aliases
Aqueous humour production
Aqueous humour drainage
Aqueous dynamics

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Aqueous humour, the clear fluid filling the anterior and posterior chambers, is secreted by the non-pigmented epithelium of the ciliary body into the posterior chamber. It flows forward through the pupil into the anterior chamber and drains out through the trabecular meshwork at the anterior chamber angle into Schlemm's canal. Neither the posterior iris surface nor the lens plays any part in its production or drainage.

## explicit_objective
State that aqueous humour is produced by the ciliary body into the posterior chamber, and drains through the anterior chamber angle.

## pitfalls
Confusing the site of production (posterior chamber, ciliary body) with the site of drainage (anterior chamber angle) — they are different chambers and different structures.

The eye's outer fibrous coat (cornea and sclera) protects against microbial invasion, in addition to the cornea's own refractive role

## canonical_key

## aliases
Fibrous tunic
Outer coat of the eye

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
The outer coat of the eyeball is the fibrous tunic, formed of the cornea anteriorly and the sclera posteriorly — the iris belongs to the middle, not the outer, coat. Beyond its mechanical protective role, the cornea additionally contributes the eye's principal refractive power, so the outer coat's function is not purely protective. Its protective role includes guarding the eye's interior against the invasion of micro-organisms.

## explicit_objective
State the two components of the outer fibrous coat and its protective (not solely protective) function.

## pitfalls
Assuming the outer coat is only protective, when the cornea also contributes the eye's principal refractive power.

The cornea's five layers are epithelium, Bowman's layer, stroma, Descemet's membrane and endothelium — not Bruch's membrane, which belongs to the choroid

cornea.structural-layers

Corneal layers

The cornea is classically described in five layers: the non-keratinised epithelium, Bowman's layer, the stroma (collagen fibrils regularly arranged in parallel lamellae, the arrangement responsible for transparency), Descemet's membrane and the endothelium. ## explicit_objective
List the cornea's five layers and distinguish Bruch's membrane (choroid) as not one of them.

## pitfalls
Confusing Bruch's membrane (choroid) with Bowman's or Descemet's membrane (both genuine corneal layers).

The slit lamp is the primary tool for anterior-segment examination, and needs accessory lenses to examine the fundus or the anterior chamber angle

## canonical_key
slit-lamp.examination-role

Slit lamp examination
Slit lamp biomicroscopy

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
The slit lamp biomicroscope is the single most important instrument for detailed anterior-segment examination (cornea, anterior chamber, iris, lens), used in essentially every routine eye examination. On its own it cannot examine the fundus or the anterior chamber angle: fundus examination needs an accessory condensing or contact lens, and angle examination needs a gonioscopy lens, because the angle cannot be viewed directly through the cornea without one (total internal reflection otherwise blocks the view).

## explicit_objective
State the slit lamp's primary role (anterior segment) and that fundus/angle examination need an accessory lens.

## pitfalls
Assuming the slit lamp alone (without an accessory lens) can examine the fundus or the anterior chamber angle.

## definition
Diabetic retinopathy is diagnosed and staged by direct examination of the retina — dilated fundus examination (ophthalmoscopy or slit-lamp biomicroscopy with a fundus lens), or fundus photography — since its defining features (microaneurysms, haemorrhages, exudates, neovascularisation) are only visible on the fundus itself. Fundus examination is therefore essential in any patient with diabetes; dense media opacity such as mature cataract obstructs, rather than eases, this assessment.

## explicit_objective
State that fundus examination is essential (not optional) for diagnosing diabetic retinopathy.

## pitfalls
Assuming diabetic retinopathy can be diagnosed without direct fundus examination.

## definition
Proptosis (forward globe displacement) results from processes that increase orbital content or congest orbital venous drainage: orbital cellulitis (inflammatory oedema), cavernous sinus thrombosis (venous congestion via the ophthalmic veins), and thyroid eye disease (expanded orbital fat/muscle). Acute angle-closure glaucoma raises intraocular, not orbital, pressure and does not displace the globe forward.

## explicit_objective
List recognised causes of proptosis and exclude acute angle-closure glaucoma (an intraocular-, not orbital-, pressure problem).

## pitfalls
Assuming any acutely painful red eye condition (including angle-closure glaucoma) causes proptosis.
