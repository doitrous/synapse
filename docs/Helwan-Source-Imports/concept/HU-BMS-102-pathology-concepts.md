# Item
## label
Red thrombi are erythrocyte-rich thrombi that form most characteristically in veins
## id
CON-FND-324D89C2933CE7
## canonical_key
pathology.thrombus.red-venous-location
## aliases
Red thrombus
Venous thrombus
Erythrocyte-rich thrombus
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A red thrombus is an erythrocyte-rich thrombus associated with slow-moving blood. It forms most characteristically in veins, where it commonly occludes the lumen and may propagate towards the heart.
## explicit_objective
Identify a red thrombus from its composition and relate its usual venous location to slow blood flow.
## pitfalls
Calling every dark-red intravascular mass a red thrombus. A post-mortem clot is also red but is soft, smooth and non-adherent, whereas a thrombus forms during life and is attached to the wall.

## definition
An antemortem thrombus is a firm, friable intravascular mass with a rough surface that is firmly adherent to the vessel wall. Venous thrombi may mould to the shape of the vessel in which they form.
## explicit_objective
Recognise the gross features that distinguish a thrombus formed during life from a post-mortem clot.
## pitfalls
Using colour alone. A thrombus may be pale, red or mixed, so adherence, surface texture and consistency are more dependable than colour.

# Item
## label
Thrombus and post-mortem clot differ in formation, attachment, surface and composition
## id
CON-FND-EBFB010E0D4121
## canonical_key
pathology.thrombus.versus-postmortem-clot
## aliases
Antemortem thrombus versus postmortem clot
Thrombus blood clot comparison
Post-mortem clot
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A thrombus forms from flowing blood during life, contains platelets and fibrin, is firm and friable, and adheres to a rough vessel wall. A post-mortem clot forms in stagnant blood after death, is composed mainly of fibrin and red cells, and is soft, moist, smooth and non-adherent.
## explicit_objective
Differentiate an antemortem thrombus from a post-mortem clot using at least four independent gross features.
## pitfalls
Calling any coagulated blood a thrombus. Formation during life and adherence to the wall are defining discriminators; a post-mortem clot may separate into red and yellow layers without becoming a thrombus.

## definition
Lines of Zahn are alternating pale platelet-fibrin layers and darker erythrocyte-rich layers formed in flowing blood. Their lamination supports formation of the thrombus during life.

## definition
Fat embolism is the entry of fat globules into ruptured veins followed by their circulation and impaction in pulmonary or systemic vessels. Severe disease may cause respiratory distress, tachycardia, a petechial skin rash and neurological impairment.
## explicit_objective
Define fat embolism and recognise the features that distinguish fat embolism syndrome from an incidental fat embolus.

Treating microscopic fat emboli and fat embolism syndrome as interchangeable. Fat globules may circulate without producing the full clinical syndrome.

Bone fracture is a major setting for fat embolism and fat embolism syndrome
## id

## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Bone fracture, particularly when marrow fat is disrupted, is a major setting for fat embolism. Progressive respiratory distress, tachycardia, petechiae and neurological deterioration in the days after a fracture indicate fat embolism syndrome.
## explicit_objective
Recognise fat embolism syndrome in the days following a major bone fracture.
## pitfalls
Assuming respiratory collapse immediately after trauma must be fat embolism. The syndrome classically evolves after marrow injury and must be distinguished from haemorrhagic shock, pulmonary thromboembolism and other causes of hypoxaemia.
