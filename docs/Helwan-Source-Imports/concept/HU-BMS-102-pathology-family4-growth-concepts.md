## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Tumour growth fraction is the proportion, expressed as a percentage, of cells within a tumour that are actively proliferating. It is distinct from total tumour size, cell-cycle duration, angiogenesis and the fraction undergoing apoptosis.
## explicit_objective
Define tumour growth fraction and distinguish it from other determinants or consequences of tumour growth.
## pitfalls
Equating growth fraction with growth rate or tumour size. It measures the proportion of proliferating cells, not how large the tumour already is.

# Item
## label
High-growth-fraction tumours are more susceptible to chemotherapy
## id
CON-FND-E2077B671EF0C8
## canonical_key
pathology.tumor.growth-fraction-chemotherapy-sensitivity
## aliases
Growth fraction and chemotherapy response
Proliferative fraction and chemotherapy
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Chemotherapeutic agents preferentially act on dividing cells, so a tumour with a high growth fraction is more susceptible to chemotherapy than one with a low growth fraction. This is a module-level kinetic principle, not a complete treatment recommendation.
## explicit_objective
Relate the proportion of proliferating tumour cells to the expected relative susceptibility to chemotherapy.
## pitfalls
Assuming every large or fast-presenting tumour necessarily has a high growth fraction, or converting this principle into a patient-specific treatment rule.
