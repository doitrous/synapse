## definition
A ganglion is a small, movable periarticular cystic swelling most often found on the dorsum of the wrist. It contains clear mucinous or gelatinous fluid and has a fibrous wall that may have an indistinct synovial lining.

## explicit_objective
Recognise a ganglion from its typical wrist location, cystic consistency, clear gelatinous contents and benign behaviour.

## pitfalls
Do not use the word synovial to imply malignancy. A ganglion may arise near a joint or tendon, but it is a benign cystic lesion rather than synovial sarcoma.

## definition
Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic multisystem inflammatory disease that characteristically affects peripheral joints symmetrically, especially the hands, wrists and feet. Chronically inflamed proliferating synovium forms pannus that erodes marginal cartilage and may progress to fibrous ankylosis and periarticular osteoporosis.

## explicit_objective
Distinguish rheumatoid arthritis from osteoarthritis by its small-joint distribution, inflammatory synovium and pannus-based marginal destruction.

## pitfalls
Do not transfer the central articular-cartilage degeneration of osteoarthritis to rheumatoid arthritis. Rheumatoid disease begins in synovium and produces inflammatory pannus with marginal erosion.

## definition
Pigmented villonodular synovitis is a benign proliferative synovial lesion with villous architecture, fibroblasts and hemosiderin-bearing histiocytes. Synovial sarcoma is instead a highly malignant soft-tissue tumour that usually arises adjacent to a joint in young adults and spreads predominantly through blood.

## pitfalls
The name synovial sarcoma is a misnomer and does not establish origin from synovium. Do not classify PVNS as a solid malignant tumour or assume lymphatic spread is the expected route for a sarcoma.
