### Bench comparison
Concept: CON-FND-EBFB010E0D4121
Difficulty: Moderate
Two intravascular masses are described at the virtual gross bench. Specimen A is firm, friable, rough-surfaced and firmly attached to the wall. Specimen B is soft, moist, smooth and can be lifted away from the wall.
Q: Identify specimens A and B.
*= A is an antemortem thrombus; B is a post-mortem clot
Why: Correct. Attachment, roughness and firm friability identify the thrombus; smooth softness and non-adherence identify the post-mortem clot.
* A is a post-mortem clot; B is an antemortem thrombus
Why: This reverses every gross discriminator supplied in the station.
* Both are thrombi because both are intravascular
Why: An intravascular location alone does not establish formation during life; a post-mortem clot also lies inside a vessel or chamber.
* Both are post-mortem clots because colour is not stated
Why: Colour is less reliable than attachment, surface and consistency, all of which are stated.
Explanation: Read the physical relationship before colour. A true thrombus forms during life and is attached to the wall, while a post-mortem clot forms after circulation ceases and is non-adherent.

### Lamination at the cut surface
Concept: CON-FND-7E61964BE1D637
Difficulty: Moderate
The cut surface of specimen A shows alternating pale and red laminae.
Q: What are these laminae called, and what do they support?
*= Lines of Zahn; they support formation in flowing blood during life
Why: Correct. Alternating platelet-fibrin and erythrocyte-rich layers are organised during antemortem thrombus growth.
* Gravity layers; they support formation after death
Why: Post-mortem settling produces dependent separation but not organised Lines of Zahn.
* Fibrin streaks; they establish a septic thrombus
Why: Lamination does not by itself establish infection.
* Hyaline bands; they establish capillary thrombosis
Why: Hyaline thrombi are microscopic capillary lesions, not this gross laminated mass.
Explanation: Lines of Zahn are a process clue rather than a decorative colour pattern. Their alternating composition records repeated deposition while blood was moving.

### Relationship to the lumen
Concept: CON-FND-6E740256B2A9E7
Difficulty: Easy
A third specimen is attached to the wall and completely fills the lumen of a small artery.
Q: How should this thrombus be classified?
*= Occlusive thrombus
Why: Correct. Complete luminal obstruction of a small artery is the source-supplied definition of an occlusive thrombus.
* Mural thrombus
Why: A mural thrombus is attached but does not completely occlude the lumen.
* Vegetation
Why: A vegetation is deposited on a cardiac valve.
* Post-mortem clot
Why: The mass is attached and therefore does not fit a post-mortem clot.
Explanation: Attachment is shared by mural and occlusive thrombi. Complete obstruction is the feature that selects the occlusive category.

### Case record
Concept: CON-FND-B308DCF18C3B33
Difficulty: Moderate
A 21-year-old sustains a femoral fracture. Over the next few days he develops progressive respiratory distress, tachycardia and a petechial rash.
Q: Which embolic process best explains the pattern?
*= Fat embolism syndrome
Why: Correct. A major bone fracture followed by progressive respiratory, cardiovascular and cutaneous findings is the source-supported pattern.
* Air embolism
Why: Air embolism follows gas entry, classically through a large neck vein or decompression, not marrow disruption with petechiae.
* Amniotic fluid embolism
Why: This is an obstetric event during delivery and cannot explain this case.
* Tumour embolism
Why: Tumour emboli consist of malignant cells and do not fit the acute post-fracture timeline.
Explanation: The setting and sequence are essential. Marrow disruption permits fat globules to enter torn venous channels; symptomatic respiratory and systemic injury is fat embolism syndrome.

### Material and route
Concept: CON-FND-2EE60F57922531
Difficulty: Hard
Q: What material enters the circulation, and through which injured structure does it enter?
*= Fat globules enter through ruptured veins
Why: Correct. This is the mechanism stated in the current BMS-102 lecture.
* Platelet aggregates enter through intact arteries
Why: That describes neither the material nor the route of fat embolism.
* Air enters through lymphatic vessels
Why: Air embolism uses an entirely different material and vascular route.
* Amniotic fluid enters through a fractured marrow cavity
Why: This combines two unrelated embolic mechanisms.
Explanation: The source distinguishes the embolic material from the clinical syndrome. Fat globules enter ruptured venous channels and may lodge in pulmonary or systemic vessels.
