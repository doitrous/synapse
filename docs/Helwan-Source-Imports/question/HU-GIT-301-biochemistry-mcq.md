## title
Disaccharides can be hydrolysed by enzymes present in:

## question
Disaccharides can be hydrolysed by enzymes present in:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Saliva

## explanation_a
Saliva contains salivary amylase, which acts on starch (a polysaccharide), not on disaccharides, so it does not hydrolyse disaccharides.

## answer_b
Pancreatic juice

## explanation_b
Pancreatic juice is the correct answer: it contains the disaccharidases (or feeds the brush-border disaccharidases) that hydrolyse dietary disaccharides such as sucrose, lactose and maltose into their constituent monosaccharides. Neither saliva's amylase nor gastric juice's pepsin has disaccharide-hydrolysing activity, and bile contains no digestive enzymes at all, only bile salts for fat emulsification. Recognising pancreatic juice as the source of disaccharide-hydrolysing activity, in contrast to these other secretions, is the point of this question.

## answer_c
Bile

## explanation_c
Bile contains no digestive enzymes at all; its role is fat emulsification via bile salts, not carbohydrate hydrolysis.

## answer_d
Gastric juice

## explanation_d
Gastric juice's only enzyme is pepsin, a protease with no carbohydrate-hydrolysing (disaccharidase) activity.

## question

Micelles

Chylomicrons

Fatty acid-albumin complex

## answer_d
Liposomes

## explanation_d
Liposomes are artificial or experimental lipid bilayer vesicles, not a physiological blood transport form of dietary fat.

The following is not a manifestation of alcoholic fatty liver:

## question
The following is not a manifestation of alcoholic fatty liver:

## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Accumulation of fats in the liver

## explanation_a
Accumulation of fats in the liver genuinely is a manifestation of alcoholic fatty liver, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_b
Oxidation of alcohol

## explanation_b
Oxidation of alcohol genuinely occurs in alcoholic fatty liver (via alcohol dehydrogenase and acetaldehyde dehydrogenase), so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_c
Decreased fatty acid oxidation

## explanation_c
Decreased fatty acid oxidation genuinely is a manifestation of alcoholic fatty liver, driven by the raised NADH/NAD+ ratio from ethanol oxidation, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_d
Decreased NADH/NAD ratio

## explanation_d
Decreased NADH/NAD ratio is the correct answer: it is NOT a genuine manifestation of alcoholic fatty liver. Ethanol oxidation actually increases, not decreases, the hepatic NADH/NAD+ ratio, and this elevated ratio is what drives decreased fatty acid oxidation and favours triacylglycerol accumulation. Recognising this direction-of-effect reversal as the exception among these four options is the point of this question.

## question
One of the following is a good index for liver synthetic functions:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)

## explanation_a
Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is instead a tumour marker, elevated in hepatocellular carcinoma, not an index of liver synthetic function.

## answer_b
Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT)

## explanation_b
Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) reflects hepatocellular damage/leakage from injured hepatocytes, not the liver's ongoing synthetic capacity.

## answer_c
Prothrombin time and concentration

## explanation_c
Prothrombin time and concentration is the correct answer: it reflects the liver's synthetic capacity for clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X), which have short half-lives, making it a sensitive, good index of current liver synthetic function. This distinguishes it from AFP (a tumour marker), ALT (a hepatocellular damage marker) and plasma ammonia (a detoxification marker). Recognising prothrombin time as specifically a synthetic-function marker is the point of this question.

## answer_d
Plasma ammonia level

## explanation_d
Plasma ammonia level reflects the liver's detoxification (urea cycle) function, not its protein-synthetic function.

## subject

## status

## explanation_a

## answer_b
Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)

## explanation_b
Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is the correct answer: it is the characteristic tumour marker that increases in liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma), an oncofetal protein normally produced by fetal liver and yolk sac that becomes re-expressed by malignant hepatocytes. This distinguishes AFP from prothrombin time and concentration (a synthetic-function marker) or AST (a hepatocellular damage marker), neither of which is tumour-specific in this way. Recognising AFP as the tumour marker for liver cancer is the point of this question.

## answer_c
Prothrombin time and concentration

## explanation_c
Prothrombin time and concentration is a marker of liver synthetic function, not a tumour marker that increases in liver cancer.

## answer_d
Serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST)

## explanation_d
Serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST) reflects hepatocellular damage/leakage, not a tumour-specific marker of liver cancer.

## question
Serum transaminases (ALT & AST) indicate:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Liver cancer

## explanation_a
Liver cancer is better indicated by a tumour marker such as AFP, not directly by transaminase elevation, which instead reflects hepatocellular damage of any cause.

## answer_b
Liver failure

## explanation_b
Liver failure is better reflected by synthetic markers such as prothrombin time and concentration, not directly by transaminase elevation.

## answer_c
Hepatocellular damage

## explanation_c
Hepatocellular damage is the correct answer: serum transaminases (ALT and AST) are intracellular hepatocyte enzymes released into the blood when hepatocyte membranes are damaged. Their elevation therefore indicates hepatocellular damage specifically, from any cause, not liver cancer, liver failure, or decreased synthetic function directly. Recognising transaminases as damage/leakage markers, distinct from tumour markers (AFP), failure indicators, or synthetic-function markers (prothrombin time), is the point of this question.

## answer_d
Decreased liver synthetic function

## explanation_d
Decreased liver synthetic function is instead reflected by markers such as prothrombin time, not directly by transaminase elevation, which reflects cell damage rather than synthetic capacity.

The role of the liver in metabolism does not include:

## question
The role of the liver in metabolism does not include:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It is the main organ responsible for regulation of blood glucose

## explanation_a
The liver genuinely is the main organ responsible for regulation of blood glucose, via glycogen synthesis/breakdown and gluconeogenesis, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_b
It produces bile salts

## explanation_b
The liver genuinely produces bile salts, for fat digestion, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_c
It is the site of storage of glucose as glycogen

## explanation_c
The liver genuinely is the site of storage of glucose as glycogen, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_d
It is the site of synthesis of gamma-globulins

## explanation_d
It is the site of synthesis of gamma-globulins is the correct answer: this is NOT a genuine liver role. Gamma-globulins (immunoglobulins) are instead synthesised by plasma cells (differentiated B lymphocytes) of the immune system, not by hepatocytes, even though the liver does synthesise most other plasma proteins (albumin, clotting factors, alpha- and beta-globulins). Recognising gamma-globulin synthesis as the exception among these otherwise genuine liver metabolic roles is the point of this question.
