All of the following are true about oral leukoplakia except:

## question
All of the following are true about oral leukoplakia except:

## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It appears as whitish mucosal patch

## explanation_a
This is a true description of leukoplakia — it does present as a whitish mucosal patch — so it is not the exception the question asks for.

## answer_b
It occurs due to chronic irritation

## explanation_b
Chronic irritation is a genuine cause of leukoplakia, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_c
Common sites are lips, tongue and cheek

## explanation_c
Lips, tongue and cheek are genuinely common leukoplakia sites, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_d
Malignant transformation into adenocarcinoma is a major complication

## explanation_d
Oral leukoplakia's malignant transformation is to squamous cell carcinoma, not adenocarcinoma, because leukoplakia arises from the stratified squamous epithelium lining the oral mucosa, and malignant change follows the cell of origin. Adenocarcinoma would require a glandular epithelial origin, which leukoplakia does not have. This is the one false statement among the four, which is exactly why it is the answer to an 'except' question about oral leukoplakia.

## question
The aetiology of sialadenitis in Sjogren's syndrome is:

## subject
gi

## status

Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Autoimmune

## explanation_a
Sjögren syndrome causes sialadenitis through an autoimmune mechanism, in which lymphocytes infiltrate and progressively destroy the salivary and lacrimal glands, producing dry mouth and dry eyes. This chronic lymphocytic sialadenitis is driven by the patient's own immune system attacking glandular tissue, not by an external pathogen or physical injury. Recognising the autoimmune basis is what separates Sjögren-related sialadenitis from the infective causes of salivary gland inflammation.

## answer_b
Traumatic

## explanation_b
Trauma is not the mechanism in Sjögren syndrome; the gland is damaged by the patient's own immune response, not by physical injury.

## answer_c
Viral infection

## explanation_c
Viral infection describes a different cause of sialadenitis, such as mumps, not the autoimmune process of Sjögren syndrome.

## answer_d
Bacterial infection

## explanation_d
Bacterial infection is a separate, usually acute and unilateral, cause of sialadenitis, not the chronic autoimmune process seen in Sjögren syndrome.

## question
Most salivary gland tumors occur in:

Most salivary gland tumours, both benign and malignant, arise in the parotid gland, which is by far the largest of the major salivary glands and accounts for roughly eight in ten salivary tumours overall. Sublingual glands are the least common site for salivary gland tumours in absolute numbers, despite carrying a high proportion of malignancy among the few that occur.

## answer_d
Minor salivary glands

## explanation_d
Minor salivary glands, scattered through the oral mucosa, develop tumours less often overall than the parotid, even though a relatively higher share of their tumours are malignant.

The most common primary malignant salivary gland tumor is:

## question
The most common primary malignant salivary gland tumor is:

## subject

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Adenoid cystic carcinoma is a recognised salivary malignancy, often notable for perineural invasion, but it is not the commonest one overall.

## answer_b
Adenolymphoma

## explanation_b
Adenolymphoma (Warthin tumour) is a benign salivary tumour, not a malignant one, so it cannot be the commonest malignant salivary tumour.

Adenocarcinoma

## explanation_c
Adenocarcinoma of salivary origin occurs but is less common than mucoepidermoid carcinoma as a cause of salivary malignancy.

It most often arises in the parotid gland, and its clinical behaviour ranges from low-grade, slow-growing tumours to high-grade, aggressive ones depending on histological grade. Recognising it as the commonest malignant subtype, rather than adenoid cystic carcinoma or adenocarcinoma, is the key fact tested here.

## title
Achalasia means:

## question
Achalasia means:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Failure of canalisation of the oesophagus

## explanation_a
Failure of canalisation of the oesophagus describes a congenital oesophageal atresia, not the acquired motor disorder of achalasia.

## answer_b
An outpouching of part of the wall of the oesophagus

## explanation_b
An outpouching of the oesophageal wall describes a diverticulum, a structural lesion, not the functional motor failure that defines achalasia.

## answer_c
Loss of the contractility of the oesophagus and failure of relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter

## explanation_c
Achalasia is defined by loss of the normal peristaltic contractility of the oesophageal body together with failure of the lower oesophageal sphincter to relax during swallowing. Both defects stem from degeneration of the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus, which normally coordinates peristalsis and sphincter relaxation. Food and liquid accumulate above the non-relaxing sphincter, and the oesophagus dilates proximally over time, producing dysphagia for both solids and liquids from the outset.

## answer_d
Protrusion of the upper part of the oesophagus into the neck

## explanation_d
Protrusion of the upper oesophagus into the neck describes a pharyngeal (Zenker) diverticulum, an anatomically distinct condition from achalasia.

## question

## subject

## status

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Protrusion of the upper part of the stomach into the thorax

## explanation_a
This is a true description of hiatus hernia — protrusion of the upper stomach into the thorax — so it is not the exception.

## answer_b
Predisposing factors include obesity, lifting heavy loads and frequent coughing fits

## explanation_b
Obesity, heavy lifting and chronic coughing are genuine predisposing factors, all raising intra-abdominal pressure, so this true statement is not the exception.

## answer_c
It is due to increased intrathoracic pressure and increased diaphragmatic tone

## explanation_c
Hiatus hernia is not caused by increased diaphragmatic tone; it results from the opposite — a lax, weakened oesophageal hiatus — combined with raised intra-abdominal, not intrathoracic, pressure pushing the stomach upward through that weak point. Predisposing factors such as obesity, heavy lifting and chronic coughing all act by raising intra-abdominal pressure against this weakened hiatus. This is the false statement among the four, which is why it is the exception the question asks for.

## answer_d
It results in regurgitation of gastric contents and GERD

## explanation_d
Hiatus hernia genuinely predisposes to reflux of gastric contents and GERD by disrupting the normal antireflux barrier at the gastro-oesophageal junction, so this true statement is not the exception.
