## title
Ptosis can occur due to the following cause EXCEPT:

## question
Ptosis can occur due to the following cause EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Congenital lid muscle weakness.

## explanation_a
Congenital lid muscle (levator) weakness is a recognised cause of ptosis, so it belongs among the true causes, not the exception.

## answer_b
Lid trauma.

## explanation_b
Lid trauma, if it damages the levator or its aponeurosis, is a recognised cause of ptosis, so it belongs among the true causes, not the exception.

## answer_c
Third nerve paralysis.

## explanation_c
Third nerve paralysis removes the oculomotor nerve's supply to the levator palpebrae superioris and is a well-recognised cause of ptosis, so it belongs among the true causes, not the exception.

## answer_d
Orbicularis muscle paralysis.

## explanation_d
Orbicularis muscle paralysis is a fact about facial (7th) nerve palsy, and orbicularis is the lid-CLOSING muscle, not the lid-opening levator. Its paralysis causes lagophthalmos (failure to close the eye), not ptosis, so among these four options it is the one that does NOT cause ptosis. Ptosis instead follows failure of the lid-opening apparatus: the levator itself (congenital weakness, trauma) or its nerve supply (third nerve palsy), never the lid-closing orbicularis.

## question
Schirmer's test is used for diagnosing:

A

## answer_a
Dry eye.

## explanation_a
Schirmer's test, a strip of filter paper placed in the lower fornix to measure the length of wetting over five minutes, directly quantifies aqueous tear production and is the standard bedside test for dry eye. A short wetting length supports a diagnosis of aqueous-deficient dry eye. It is simple, inexpensive and widely used as a first-line diagnostic test.

## answer_b
Infective keratitis.

## explanation_b
Infective keratitis is diagnosed by slit-lamp examination, corneal scraping and culture, not by a tear-production test.

## answer_c
Corneal ulcers.

## explanation_c
Corneal ulcers are diagnosed by slit-lamp examination with fluorescein staining, not by a tear-production test.

## answer_d
Horner's syndrome.

## explanation_d
Horner's syndrome is diagnosed by pharmacological pupil testing (such as apraclonidine or cocaine drops) and its associated ptosis/miosis/anhidrosis triad, not by a tear-production test.

## answer_d
Meningioma.

## explanation_d
Meningioma (of the optic nerve sheath or sphenoid wing) is a recognised but much less common cause of adult proptosis than thyroid orbitopathy.

## title
Mucin layer of tears is secreted by:

## question
Mucin layer of tears is secreted by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Goblet cells.

## explanation_a
Conjunctival goblet cells secrete the tear film's innermost mucin layer, which allows the aqueous layer above it to wet the otherwise hydrophobic corneal epithelium evenly. Goblet cells are most concentrated in the bulbar and fornical conjunctiva. Their loss, as in vitamin A deficiency or cicatrizing conjunctival disease, destabilises the whole tear film and produces mucin-deficient dry eye.

## answer_b
Main lacrimal gland.

## explanation_b
The main lacrimal gland secretes the tear film's middle aqueous layer, not the mucin layer.

## answer_c
Meibomian glands.

## explanation_c
The meibomian glands secrete the tear film's outer lipid layer, which retards evaporation, not the mucin layer.

## answer_d
Accessory lacrimal glands.

## explanation_d
Accessory lacrimal glands (Krause and Wolfring) contribute basal aqueous tear secretion, not the mucin layer.

## title
You can differentiate between a stye and a chalazion by:

## question
You can differentiate between a stye and a chalazion by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The relation of the lesion to the caruncle.

## explanation_a
The relation of a lid lesion to the caruncle (at the medial canthus) is not the feature used to distinguish a stye from a chalazion; both can occur anywhere along the lid, not specifically near the caruncle.

## answer_b
Relation to the lid margin.

## explanation_b
A stye (hordeolum externum) is an acute infection of a lash-follicle gland and so points at the lash-bearing lid margin itself, while a chalazion is a chronic granuloma of a deeper meibomian gland within the tarsal plate, away from the margin. This difference in position relative to the lid margin is the classic bedside distinguishing feature between the two. A stye is also acutely tender, while a chalazion is typically painless, reinforcing the same anatomical distinction.

## answer_c
The amount of discharge.

## explanation_c
The amount of discharge is not a reliable distinguishing feature between a stye and a chalazion; neither characteristically discharges externally.

## answer_d
Everting the eyelid.

## explanation_d
Everting the eyelid is a general examination step, not the specific distinguishing feature between a stye and a chalazion.

Wolfring's gland

## title
Severe congenital ptosis, with no levator function can be treated by:

## question
Severe congenital ptosis, with no levator function can be treated by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Levator resection from skin side

## explanation_a
Levator resection from the skin side needs measurable levator function to shorten; it is not the operation of choice when levator function is absent.

## answer_b
Surgical intervention between age of 6 till 10 years

## explanation_b
Deferring surgical intervention to age 6-10 is a timing consideration for milder ptosis, not the specific answer to how severe ptosis with no levator function should be treated.

## answer_c
Frontalis sling operation

## explanation_c
When levator function is severely poor or absent, as in severe congenital ptosis, the levator cannot be usefully shortened by resection. A frontalis sling instead harnesses the frontalis muscle, via a sling material passed through the lid and anchored to the forehead, to lift the lid mechanically using brow movement. This bypasses the non-functioning levator entirely, which is why it is the standard operation for ptosis with no levator function.

## answer_d
Tarsorrhaphy operation

## explanation_d
Tarsorrhaphy narrows the palpebral fissure by suturing the lid margins together; it protects the cornea in lagophthalmos, but it does not correct or elevate a ptotic lid.

## answer_d
Tear lysozyme level

## explanation_d
Tear lysozyme level reflects one of the tear film's protective components and is used as a dry-eye test, so it is not the exception being sought.

Dry eye can be caused by the following EXCEPT:

## question
Dry eye can be caused by the following EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Obstruction of the lacrimal puncti.

## explanation_a
Obstruction of the lacrimal puncti blocks tear drainage and causes epiphora (excess tearing), the clinical opposite of dry eye, rather than dryness itself. It is therefore not a recognised cause of dry eye, which is exactly what the question asks for. This is the same drainage-versus-production/stability distinction that separates epiphora from dry eye throughout this topic.

## answer_b
Rheumatoid arthritis.

## explanation_b
Rheumatoid arthritis, a collagen-vascular disease, causes aqueous-deficient dry eye through lacrimal gland infiltration, so it belongs among the true causes, not the exception.

## answer_c
Chronic trachoma.

## explanation_c
Chronic trachoma's cicatrizing conjunctival disease causes goblet-cell loss and mucin-deficient dry eye, so it belongs among the true causes, not the exception.

## answer_d
Vitamin A deficiency.

## explanation_d
Vitamin A deficiency causes mucin-deficient dry eye through goblet-cell loss, so it belongs among the true causes, not the exception.

## title
A positive regurge test is diagnostic of:

## question
A positive regurge test is diagnostic of:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Punctal occlusion.

## explanation_a
Punctal occlusion would prevent tears from reaching the sac at all, rather than producing the sac-reflux that a positive regurge test relies on.

## answer_b
Canalicular obstruction.

## explanation_b
Canalicular obstruction, proximal to the sac, would likewise prevent tears from reaching the sac to be regurgitated.

## answer_c
Nasolacrimal duct obstruction.

## explanation_c
A positive lacrimal regurgitation test, in which pressure over the sac reflux material back through the puncti, confirms the sac is patent to the puncti but obstructed distally, localising the block to the nasolacrimal duct. This distal, sac-to-nose localisation is precisely the finding expected in chronic nasolacrimal duct obstruction. The test's underlying mechanism, forcing trapped sac contents backward because their forward route through the duct is blocked, is what makes this localisation reliable.

## answer_d
Ectropion.

## explanation_d
Ectropion is an eyelid-margin malposition, unrelated to the mechanism a regurge test assesses.

## question
The following test is used for diagnosis of dry eye:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Fluorescein dye disappearance test.

## explanation_a
The fluorescein dye disappearance test assesses lacrimal drainage function, not dry eye, so it is not the test this key selects.

## answer_b
Tear break up time.

## explanation_b
Tear break-up time, the interval before the tear film first disrupts after a blink, directly assesses tear film stability and is shortened in dry eye, particularly the mucin-deficient and evaporative forms. It is a standard, widely used diagnostic test for dry eye. A short break-up time supports the diagnosis alongside Schirmer's test and ocular surface staining.

## answer_c
Lacrimal syringing.

## explanation_c
Lacrimal syringing tests drainage-pathway patency, not dry eye, so it is not the test this key selects.

## answer_d
Regurge test.

## explanation_d
The regurge test assesses lacrimal sac and drainage-pathway patency (as in dacryocystitis), not dry eye, so it is not the test this key selects.

## title
Main line of treatment in acquired nasolacrimal obstruction in adults is:

## question
Main line of treatment in acquired nasolacrimal obstruction in adults is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
IV antibiotics.

## explanation_a
Systemic IV antibiotics treat an acute infective flare, not the definitive, main-line treatment for acquired nasolacrimal obstruction itself.

## answer_b
NLD massage.

## explanation_b
Nasolacrimal duct massage is used for CONGENITAL nasolacrimal duct obstruction in infants, where the duct may still open spontaneously, not for acquired obstruction in adults.

## answer_c
DCR.

## explanation_c
Dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR), surgically connecting the lacrimal sac to the nasal cavity to bypass the obstructed nasolacrimal duct, is the standard, definitive treatment for acquired nasolacrimal duct obstruction in adults. Because the obstruction in adults is typically a fixed anatomical narrowing rather than a developmental one likely to resolve on its own, surgical bypass rather than conservative massage is the appropriate main-line approach. This is the same DCR procedure used for chronic dacryocystitis once inflammation has settled.

## answer_d
Intubation.

## explanation_d
Intubation (silicone tube placement) is used for partial stenosis or as an adjunct, not as the main line of treatment for established acquired nasolacrimal duct obstruction.
