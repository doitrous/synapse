The adult cornea's horizontal diameter normally measures about 12mm, larger than its vertical diameter of about 11mm, since the cornea is not perfectly circular. ## question
The method of examination of anterior chamber angle is called:

## answer_a

## explanation_a

## answer_b
Retinoscopy

## explanation_b
Retinoscopy measures refractive error, not the anterior chamber angle.

## answer_c
Gonioscopy

## explanation_c
Gonioscopy is the technique for examining the anterior chamber angle, using a specially designed contact lens with mirrors (or a prism) to overcome the total internal reflection that otherwise blocks a direct view of the angle through the cornea. No other routine technique substitutes for gonioscopy in directly visualising the angle.

Indirect ophthalmoscopy examines the fundus, not the anterior chamber angle.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
The slit lamp biomicroscope, on its own, examines the anterior segment in detail — cornea, anterior chamber, iris and lens. ## explanation_d
Anterior chamber angle examination needs a gonioscopy lens, not just any accessory lens, because the angle cannot be viewed directly even with the slit lamp alone, due to total internal reflection at the cornea.

On testing visual acuity, if the patient is unable to identify any letters on the Snellen chart you should next test for the ability to:

## question
On testing visual acuity, if the patient is unable to identify any letters on the Snellen chart you should next test for the ability to:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Count fingers.

## explanation_a
When a patient cannot identify any letters even on the largest line of the Snellen chart, acuity testing steps down the hierarchy: the next step is to test the ability to count fingers, typically starting close up and moving the examiner's hand further away to find the maximum testable distance. Only if counting fingers also fails does testing move further down, to hand movements and then to light perception. Skipping straight to a lower step would understate whatever residual vision the patient retains.

## answer_b
Identify direction of hand movements.

## explanation_b
Hand-movement testing is a step below counting fingers in the hierarchy, tried only once counting fingers has also failed — it is not the immediate next step after the Snellen chart.

## answer_c
Identify position of a light.

## explanation_c
Light-projection testing sits near the bottom of the acuity hierarchy, well below counting fingers, and is not the immediate next step.

## answer_d
Identify presence of a light.

## explanation_d
Light-perception testing sits near the bottom of the acuity hierarchy, well below counting fingers, and is not the immediate next step.

## question

## explanation_a

## answer_b
Used for fundus examination in retinal detachment without media opacity

## explanation_b
B-scan is used for fundus assessment specifically when media opacity blocks the view, not simply in any eye with a clear view where direct examination would already suffice.

This diagnostic role, rather than biometry, is B-scan's principal ophthalmic use.

## explanation_d
Diagnostic ocular ultrasound uses high-, not very low-, frequency sound waves, which is what gives it the resolution needed to image fine ocular structures.

In cases of retinal detachment the red reflex becomes:

## question
In cases of retinal detachment the red reflex becomes:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Greyish

## explanation_a
In retinal detachment, the red reflex takes on a greyish quality rather than the normal bright orange-red colour, because the elevated, detached retina — pale and opacified compared with a normally attached retina against the vascular choroid — scatters and dulls the light reflected back through the pupil. This greyish change is one of several diagnostic red-reflex colour patterns, alongside the white reflex of mature cataract, the black reflex of dense vitreous haemorrhage, and the yellow reflex of endophthalmitis. Recognising it can prompt urgent fundus assessment even before formal ophthalmoscopy confirms the detachment.

## answer_b
Whitish

## explanation_b
A whitish reflex is the pattern associated with mature cataract, not retinal detachment.

## answer_c
Yellowish

## explanation_c
A yellowish reflex is the pattern associated with endophthalmitis, not retinal detachment.

## answer_d
black

## explanation_d
A black reflex is the pattern associated with dense vitreous haemorrhage, not retinal detachment.

## title
Retinoscopy is done for:

## question
Retinoscopy is done for:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Examination of the retina

## explanation_a
Retinoscopy does not examine the retina's structure, despite the similarity of the name and instrument; it assesses refraction, not retinal anatomy.

## answer_b
Degree of strabismus

## explanation_b
The degree of strabismus is assessed by cover testing and related techniques, not by retinoscopy.

## answer_c
Refractive power of eye

## explanation_c
Retinoscopy objectively measures the eye's refractive power by observing how a reflex from the fundus moves through the pupil as lenses are interposed in front of the eye. Because it does not require the patient to respond or read letters, it is especially useful in young children and others unable to cooperate with subjective refraction. This objective refractive assessment, not fundus examination, motility testing, or biometry, is retinoscopy's defining purpose.

## answer_d
Axial length of eye

## explanation_d
Axial length of the eye is measured by biometry (A-scan or optical biometry), not by retinoscopy.

## answer_e
Assessment of visual acuity

## explanation_e
Visual acuity is assessed with a Snellen chart or equivalent, not by retinoscopy, which instead measures refractive error objectively.

## title
Detailed examination of the anterior segment of the eye is done using:

## question
Detailed examination of the anterior segment of the eye is done using:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Slit lamp

## explanation_a
Detailed examination of the anterior segment — cornea, anterior chamber, iris and crystalline lens — is performed using the slit lamp biomicroscope, which provides magnified, illuminated, stereoscopic viewing of these structures. This makes it the primary anterior-segment examination tool in routine ophthalmic practice, distinct from instruments aimed at other purposes such as refraction or fundus viewing. Its versatility, extended further by accessory lenses for the fundus or the angle, is why it is central to almost every eye examination.

## answer_b
Retinoscopy

## explanation_b
Retinoscopy assesses refractive error, not detailed anterior-segment anatomy.

## answer_c
Prisms

## explanation_c
Prisms are used to measure the angle of a manifest deviation (strabismus), not to examine anterior-segment anatomy in detail.

## answer_d
Ophthalmoscopy

## explanation_d
Ophthalmoscopy examines the fundus (posterior segment), not the anterior segment in detail.

## answer_e
Exophthalmometer

## explanation_e
An exophthalmometer measures the degree of globe protrusion (proptosis), not anterior-segment anatomy in detail.
