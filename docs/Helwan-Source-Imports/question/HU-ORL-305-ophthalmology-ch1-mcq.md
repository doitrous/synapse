## title
Aqueous humour is secreted by:

## question
Aqueous humour is secreted by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Angle of the anterior chamber.

## explanation_a
The anterior chamber angle is the site of aqueous drainage via the trabecular meshwork, not its site of secretion.

## answer_b
The ciliary body.

## explanation_b
Aqueous humour is secreted by the ciliary body, specifically the non-pigmented epithelium of its pars plicata, into the posterior chamber. From there it flows through the pupil into the anterior chamber before draining at the angle. This secretory role is what maintains normal intraocular pressure and nourishes the avascular cornea and lens.

## answer_c
Posterior surface of iris.

## explanation_c
The posterior iris surface is not a secretory structure for aqueous humour; the ciliary body immediately behind it is.

## answer_d
Lens.

## explanation_d
The lens has no secretory function; it is a refractive structure suspended by the zonules, not a source of aqueous humour.

Aqueous humor is drained through:

## question
Aqueous humor is drained through:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Angle of the anterior chamber.

## explanation_a
Aqueous humour drains through the anterior chamber angle, where the trabecular meshwork channels it into Schlemm's canal and onward into the episcleral venous system. This drainage pathway regulates intraocular pressure, since production and drainage must balance for pressure to stay normal. Obstruction along this route, most classically at the trabecular meshwork, is the basis of glaucoma.

## answer_b
The ciliary body.

## explanation_b
The ciliary body is where aqueous humour is produced, not where it drains.

## answer_c
Posterior surface of iris.

## explanation_c
The posterior iris surface plays no role in aqueous drainage; it lies upstream, near the site of secretion.

## answer_d
Lens.

## explanation_d
The lens is not part of the drainage pathway; aqueous flows around it, not through it.

Aqueous humour is produced in which chamber of the eye:

## question
Aqueous humour is produced in which chamber of the eye:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Anterior chamber

## explanation_a
Aqueous humour is produced in the posterior, not the anterior, chamber; it only reaches the anterior chamber after flowing through the pupil.

## answer_b
Posterior chamber

## explanation_b
Aqueous humour is produced in the posterior chamber, behind the iris and in front of the lens and its zonules, by the ciliary body's non-pigmented epithelium. It then passes forward through the pupil into the anterior chamber, where it eventually drains at the angle. Because the posterior, not the anterior, chamber is the true production site, confusing the two is a common source of error.

## answer_c
Vitreous cavity

## explanation_c
The vitreous cavity is filled with vitreous gel, not aqueous humour, and plays no part in aqueous production.

## answer_d
Trabecular meshwork.

## explanation_d
The trabecular meshwork is a drainage structure at the angle, not a site of aqueous production.

## question
The outer coat of the eye:

## explanation_b
The outer coat is not only protective — the cornea, its anterior part, also provides the eye's principal refractive power.

This protective function is distinct from, and in addition to, the cornea's separate optical role in refraction.

corneal transparency is due to all of the following factors EXCEPT:

## question
corneal transparency is due to all of the following factors EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
compact arrangement of corneal stromal fibers

## explanation_a
Compact, regular arrangement of the stromal collagen fibres is a genuine transparency factor, so it is not the exception.

## answer_b
Lack of blood vessels

## explanation_b
Avascularity (lack of blood vessels) is a genuine corneal transparency factor, so it is not the exception.

## answer_c
Myelinated nerve fibers

## explanation_c
The cornea's sensory nerve fibres, making it one of the most densely innervated tissues in the body, are unmyelinated within the stroma and epithelium — myelination only begins more peripherally, near the limbus. Myelinated fibres running through the central cornea would scatter light and disrupt transparency, so the cornea's nerve supply is specifically non-myelinated as a transparency-preserving feature. This is why 'myelinated nerve fibres' is the false statement among genuine transparency factors such as avascularity, epithelial non-keratinisation, and regular stromal collagen spacing.

## answer_d
Non - keratinized stratified squamous epithelium

## explanation_d
Non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium is a genuine transparency factor, so it is not the exception.

## title
The cornea has:

## question
The cornea has:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
3 layers

## explanation_a
The cornea has five histological layers (epithelium, Bowman's, stroma, Descemet's, endothelium), not three.

## answer_b
Rich vasculature

## explanation_b
The cornea is avascular, not richly vascularised; rich vasculature would in fact impair, not support, its transparency.

## answer_c
Active endothelial pumps

## explanation_c
The corneal endothelium maintains the stroma's relative dehydration through active Na+/K+-ATPase ion pumps, continuously pumping fluid out of the stroma against the cornea's tendency to imbibe water. This active endothelial pump function is essential for corneal transparency: stromal oedema from pump failure clouds the cornea. It is this active, energy-dependent pumping, not passive properties like vascularity or fibre arrangement, that this question's key identifies as the defining feature here.

## answer_d
Irregularly arranged fibers.

## explanation_d
The stromal collagen fibrils are regularly, not irregularly, arranged in parallel lamellae; irregular arrangement would scatter light and impair transparency.

## answer_e
Rich water content

## explanation_e
The cornea in fact maintains relative dehydration, not rich water content; excess water content (oedema) would impair, not support, transparency.

## question
The refractive power of the eye lens is about… in diopters:

## subject
mul

## status

Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a

## answer_b
20

## explanation_b
The relaxed crystalline lens contributes roughly 20 dioptres to the eye's total refractive power of about 60 dioptres, with the cornea supplying the larger remaining share. This division of labour between cornea (fixed, larger contribution) and lens (variable, smaller contribution, able to increase with accommodation) lets the eye focus on both distant and near objects. The lens's own power can rise further during accommodation as the ciliary muscle contracts and the lens rounds up.

## explanation_d
40 dioptres is closer to the cornea's refractive contribution, not the lens's, which is roughly half that.

## question
Regarding the anatomy of the cornea:

## subject
mul

## status

## vignette

B

## answer_a
Cornea is made up of 7 layers.

## explanation_a
The cornea has five histological layers (epithelium, Bowman's, stroma, Descemet's, endothelium), not seven, so this statement is false.

## answer_b
The stroma is made of collagen fibers arranged in lamellae.

## explanation_b
The corneal stroma, the cornea's thickest layer, is made of collagen fibrils arranged in regularly spaced parallel lamellae. This precise, near-crystalline regularity of spacing, not the mere presence of collagen, is what allows destructive light-scattering interference between adjacent fibrils to cancel out, keeping the stroma transparent. It is this genuine structural fact, among several false statements about corneal anatomy, that the key affirms here.

## answer_c
Corneal transparency is due to vascularity.

## explanation_c
Corneal transparency is due to avascularity, not vascularity — blood vessels in the cornea would scatter light and cause opacity, which is why corneal vascularisation is a pathological finding.

## answer_d
Active endothelial pump plays a minor role in transparency of cornea.

## explanation_d
The active endothelial pump plays a major, not minor, role in transparency, continuously removing fluid to keep the stroma relatively dehydrated.

## question
If the corneal light reflex is 2mm temporal to the pupil in the right eye, the deviation is estimated to be:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
10 diopters esotropia.

## explanation_a
10 dioptres corresponds to about 1mm of reflex decentration, not the 2mm described here.

## answer_b
20 diopters esotropia.

## explanation_b
20 dioptres would correspond to roughly 1.3mm of decentration on the working Hirschberg estimate, not the full 2mm described here.

## answer_c
30 diopters esotropia.

## explanation_c
The Hirschberg test estimates the angle of manifest strabismus from how far the corneal light reflex is displaced from the pupil centre, using the working estimate that each millimetre of decentration corresponds to roughly 15 dioptres of deviation. A reflex displaced 2mm temporal to the pupil in the right eye is consistent with the eye being turned inward (esotropia), and 2mm at ~15 dioptres/mm works out to about 30 dioptres. This bedside estimate lets a clinician quantify a squint without needing a prism bar.

## answer_d
40 diopters esotropia.

## explanation_d
40 dioptres would correspond to a decentration greater than the 2mm described in this scenario.

The crystalline lens is suspended from ciliary body processes by:

## answer_a
Sutures.

## explanation_a
'Sutures' refers to the internal lines where lens fibres meet within the lens substance, not to the suspensory mechanism.

## answer_b
Zonules.

## explanation_b
The crystalline lens is suspended in position by the zonule of Zinn — a ring of fine fibres running from the ciliary body's processes to the lens capsule's equator. Zonular tension at rest flattens the lens for distance focus; when the ciliary muscle contracts during accommodation, zonular tension relaxes and the lens rounds up to increase its refractive power for near vision. This suspension keeps the lens centred on the visual axis between the iris and the vitreous.

Lens capsule.

## explanation_c
The lens capsule is the lens's own outer covering, not the structure that suspends it from the ciliary body.

## answer_d
Lens fibres.

## explanation_d
Lens fibres are the lens's own internal cellular structure, not the suspensory apparatus that holds the lens in place.
