## question
In tumour-cell kinetics, what does the “growth fraction” refer to?
## format
single best answer
## derived_from

## correct_answer
C
## answer_a
The rate of angiogenesis
## explanation_a
Angiogenesis supplies a growing tumour with vessels, but it is not the definition of growth fraction. The lecture treats angiogenesis as a separate controller of tumour-mass expansion. Choosing it confuses vascular support with the proportion of cycling tumour cells.
## answer_b
The percentage of cells undergoing apoptosis
## explanation_b
Apoptosis contributes to cell loss and therefore affects net tumour accumulation. Growth fraction counts proliferating cells, not cells undergoing programmed death. The solved source lists these as distinct quantities.
## answer_c
The percentage of proliferating cells within a tumour
## explanation_c
Growth fraction is the percentage of tumour cells that are proliferating. The current BMS-102 lecture states this definition directly on p47, and solved Family-4 item C04 prints option C. It is a proportion of the cell population, not a measure of total size.
## answer_d
The total tumour size
## explanation_d
Total size is the accumulated tumour mass and can be influenced by production, loss and supply. Growth fraction instead asks what proportion of cells are proliferating at the time considered. A large tumour can still have a low growth fraction.
## answer_e
The duration of the cell cycle
## explanation_e
Cell-cycle duration is a separate kinetic variable. Growth fraction describes how many cells are in the proliferative pool, not how long one cycling cell takes to complete its cycle. The lecture lists both under tumour-growth kinetics.
