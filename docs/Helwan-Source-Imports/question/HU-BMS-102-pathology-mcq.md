## question
In which type of vessel is a red thrombus most commonly found?
## correct_answer
B
## answer_a
Arteries
## explanation_a
Arterial thrombi are usually platelet-fibrin rich and therefore paler. Rapid arterial flow makes the classic erythrocyte-rich red pattern less characteristic.
## answer_b
Veins
## explanation_b
Venous thrombi form in slow-moving blood and trap abundant erythrocytes, giving the gross red appearance. The current BMS-102 lecture also classifies venous thrombi as the commonest red thrombi. This is the printed answer in solved auxiliary Q5.
## answer_c
Capillaries
## explanation_c
Capillary thrombi are classified in this module as hyaline rather than red thrombi. Their scale and composition do not produce the gross venous pattern.
## answer_d
Lymphatic vessels
## explanation_d
Thrombi arise from blood constituents within the cardiovascular circulation. Lymphatic obstruction is not classified as a red thrombus.

Attachment to the vessel wall is a defining gross discriminator of an antemortem thrombus. A post-mortem clot is non-adherent.

A

Alternating white and red lines
## explanation_a
Lines of Zahn are an organised lamination of pale platelet-fibrin and darker erythrocyte-rich layers. They are produced as a thrombus grows in flowing blood and support an antemortem origin. This is the printed answer in solved auxiliary Q9.

Uniform red coloration
## explanation_b
Uniform red coloration may describe an erythrocyte-rich venous thrombus but not its organised lamination. Lines of Zahn require alternating layers.
## answer_c
White streaks only
## explanation_c
The pale platelet-fibrin component is only one half of the pattern. The diagnostic appearance alternates pale and red layers.
## answer_d
No lines present
## explanation_d
Absence of lamination cannot define Lines of Zahn. It may occur in poorly laminated venous thrombi or a post-mortem clot.

Hyaline thrombi are microscopic capillary thrombi in this module's classification. They are not the named gross type defined by complete small-artery occlusion.

Stroke is commonly the consequence of vascular occlusion rather than a source of fat globules. It does not provide the disrupted fat-containing tissue required here.
## answer_d
Aneurysm
## explanation_d
An aneurysm promotes turbulent flow and arterial thrombosis. It does not characteristically produce fat embolism.

A septic embolus carries infected thrombotic material and may produce a pyaemic abscess. Its defining content is infection, not fat.
