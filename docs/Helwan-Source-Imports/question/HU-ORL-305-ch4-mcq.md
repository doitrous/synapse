## title
A patient corrected with a minus 2 diopter cylinder axis 180 has:

## question
A patient corrected with a minus 2 diopter cylinder axis 180 has:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Simple hypermetropic astigmatism

## explanation_a
Simple hypermetropic astigmatism would need a plus, not minus, cylinder power; the sign here is minus, so this is myopic, not hypermetropic, astigmatism.

## answer_b
Simple myopic astigmatism

## explanation_b
A prescription that is pure cylinder, with no sphere component, means only one meridian carries refractive error while the other is left at zero power (emmetropic) — this is by definition simple astigmatism. Because the cylinder power is minus, the affected meridian is myopic, making this simple myopic astigmatism. Had a sphere component also been present, or had both meridians been ametropic, it would be compound rather than simple.

## answer_c
Compound myopic astigmatism

## explanation_c
Compound myopic astigmatism needs a spherical component alongside the cylinder (both meridians ametropic); a prescription with only a cylinder and no sphere is simple, not compound.

## answer_d
Simple myopia

## explanation_d
Simple myopia needs no cylindrical component at all (spherical error only); a cylinder-only prescription is astigmatism, not simple myopia.

In keratoconus, all the following statements are true EXCEPT:

## question
In keratoconus, all the following statements are true EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Munson sign is positive

## explanation_a
Munson sign, a V-shaped indentation of the lower lid on downgaze from the cone-shaped cornea, is a genuine keratoconus sign, so it is true and not the exception being sought.

## answer_b
Corneal opacity may be seen

## explanation_b
Corneal opacity (scarring) can occur in advanced keratoconus, particularly after acute hydrops, so this is true and not the exception being sought.

## answer_c
Corneal topography is diagnostic

## explanation_c
Corneal topography, showing an asymmetric, inferiorly steep pattern, is the key diagnostic test for keratoconus, so this is true and not the exception being sought.

## answer_d
Kayser Fleisher ring is present.

## explanation_d
The Kayser-Fleischer ring is a golden-brown ring of copper deposition in Descemet's membrane, a sign of Wilson's disease (hepatolenticular degeneration), not of keratoconus. It has no association with corneal ectasia or thinning, so it does not belong among keratoconus's true features. Because this question asks which statement is NOT true, and the other three are genuine keratoconus facts, the Kayser-Fleischer ring is correctly the exception.

## title
Early senile nuclear cataract is accompanied by:

## question
Early senile nuclear cataract is accompanied by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Myopic shift of refraction.

## explanation_a
Early senile nuclear cataract increases the lens nucleus's refractive index as it sclerosis, producing a myopic (not hyperopic) shift of refraction — the classic "second sight" phenomenon where near vision transiently improves. This is one of the earliest detectable refractive changes in nuclear cataract, sometimes noticed before any visible opacity. It reverses as the cataract matures and vision progressively declines.

## answer_b
Hyperopic shift of refraction.

## explanation_b
The refractive shift in early nuclear cataract is myopic, not hyperopic; the increased lens power shifts focus forward, not backward.

## answer_c
Night blindness.

## explanation_c
Night blindness is a retinal symptom (as in retinitis pigmentosa or vitamin A deficiency), not a feature of early nuclear cataract.

## answer_d
Ocular pain.

## explanation_d
Cataract is characteristically painless; ocular pain suggests a different process, such as acute angle closure glaucoma or inflammation, not cataract itself.

## title
Which refractive error is characteristic of keratoconus?

## question
Which refractive error is characteristic of keratoconus?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Regular astigmatism

## explanation_a
Regular astigmatism has perpendicular principal meridians correctable by a spectacle cylinder; keratoconus instead produces irregular astigmatism.

## answer_b
Hypermetropia

## explanation_b
Keratoconus shifts refraction toward myopia, not hypermetropia.

## answer_c
Irregular astigmatism

## explanation_c
Keratoconus's asymmetric corneal steepening does not produce the regular, perpendicular-meridian astigmatism a spectacle cylinder can fully correct; instead it produces irregular astigmatism, whose surface is non-uniform, which is why rigid contact lenses often outperform spectacles. This irregular astigmatism, superimposed on progressive myopia, is the hallmark refractive picture of keratoconus. It is also why corneal topography, not keratometry alone, is needed to fully characterise it.

## answer_d
Emmetropia

## explanation_d
Emmetropia means no refractive error; keratoconus produces significant refractive error, not emmetropia.

## title
Hypermetropia means that the refractive system of the eye is:

## question
Hypermetropia means that the refractive system of the eye is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Weaker than the emmetrope.

## explanation_a
Hypermetropia means the eye's overall refractive system is weaker than an emmetropic eye's, whether from a shorter axial length, flatter cornea, or weaker lens, so that parallel rays would focus behind the retina without extra convergence. This weaker system is why a hypermetropic eye must often accommodate even for distance vision. It is corrected by adding convergent power with a convex lens.

## answer_b
Stronger than the emmetrope.

## explanation_b
A refractive system stronger than the emmetrope's describes myopia, not hypermetropia.

## answer_c
The cornea is very steep.

## explanation_c
A very steep cornea would make the eye's refractive system stronger, tending toward myopia, not the weaker system that defines hypermetropia.

## answer_d
The eye has long axial length.

## explanation_d
Long axial length is a feature of myopia, not hypermetropia, which instead often has a short axial length.

## title
Presbyopia causes:

## question
Presbyopia causes:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Difficulty in seeing far.

## explanation_a
Presbyopia affects near vision, not distance vision; difficulty seeing far suggests myopia or another distance refractive error, not presbyopia.

## answer_b
Difficulty in reading.

## explanation_b
Presbyopia is the age-related weakening of accommodation that recedes the near point, making close tasks such as reading uncomfortable or blurred while distance vision, if otherwise emmetropic, remains unaffected. It typically becomes symptomatic from the mid-40s as the lens stiffens and ciliary efficiency declines. It is corrected with a convex reading addition, not a distance-vision change.

## answer_c
Unocular diplopia.

## explanation_c
Unocular (monocular) diplopia is caused by conditions distorting one eye's optical path, such as lens subluxation or irregular astigmatism, not by presbyopia.

## answer_d
Subluxation of crystalline lens.

## explanation_d
Subluxation of the crystalline lens is a structural zonular problem (for example, trauma or Marfan syndrome), unrelated to presbyopia's age-related accommodative decline.

## title
The simplest treatment of astigmatism is:

## question
The simplest treatment of astigmatism is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cycloplegia.

## explanation_a
Cycloplegia (paralysing accommodation with drops) is used to obtain an accurate refraction for diagnosis, not as a treatment for astigmatism itself.

## answer_b
Cylindrical lenses glass prescription.

## explanation_b
Cylindrical lenses, whether in spectacles or as part of a sphero-cylindrical prescription, are the simplest, safest and most direct way to correct astigmatism, bending light differently along the two principal meridians to bring both into focus on the retina. They require no surgery and can be adjusted easily as the refractive error changes. Laser surgery and keratoplasty are reserved for cases where spectacle or contact lens correction is inadequate or undesired, not as first-line treatment.

## answer_c
Laser surgery.

## explanation_c
Laser surgery (such as LASIK) can correct astigmatism but is a more invasive, non-simplest option compared with cylindrical lenses.

## answer_d
Keratoplasty.

## explanation_d
Keratoplasty (corneal transplantation) is a major surgical procedure reserved for corneal disease, far from the simplest astigmatism treatment.

Keratoconus:

Can cause astigmatism.

Keratoconus is not treated with eye drops; management is contact lenses, collagen cross-linking or keratoplasty depending on severity.

Is not a progressive disease.

Keratoplasty means:

## question
Keratoplasty means:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Intra-ocular lens implantation.

## explanation_a
Intra-ocular lens implantation replaces the crystalline lens, typically after cataract surgery; it does not involve the cornea and is not keratoplasty.

## answer_b
Transplantation of a cornea from eye bank.

## explanation_b
Keratoplasty literally means transplanting corneal tissue, typically from an eye bank donor, to replace diseased or scarred host cornea, whether as a full-thickness (penetrating) or partial-thickness (lamellar) graft. It restores corneal clarity or shape when the patient's own cornea cannot be corrected by lenses or contact lenses alone. The depth of the recipient's disease dictates which specific keratoplasty type is used.

## answer_c
Excimer laser treatment of the cornea.

## explanation_c
Excimer laser treatment of the cornea (as in LASIK or PRK) reshapes the patient's own corneal tissue; it does not transplant donor tissue and so is not keratoplasty.

## answer_d
Plastic surgery of the eyelids.

## explanation_d
Plastic surgery of the eyelids is blepharoplasty, an entirely different procedure on a different structure from keratoplasty.

## title
Mature intumescent cataract can be associated with:

## question
Mature intumescent cataract can be associated with:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Visual acuity better than 2/60.

## explanation_a
An intumescent cataract typically causes significantly reduced visual acuity, not acuity better than 2/60; the swollen opaque lens blocks substantial light and image detail.

## answer_b
Secondary angle closure glaucoma.

## explanation_b
An intumescent cataract's swollen lens crowds the anterior segment, pushing the iris-lens diaphragm forward and shallowing the anterior chamber, which can precipitate secondary (phacomorphic) angle closure glaucoma with acutely raised intraocular pressure. This is a recognised, sight-threatening complication that makes prompt surgical removal important. Watching and waiting risks an acute pressure rise and permanent optic nerve damage.

## answer_c
Good red reflex on retinoscopy.

## explanation_c
A dense, swollen cataract blocks the fundus view, so the red reflex is dim, absent or glistening white, not "good."

## answer_d
Can be treated medically.

## explanation_d
Cataract, intumescent or otherwise, does not respond to medical treatment; surgical removal is required.

Myopia can be corrected by:

## answer_a
Concave lenses

## explanation_a
Myopia is corrected by concave (minus, diverging) lenses, which diverge incoming light before it enters the eye so the focal point moves back from in front of the retina onto it. This is the standard, direct correction for pure myopia. Concave lenses are identifiable by their negative dioptric power on a prescription.

Convex lenses

## explanation_b
Convex lenses converge light and correct hypermetropia, the opposite refractive error from myopia.

## answer_c
Cylindrical lenses

## explanation_c
Cylindrical lenses correct astigmatism specifically, not pure spherical myopia.

## answer_d
Spherocylindrical lenses

## explanation_d
Spherocylindrical lenses are used when astigmatism coexists with a spherical error; for pure myopia alone, a simple concave (spherical) lens suffices.

Fluorescein angiography

## explanation_e
Fluorescein angiography images retinal and choroidal blood vessels; it has no role in diagnosing a corneal ectasia like keratoconus.

## question
On cycloplegic refraction, when one meridian is hyperopic and the other is myopic:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Helwan Year-3 authoring lane

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Simple astigmatism.

## explanation_a
Simple astigmatism requires one meridian to be emmetropic (zero power) while only the other is ametropic; here both meridians are ametropic (one hyperopic, one myopic), so this is not simple astigmatism.

## answer_b
Compound astigmatism.

## explanation_b
Compound astigmatism requires both meridians to be ametropic in the same direction (both hyperopic or both myopic); here one is hyperopic and the other myopic (opposite directions), so this is not compound astigmatism.

## answer_c
Mixed astigmatism.

## explanation_c
When cycloplegic refraction shows one principal meridian is hyperopic and the other is myopic, the two meridians are ametropic in opposite directions, which by definition is mixed astigmatism. This differs from simple astigmatism (one meridian emmetropic) and compound astigmatism (both meridians ametropic in the same direction). Recognising this pattern is important because mixed astigmatism's correction (a sphero-cylindrical lens straddling plano) differs from the other types.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Since mixed astigmatism is the correct, specific term for one hyperopic and one myopic meridian, "none of the above" is incorrect.

Posterior staphyloma

Posterior staphyloma is likewise a complication of high (pathological) myopia's ectatic, thinned posterior globe wall, not of hypermetropia.

When a dense cataract or other media opacity prevents formal visual acuity or fundus examination, projection of light — testing whether the patient can correctly identify the direction a light is shone from — estimates whether the retina and optic nerve behind the opacity are still functioning well enough to justify surgery. Corneal topography.

Corneal topography assesses corneal shape, unrelated to retinal or optic nerve function, and so does not serve to estimate visual prognosis through a lens opacity.
