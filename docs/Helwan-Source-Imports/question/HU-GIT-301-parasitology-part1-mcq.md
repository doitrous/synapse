## answer_a

## explanation_a
Taenia solium is a cestode, not the fluke this triclabendazole question concerns, and it is treated with praziquantel, not triclabendazole.

## answer_b
Taenia saginata

## explanation_b
Taenia saginata is a cestode treated with praziquantel; triclabendazole is not indicated for it.

## answer_c
Hymenolepis nana

## explanation_c
Hymenolepis nana is a cestode treated with praziquantel; triclabendazole is not indicated for it.

## answer_d
Fasciola hepatica

## explanation_d
Triclabendazole is the drug of choice for Fasciola hepatica infection, because the adult and migrating immature fluke are both susceptible to its mechanism, unlike the case with praziquantel, which is ineffective against Fasciola despite being broadly active against most other trematodes and cestodes. This drug-species pairing is a standard, frequently tested exception in medical parasitology: praziquantel covers almost everything except Fasciola, and triclabendazole is the specific answer whenever a Fasciola treatment question is asked. Recognising Fasciola as the exception to praziquantel's broad spectrum is exactly the fact this question is testing.
