## summary
Muscles of facial expression (second arch, facial nerve) are classified by embryology and nerve supply, not location: temporalis is a muscle of mastication despite lying in the head, and buccinator is a muscle of facial expression despite assisting chewing.

## sections
### The classifying rule
Facial-expression muscles develop from the second pharyngeal arch and are supplied by the facial nerve (CN VII). Muscles of mastication develop from the first arch and are supplied by the trigeminal nerve's mandibular division (V3).

### Two counterintuitive cases
Temporalis, though in the head, is a muscle of mastication (excluded from facial expression). Buccinator, though it assists chewing by pressing the cheek to the teeth, is a muscle of facial expression (facial nerve), not mastication.

## hold_these
Facial expression = 2nd arch + facial nerve. Mastication = 1st arch + trigeminal (V3). Temporalis = mastication. Buccinator = facial expression.

## lose_the_mark
Classifying by chewing-related function rather than by embryological arch and nerve supply.

## summary
The triangular "dangerous area" of the face, around the nose and upper lip, drains via valveless veins directly to the cavernous sinus, so infection there can spread intracranially.

## sections
### Why it is dangerous
The facial vein, deep facial vein, pterygoid plexus and ophthalmic veins connecting this triangle to the cavernous sinus are all valveless, so infection (e.g. a squeezed pimple) can travel backward into the cavernous sinus.

## hold_these
Dangerous area of the face drains, via valveless veins, to the cavernous sinus.

## lose_the_mark
Naming an unrelated dural sinus (superior/inferior sagittal, sigmoid) instead of the cavernous sinus.

## summary
The scalp runs from the eyebrows (anterior) to the superior nuchal line (posterior) and the superior temporal line (lateral), gliding at its loose areolar (fourth) layer, which also carries emissary veins.

## sections
### Boundaries
Anterior: eyebrows. Posterior: superior nuchal line. Lateral: superior temporal line, below which the temporal region takes over.

The outer three scalp layers glide as one unit over the pericranium at the loose areolar (fourth) layer, which also carries emissary veins connecting the scalp's veins to the intracranial dural sinuses.

## lose_the_mark

## summary
The scalp's cutaneous supply splits by embryological source: trigeminal nerve branches (ophthalmic V1 anteriorly, auriculotemporal V3 in front of the ear) supply the anterior/lateral scalp, while cervical spinal nerves (lesser/greater occipital, great auricular) supply behind and around the ear.

## sections
### Trigeminal (cranial nerve) territory
The ophthalmic division's supraorbital/supratrochlear nerves supply the anterior scalp toward the vertex; the mandibular division's auriculotemporal nerve supplies the scalp in front of the ear.

### Cervical plexus (spinal nerve) territory
The lesser occipital nerve (ventral ramus C2) supplies behind the auricle; the greater occipital nerve (dorsal ramus C2) supplies the posterior scalp/occiput; the great auricular nerve (ventral rami C2-C3) supplies the auricle and angle of the mandible.

## hold_these
Trigeminal nerve: anterior scalp (V1) and in front of the ear (V3, auriculotemporal). Cervical spinal nerves: behind and around the ear.

## lose_the_mark
Assuming the whole scalp is trigeminal (cranial nerve) territory; the posterior/behind-the-ear zone is cervical spinal nerve territory instead.

## summary
Orbicularis oculi (orbital/palpebral/lacrimal parts) is supplied throughout by the facial nerve's temporal and zygomatic branches; its lacrimal part specifically pumps tears via the lacrimal sac.

## sections
### Three parts, one nerve
Orbital (firm closure), palpebral (gentle blink) and lacrimal (tear pump) parts are all supplied by the facial nerve (temporal and zygomatic branches).

### The tear pump
The lacrimal part encircles the lacrimal sac; blinking compresses and releases it, pumping tears from the punctum/canaliculi into the sac and onward to the nasolacrimal duct.

## hold_these
Orbicularis oculi = facial nerve (temporal + zygomatic branches). Lacrimal part = tear pump.

## lose_the_mark
Confusing orbicularis oculi's motor (facial nerve) supply with the eyelids' separate sensory (trigeminal) supply.

## summary
Facial wounds gape because facial muscles insert into skin rather than bone; occipitofrontalis's frontal belly pulls the scalp forward (corrugating the forehead) while its occipital belly pulls it backward.

## sections
### Cutaneous insertion and gaping
Facial-expression muscles insert into skin, not bone; a wound crossing their line of pull is actively separated (gapes) by ongoing muscle tone.

### Occipitofrontalis's two bellies
The frontal belly (no bony origin, inserts into forehead/eyebrow skin) raises the eyebrows and corrugates the forehead; lost in facial nerve palsy. The occipital belly (from the occipital bone) pulls the scalp backward.

## hold_these
Cutaneous insertion causes wound gaping. Frontal belly = forward pull/corrugation. Occipital belly = backward pull.

## lose_the_mark
Confusing the frontal belly's forward, corrugating action with the occipital belly's backward action.

Orbicularis oris is the lip's sphincter muscle; platysma, supplied by the facial nerve's cervical branch, tenses neck skin and is described as counteracting atmospheric pressure on the superficial neck veins.

## sections

The circular sphincter muscle of the lips; its paralysis prevents proper mouth closure, causing drooling.

### Platysma and the cervical branch
Platysma, a facial-expression muscle in the neck's superficial fascia, is supplied by the facial nerve's cervical branch (its most inferior terminal branch) and tenses the skin of the neck, helping counteract atmospheric pressure on the superficial neck veins.

## summary
The auriculotemporal nerve arises from the mandibular division (V3); this source's printed keys name the supratrochlear nerve as closest to the midline (ophthalmic division) and the ophthalmic nerve as the external nasal nerve's parent at the divisional level, both flagged with a doubt against more precise standard teaching.

## sections
### Auriculotemporal nerve
Arises from V3, encircles the middle meningeal artery, supplies the temporal region and auricle, and carries facial-nerve parasympathetic fibres to the parotid.

### Ophthalmic division branch order (doubt)
This source names the supratrochlear nerve as closest to the midline; many standard references instead name the infratrochlear nerve. The external nasal nerve's most immediate parent is the anterior ethmoidal nerve, though it belongs to the ophthalmic division's chain overall, which this source's printed key (ophthalmic nerve) reflects at the divisional level.

## hold_these
Auriculotemporal nerve = mandibular division (V3). This source: supratrochlear = closest to midline; external nasal nerve = ophthalmic division (via anterior ethmoidal).

## lose_the_mark
Assuming the auriculotemporal nerve is a facial-nerve branch because it carries parasympathetic fibres to the parotid; its origin is trigeminal (V3).

## summary
The face and scalp drain by region: lower lip to submental nodes; anterior scalp, forehead and cheek to preauricular nodes; posterior scalp to occipital nodes.

## sections
### The regional map
Lower lip (midline) drains to submental nodes; anterior scalp/forehead and the cheek both drain to preauricular nodes; the most posterior scalp drains to occipital nodes, with postauricular nodes as an intermediate group just behind the ear.

## hold_these
Lower lip = submental. Anterior scalp/forehead/cheek = preauricular. Posterior scalp = occipital.

## lose_the_mark
Confusing the postauricular (just behind the ear) group with the occipital (very back of the scalp) group.

## summary
Inability to close the eye together with drooping of the mouth angle on the same side indicates a facial nerve (CN VII) lesion, since both orbicularis oculi and the mouth muscles are facial-nerve targets.

## sections
### The sign combination
Orbicularis oculi (eye closure) and the muscles around the mouth are both facial-nerve muscles; a facial nerve lesion therefore produces both signs together on the affected side, the classic Bell's palsy presentation.

## hold_these
Eye-closure failure + mouth-angle droop, same side = facial nerve (CN VII) lesion.

## lose_the_mark
Attributing this combination to a purely sensory nerve rather than loss of facial-nerve motor supply.

## summary
The supraorbital artery is a branch of the ophthalmic artery (internal carotid), not the external carotid, reaching the forehead through the supraorbital notch.

## sections
### Internal carotid route to the forehead
The supraorbital artery arises from the ophthalmic artery within the orbit, passing through the supraorbital notch/foramen to the forehead and anterior scalp, illustrating the internal/external carotid anastomosis over the scalp.

## hold_these
Supraorbital artery = branch of the ophthalmic artery (internal carotid route).

## lose_the_mark
Assuming all scalp/forehead arteries arise from the external carotid; the supraorbital (and supratrochlear) arteries instead come via the internal carotid's ophthalmic artery.
