## summary
Sympathetic pelvic outflow drives ejaculation and vasoconstricts (shrinks) the external genitalia, while the parasympathetic pelvic nerve (S2-S4) vasodilates the same vessels for erection. The adrenal medulla is a modified sympathetic ganglion — direct preganglionic cholinergic supply from the lateral horn of the lower thoracic cord, basal secretion reduced during sleep, and an epinephrine-dominant (about 80:20) catecholamine output. Only cranial nerves III, VII, IX and X carry parasympathetic fibres. Every preganglionic autonomic neuron releases acetylcholine (nicotinic); parasympathetic postganglionic neurons release only acetylcholine (muscarinic), while sympathetic postganglionic fibres are mostly noradrenergic, sit distally in a short pre-/long post-ganglionic arrangement, and can be interrupted (e.g. in the T1-T2-to-cervical-ganglion pathway) to produce a Horner's-type picture.

## sections
### Definition
This article reviews the autonomic-nervous-system facts recurring across the AEP department's "Previous Exams" papers — pelvic visceral effects, adrenal medullary anatomy and secretion, cranial nerve parasympathetic outflow, ganglion anatomy, and pre-/postganglionic neurotransmitters — that are not already covered by this lane's earlier adrenoceptor-pharmacology (aepphysio12) or basic-neuroanatomy (aepcns) clusters.

### Mechanism
Sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions act as functional opposites at the pelvic viscera: sympathetic outflow contracts the vas deferens/seminal vesicles/prostate (ejaculation) and vasoconstricts the external genitalia (shrinkage), while the parasympathetic pelvic nerve vasodilates the same vessels (erection) and mediates micturition/defecation, which sympathetic outflow instead inhibits. Ganglion location sets fibre length: sympathetic ganglia sit near the spinal cord (short preganglionic, long postganglionic fibres, cell bodies distal to the effector organ), while parasympathetic ganglia sit at the effector organ itself (long preganglionic, short postganglionic fibres). The adrenal medulla is an exception to ordinary sympathetic anatomy — a modified ganglion reached directly by preganglionic cholinergic fibres from the lateral (not anterior) horn of the lower thoracic cord, its chromaffin cells acting as the "postganglionic" secretory unit.

### Key determinants
Only cranial nerves III, VII, IX and X carry parasympathetic fibres (IX to the parotid gland via the otic ganglion); sympathetic outflow to the head and neck originates specifically from T1-T2 and ascends the cervical sympathetic chain to the cervical ganglia — interruption anywhere along this path can produce a Horner's-type picture (miosis, ptosis from superior tarsal muscle paralysis, anhidrosis). Every preganglionic autonomic neuron, sympathetic or parasympathetic, releases acetylcholine onto nicotinic receptors; parasympathetic postganglionic neurons release only acetylcholine (muscarinic), while sympathetic postganglionic fibres are mostly noradrenergic (sweat glands are the cholinergic exception). The adrenal medulla secretes catecholamines continuously at a basal, sleep-reduced rate, in a roughly 80:20 epinephrine:norepinephrine ratio.

### Clinical significance
Organophosphate (insecticide or nerve-agent) poisoning irreversibly inhibits acetylcholinesterase, flooding muscarinic receptors with acetylcholine and producing a muscarinic-excess picture (salivation, lacrimation, GI cramps/diarrhoea, urination, miosis, bronchospasm) — the mechanism behind a farm worker's insecticide-exposure presentation. A Horner's-type picture (miosis, ptosis, anhidrosis) points to interruption of the T1-T2-to-cervical-ganglion sympathetic pathway, including from cervical/upper-thoracic spinal surgery or injury. Sympathetic and parasympathetic pelvic outflow together explain the two phases of the sexual response (parasympathetic erection, sympathetic ejaculation), and disruption of either can present as sexual dysfunction.

## hold_these
Sympathetic pelvic outflow: ejaculation + vasoconstriction/shrinkage of external genitalia. Parasympathetic pelvic nerve (S2-S4): vasodilation/erection, micturition, defecation. Adrenal medulla: preganglionic cholinergic supply from the LATERAL horn of the lower thoracic cord (not anterior horn); basal secretion, decreased in sleep; ~80% epinephrine, ~20% norepinephrine. Cranial parasympathetic nerves: III, VII, IX, X only (IX → parotid via otic ganglion). Sympathetic ganglia near the spinal cord (short pre-/long post-ganglionic fibres); parasympathetic ganglia at the effector organ (long pre-/short post-ganglionic fibres). Sympathetic outflow to head/neck: T1-T2, via the cervical sympathetic chain. Preganglionic transmitter (both divisions): acetylcholine, nicotinic. Parasympathetic postganglionic transmitter: acetylcholine only, muscarinic; sympathetic postganglionic: mostly noradrenergic, sweat glands cholinergic.

## lose_the_mark
Reversing sympathetic and parasympathetic pelvic effects (sympathetic = ejaculation/shrinkage; parasympathetic = erection). Citing the anterior (rather than lateral) horn as the adrenal medulla's preganglionic spinal origin. Naming any cranial nerve besides III, VII, IX, X as parasympathetic. Reversing which division has short vs long postganglionic fibres. Assuming atropine blocks the motor end-plate (it blocks muscarinic, not nicotinic, receptors). Confusing acetylcholine's synthesising enzyme (choline acetyltransferase) with its degrading enzyme (acetylcholinesterase).
