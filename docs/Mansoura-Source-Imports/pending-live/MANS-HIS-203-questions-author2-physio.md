Incorrect. 6-8.5 million/mm3 is well above the normal adult range and would suggest polycythaemia, not a normal count.

5-5.5 million/mm3

Correct. The normal RBC count for adult males is 5-5.5 million/mm3. ## title
Chain composition of adult haemoglobin (HbA)

## question
Which of the following haemoglobin types contains 2 alpha and 2 beta chains?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Hb-A

## explanation_a
Correct. Adult haemoglobin, HbA, is a tetramer of 2 alpha and 2 beta globin chains and makes up about 96-98% of total adult haemoglobin. This 2-alpha/2-beta pairing is what a question means whenever it names "normal adult haemoglobin" without further qualification.

## answer_b
Hb-A2

## explanation_b
Incorrect. HbA2 pairs 2 alpha chains with 2 delta chains, not 2 beta chains, and makes up only about 2-4% of adult haemoglobin.

## answer_c
Hb-F

## explanation_c
Incorrect. HbF (fetal haemoglobin) pairs 2 alpha chains with 2 gamma chains, giving it a higher oxygen affinity than adult HbA — a different pairing from the one this question asks for.

## answer_d
Immunoglobulin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Immunoglobulin is an antibody protein with no haem group and no globin-chain structure at all; it is unrelated to hemoglobin's chain composition.

## answer_e
Myoglobin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Myoglobin is a single-chain, single-haem protein found in muscle, not a tetramer, and so cannot be described by a "2 and 2" chain pairing.

## title
Chain composition of fetal haemoglobin (HbF)

## question
The structure of fetal haemoglobin (Hb F) consists of 4 polypeptide chains:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
2 alpha and 2 beta polypeptide chains

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 2 alpha and 2 beta chains describe HbA, the adult form, not HbF.

## answer_b
2 alpha and 2 delta polypeptide chains

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 2 alpha and 2 delta chains describe HbA2, a minor adult haemoglobin (about 2-4%), not HbF.

## answer_c
2 alpha and 2 gamma polypeptide chains

## explanation_c
Correct. Fetal haemoglobin, HbF, is a tetramer of 2 alpha and 2 gamma globin chains. Its gamma chain binds 2,3-BPG less tightly than the adult beta chain does, giving HbF a higher oxygen affinity than HbA — the mechanism that lets the fetus pull oxygen across the placenta from the mother's own, lower-affinity, adult haemoglobin. HbF predominates before birth and is largely replaced by HbA by about four months of age.

## answer_d
2 alpha and 2 theta polypeptide chains

## explanation_d
Incorrect. "Theta" is not a globin chain used in any normal human haemoglobin type.

## answer_e
2 alpha and 2 kappa polypeptide chains

## explanation_e
Incorrect. "Kappa" is an immunoglobulin light-chain type, not a haemoglobin globin chain.

Average diameter of an RBC

## subject

7.5 um

## explanation_b
Correct. The mature erythrocyte's average diameter is about 7.5 micrometres, corresponding to a mean corpuscular volume of about 87-95 cubic micrometres. ## answer_e

## explanation_e
Incorrect. 10 micrometres is closer to the size of a neutrophil or lymphocyte, not an RBC.

## title
Major function of RBCs

## question
The major function of RBCs is:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Carriage of oxygen from the lungs to the tissues

## explanation_a
Correct. The major function of the erythrocyte is carriage of oxygen from the lungs to the tissues, bound to haemoglobin. Every other function on this list is real but secondary — none of them is what the red cell mass exists primarily to do.

## answer_b
Transport of CO2

## explanation_b
Incorrect. RBCs do carry some CO2 (partly as carbamino-haemoglobin), but most CO2 transport is as plasma bicarbonate; this is a secondary contribution, not the major function.

## answer_c
Haemoglobin as an excellent acid-base buffer

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Haemoglobin's acid-base buffering is real but secondary to its oxygen-carrying role, which is why it is not the answer described as the "major function."

## answer_d
Increasing blood viscosity and peripheral resistance

## explanation_d
Incorrect. RBCs do contribute to blood viscosity, but this is an incidental physical consequence of the red cell mass, not a function the erythrocyte exists to perform.

## answer_e
Storage of iron

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Iron storage is the role of ferritin and haemosiderin, mainly in the liver, spleen and bone marrow, not a function of the circulating erythrocyte.

QST-MANSHIS203-PHYSIOLOGY-REFRANGE-IRON-DEFICIENCY-RBC-MORPHOLOGY

## title
RBC morphology in iron deficiency anaemia

## question
The red cells in iron deficiency anaemia are:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Macrocytic normochromic

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Macrocytic (large) red cells with normal haemoglobin content describe megaloblastic anaemia (folate or B12 deficiency), not iron deficiency.

## answer_b
Normocytic normochromic

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Normocytic normochromic red cells describe anaemias of marrow depression or acute blood loss, where cell size and haemoglobin content per cell are unchanged, not iron deficiency.

## answer_c
Microcytic hypochromic

## explanation_c
Correct. Iron deficiency anaemia produces microcytic (small, MCV under 80 cubic micrometres) hypochromic (pale, low Hb content per cell, MCH under 25 pg) red cells. Insufficient iron limits haemoglobin synthesis during erythropoiesis, leaving each new cell both smaller and paler than normal. This is the pattern that distinguishes iron deficiency from the normocytic and macrocytic anaemias caused by other mechanisms.

## answer_d
Normocytic hypochromic

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Normocytic hypochromic is not the classic iron-deficiency pattern; iron deficiency's defining feature is a reduced cell size (microcytic) together with reduced haemoglobin content (hypochromic).

## answer_e
Spherocytic

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Spherocytic red cells describe hereditary spherocytosis, a membrane defect causing haemolysis, not the cell shape produced by iron deficiency.

QST-MANSHIS203-PHYSIOLOGY-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-MARROW-DEPRESSION-APLASTIC

## title
Result of severe bone marrow depression

## question
Severe depression of the bone marrow may result in:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Microcytic hypochromic anaemia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Microcytic hypochromic anaemia follows from iron deficiency limiting haemoglobin synthesis in an otherwise functioning marrow, not from the marrow itself being suppressed.

## answer_b
Increased number of granulocytes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Marrow depression reduces the output of every cell line, including granulocytes, so a fall (leucopenia), not a rise, is what follows.

## answer_c
Increased coagulability of the blood

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Marrow depression reduces platelet output (thrombocytopenia), which impairs clotting rather than increasing coagulability.

## answer_d
Aplastic anaemia, leucopenia, and thrombocytopenia

## explanation_d
Correct. Severe bone marrow depression fails every cell line at once, producing aplastic anaemia together with leucopenia and thrombocytopenia — a pancytopenia. This is distinct from anaemias caused by a specific nutrient deficiency or a haemoglobin defect, where the marrow itself is intact and only one line (or one product of it) is short.

## id
QST-MANSHIS203-PHYSIOLOGY-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-PERNICIOUS-ANAEMIA-B12

## title
Cause of improper RBC maturation

## question
Improper maturation of the red blood corpuscles can be caused by:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Bone marrow aplasia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bone marrow aplasia reduces the NUMBER of red cells produced (and every other line with them); it does not by itself cause the maturation defect this question describes.

## answer_b
Hypoxia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hypoxia is the physiological stimulus that increases erythropoietin and drives MORE erythropoiesis, not a cause of impaired maturation.

## answer_c
Iron deficiency

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Iron deficiency impairs haemoglobin synthesis, producing microcytic hypochromic cells, which is a different maturation defect from the one caused by vitamin B12 deficiency (megaloblastic change).

## answer_d
Vitamin B12 deficiency

## explanation_d
Correct. Vitamin B12 deficiency — classically from pernicious anaemia, where the stomach fails to produce intrinsic factor needed for B12 absorption — impairs DNA synthesis in the developing erythroblast, producing large, immature-looking (megaloblastic) cells that mature improperly. Hydroxocobalamin injection is the treatment, bypassing the absorption defect intrinsic factor deficiency causes.

## answer_e
Vitamin C deficiency

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy) affects collagen synthesis and wound healing; it is not a recognised cause of impaired red cell maturation.

QST-MANSHIS203-PHYSIOLOGY-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-EPO-STIMULATED-STAGE

## title
RBC development stage mainly stimulated by erythropoietin

## question
Which stage of RBC development is mainly stimulated by erythropoietin?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Proerythroblasts

## explanation_a
Correct. Erythropoietin acts mainly on the proerythroblast, the earliest committed cell of the erythroid series, stimulating its proliferation and differentiation and so setting the pace at which the whole erythroid line is produced. This is consistent with erythropoietin stimulating every step of erythropoiesis but exerting its main regulatory action at this earliest committed stage.

## answer_b
Normoblasts

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The normoblast is a later stage in the series, already committed and progressing toward enucleation; erythropoietin's main site of action is earlier, at the proerythroblast.

## answer_c
Reticulocytes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The reticulocyte is the last precursor stage before the mature erythrocyte, well past the point erythropoietin exerts its main proliferative stimulus.

## answer_d
Hemocytoblasts

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The haemocytoblast is the pluripotent stem cell that gives rise to all blood cell lines, not a stage specific to (or mainly stimulated by) erythropoietin.

## answer_e
Erythrocyte

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The mature erythrocyte no longer divides or responds to erythropoietin at all; erythropoietin's action is on the developing precursor stages, not the end product.

## title
Main site of erythropoietin formation in adult life

## question
What is the main site of erythropoietin hormone formation in adult life?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Adrenal gland

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The adrenal gland produces corticosteroids and catecholamines; it is not a site of erythropoietin production.

## answer_b
Bone marrow

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The bone marrow is where erythropoietin acts (on the proerythroblast and later stages), not where it is made.

## answer_c
Kidney

## explanation_c
Correct. In adult life, the kidney (mainly the peritubular interstitial cells) is the main site of erythropoietin production, accounting for about 85% of it, released in response to tissue hypoxia. The liver contributes the remaining minority (about 15%) and becomes relatively more important as an erythropoietin source before birth.

## answer_d
Liver

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The liver is erythropoietin's main source before birth and a minor secondary source in adult life, but the kidney, not the liver, is the main adult site.

## answer_e
Pituitary gland

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The pituitary gland secretes trophic hormones controlling other endocrine glands; it plays no role in erythropoietin production.
