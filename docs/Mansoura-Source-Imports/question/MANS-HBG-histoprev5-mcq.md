## title
Defining feature of simple columnar absorptive epithelium

## question
Simple columnar absorptive epithelium:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Has cilia on its apical surface

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cilia mark a ciliated epithelium (e.g. the respiratory tract or the Fallopian tube); the absorptive columnar cell's apical specialisation is a brush border of microvilli, not cilia.

## answer_b
The cells have vacuolated cytoplasm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A vacuolated, foamy cytoplasm is not this cell's defining feature — its cytoplasm is otherwise unremarkable apart from the apical brush border, unlike, say, the lipid-filled cells of a sebaceous gland.

## answer_c
Specialized in mucous secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mucous secretion is the job of the goblet cells interspersed between the absorptive cells, not of the absorptive cells themselves, whose job is absorption.

## answer_d
Lines the intestine

## explanation_d
Correct. Simple columnar absorptive epithelium, with its apical microvillous brush border that vastly increases surface area, lines the small and large intestine, where it is specialised for absorbing digested nutrients, water and electrolytes.

## answer_e
Formed of many layers

## explanation_e
Incorrect. It is a simple epithelium, a single layer of cells resting on the basement membrane, not a stratified (many-layered) one.

## title
Epithelial lining of the trachea and bronchi

## question
Trachea and bronchi are lined with:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Simple squamous epithelium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Simple squamous epithelium lines sites built for diffusion or filtration, such as the lung alveoli or the endothelium — it has no cilia and could not clear mucus.

## answer_b
Stratified columnar epithelium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Stratified columnar is a rare epithelium (parts of the conjunctiva, some gland ducts); it does not line the conducting airway.

## answer_c
Simple columnar epithelium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Simple (non-ciliated, single-layered) columnar epithelium lines absorptive/secretory surfaces such as the intestine, not the airway.

## answer_d
Pseudostratified columnar ciliated epithelium with goblet cells

## explanation_d
Correct. The trachea and bronchi, like the rest of the conducting portion of the respiratory tract, are lined by pseudostratified columnar ciliated epithelium: every cell touches the basement membrane but not all reach the free surface, giving a false (pseudo-) impression of layering. Interspersed mucous goblet cells and coordinated ciliary beating together trap and sweep particles and mucus out of the airway.

## answer_e
Transitional epithelium

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Transitional epithelium (urothelium) is confined to the urinary tract — renal pelvis, ureter, bladder and prostatic urethra — and is unrelated to the airway.

## title
What merocrine secretion spares the cell

## question
Regarding the Merocrine glands:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The secretory cells are destroyed during secretion

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Whole-cell destruction is the holocrine mode (e.g. the sebaceous gland), not merocrine.

## answer_b
The secretory cells are intact during secretion

## explanation_b
Correct. Merocrine secretion is released from the cell by exocytosis with no loss of cytoplasm or cell material at all — the secretory cell stays completely intact through repeated cycles of secretion. It is the commonest of the three secretory modes (merocrine, apocrine, holocrine); the salivary glands are a standard example.

## answer_c
The apical part of secretory cells is destroyed during secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Loss of just the apical cytoplasm with the secreted product is the apocrine mode (e.g. the lactating mammary gland), not merocrine.

## answer_d
Present in the liver

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Merocrine describes a mechanism of secretion, not a named gland, and is not specific to the liver — many exocrine glands throughout the body (most notably the salivary glands and pancreas) secrete this way.

## answer_e
Type of gland according to nature of secretion

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Merocrine/apocrine/holocrine classify glands by their MODE (mechanism) of secretion, not by the nature (serous/mucous) of what they secrete — that is a separate classification axis.

## title
What pinocytosis actually involves

## question
What is the process of pinocytosis:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is a form of exocytosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pinocytosis moves material INTO the cell (endocytosis), the opposite direction to exocytosis, which moves material out.

## answer_b
It involves ingestion of liquids rather than particles

## explanation_b
Correct. Pinocytosis ("cell drinking") is a form of endocytosis that takes in extracellular fluid and whatever is dissolved in it, through small invaginations of the plasma membrane — unlike phagocytosis ("cell eating"), which engulfs solid particles with pseudopodia.

## answer_c
It does not require ATP

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Endocytosis, pinocytosis included, is an active, energy-dependent process that consumes ATP to form and pinch off the vesicle.

## answer_d
It means digestion of pinocytotic vesicle

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pinocytosis is the uptake step (vesicle formation at the surface), not the subsequent digestion of the vesicle's contents, which is a separate, later lysosomal event.

## answer_e
Formation of vesicles only when large amount of material are being transported

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Pinocytotic vesicles form continuously for ordinary fluid-phase uptake, not only when large amounts of material must be moved.

## title
Phase when DNA doubles and the chromosome count follows

## question
DNA is synthesized and chromosome number is doubled in which of the following stages:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
G1 phase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. In G1 the cell is still in its pre-replication single (46-s) chromatid state; DNA has not yet been synthesized.

## answer_b
S phase

## explanation_b
Correct. S-phase is when the cell replicates its entire DNA content, converting each of the 46 chromosomes from a single chromatid (46-s) to two identical sister chromatids (46-d) joined at the centromere. The chromosome NUMBER stays 46 throughout — it is the chromatid content that doubles.

## answer_c
G0 phase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. G0 is a resting state cells exit the cycle into; no DNA replication happens there.

## answer_d
G2 phase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. G2 follows S-phase, once DNA has already doubled; G2 is a preparatory gap before mitosis, not the synthesis step itself.

## answer_e
Meiosis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Meiosis is not a phase of the mitotic cell cycle but two successive divisions; the single round of DNA replication that precedes it still happens in a preceding S-phase.

## question

## subject
fnd

## status

Oily

## explanation_a
Correct. The sebaceous gland's cells fill with lipid droplets that are released, together with the whole disintegrating cell, as sebum by holocrine secretion — an oily, lipid-rich product, not a watery, serous, or purely mucous one.

Waxy

Incorrect. Mucous secretion is viscous and glycoprotein-rich (goblet cells, mucous glands), unrelated to the lipid product of the sebaceous gland.

## answer_e
Mixed

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A mixed (mucoserous) gland contains both serous and mucous secretory units; the sebaceous gland's product is neither — it is oily sebum from holocrine breakdown of lipid-filled cells.

## title
Where a true flagellum is found in the human body

## question
Which of the followings is a site of flagella:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Esophagus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The esophagus is lined by stratified squamous non-keratinized epithelium, which carries no motile surface processes at all.

## answer_b
Fallopian tube

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The Fallopian tube is lined by ciliated (and secretory) simple columnar epithelium — many short cilia per cell, not a single flagellum.

Small intestine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The small intestine's absorptive cells carry microvilli (the brush border), not cilia or a flagellum.

Tail of sperm

## answer_e
Ureter

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The ureter is lined by transitional epithelium, which has no cilia or flagella; its wall moves urine by peristalsis, not ciliary or flagellar motion.

## title
Which ribosomes make the cell's secretory proteins

## question
Which of the followings synthesis protein used within the cell:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Attached ribosomes

## explanation_a
Correct. Ribosomes attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum synthesize proteins destined for secretion or for membrane insertion — the enzymes and hormones the cell exports.

## answer_b
Free ribosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This is reversed: free (unattached) cytoplasmic ribosomes make proteins the cell keeps and uses within itself — its own structural and glycolytic enzymes — while attached ribosomes make what is exported.

Golgi apparatus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts and packages proteins after they have already been synthesized by ribosomes; it does not itself synthesize protein.

## answer_d
Free ribosomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Duplicate distractor of option B — free ribosomes make the cell's own internal proteins, not its secreted (exported) ones.

## answer_e
sER

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Smooth ER has no ribosomes at all and does not synthesize protein; its jobs are lipid/steroid synthesis, detoxification and calcium regulation.
