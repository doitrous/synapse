# Item

## title
Effect of hepatic microsomal enzyme induction on drug dosing

## question
Stimulation of liver microsomal enzymes:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Requires the dose increase of some drugs.

## explanation_a
Correct. An enzyme inducer increases the synthesis of hepatic microsomal enzymes over days to weeks. This clears the induced drug (and any co-administered drug metabolised by the same enzymes) faster than before, so the drug loses effect at its usual dose. To restore the same plasma level and effect, the dose of that drug must be increased while induction persists.

## answer_b
Requires the dose decrease of some drugs.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A dose decrease is what enzyme inhibition, not induction, calls for — inhibition slows clearance and lets the drug accumulate.

## answer_c
Prolongs the duration of the action of a drug.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Induction speeds elimination, which shortens rather than prolongs a drug's duration of action.

## answer_d
Intensifies the unwanted reaction of a drug.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Induction does not by itself intensify an unwanted reaction; if the toxic species is a metabolite rather than the parent drug, induction can increase toxicity, but that is a specific exception, not the general rule being tested here.

## answer_e
Potentiates the efficacy of drugs.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Faster clearance from induction reduces, rather than potentiates, a drug's efficacy at a fixed dose.

## title
Term for a summed effect of two co-administered drugs

## question
If two drugs with the same effect, taken together, produce an effect that is equal in magnitude to the sum of the effects of the drugs given individually, it is called as:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Synergism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Synergism describes a combined effect greater than the simple sum of the two individual effects, not exactly equal to their sum.

## answer_b
Antagonism

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Antagonism is a combined effect that is reduced, the opposite of the summation described here.

## answer_c
Potentiation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Potentiation is when a drug with little or no effect of its own increases the effect of a second, active drug — not two active drugs summing.

## answer_d
Additive effect

## explanation_d
Correct. An additive effect is defined precisely as the combined action of two drugs with the same effect being equal to the arithmetic sum of their individual effects — for example, two mild analgesics together giving pain relief equal to the sum of each drug's own effect. This is the baseline case against which synergism (more than the sum) and antagonism (less than expected) are both defined.

## answer_e
Cumulative effect

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cumulative effect describes a drug's action building up with repeated dosing (as with slow elimination), not two different drugs' effects summing in one administration.

## title
Definition of drug tolerance

## question
Drug tolerance is a/an:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Decreased response to the usual dose of a drug.

## explanation_a
Correct. Drug tolerance is a decrease in the intensity of response to a drug upon repeated administration of the usual dose, so that a larger dose is needed to reproduce the original effect. It can be pharmacokinetic (the body clears the drug faster, e.g. through enzyme induction) or pharmacodynamic (the receptor itself becomes less responsive), and it develops gradually over a course of repeated dosing.

## answer_b
Increased response to the usual dose of a drug.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An increased response to the usual dose describes hypersensitivity or supersensitivity, the opposite of tolerance.

## answer_c
Abnormal reaction to drug due to genetic defect.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An abnormal reaction from a genetic defect is idiosyncrasy, a distinct concept from tolerance.

## answer_d
Inactivation of a drug by the kidney.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Renal inactivation of a drug is a metabolic/excretory process, not the definition of tolerance.

## answer_e
Immediate hypersensitivity reaction.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Immediate hypersensitivity is an IgE-mediated allergic reaction, unrelated to the gradual decrease in response that defines tolerance.

## question
Pharmacokinetics is the study of:

## subject
pharm

## status

E

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Mechanisms of drug action are the subject of pharmacodynamics — what the drug does to the body — not pharmacokinetics.

## answer_b
Genetic variation in drug response

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Genetic variation in drug response is the subject of pharmacogenetics, a narrower field distinct from pharmacokinetics.

## answer_c
Methods of new drug development

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Methods of developing new drugs fall under drug discovery and pharmaceutics, not the definition of pharmacokinetics.

Incorrect. Biological and therapeutic effects of a drug are what pharmacodynamics studies, the mirror-image discipline to pharmacokinetics.

Absorption, distribution, metabolism & excretion of drugs

## explanation_e
Correct. Pharmacokinetics is what the body does to the drug: it is the study of a drug's absorption into the body, distribution to its sites of action, metabolism (biotransformation) and excretion from the body — together abbreviated ADME. This is the counterpart to pharmacodynamics, which instead studies what the drug does to the body.

## title
Reason a loading dose is given

## question
Loading dose of a drug is given:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
To reduce complications

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Reducing complications is not the defining reason for a loading dose; a loading dose can in fact cause unexpected toxicity if given too fast or too large.

## answer_b
For drugs with long half life

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A long half-life is exactly why an ordinary maintenance-dose regimen would take four to five half-lives to reach steady state — the problem a loading dose solves, not a condition tied only to long-half-life drugs.

## answer_c
For drugs with short half life

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Short-half-life drugs reach steady state quickly on their own with regular maintenance dosing, so they need a loading dose less, not more.

## answer_d
When the drug eliminated by first order kinetic

## explanation_d
Incorrect. First-order elimination is the usual kinetic assumption behind dosing calculations generally; it is not itself the reason a loading dose is chosen.

## answer_e
To achieve steady state concentration in rapid time

## explanation_e
Correct. A loading dose is an initial, larger dose given to raise the plasma drug concentration rapidly to the target (steady-state) concentration, rather than waiting the four to five half-lives that regular maintenance dosing alone would take to reach that same level. It is calculated from the target concentration and the drug's volume of distribution.

## title
Effect of decreased hepatic microsomal enzyme activity on drug duration

## question
In case of liver disorders with a decline in microsomal enzyme activity the duration of action of some drugs is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Enlarged

## explanation_a
Correct. A decline in hepatic microsomal enzyme activity — whether from liver disease or an enzyme inhibitor — slows the metabolism (biotransformation) of drugs that are normally cleared by those enzymes. Because the drug is eliminated more slowly, it stays in the body, and hence stays active, for longer, so its duration of action is enlarged (prolonged) rather than shortened.

## answer_b
Decreased

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A decreased duration of action would follow from faster, not slower, clearance — the opposite of what reduced enzyme activity causes.

## answer_c
Potentiated

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 'Potentiated' describes an increase in a drug's intensity or effect from another agent's action, not the specific prolongation-of-duration effect being asked about here.

## answer_d
Remained unchanged

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Duration of action does change (it lengthens) when the metabolising enzymes that normally clear the drug are less active.

## answer_e
Changed insignificantly

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The change from reduced hepatic clearance can be clinically significant, not insignificant, particularly for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index.

Norepinephrine.

Incorrect. Norepinephrine (noradrenaline) is released by most postganglionic sympathetic fibres, not by preganglionic fibres.
