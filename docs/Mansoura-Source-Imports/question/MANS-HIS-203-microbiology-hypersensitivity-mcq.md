## title
Pollen and hypersensitivity type

## question
Pollen would most likely evoke which type of hypersensitivity?

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cytotoxic (Type II)

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Type II is antibody bound directly to a fixed antigen on a cell or tissue surface, destroying that cell; pollen allergy has no fixed cellular target of that kind.

## answer_b
Immune complex (Type III)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Type III is antibody bound to soluble circulating antigen forming immune complexes that deposit in tissue; that is not the mechanism behind pollen allergy either.

## answer_c
Immediate type I

## explanation_c
Correct. Pollen most likely evokes a type I (immediate) hypersensitivity reaction. Pollen-specific IgE sits bound to mast cells and basophils, and pollen cross-linking that surface IgE triggers degranulation within minutes. That immediate histamine release is what produces the symptoms of allergic rhinitis or asthma.

## answer_d
Cell mediated (Type IV)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Type IV is T-cell-mediated and delayed, developing over 24-72 hours with no antibody involved; pollen allergy is instead an immediate, antibody (IgE)-mediated reaction.

## title
Antibody class that cross-links mast cells

## question
Which of the following binds to mast cells and cross-links, resulting in degranulation and release of histamine?

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
IgM

## explanation_a
Incorrect. IgM is the first antibody made in a primary immune response and fixes complement efficiently; it does not bind mast-cell surface receptors the way IgE does.

## answer_b
IgG

## explanation_b
Incorrect. IgG mediates type II and III hypersensitivity mechanisms and crosses the placenta, but it is not the antibody class that sits bound to resting mast cells awaiting antigen.

## answer_c
IgD

## explanation_c
Incorrect. IgD is mainly a B-cell surface receptor involved in B-cell activation, not an antibody that arms mast cells for degranulation.

## answer_d
IgE

## explanation_d
Correct. IgE is the antibody class that binds high-affinity receptors on the surface of mast cells and basophils. When antigen cross-links two adjacent surface-bound IgE molecules, the cell degranulates within minutes, releasing histamine and other mediators — the mechanism behind type I hypersensitivity.

## title
Mechanism of type I hypersensitivity

## question
The mechanism of action of type I hypersensitivity involves:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Production of IgE in response to certain antigens

## explanation_a
Correct. Type I hypersensitivity begins with IgE production against a specific antigen (an allergen). That IgE binds mast cells and basophils, and re-exposure to the same antigen cross-links the surface-bound IgE, triggering immediate degranulation and the type I reaction.

## answer_b
Production of IgG in response to certain antigens

## explanation_b
Incorrect. IgG production is the antibody mechanism behind type II (cytotoxic) and type III (immune complex) hypersensitivity, not type I.

## answer_c
Production of IgM in response to certain antigens

## explanation_c
Incorrect. IgM is the antibody typically made early in a primary response and, like IgG, can participate in type II mechanisms via complement fixation; it is not the antibody class defining type I.

## answer_d
Production of IgA in response to certain antigens

## explanation_d
Incorrect. IgA is the antibody of mucosal secretions and is not the mechanism that defines type I hypersensitivity.

## title
Penicillin and pollen hypersensitivity share a mechanism

## question
Hypersensitivity to drugs like penicillin and hypersensitivity to pollen grains are both:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Mediated by IgM antibody

## explanation_a
Incorrect. IgM is not the antibody class responsible for either penicillin's immediate allergic reactions or pollen allergy.

## answer_b
Mediated by IgE antibody

## explanation_b
Correct. Penicillin hypersensitivity and pollen hypersensitivity are both mediated by IgE antibody, even though the two triggers — a drug and a plant allergen — are completely different substances. The Gell and Coombs classification sorts a reaction by its mechanism, not by what triggered it, and both of these are type I, IgE-mediated reactions.

## answer_c
Mediated by IgG antibody

## explanation_c
Incorrect. IgG mediates other hypersensitivity mechanisms (type II, type III), not the immediate, mast-cell-degranulating mechanism shared by penicillin and pollen allergy.

## answer_d
Mediated by IgA antibody

## explanation_d
Incorrect. IgA is the secretory antibody of mucosal surfaces, not the mediator of either penicillin's or pollen's immediate allergic reactions.

## answer_e
Mediated by IgD antibody

## explanation_e
Incorrect. IgD functions mainly as a B-cell surface receptor, not as the effector antibody in either of these two reactions.
