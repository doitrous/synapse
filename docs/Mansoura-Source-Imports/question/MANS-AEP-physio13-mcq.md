## title
What causes the resting membrane potential

## question
Which of the following characterizes the resting membrane potential in nerve fibers?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Normally about 10 mV

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The resting membrane potential of a nerve fibre is on the order of -70 to -90 mV, not 10 mV.

## answer_b
Due to K+ diffusion from outside to inside the nerve fibres

## explanation_b
Incorrect. K+ diffuses down its concentration gradient from inside to outside the nerve fibre at rest, not the other direction.

## answer_c
Due to diffusion of intracellular protein to outside the nerve fibres

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Intracellular proteins are large anions that cannot cross the membrane; they help set up the ionic imbalance but do not themselves diffuse out.

## answer_d
A passive process only

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The Na+-K+ pump is an active, ATP-dependent process that helps maintain the ionic gradients the resting potential depends on, so the resting state is not purely passive.

## answer_e
Due to unequal distribution of ions on both sides of the cell membrane

## explanation_e
Correct. The resting membrane potential arises because Na+, K+ and Cl- are unequally distributed across the membrane, with K+ concentrated inside and Na+ and Cl- concentrated outside. Because the membrane at rest is selectively far more permeable to K+ than to the other ions, this unequal distribution drives a net outward K+ leak that leaves the inside of the cell electrically negative relative to the outside. This ionic imbalance, maintained continuously by the Na+-K+ pump, is the fundamental basis of the resting potential.

## title
Selective permeability and the resting membrane potential

## question
Which one of the following does characterize the resting membrane potential in nerve fibers?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is a passive process only

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The Na+-K+ pump actively maintains the ionic gradients that the resting potential depends on, so the process is not passive alone.

## answer_b
It is caused by selective permeability of the cell and unequal distribution of ions in ICF and ECF

## explanation_b
Correct. The resting membrane potential is caused jointly by the cell membrane's selective permeability, which at rest favours K+ far more than Na+ or Cl-, and by the unequal distribution of these ions between the intracellular fluid (ICF) and extracellular fluid (ECF). Selective permeability alone would produce no potential without an ionic gradient to drive diffusion, and an ionic gradient alone would produce no potential without a membrane selectively permeable to one of the ions; the resting potential requires both together.

## answer_c
It is due to diffusion of intracellular protein to outside the nerve fibres

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Intracellular proteins are impermeant anions that help create the ionic imbalance but do not themselves diffuse across the membrane.

## answer_d
It is due to K+ diffusion from outside to inside nerve fibres

## explanation_d
Incorrect. K+ diffuses from inside to outside the nerve fibre at rest, not from outside to inside.

## answer_e
Its value is normally about 10 mV

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The resting membrane potential is roughly -70 to -90 mV in nerve fibres, not 10 mV.

## title
Measuring the resting membrane potential

## question
How could the resting membrane potential be measured?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Put the two electrodes inside the nerve fiber during rest

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Placing both electrodes inside the fibre records no potential difference at all, since both tips sit on the same side of the membrane.

## answer_b
Put the two electrodes outside the nerve fiber during rest

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Placing both electrodes outside the fibre likewise records no potential difference, since neither tip crosses the membrane.

## answer_c
Put the two electrodes outside the nerve fiber during stimulation of the nerve fiber by a strong stimulus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This arrangement records an action potential during stimulation, not the resting potential, and neither electrode is inside the cell.

## answer_d
Put the two electrodes outside the nerve fiber during stimulation of the nerve fiber by a weak stimulus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This arrangement records a subthreshold local response at best, not the resting potential.

## answer_e
Use a special voltmeter with one microelectrode inside and the second outside the nerve fibre

## explanation_e
Correct. The resting membrane potential is measured with a special voltmeter connected to one microelectrode inserted inside the nerve fibre and a second, reference electrode kept outside it, with the fibre undisturbed at rest. Because the potential difference is only measured across the membrane when one electrode punctures it and the other stays on the far side, this inside-outside arrangement is required to record any transmembrane potential at all. The reading obtained this way, without any stimulus applied, is by definition the resting membrane potential.

## title
Ion whose small concentration change most affects the resting potential

## question
The resting membrane potential of a cell is most affected by even a small change in the plasma concentration of:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Na+

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Because resting membrane permeability to Na+ is low, a small change in plasma Na+ concentration has little effect on the resting potential.

## answer_b
K+

## explanation_b
Correct. The resting membrane is far more permeable to K+ than to any other ion, so the resting potential sits close to the K+ equilibrium potential predicted by the Nernst equation. Because that equilibrium potential is a logarithmic function of the K+ concentration ratio across the membrane, even a small change in plasma (extracellular) K+ concentration shifts the ratio enough to noticeably change the resting potential — which is exactly why clinical hypo- or hyperkalemia disturbs excitable-tissue function. No other ion's plasma concentration has this outsized effect at rest, precisely because the membrane is not nearly as permeable to them.

## answer_c
Cl-

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Resting Cl- permeability, while present, does not dominate the resting potential the way K+ permeability does.

## answer_d
Ca2+

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Resting Ca2+ permeability is low, so small plasma Ca2+ changes have little direct effect on the resting membrane potential itself, even though Ca2+ affects the threshold for firing.

Highest resting membrane permeability

## question
Which ion has the highest permeability at rest?

## subject
neuro

## status

## explanation_a
Correct. At rest, the nerve fibre membrane is far more permeable to K+ than to any other ion, because far more of the membrane's resting (leak) channels are potassium-selective than sodium-, chloride- or calcium-selective. Ca2+

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Resting Ca2+ permeability is low compared with resting K+ permeability.

## answer_e
Mg2+

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Mg2+ is not a major determinant of resting membrane permeability in nerve fibres.

## title
Definition of equilibrium potential

## question
In equilibrium potential:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
For K+ is about +60 mV

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The K+ equilibrium potential is about -94 mV (approximately -90 mV in round numbers), not +60 mV — that value belongs to Na+.

## answer_b
For Na+ is about -90 mV

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The Na+ equilibrium potential is about +61 mV, not -90 mV — the values for Na+ and K+ have been swapped in this option.

## answer_c
The diffusion force equals the repulsion force, and no diffusion occurs

## explanation_c
Correct. At an ion's equilibrium potential, the electrical force pulling the ion back (the repulsion/attraction generated by the charge separation already present) exactly balances the chemical force driving it to diffuse down its concentration gradient. Because these two opposing forces are equal and opposite at that specific voltage, there is no further net diffusion of the ion across the membrane, even though individual ions continue to move in both directions. This is precisely what the Nernst equation calculates for a given ion and concentration gradient.

## answer_d
The diffusion of ions occurs through voltage-gated ion channels

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Reaching equilibrium potential does not require voltage-gated channels specifically; it is a property of any membrane permeable to the ion, including via resting leak channels.

## answer_e
Ions diffuse against their concentration gradient

## explanation_e
Incorrect. At equilibrium, net diffusion stops entirely; ions do not diffuse against their concentration gradient at equilibrium.

## title
Direction of Na+-K+ pump transport

## question
Which of the following is the action of the sodium-potassium pump in maintaining the resting membrane potential (RMP) of neurons?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Transferring Na+ and K+ into the cell

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This is the opposite of both ions' true pumped direction.

## answer_b
Transferring Na+ and K+ out of the cell

## explanation_b
Incorrect. K+ is pumped into, not out of, the cell.

## answer_c
Transferring Na+ into the cell and K+ out of the cell

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This reverses both ions' true pumped direction — the pump moves Na+ out and K+ in, not the reverse.

## answer_d
Transferring Na+ out of the cell and K+ into the cell

## explanation_d
Correct. The Na+-K+ pump is an antiport that uses the energy of ATP hydrolysis to move Na+ out of the cell and K+ into the cell, both against their electrochemical gradients — specifically, three Na+ ions out for every two K+ ions in. This active, continuous transport is what maintains the high intracellular K+ and high extracellular Na+ concentrations on which the resting membrane potential ultimately depends. Without it, the ionic gradients that set up the resting potential would gradually run down.

## answer_e
Transferring Na+ and K+ into the cell and H+ out of the cell

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The Na+-K+ pump does not transport H+; it exchanges Na+ and K+ only.

## title
Definition of membrane potential

## question
Membrane potential is the difference in electrical charge between which of the following?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Potassium and sodium ions

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Potassium and sodium are two of the ions whose unequal distribution helps generate the potential, but the potential itself is not defined as a difference between two ion species.

## answer_b
The inside and outside of the cell

## explanation_b
Correct. Membrane potential is, by definition, the electrical potential difference measured across the cell membrane — that is, between the inside (intracellular) and outside (extracellular) surfaces of the membrane. It is this transmembrane voltage, generated by the unequal distribution and selective permeability of ions, that is recorded when one electrode is placed inside the cell and a reference electrode outside it. All specific forms of membrane potential — resting, action and local potentials — are instances of this same inside-versus-outside electrical difference.

## answer_c
Phosphoric acid and glycolipids

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Phosphoric acid and glycolipids are membrane chemical constituents, not the basis of the electrical potential definition.

## answer_d
Resting and action potentials

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Resting and action potentials are two states of the membrane potential over time, not the two sides across which it is measured.

## answer_e
Sodium and chloride ions

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Sodium and chloride are two of the ions involved in generating the potential, not the two sides across which it is defined.

## answer_a
These channels allow only specific ions to pass while blocking others because of their size, charge, or state of hydration

## explanation_a
Correct. Ion channels are selective pores that allow only specific ions to cross the membrane while excluding others, and this selectivity is determined by the channel's pore size, the charge lining the pore, and the hydration state the ion must shed to pass through. Passive channels open only when there is a difference in concentration gradient

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Passive (leak) channels are typically open continuously and are not gated open only by a concentration gradient; a concentration gradient determines the direction of net flow through an already-open channel, not whether it opens.

## answer_d
Na+ channels allow all positively charged ions to pass through them

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Na+ channels are selective for Na+, not for all positively charged ions indiscriminately.

## answer_e
Chemically activated ion channels (receptors) are not gated

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Chemically activated (ligand-gated) ion channels are, by definition, gated — they open in response to binding a chemical ligand.
