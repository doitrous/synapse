QST-MANSAEP-AEPCNS-Q01

## title
Process that forms the spinal cord

## question
Formation of the spinal cord is called:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Folding

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Folding describes the embryo's overall shape change from a flat disc into a cylindrical body, a separate process from the specific formation of the neural tube.

## answer_b
Fertilization

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fertilization is the union of sperm and oocyte at the very start of development, weeks before the neural tube exists.

## answer_c
Implantation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Implantation is the embedding of the blastocyst in the endometrium, an even earlier event than fertilization's neural consequences.

## answer_d
Gastrulation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gastrulation is the process that forms the three germ layers; it precedes and sets up neurulation but is not itself the formation of the spinal cord.

## answer_e
Neurulation

## explanation_e
Correct. Neurulation is the process by which the ectodermal neural plate folds into the neural tube, which then differentiates into the brain and spinal cord. Fusion of the neural folds begins in the middle of the embryo and spreads towards both ends, closing the tube. This is the specific developmental process the question is naming.

## title
Cells that synthesize myelin in the peripheral nervous system

## question
Which of the following cells synthesize myelin in the peripheral nervous system?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Microglias

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Microglia are the CNS's resident phagocytes and immune cells; they do not produce myelin anywhere in the nervous system.

## answer_b
Schwann cells

## explanation_b
Correct. Schwann cells wrap around peripheral axons and produce the myelin sheath along the length of a peripheral nerve fibre. One Schwann cell myelinates a single internode of a single axon, unlike the CNS's oligodendrocytes, which myelinate segments of several different axons.

## answer_c
Ependymal cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ependymal cells line the CSF-filled ventricles and central canal; they play no part in myelin production.

## answer_d
Astrocytes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Astrocytes support neurons metabolically and help form the blood-brain barrier in the CNS, but they do not produce myelin.

## answer_e
Oligodendrocytes

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Oligodendrocytes are the CNS's myelinating cell, the central counterpart to the peripheral Schwann cell asked for here.

The functional unit of the nervous system is:

## subject
neuro

## status

Incorrect. The oligodendrocyte is a myelinating glial cell of the CNS, again a support cell rather than the functional unit itself.

Incorrect. Microglia are the CNS's resident phagocytes, an immune support role rather than the functional unit that conducts impulses.

## answer_e
Schwann cells

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Schwann cells myelinate peripheral axons, a supporting role rather than the impulse-conducting functional unit itself.

## title
Location of pseudounipolar nerve cells

## question
Pseudounipolar nerve cells are present in the:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Sense organs

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sense organs contain a variety of receptor and sensory cell types, but the pseudounipolar shape specifically names spinal and cranial sensory ganglion cells, not sense organs generally.

## answer_b
Cerebellum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cerebellum's characteristic neuron is the multipolar Purkinje cell, not a pseudounipolar cell.

## answer_c
Dorsal root ganglia

## explanation_c
Correct. Spinal and cranial sensory ganglion cells, including those of the dorsal root ganglia, have a single process leaving the cell body. That single process divides at once into two branches, which is why these cells are described as pseudounipolar rather than truly unipolar. This makes the dorsal root ganglion the standard location taught for this neuron shape.

## answer_d
Cerebral cortex

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cerebral cortex is dominated by multipolar neurons, cells with one axon and many dendrites, not pseudounipolar cells.

## answer_e
Retina

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The retina's bipolar cells, which relay signals from photoreceptors to ganglion cells, have two processes rather than the single, later-dividing process of a pseudounipolar cell.

## subject

Spinal cord

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The spinal cord's cavity is the central canal, a narrow continuation of the ventricular system, not the lateral ventricle.

## title
How spinal nerves are formed

## question
Which of the following statements about the spinal nerves is correct?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Are 12 pairs in number

## explanation_a
Incorrect. There are 31 pairs of spinal nerves, not 12; twelve is instead the number of thoracic segments and, separately, the number of cranial nerve pairs.

## answer_b
Are formed by union of ventral and dorsal rami

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The dorsal and ventral rami are the two branches a spinal nerve divides into after it has already formed, just outside the vertebral canal, not the structures whose union forms the nerve.

## answer_c
Are formed by union of ventral and dorsal roots

## explanation_c
Correct. Each spinal nerve forms, within the vertebral canal, from the union of a dorsal (sensory) root and a ventral (motor) root. Once united, the resulting nerve trunk is mixed, carrying both sensory and motor fibres.

## answer_d
Loss of sensation results from injury of the ventral root

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Injury to the ventral root, which carries motor fibres, causes loss of movement, not loss of sensation; sensory loss instead follows injury to the dorsal root.

## answer_e
Their number equals the number of the vertebrae

## explanation_e
Incorrect. There are 31 pairs of spinal nerves but only 33 vertebrae grouped into five regions with mismatched segment counts, most notably 8 cervical spinal nerve pairs against only 7 cervical vertebrae, so the two counts do not match.

## title
Origin of the parasympathetic system

## question
Which of the following is true of the parasympathetic system?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Increases the heart rate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Increasing the heart rate is a sympathetic effect; parasympathetic activity instead slows the heart rate.

## answer_b
Dilates the bronchi

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dilating the bronchi (bronchodilatation) is a sympathetic effect; parasympathetic activity instead produces bronchoconstriction.

## answer_c
Arises from the thoracic segments of the spinal cord

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The thoracic (and upper two lumbar) spinal cord segments are the sympathetic outflow's origin, not the parasympathetic system's.

## answer_d
Arises from S2, 3 & 4 spinal cord segments

## explanation_d
Correct. The parasympathetic system has a craniosacral outflow. Preganglionic fibres leave the CNS from the brainstem (the cranial outflow) and separately from the second, third and fourth sacral spinal cord segments (the sacral outflow). At sacral level, the spinal cord's lateral horn carries these preganglionic cell bodies.

## answer_e
Has no cranial outflow

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The parasympathetic system does have a cranial outflow, carried by cranial nerves III, VII, IX and X, alongside its separate sacral outflow.

## subject

Axon terminal

## explanation_a
Correct. Synaptic vesicles, which store the presynaptic neuron's neurotransmitter, cluster in the axon terminal, immediately adjacent to the synaptic cleft. ## answer_b
Dendrites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dendrites are typically the postsynaptic element receiving a signal, carrying receptors rather than the presynaptic vesicles that release neurotransmitter.

Postsynaptic element

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The postsynaptic element carries neurotransmitter receptors, not the vesicles that store and release neurotransmitter, which belong to the presynaptic side.

## answer_d
Synaptic cleft

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The synaptic cleft is the fluid-filled gap the released neurotransmitter crosses; it is empty space, not a storage site for vesicles.

## answer_e
Nerve cell body

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The nerve cell body is where the neurotransmitter's components are synthesised, but the finished vesicles are transported to and concentrated in the axon terminal, not held in bulk at the cell body.
