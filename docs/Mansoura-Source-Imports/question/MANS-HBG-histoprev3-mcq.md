## title
Endoneurium's connective tissue type

## question
Endoneurium is a:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
CT covering of the nerve trunk

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Covering the whole nerve trunk from the outside is the epineurium's job, the outermost of the nerve's three connective tissue layers, not the endoneurium, which sits at the innermost, single-fibre level.

## answer_b
Dense CT that surrounds the bundles of nerve fibers

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A dense connective tissue layer around a bundle of fibres describes the perineurium, the fascicle-level covering. The endoneurium is loose, not dense, and wraps one fibre, not a bundle.

## answer_c
Dense CT that surrounds single nerve fiber

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The endoneurium is correctly placed at the single-fibre level, but it is loose connective tissue, not dense — density is what marks the perineurium instead.

## answer_d
Loose CT that divides the nerve trunk into bundles

## explanation_d
Incorrect. No connective tissue layer divides the trunk into bundles by that description; the perineurium groups fibres into fascicles rather than dividing a pre-formed bundle.

## answer_e
Loose CT that separates the individual nerve fibers

## explanation_e
Correct. The endoneurium is the innermost of the nerve's three connective tissue coverings — a thin sleeve of loose connective tissue, carrying reticular fibres and capillaries, that surrounds each individual nerve fibre and its Schwann cell sheath. It sits inside the denser perineurium, which bundles many endoneurium-wrapped fibres into a fascicle, and the epineurium, which binds the fascicles into the whole nerve trunk. Being loose and fibre-level, rather than dense and fascicle- or trunk-level, is exactly what identifies it here.

## title
The phase with chromosomes at the equator

## question
The phase in which chromosomes become arranged in the center or equator of the cell is:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Anaphase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Anaphase is the stage after this one, when the sister chromatids, already aligned at the equator, separate and move toward opposite poles.

## answer_b
Interphase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Interphase is not a division stage at all — it is the period between divisions (G1, S, G2) when the cell grows and replicates its DNA, well before any chromosome alignment.

## answer_c
Metaphase

## explanation_c
Correct. Metaphase is the stage of mitosis at which the condensed chromosomes, each still made of two sister chromatids, become arranged at the equatorial (metaphase) plate, the imaginary plane midway between the two spindle poles, attached to spindle microtubules via their kinetochores. This alignment is a checkpoint: the cell verifies every chromosome is correctly attached before anaphase is allowed to begin.

## answer_d
Prophase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prophase is the stage before this one, when chromosomes condense and become visible but have not yet aligned at the cell's equator.

## answer_e
Telophase

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Telophase is the final stage, when the chromosomes have already reached the poles and the nuclear envelope reforms around each daughter set.

## title
The structure formed at the end of prophase

## question
Which of the following structures is formed at the end of prophase:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Chromosomal condensation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Chromosomal condensation is a process that happens during prophase, not a discrete structure formed at its end.

## answer_b
Separation of sister chromatids

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Separation of sister chromatids is an event of anaphase, well after prophase has ended.

## answer_c
Cleavage furrow

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The cleavage furrow appears during cytokinesis, at the very end of the division, not at the end of prophase.

## answer_d
Kinetochore

## explanation_d
Correct. By the end of prophase each condensed chromosome's centromere has assembled its kinetochore, the protein plate on which the spindle's microtubules will attach. This attachment is what lets the two sister chromatids be pulled apart to opposite poles once anaphase begins. The kinetochore therefore has to be in place before metaphase alignment or anaphase separation can happen at all.

## answer_e
Nucleolus

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The nucleolus disappears, rather than forms, toward the end of prophase, as the nucleus prepares to break down.

Where the eccrine sweat gland duct opens

Duct of Eccrine sweat gland opens in the:-

## subject

Surface of skin

This is unlike the apocrine sweat gland, whose duct instead opens into the upper part of a hair follicle. The duct's opening site is the landmark that separates the two gland types once their secretory mechanism has already been asked about.

## title
The organelle synthesising secretory protein

## question
Which of the followings is responsible for synthesis of secretory proteins:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Attached ribosomes

## explanation_a
Correct. Attached (rough-ER-bound) ribosomes make the proteins destined to leave the cell. As each chain grows, it is threaded directly into the rough endoplasmic reticulum's lumen rather than released into the cytosol. This is what marks a protein for the secretory pathway, on to the Golgi apparatus and then export, rather than for the cell's own cytoplasmic use.

## answer_b
Free ribosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Free ribosomes, suspended in the cytosol, synthesise proteins the cell keeps for its own cytoplasmic use, not proteins destined for secretion.

## answer_c
Lysosomes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Lysosomes digest material; they do not synthesise protein.

## answer_d
Peroxisomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peroxisomes are oxidative organelles handling detoxification and fatty acid breakdown; they play no role in protein synthesis.

## answer_e
SER

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum lacks ribosomes altogether and instead handles lipid synthesis, calcium storage and detoxification, not protein synthesis.

A general character of epithelial tissue

## question
Which of the followings is a character of epithelial tissue:-

## subject
fnd

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cells do not rest on basement membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Epithelial cells characteristically do rest on a basement membrane, which separates them from the underlying connective tissue; the opposite of this option is the true character.

## answer_b
There is continuous degeneration and regeneration

## explanation_b
Correct. Epithelial surfaces face constant wear, whether from the exterior, a body cavity or a lumen. Because of that wear, epithelium has a high, continuous capacity for regeneration. Its basal or otherwise proliferative cells divide by mitosis throughout life to replace whatever is lost from the surface.

## answer_c
Widely separated cells with little intercellular substance

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Epithelial cells are closely packed with minimal intercellular substance between them; wide separation with abundant intercellular substance instead describes connective tissue.

## answer_d
There are blood vessels between cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Epithelium is avascular — no blood vessel penetrates between epithelial cells, which are instead nourished by diffusion from the underlying connective tissue across the basement membrane.

## answer_e
Nerves can't enter between cells

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Nerve endings, unlike blood vessels, can and do enter between epithelial cells, for example the free nerve endings supplying the epidermis.

## question
DNA is synthesized and chromosome number is doubled in which of the following stages:-

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. G1 phase precedes DNA replication; the cell grows and prepares but chromosomes are still unreplicated (single chromatid, 46-s) throughout G1.

## answer_b
S phase

## explanation_b
Correct. S phase is when the cell replicates its DNA. That replication converts each of the 46 single-chromatid chromosomes (46-s) into a double-chromatid chromosome (46-d). The chromosome count itself stays at 46 throughout; it is only the chromatid content of each chromosome that doubles.

G0 phase

Incorrect. G0 is a resting, non-cycling state some cells enter instead of proceeding through the cycle; no DNA replication happens there.

G2 phase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. G2 phase follows S phase, after replication is already complete, and is a preparatory gap before mitosis, not the replication stage itself.

## answer_e
Meiosis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Meiosis is not itself a chromosome-doubling stage; the doubling by DNA replication occurs in the S phase that precedes meiosis I, exactly as it does before mitosis.

## question
What type of collagen is present in basal lamina of basement membrane:-

## subject

D

## answer_a
Type I

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Type I collagen forms the strong, bundled fibres of connective tissue proper, tendon and bone, not the basal lamina.

## answer_b
Type II

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Type II collagen is the fine fibre of cartilage, not the basal lamina.

## answer_c
Type III

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Type III collagen is the reticular fibre of organ stroma, not the basal lamina.

## answer_d
Type IV

## explanation_d
Correct. Type IV collagen is not fibrous in form at all, unlike the other collagen types. Instead it forms a granular, sheet-like network within the basal lamina of the basement membrane. It is secreted by the epithelial cells that rest on that basal lamina.

## answer_e
Type V

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Type V collagen is a minor fibril-associated collagen; the basal lamina's defining collagen is type IV.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fibroblasts synthesise collagen and ground substance; eosinophil chemotactic factor is not among their products.

Mast cells

## explanation_b
Correct. The mast cell's granules store heparin and histamine, but not only those two. They also store eosinophil chemotactic factor of anaphylaxis, ECF-A. Once the cell degranulates, ECF-A recruits eosinophils to the site of the allergic reaction.

Plasma cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Plasma cells secrete antibodies, not eosinophil chemotactic factor.

Adipose cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adipose cells store and release fat; they do not secrete this chemotactic factor.

## answer_e
Macrophages

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Macrophages secrete their own set of cytokines and chemotactic signals, but eosinophil chemotactic factor specifically is a mast cell granule product.
