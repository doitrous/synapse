## title
The dominant plasma protein

## question
The plasma protein with the highest concentration and the lowest molecular weight is:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Fibrinogen

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fibrinogen is present at a far lower plasma concentration than albumin, even though its contribution to plasma viscosity is disproportionate to that low concentration.

## answer_b
Albumin

## explanation_b
Correct. Albumin is both the most concentrated plasma protein and the one with the lowest molecular weight of the major fractions. Its high concentration is exactly why it is the main determinant of plasma colloid osmotic pressure, holding fluid inside the capillaries against the outward pull of hydrostatic pressure.

## answer_c
Gamma globulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gamma globulins are a minority fraction by concentration compared with albumin, and their molecules (immunoglobulins) are considerably larger than albumin, not smaller.

## answer_d
Alpha-1 antitrypsin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Alpha-1 antitrypsin is a minor plasma protein fraction; its clinical relevance (a deficiency causing emphysema) has nothing to do with plasma protein concentration or molecular weight ranking.

## answer_e
Transferrin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Transferrin is present at a much lower concentration than albumin and is named for its iron-carrying role, not for being the dominant plasma protein by mass or the smallest by molecular weight.

## title
Where gamma globulins are synthesised

## question
Which plasma protein is the one major fraction that the liver does NOT synthesise?

## subject
haem

## status

## owner

D

## answer_a
Albumin

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the exception this question is testing for. Albumin is synthesised in the liver, one of the fractions the liver does make.

## answer_b
Fibrinogen

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the exception. Fibrinogen is a liver-synthesised clotting-factor protein, not the fraction that escapes hepatic synthesis.

## answer_c
Prothrombin

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the exception. Prothrombin is also synthesised in the liver, alongside albumin and fibrinogen.

## answer_d
Gamma globulin

## explanation_d
Correct. Gamma globulins (immunoglobulins) are the one major plasma protein fraction the liver does not make. They are produced by plasma cells (differentiated B-lymphocytes) in lymphoid tissue, not by hepatocytes — unlike albumin, fibrinogen, prothrombin and roughly half of the other globulins, which the liver does synthesise.

## answer_e
Alpha globulin

## explanation_e
Incorrect as the exception. Alpha globulins are among the roughly half of the globulin fraction the liver does synthesise; only the gamma globulins fall outside hepatic production.

Gamma globulins' defensive function

## question
The plasma protein fraction responsible for defence against microorganisms and their toxins is:

## subject

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Albumin's role is oncotic pressure regulation and carrying small molecules, not antibody-mediated defence.

## answer_b
Fibrinogen

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fibrinogen's role is blood coagulation, not defence against microorganisms.

## answer_c
Alpha-1 globulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Alpha-1 globulin (e.g. alpha-1 antitrypsin) has an antiprotease role, not an antibody-mediated defensive one.

## answer_d
Gamma globulin

## explanation_d
Correct. Gamma globulins are the immunoglobulins, and they mediate humoral defence against microorganisms and their toxins by acting as circulating antibodies. This antibody-mediated (humoral) defence is exactly what distinguishes the gamma globulin fraction functionally from albumin's osmotic role and fibrinogen's clotting role.

## answer_e
Beta globulin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Beta globulins include carrier and complement proteins, but the antibody-mediated defensive function specifically belongs to the gamma globulin (immunoglobulin) fraction.

## title
Separating the plasma protein fractions

## question
The method used to separate plasma proteins into their named fractions (albumin, alpha, beta, gamma globulins) is:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Electrophoresis

## explanation_a
Correct. Electrophoresis separates plasma proteins into their albumin and alpha-1, alpha-2, beta and gamma globulin fractions. The proteins migrate through a supporting medium under an electric field at rates set by their charge, size and shape, producing the characteristic banded pattern clinicians read for fraction-specific abnormalities such as a gamma-region spike or an albumin-globulin ratio shift.

## answer_b
Centrifugation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Centrifugation separates blood into plasma and formed elements (or separates particles by density and mass), not plasma proteins into their individual named fractions.

## answer_c
Osmometry

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Osmometry measures osmotic pressure or osmolality; it does not separate proteins into distinct fractions.

## answer_d
Chromatography

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chromatography can separate proteins in a research setting, but it is not the standard clinical method named for routine plasma protein fractionation; electrophoresis is.

## answer_e
Spectrophotometry

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Spectrophotometry measures light absorbance, used for example to quantify total protein concentration, but it does not itself separate the proteins into fractions.

## title
The plasma protein that sets plasma viscosity

## question
The plasma protein mainly responsible for plasma's viscosity is:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Albumin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Albumin's high concentration makes it the main determinant of plasma's oncotic (osmotic) pressure, but plasma viscosity specifically is set by fibrinogen, not albumin, despite albumin's much greater concentration.

## answer_b
Fibrinogen

## explanation_b
Correct. Fibrinogen is the plasma protein mainly responsible for plasma's own viscosity. Although present at a far lower concentration than albumin, its large, elongated, asymmetric molecule disproportionately raises resistance to flow, so a rise in fibrinogen (for example as an acute-phase reactant) raises plasma viscosity out of proportion to its concentration. This is distinct from whole-blood viscosity, whose major determinant is the red cell mass.

## answer_c
Gamma globulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gamma globulins are the antibody-mediated defence fraction; they are not the fraction that principally sets plasma viscosity.

## answer_d
Transferrin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Transferrin's role is carrying iron in the plasma, not determining plasma viscosity.

## answer_e
Alpha-1 antitrypsin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Alpha-1 antitrypsin's role is antiprotease activity; its deficiency causes emphysema, not a change in plasma viscosity.

## title
The earliest sign of hypoproteinemia

## question
The first and most important clinical sign of hypoproteinemia is:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Jaundice

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Jaundice reflects raised bilirubin, not a fall in plasma protein.

## answer_b
Swelling of the face and oedema

## explanation_b
Correct. When total plasma protein falls — chiefly albumin, the main determinant of plasma colloid osmotic pressure — the reduced oncotic pull can no longer hold fluid inside the capillaries against hydrostatic pressure, so fluid shifts into the interstitial space. Facial and dependent oedema is therefore the earliest and most clinically important sign of hypoproteinemia, seen for example in nephrotic syndrome, chronic liver disease and severe protein malnutrition.

## answer_c
Bleeding tendency

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A bleeding tendency can follow from low levels of specific clotting-factor proteins (e.g. fibrinogen, prothrombin) in severe liver disease, but it is not the first or most characteristic sign of hypoproteinemia in general — oedema is.

## answer_d
Cyanosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cyanosis reflects deoxygenated haemoglobin, unrelated to plasma protein level.

## answer_e
Muscle wasting

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Muscle wasting can accompany severe, prolonged protein malnutrition, but it is a later, structural consequence, not the first clinical sign that follows directly from the drop in oncotic pressure.
