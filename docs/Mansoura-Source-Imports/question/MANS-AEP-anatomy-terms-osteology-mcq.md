QST-MANSAEP-ANATOMY-TERMS-AND-OSTEOLOGY-BASICS-Q01

## title
Direction of the palm in the anatomical position

## question
In the normal anatomical position, what is the direction of the palm of the hand?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Medial

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Medial describes nearness to the median plane, not the direction a surface faces, so it does not answer what direction the palm points in.

## answer_b
Posterior

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Posterior is the opposite of the actual answer — in the anatomical position the palm faces the front of the body, not the back.

## answer_c
Anterior

## explanation_c
Correct. The anatomical position is defined with the upper limbs hanging by the sides and the palms facing forwards, which is the anterior direction. This deliberate rotation of the forearm into full supination is the one part of the position that is not simply how a person naturally stands, which is exactly why examiners test it. Every other term of position in the body is then stated as though the body were fixed in this reference posture.

## answer_d
Superior

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Superior means towards the head, a direction along the long axis of the body, not the direction the palm surface faces.

## answer_e
Lateral

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Lateral means away from the median plane; in the anatomical position it is the thumb, not the palm, that is described as pointing laterally.

## title
Plane dividing the body into unequal right and left parts

## question
Which anatomical plane divides the body into two unequal parts: right and left?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Coronal plane.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The coronal plane is vertical but divides the body into anterior and posterior parts, not right and left.

## answer_b
Midsagittal plane.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The midsagittal (median) plane also runs right-to-left, but it passes exactly through the midline, so the two halves it leaves are equal, not unequal.

## answer_c
Parasagittal plane.

## explanation_c
Correct. A parasagittal plane is any plane parallel to the median plane but offset from the midline. Because it is off-centre it still divides the body into a right and a left part, but the two parts are unequal in size. This is exactly what distinguishes it from the true midsagittal plane, which gives equal halves.

## answer_d
Transverse plane.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The transverse (horizontal) plane divides the body into superior and inferior parts, not right and left.

## answer_e
Oblique plane.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. An oblique plane runs at an angle to the three standard planes and is not one of the three named anatomical planes tested here.

## title
Term for movement away from the median plane

## question
Which term describes movement of the limb away from the median plane?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Abduction.

## explanation_a
Correct. Abduction is the movement of a part of the body away from the median plane of the body. For a limb as a whole this reference is the body's midline; the same word is used for the fingers and toes, where the reference axis is instead the middle finger or second toe. Its opposite, adduction, brings the part back towards that reference line.

## answer_b
Adduction.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Adduction is the opposite movement — bringing a part towards the median plane, not away from it.

## answer_c
Medial rotation.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Medial rotation turns the front of a limb to face medially; it is a rotation about the limb's long axis, not a movement towards or away from the midline.

## answer_d
Lateral rotation.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lateral rotation is the opposite rotation, turning the front of the limb to face laterally, again a rotation rather than an abduction/adduction movement.

## answer_e
Inversion.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Inversion is a foot-specific movement that tilts the sole to face medially; it is not the general term for moving a limb away from the median plane.

Protraction

Incorrect. Protraction is a forward-gliding movement (as of the mandible or scapula) and is not a term used for ankle movement.
