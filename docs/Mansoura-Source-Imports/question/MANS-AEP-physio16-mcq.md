## title
Most common CNS site for electrical synapses

## question
The most common site for electrical synapses in the C.N.S. is:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Inner ear (cochlea)

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cochlea is a peripheral sensory organ, not the CNS site given for electrical synapses.

## answer_b
Cerebral cortex

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cerebral cortex is dominated by chemical, not electrical, synapses.

## answer_c
Reticular activating system

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The reticular activating system is not the site named for electrical synapses in this course.

## answer_d
Hippocampus

## explanation_d
Correct. These synapses transmit with essentially no synaptic delay, since there is no transmitter release-and-binding step to wait for. The hippocampus is the classic example this course gives of where they occur in the CNS.

## answer_e
Raphe magnus nucleus

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The raphe magnus nucleus is not the site named for electrical synapses in this course.

## title
A mechanism of synaptic fatigue

## question
Fatigue in synapses may be related to which of the following?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Simple excitation of the presynaptic fibre is the normal triggering event for transmission, not the cause of fatigue.

## answer_b
Progressive increase in Ca++ available for neurotransmitter release

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fatigue is associated with exhaustion, not a progressive increase, in the machinery available for neurotransmitter release.

## answer_c
Progressive inactivation of many of the postsynaptic receptors

## explanation_c
Correct. With sustained transmitter exposure, the postsynaptic receptors themselves can become progressively inactivated, or desensitised. This reduces the postsynaptic response even while transmitter continues to be released from the presynaptic terminal. It is a secondary, postsynaptic contributor to synaptic fatigue, alongside the dominant presynaptic transmitter-depletion mechanism.

## answer_d
Development of a high concentration of K+ ions inside the postsynaptic neuron

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Synaptic fatigue is not attributed to a rising intracellular K+ concentration in the postsynaptic neuron.

## answer_e
Development of a normal concentration of Cl- inside the postsynaptic neuron

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A normal (unchanged) intracellular Cl- concentration describes a resting, not a fatigued, state and is not the mechanism tested here.

## title
What determines whether a neurotransmitter is excitatory or inhibitory

## question
The excitatory or inhibitory action of a neurotransmitter is determined by which of the following?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Function of its postsynaptic receptor

## explanation_a
Correct. The postsynaptic receptor's ion selectivity, not any property of the transmitter itself, decides whether a given synapse excites or inhibits. Binding a receptor that opens Na+ channels gives excitation (EPSP), while binding a receptor that opens Cl- channels gives inhibition (IPSP). Because of this, the same transmitter can be excitatory at one receptor and inhibitory at another.

## answer_b
Molecular composition

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A transmitter's molecular composition does not by itself fix its excitatory or inhibitory action, since the same molecule can act either way depending on the receptor it binds.

## answer_c
Shape of the synaptic vesicle in which it is contained

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Vesicle shape is not what determines whether the released transmitter excites or inhibits the postsynaptic neuron.

## answer_d
Distance between the presynaptic and postsynaptic membranes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The synaptic-cleft distance is not the factor that sets a synapse's excitatory or inhibitory sign.

## answer_e
Number of receptors on the postsynaptic membrane

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Receptor number can affect the size of the postsynaptic response, but it is receptor function (ion selectivity), not receptor number, that sets whether the response is excitatory or inhibitory.

## title
A characteristic event at an excitatory synapse

## question
Which of the following is characteristic of the events occurring at an excitatory synapse?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
There is massive efflux of calcium from the presynaptic terminal

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Calcium moves into, not out of, the presynaptic terminal, and this influx (not an efflux) is what triggers transmitter release.

## answer_b
Synaptic vesicles bind to the postsynaptic membrane

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Synaptic vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane to release their contents; they do not themselves bind to the postsynaptic membrane.

## answer_c
Voltage-gated potassium channels are closed

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Excitatory synaptic events are not defined by closure of voltage-gated potassium channels; the defining postsynaptic event is opening of ligand-gated sodium channels.

## answer_d
Ligand-gated channels are opened to allow sodium entry into the postsynaptic neuron

## explanation_d
Correct. At an excitatory synapse, transmitter binding opens ligand-gated channels that let Na+ enter the postsynaptic neuron. This Na+ influx depolarises the postsynaptic membrane, producing the EPSP. It is the ligand-gated (receptor-controlled) nature of these channels that distinguishes them from the voltage-gated channels used elsewhere in nerve conduction.

## answer_e
Ligand-gated channels are opened to allow Ca++ entry into the postsynaptic neuron

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Postsynaptic ligand-gated Ca++ entry is not the defining ionic event of a typical excitatory synapse; that role is filled by Na+ entry.

## title
The main basis of synaptic fatigue

## question
Which of the following is correct?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Synaptic fatigue means the presynaptic neuron fails to discharge

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Synaptic fatigue is not a failure of the presynaptic neuron to fire at all; the presynaptic neuron still discharges, but the transmitter it can release is depleted.

## answer_b
Synaptic fatigue is mainly due to depletion of neurotransmitter in the postsynaptic knobs

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Neurotransmitter is stored and released from the presynaptic, not the postsynaptic, terminal, so it is the presynaptic knobs that become depleted.

## answer_c
Synaptic fatigue is mainly due to depletion of neurotransmitter in the presynaptic knobs

## explanation_c
Correct. Synaptic fatigue arises mainly at the presynaptic terminal. Sustained, repetitive use lets the rate of neurotransmitter release from the presynaptic knobs outpace the rate of its resynthesis there. This imbalance progressively depletes the transmitter store, which is why the synapse's response falls off with continued firing.

## answer_d
Secondary hyperalgesia is due to hypersensitivity of pain receptors

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This statement about secondary hyperalgesia, while a true fact elsewhere, is not the correct answer among these options for this question.

## answer_e
In the pathway of crude touch, third-order neurons terminate in the motor cortex

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Third-order neurons of the crude touch pathway terminate in the sensory (not motor) cortex; this option is not the correct answer here.

## title
The rate imbalance underlying synaptic fatigue

## question
Synaptic fatigue is due to:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Decreased synthesis of neurotransmitters

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Neurotransmitter synthesis itself is not described as decreasing; the problem is that release outpaces the ongoing rate of synthesis, not a fall in synthesis alone.

## answer_b
Inability to release neurotransmitters by exocytosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The presynaptic terminal does not lose the ability to release transmitter by exocytosis; rather, it runs short of transmitter to release because of the synthesis/release rate imbalance.

## answer_c
Failure of action potentials to open voltage-gated Ca channels in the presynaptic terminal

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Voltage-gated Ca2+ channels continue to open normally with each arriving action potential; synaptic fatigue is not a failure of this Ca2+-triggering step.

## answer_d
Imbalance between the rates of synthesis and release of neurotransmitters

## explanation_d
Correct. With sustained high-frequency stimulation, the rate at which neurotransmitter is released from the presynaptic terminal outpaces the rate at which it can be resynthesised. The store of releasable transmitter is therefore progressively exhausted. This synthesis-versus-release imbalance is the underlying mechanism behind synaptic fatigue.

Incorrect (i.e. this statement is TRUE). Acidosis does inhibit synaptic transmission and depress neuronal activity.

Prolonged activity of the synapse

Incorrect (i.e. this statement is TRUE). Prolonged activity of the synapse does inhibit transmission, through synaptic fatigue from presynaptic transmitter depletion.

## title
Effect of drugs that open chloride channels at synapses

## question
Drugs which open Cl- channels in synapses:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cause depolarization of the postsynaptic membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cl- entry hyperpolarises, rather than depolarises, the postsynaptic membrane.

## answer_b
Cause hyperpolarization of the postsynaptic membrane

## explanation_b
Correct. Opening postsynaptic Cl- channels lets Cl- enter the postsynaptic neuron. This hyperpolarises the membrane, moving it further from firing threshold. The resulting hyperpolarisation is the inhibitory postsynaptic potential (IPSP), the same mechanism an inhibitory neurotransmitter's own receptor produces.

## answer_c
Enhance release of neurotransmitters from synaptic knobs

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Opening postsynaptic Cl- channels is a postsynaptic, inhibitory event; it does not act on presynaptic neurotransmitter release.

## answer_d
Enhance the response of postsynaptic neurons

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hyperpolarising the postsynaptic membrane dampens, rather than enhances, the postsynaptic neuron's response to further excitatory input.
