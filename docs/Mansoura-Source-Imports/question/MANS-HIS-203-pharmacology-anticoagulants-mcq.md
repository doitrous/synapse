## title
Coagulation pathway selectively affected by low-dose heparin

## question
Low concentrations of heparin selectively interfere with which of the following coagulation pathway(s)?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Intrinsic pathway

## explanation_a
Correct. At low concentration, heparin (via antithrombin III) preferentially inhibits factor Xa relative to thrombin, which has a disproportionate effect on the intrinsic pathway's amplification through the tenase complex. At higher concentrations, heparin's thrombin inhibition becomes prominent as well, extending its effect to the common pathway. This dose-dependent selectivity is why aPTT (sensitive to the intrinsic and common pathways) is the standard heparin-monitoring test.

## answer_b
Extrinsic pathway

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Heparin has little effect on the extrinsic pathway (tested by PT), which is why PT/INR is used to monitor warfarin, not heparin.

## answer_c
Common pathway

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The common pathway is only prominently affected at higher heparin concentrations, not at low, selective doses.

## answer_d
Both 'A' and 'C'

## explanation_d
Incorrect. At low concentration, heparin's selective effect is on the intrinsic pathway alone, not both intrinsic and common together.

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Broad-spectrum antibiotics reduce gut-flora vitamin K synthesis, which potentiates (not reduces) warfarin's effect.

## answer_b
Cimetidine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cimetidine inhibits hepatic metabolism of warfarin, which potentiates (not reduces) its effect.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Aspirin adds antiplatelet bleeding risk on top of warfarin rather than reducing its anticoagulant effect.

## explanation_d
Correct. Estrogen-containing oral contraceptives increase hepatic synthesis of several vitamin-K-dependent clotting factors, which partially offsets warfarin's inhibition of that same synthesis. The net effect is a reduced anticoagulant response to a given warfarin dose in a patient also taking oral contraceptives, requiring closer INR monitoring and possible dose adjustment when the two are combined.

Correct. As a directly-acting, rapidly-onset anticoagulant, heparin's dose-limiting and most clinically important adverse effect is bleeding, ranging from minor bruising and injection-site haematoma to major internal or intracranial haemorrhage. Allergic reactions

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Allergic reactions can occur with heparin but are not its major, most clinically significant adverse effect — bleeding is.

## answer_e
Inhibiting aldosterone synthesis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Heparin can rarely cause hypoaldosteronism with prolonged use, but this is not its major adverse effect.

Fetal malformations

## explanation_b
Correct — this is the exception (NOT a heparin adverse effect), and therefore the answer. Heparin is a large, highly charged molecule that does not cross the placenta, so it does not reach the fetal circulation and is not associated with fetal malformations. Alopecia

This is a recognised heparin adverse effect, not the exception. Alopecia can occur with prolonged heparin use.

## answer_e
Osteoporosis

## explanation_e
This is a recognised heparin adverse effect, not the exception. Osteoporosis can occur with prolonged heparin use, from its effect on osteoblast/osteoclast activity.

## title
Effect of sudden warfarin withdrawal

## question
Sudden withdrawal of warfarin leads to which of the following?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hemorrhage would be expected from excess, not withdrawal, of warfarin's anticoagulant effect.

## answer_c
Osteoporosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Osteoporosis is associated with prolonged heparin, not warfarin withdrawal.

## answer_d
Tachycardia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tachycardia is not the recognised effect of sudden warfarin withdrawal.

This is why warfarin is stopped with attention to timing rather than abruptly.

Injectable substitute for heparin in severe thrombocytopenia

## question
If a patient developed severe thrombocytopenia, an appropriate injectable substitute to heparin is:

## subject
pharm

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

D

## answer_a
Enoxaparin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Enoxaparin, a low-molecular-weight heparin, still cross-reacts immunologically with heparin-PF4 antibodies in most heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) patients and is not considered a safe substitute.

Incorrect. Dicumarol is an oral vitamin-K antagonist, not an injectable heparin substitute for this setting.

Hirudin, a direct thrombin inhibitor, works independently of antithrombin and does not share heparin's antigenic complex, making it an appropriate injectable substitute in this setting.

## answer_e
Phenindione

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Phenindione is an oral vitamin-K antagonist, not an injectable heparin substitute.

## title
How LMWH differs from unfractionated heparin

## question
Low molecular weight heparins differ from unfractionated heparin in which of the following?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
They selectively inhibit factor Xa

## explanation_a
Correct. Unfractionated heparin's longer chains can bind both antithrombin and thrombin simultaneously, potentiating antithrombin's inhibition of thrombin as well as factor Xa. Low-molecular-weight heparins, being shorter chains (e.g. enoxaparin), are usually too short to bridge antithrombin to thrombin but retain the ability to potentiate antithrombin's inhibition of factor Xa, giving them a selectively anti-Xa profile. This more predictable dosing profile is why LMWHs are widely used without requiring routine aPTT monitoring, unlike unfractionated heparin.

## answer_b
They significantly prolong clotting time

## explanation_b
Incorrect. LMWH's anti-Xa-predominant effect does not prolong clotting time (a thrombin-dependent test) the way unfractionated heparin does — this is part of why aPTT does not reliably reflect LMWH dosing.

## answer_c
They have a shorter duration of action

## explanation_c
Incorrect. LMWH actually has a longer, not shorter, duration of action and half-life than unfractionated heparin, allowing once- or twice-daily dosing.

## answer_d
They are administered IV only

## explanation_d
Incorrect. LMWH is typically given subcutaneously, not IV only.

## answer_e
They require more frequent monitoring

## explanation_e
Incorrect. LMWH requires less frequent (often no routine) monitoring, the opposite of unfractionated heparin, which needs individualised aPTT titration.

## title
Contraindication of heparin

## question
Which of the following is a contraindication of heparin?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
COVID-19 pneumonia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. COVID-19 pneumonia is, if anything, associated with increased thrombosis risk, making heparin prophylaxis appropriate, not contraindicated.

## answer_b
Bleeding peptic ulcer

## explanation_b
Correct. Because bleeding is heparin's major adverse effect, active or recent significant bleeding — such as a bleeding peptic ulcer — is a clear contraindication, since anticoagulation would worsen an already-bleeding lesion. Conditions like pulmonary embolism or post-surgical thrombosis risk, by contrast, are indications for heparin, because they carry thrombosis risk that heparin's benefit is meant to address.

## answer_c
Pulmonary embolism

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pulmonary embolism is a treatment indication for heparin, not a contraindication.

## answer_d
Hip replacement surgery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hip replacement surgery is a classic indication for thromboprophylaxis with heparin (or LMWH), not a contraindication.

## answer_e
Pregnancy

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Pregnancy is not a contraindication to heparin; heparin does not cross the placenta and is the anticoagulant of choice in pregnancy.

## title
Primary mechanism by which heparin prevents coagulation

## question
Which of the following is the primary mechanism by which heparin prevents coagulation of blood?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Direct inhibition of prothrombin to thrombin conversion

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Heparin does not directly inhibit the prothrombin-to-thrombin conversion enzymatically; it works through antithrombin III.

## answer_b
Facilitation of antithrombin III mediated inhibition of factor Xa and thrombin

## explanation_b
Correct. Heparin facilitates antithrombin III's inhibition of activated clotting factors, most importantly factor IXa, Xa, XIa and XIIa, and thrombin (IIa) — the option here summarising this as facilitation of antithrombin-mediated inhibition of factor Xa and thrombin. Heparin itself has no direct enzymatic anticoagulant action; it works entirely by massively accelerating antithrombin's own natural inhibitory activity against these activated factors.

## answer_c
Activation of antithrombin III to inhibit factors IX and XI

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Heparin's target factors for antithrombin-mediated inhibition are primarily Xa and thrombin (IIa), not specifically 'IX and XI' as framed here.

## answer_d
Inhibition of factors XIIa and XIIIa

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Heparin's mechanism is not framed as direct inhibition of XIIa and XIIIa; its action is via antithrombin potentiation.

## answer_e
Inhibition of the synthesis of prothrombin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Heparin does not inhibit prothrombin synthesis (that is warfarin's mechanism); it inhibits already-activated clotting factors via antithrombin.

## title
Proposed mechanism of action of warfarin

## question
Warfarin is an anticoagulant. Its proposed mechanism of action is that it:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Inhibits platelet synthesis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Warfarin does not inhibit platelet production; that is not its mechanism.

## answer_b
Blocks prostaglandin synthesis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Blocking prostaglandin synthesis describes aspirin's antiplatelet mechanism, not warfarin's.

## answer_c
Prohibits absorption of vitamin K

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Warfarin does not block vitamin K absorption in the gut; it acts intracellularly in the liver on vitamin K recycling.

## answer_d
Binds to fibrinogen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Warfarin does not bind fibrinogen directly.

## answer_e
Inhibits synthesis of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors

## explanation_e
Correct. Coumarins (warfarin) competitively inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase, blocking the recycling of vitamin K needed for hepatic gamma-carboxylation and formation of prothrombin and factors VII, IX and X. Unlike heparin, which acts on already-formed, circulating clotting factors, warfarin acts by suppressing new synthesis of these factors in the liver, which is also why its onset is slow (limited by the clearance of pre-existing factors) and why it needs bridging with heparin at initiation.

## title
Primary advantage of enoxaparin over heparin

## question
The primary advantage of enoxaparin over heparin is that it:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Is unlikely to cause bleeding

## explanation_a
Correct (as printed). This department bank's printed answer credits enoxaparin with being less likely to cause bleeding than unfractionated heparin. The more standard pharmacology teaching frames LMWH's advantages as more predictable, weight-based dosing, a longer half-life allowing once- or twice-daily dosing without routine monitoring, and a lower risk of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia and osteoporosis — with bleeding risk itself considered broadly comparable in most comparative studies. The printed key is followed here as authored, with this discrepancy flagged.

## answer_b
More effectively inhibits the synthesis of clotting factors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Neither LMWH nor unfractionated heparin inhibits clotting factor synthesis; that is warfarin's mechanism, not heparin-family drugs'.

## answer_c
Has a more rapid onset

## explanation_c
Incorrect. LMWH does not have a more rapid onset than unfractionated heparin; both act quickly, but this is not the cited advantage.

## answer_d
Does not cause thrombocytopenia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. LMWH has a lower, but not zero, risk of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia — 'does not cause' overstates the advantage.

## answer_e
Has a shorter half-life

## explanation_e
Incorrect. LMWH has a longer, not shorter, half-life than unfractionated heparin, which is part of why it can be dosed once or twice daily.
