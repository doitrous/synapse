## title
Chemical group uniting the pyrrole rings of a porphyrin

## question
The 4 pyrrole rings in porphyrin are united together by:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Methyl group

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A methyl group (-CH3) is a substituent found on the porphyrin ring's side chains, not the bridge joining the pyrrole rings together.

## answer_b
Methenyl group

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Methenyl is a similarly named but distinct chemical group; it is not the bridge that unites the pyrrole rings in a porphyrin.

## answer_c
Methylene group

## explanation_c
Correct. The four pyrrole rings of a porphyrin are joined into a larger ring by four methylene (=CH-) bridges connecting the alpha-carbons of adjacent pyrrole rings. This tetrapyrrole ring, with its central nitrogen atoms able to chelate a metal ion, is the structural scaffold that becomes haem once ferrous iron is inserted by ferrochelatase at protoporphyrin IX, the final step of haem synthesis.

## answer_d
Methanol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Methanol is a simple alcohol, unrelated to the covalent bridges of the porphyrin ring structure.

## answer_e
None of the above

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Methylene group is a correct, listed answer, so "none of the above" is wrong.

## id
QST-MANSHIS203-BIOCHEMISTRY-HEMOGLOBIN-STRUCTURE-METHEMOGLOBINEMIA-FROM-IRON-OXIDATION

## title
Disease from oxidation of iron in hemoglobin

## question
If iron in hemoglobin is oxidized, what disease may occur?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cystic fibrosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cystic fibrosis is a chloride-channel (CFTR) genetic disease unrelated to haemoglobin iron chemistry.

## answer_b
Sickle cell anemia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sickle cell anaemia results from a structural globin-chain amino acid substitution, not from oxidation of the haem iron.

## answer_c
Carbon monoxide poisoning

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Carbon monoxide poisoning is a competitive-binding condition at the still-ferrous iron of haem — CO does not oxidise the iron, it occupies the same binding site oxygen would use, with higher affinity than O2.

## answer_d
Methemoglobinemia

## explanation_d
Correct. Oxidation of haemoglobin's iron from the ferrous (Fe2+) to the ferric (Fe3+) state produces methemoglobin, which cannot bind oxygen at all — unlike normal oxyhaemoglobin's reversible, non-oxidative binding of O2 to ferrous iron. A build-up of methemoglobin (methemoglobinemia) reduces the blood's effective oxygen-carrying capacity even though total haemoglobin and iron content are unchanged, and is treated with methylene blue, which reduces the iron back to the ferrous state.

## answer_e
Haem-haem interactions

## explanation_e
Incorrect. "Haem-haem interaction" describes the cooperative binding behaviour between the four haem groups of haemoglobin, not a disease from iron oxidation.

QST-MANSHIS203-BIOCHEMISTRY-HEMOGLOBIN-STRUCTURE-HEMOGLOBIN-OXYGEN-BINDING-SITE

## title
Oxygen binding site of hemoglobin

## question
Which of the following is the oxygen binding site of the hemoglobin?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
N-terminal of the beta subunit

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The N-terminal of the beta subunit is involved in 2,3-BPG binding, which modulates oxygen affinity, but it is not the actual oxygen-binding site itself.

## answer_b
Carboxyl terminal of both alpha and beta subunits

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The carboxyl termini of the globin chains participate in the allosteric transitions between the taut and relaxed states of haemoglobin, not in oxygen binding directly.

## answer_c
Ferric ion (Fe+3) of the heme molecule

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ferric (Fe3+) iron is the oxidised state found in methemoglobin, which cannot bind oxygen at all — the opposite of a functional binding site.

## answer_d
Ferrous ion (Fe+2) of the heme molecule

## explanation_d
Correct. The ferrous (Fe2+) iron atom held at the centre of each haem group's porphyrin ring is haemoglobin's actual oxygen-binding site. Oxygen binds reversibly and without oxidising this ferrous iron, which is what allows haemoglobin to load oxygen in the lungs and release it in the tissues repeatedly across its 120-day lifespan.

## answer_e
Histidine residue of the globin chain

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A histidine residue of the globin chain (the proximal histidine) coordinates and anchors the haem group's iron within the pocket, but the oxygen itself binds directly to the ferrous iron, not to the histidine.

QST-MANSHIS203-BIOCHEMISTRY-HEMOGLOBIN-STRUCTURE-HEMOGLOBIN-CONJUGATED-PROTEIN

## title
Protein classification of hemoglobin

## question
Hemoglobin is a:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Simple protein

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A simple protein is composed only of amino acids; haemoglobin's globin portion alone would qualify, but the whole molecule also carries a non-protein haem group, disqualifying "simple" as the classification for haemoglobin overall.

## answer_b
Conjugated protein

## explanation_b
Correct. Haemoglobin is a conjugated protein — its globin portion is a simple protein (four polypeptide chains), but each chain is combined with a haem prosthetic group, a non-protein, iron-containing porphyrin essential to the molecule's oxygen-carrying function. Neither the globin chains alone nor the haem groups alone constitute functional haemoglobin.

## answer_c
Derived protein

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A derived protein is a product of protein degradation or denaturation, not an intact functional molecule like haemoglobin.

## answer_d
Lipoprotein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A lipoprotein combines protein with lipid, not with a haem prosthetic group; haemoglobin carries no lipid component.

## answer_e
Nucleoprotein

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A nucleoprotein combines protein with nucleic acid, unrelated to haemoglobin's actual haem-globin composition.

QST-MANSHIS203-BIOCHEMISTRY-HEMOGLOBIN-STRUCTURE-HEPATOCELLULAR-JAUNDICE-MIXED-PATTERN

## title
Jaundice type with both conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin elevated

## question
Which type of jaundice is associated with elevation of both conjugated and unconjugated forms of bilirubin?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Hemolytic jaundice

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Haemolytic jaundice, from excess red cell breakdown, raises unconjugated bilirubin predominantly, not both fractions together.

## answer_b
Obstructive jaundice

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Obstructive jaundice, from blocked bile flow, raises conjugated (direct) bilirubin predominantly, not both fractions together.

## answer_c
Hepatocellular jaundice

## explanation_c
Correct. Hepatocellular jaundice, from damaged liver cells (as in hepatitis), impairs uptake, conjugation and excretion of bilirubin all at once, because the liver cell itself is responsible for each of those three functions. This impairs everything together, elevating both conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin — a mixed pattern that distinguishes it from the single-fraction-predominant patterns of haemolytic and obstructive jaundice.

## answer_d
Physiological jaundice of neonate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Physiological jaundice of the neonate is from transient unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia (immature glucuronyl transferase activity), predominantly unconjugated, not a mixed pattern.

## answer_e
Dubin-Johnson syndrome

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Dubin-Johnson syndrome is a hereditary defect in conjugated bilirubin excretion, predominantly raising conjugated bilirubin, not both fractions together.
