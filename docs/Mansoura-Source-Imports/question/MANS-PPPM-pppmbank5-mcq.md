## title
Host in which a parasite reaches sexual maturity

## question
The parasite reaches sexual maturity in:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Intermediate host.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The intermediate host carries a larval or asexual stage of the parasite while it develops toward the infective stage; it is not where the parasite becomes sexually mature.

## answer_b
Paratenic host.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A paratenic (transport) host merely carries the infective stage without the parasite developing further in it, so sexual maturity is never reached there.

## answer_c
Dead-end host.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A dead-end host is a host in which the parasite can survive but cannot complete its life cycle or transmit onward, which rules out reaching sexual maturity there.

## answer_d
Definitive host.

## explanation_d
Correct. The definitive (final) host is, by definition, the host that harbours the adult, sexually mature stage of the parasite -- it is where the parasite reproduces sexually, or where an asexually-reproducing parasite reaches full adult development. Every other host role in a life cycle (intermediate, paratenic, reservoir) is defined relative to this one central fact: sexual maturity marks the definitive host and nothing else. This is why parasitologists identify the definitive host first when mapping any life cycle -- it fixes where the sexual/adult stage, and therefore egg or oocyst production, actually occurs.

## answer_e
Amplifier host.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. 'Amplifier host' describes a host in which a pathogen multiplies to high numbers to boost onward transmission (a concept used more in arbovirus ecology); it is not a standard parasitology term for the site of sexual maturity.

## title
Mosquito stage targeted by wire screening

## question
Which stage of mosquito is controlled by wire screening?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Adult.

## explanation_a
Correct. Wire screening (mesh fitted over windows, doors and eaves) is a mechanical control measure aimed at the flying adult mosquito -- it physically excludes the winged, host-seeking adult from entering a building, rather than acting on the aquatic egg, larval or pupal stages that live in water outside. This is why wire screening is grouped with bed nets and window screens as adult-stage exclusion methods, distinct from larvicides, oiling of breeding water, or drainage, which all target the immature aquatic stages instead.

## answer_b
Egg.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Mosquito eggs are laid on or near water and are controlled by source reduction (removing/draining breeding sites) or larvicidal measures, not by wire screening, which acts on flying adults in air.

## answer_c
Larva.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The larval stage is aquatic and free-swimming; it is targeted by larvicides, larvivorous fish or oiling the water surface, not by a mesh screen fitted to a building opening.

## answer_d
Pupa.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pupal stage is also aquatic (and non-feeding); like the larva, it is addressed by measures applied to the breeding water itself, not by excluding flying insects from a room.

## answer_e
Nymph.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Mosquitoes do not have a nymph stage at all -- they undergo complete (holometabolous) metamorphosis through egg, larva and pupa before emerging as the winged adult, so 'nymph' does not apply to their life cycle.

## title
Classification criterion for protozoa

## question
Protozoa are classified according to organ of:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Locomotion.

## explanation_a
Correct. The classical (pre-molecular) scheme still taught in introductory parasitology sorts protozoa into four groups by their organ of locomotion: Sarcodina, which move by pseudopodia (e.g. Entamoeba); Mastigophora, which move by flagella; Ciliophora, which move by cilia; and Sporozoa/Apicomplexa, which have no distinct locomotor organelle at any stage. Locomotion is used as the classifying feature because it is a stable, visually distinctive trait that is easy to identify under the microscope and correlates well with the group's other biological features.

## answer_b
Secretion.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Secretory activity is not the basis of this classical scheme -- protozoa across all four locomotion-based groups secrete various products, so secretion does not distinguish them from one another.

## answer_c
Nutrition.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mode of nutrition (e.g. phagocytosis via a cytostome) varies within and across the locomotion-based groups and is not the organising principle of this particular classification.

## answer_d
Respiration.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Protozoa mostly respire by simple diffusion across the cell surface regardless of group, so respiration does not distinguish one locomotion-based group from another.

## answer_e
Excretion.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Excretory mechanism (e.g. the contractile vacuole) is not the feature used to separate Sarcodina, Mastigophora, Ciliophora and Sporozoa from one another in this classical scheme.

## title
Parasite requiring a snail intermediate host

## question
Which one of the following parasites needs snail as an intermediate host to complete its life cycle?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Nematoda.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Nematode (roundworm) life cycles either have no intermediate host (the soil-transmitted helminths) or use an arthropod or paratenic vertebrate host when one is required, not a snail.

## answer_b
Cestoda.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cestodes (tapeworms) typically use arthropod (e.g. fleas, beetles) or vertebrate intermediate hosts -- Taenia uses cattle or pigs, Diphyllobothrium uses a crustacean then a fish -- not a snail.

## answer_c
Trematoda.

## explanation_c
Correct. Trematodes (flukes) are the helminth group whose life cycle characteristically requires a freshwater snail as the first intermediate host, in which the miracidium develops through sporocyst and redia stages into free-swimming cercariae. This snail-obligate step is a defining feature of trematode biology and underlies the geographic distribution of fluke infections such as schistosomiasis and fascioliasis, which track the distribution of the specific snail species each fluke needs.

## answer_d
Intestinal protozoa.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Intestinal protozoa (e.g. Entamoeba, Giardia) have direct faecal-oral life cycles with no intermediate host of any kind, let alone a snail.

## answer_e
Urogenital protozoa.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Urogenital protozoa (e.g. Trichomonas vaginalis) are transmitted directly between hosts (typically sexually) and likewise require no intermediate host.

## title
Taxonomic group of Amoeba

## question
Amoeba belongs to:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Sarcodina

## explanation_a
Correct. Amoeba (and medically important genera such as Entamoeba) belongs to Sarcodina, the classical protozoan group defined by locomotion via pseudopodia -- temporary cytoplasmic extensions that the organism projects and flows into, producing its characteristic amoeboid movement. This pseudopodial movement is precisely what distinguishes Sarcodina from the other locomotion-based groups, making 'Amoeba = Sarcodina' one of the most basic pairings in classical protozoan taxonomy.

## answer_b
Microspora

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Microspora are obligate intracellular spore-forming organisms (now reclassified near the fungi) and are unrelated to amoeboid, pseudopod-based movement.

## answer_c
Mastigophora

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mastigophora is the flagellate group (e.g. Giardia, Trypanosoma, Trichomonas), defined by whip-like flagellar movement, not pseudopodia.

## answer_d
Ciliophora

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ciliophora is the ciliate group (e.g. Balantidium coli), defined by coordinated ciliary beating over the cell surface, not pseudopodia.

## answer_e
Apicomplexa

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Apicomplexa (Sporozoa) -- e.g. Plasmodium, Toxoplasma -- have no distinct locomotor organelle at any stage and move (when motile at all) by gliding, unrelated to Amoeba's pseudopodial movement.

## title
Function of flagella in protozoa

## question
What is the function of flagella in protozoa?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Nutrition

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Nutrient uptake in flagellates occurs through the cytostome or by diffusion/pinocytosis, not through the flagellum itself.

## answer_b
Secretion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Flagella are not secretory structures; any secretory activity in a flagellate protozoan occurs elsewhere in the cell.

## answer_c
Locomotion

## explanation_c
Correct. The flagellum is a long, whip-like organelle whose rhythmic, undulating beat propels the protozoan through its fluid environment, giving Mastigophora (flagellates such as Giardia, Trypanosoma and Trichomonas) their defining mode of locomotion. This is the direct counterpart to pseudopodial movement in Sarcodina and ciliary movement in Ciliophora -- each of the classical locomotion-based protozoan groups is named for, and identified by, its characteristic locomotor organelle.

## answer_d
Excretion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Excretion of metabolic waste in protozoa is handled by diffusion or by the contractile vacuole, not by the flagellum.

## answer_e
Respiration

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Gas exchange in protozoa occurs by simple diffusion across the cell surface; the flagellum plays no respiratory role.

## title
Parasite group with rostellum and hooks

## question
Which of the following parasites are characterized by having rostellum and hooks?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Cestodes.

## explanation_a
Correct. In armed cestode species (e.g. Taenia solium, Echinococcus granulosus), the scolex (head) bears a rostellum -- a small, retractable, cone-shaped protrusion armed with a crown of hooks -- in addition to suckers, which together anchor the worm to the host intestinal wall. This armed-scolex feature, alongside the segmented proglottid chain, is one of the two classical identifying features of the cestode body plan; unarmed species such as Taenia saginata have suckers but lack the rostellum and hooks.

## answer_b
Nematodes.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Nematodes attach and feed via a buccal cavity/mouthparts appropriate to their species (e.g. cutting plates or teeth in hookworms) but do not have a scolex, rostellum or hooks -- those are cestode-specific structures.

## answer_c
Protozoa.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Protozoa are unicellular and have no scolex-type attachment organ at all.

## answer_d
Schistosomes.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Schistosomes are trematodes and attach via oral and ventral suckers, not a rostellum with hooks.

## answer_e
Trematodes

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Trematodes generally attach using oral and ventral suckers; the rostellum-and-hooks arrangement is specific to armed cestodes, not flukes.

## title
Arthropod transmitting bacterial disease mechanically

## question
Which of the following arthropods can transmit bacterial diseases mechanically?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pubic lice

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pubic lice (Phthirus pubis) are ectoparasites of the genital region causing pediculosis pubis; they are not established mechanical vectors of bacterial disease.

## answer_b
Itch mites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Itch mites (Sarcoptes scabiei) cause scabies by direct burrowing infestation; they are not vectors, mechanical or biological, of bacterial disease.

## answer_c
Mosquitoes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mosquitoes transmit pathogens biologically (the pathogen develops or multiplies within the mosquito before transmission, as with malaria or filariasis), which is a different transmission mode from simple mechanical carriage.

## answer_d
House flies

## explanation_d
Correct. House flies (Musca domestica) transmit bacterial (and other) pathogens mechanically: contaminated mouthparts, legs and body surface pick up organisms from faeces, refuse or wound discharge and physically deposit them on food or open wounds, with no biological development of the pathogen inside the fly. This mechanical (as opposed to biological) transmission is how house flies are implicated in the spread of enteric bacterial diseases such as typhoid fever, cholera and bacillary dysentery.

## answer_e
Bed bugs

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Bed bugs feed on blood and can carry bacteria on their mouthparts in principle, but they are not the classical example of mechanical bacterial transmission taught in this context; that role belongs to the house fly.

## title
Best diagnostic method for myiasis

## question
The most suitable method for diagnosis of myiasis is:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Finding the larvae

## explanation_a
Correct. Myiasis is diagnosed directly by finding (and, where possible, extracting and identifying) the fly larvae themselves within the affected tissue, wound or body cavity -- a straightforward clinical/parasitological identification rather than an indirect laboratory test, since the causative organism is macroscopic and directly visible. Identifying the species of larva recovered can further guide management, since specific (obligatory) myiasis species may require complete extraction while semi-specific species are more often managed with wound care alone.

## answer_b
Finding the eggs

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fly eggs are laid on tissue or wound edges but it is the tissue-invading larva, not the egg, that is found in and diagnostic of an established case of myiasis.

## answer_c
Serology

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Serology (antibody detection) is useful for tissue-invasive or occult parasitic infections where the organism itself cannot easily be seen (e.g. some helminth infections); it is unnecessary and non-specific for a condition diagnosed by direct visualisation of larvae.

## answer_d
Skin test

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Skin testing (delayed-hypersensitivity/allergy-type testing) is not the diagnostic approach for myiasis, which is confirmed by direct larval identification.

## answer_e
Blood culture

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Blood culture detects bloodstream bacterial or fungal infection and has no role in diagnosing a localised tissue infestation by fly larvae.

Praziquantal

## answer_e
Paris green

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Paris green is a mosquito larvicide applied to breeding water; it is not a pediculicide used on the scalp.

Incorrect. Secretory activity occurs elsewhere in the cell, not as a nuclear function.

## title
Function of the protozoan cytostome

## question
Protozoan cytostome is one of the organs of which of the following?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Food intake

## explanation_a
Correct. The cytostome ('cell mouth') is a permanent or semi-permanent opening in the pellicle of some protozoa (notably ciliates such as Balantidium coli) through which food particles are ingested by phagocytosis, forming a food vacuole -- its function is food intake, the protozoan equivalent of a feeding structure. Not all protozoa have a cytostome (amoebae, for example, ingest food by engulfing it with pseudopodia anywhere on the cell surface), so its presence is itself a useful identifying feature of ciliates and some flagellates.

## answer_b
Metabolism

## explanation_b
Incorrect. General cellular metabolism occurs throughout the cytoplasm, not specifically at the cytostome, which is a feeding-intake structure rather than a metabolic one.

## answer_c
Multiplication

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Multiplication (reproduction) occurs via nuclear division and fission of the whole organism, not through the cytostome.

## answer_d
Respiration

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gas exchange occurs by diffusion across the cell surface generally, not through the cytostome specifically.

## answer_e
Secretion

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The cytostome is a feeding-intake opening, not a secretory structure.

## title
Structures originating from the ectoplasm

## question
Which of the following originate from the ectoplasm?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Food vacuole.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The food vacuole forms in the endoplasm during phagocytosis/pinocytosis of ingested material, not from the ectoplasm.

## answer_b
Chromatin body.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The chromatin body is nuclear material, located within the endoplasm-enclosed nucleus, unrelated to the ectoplasm.

## answer_c
Contractile vacuole.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. In this stem's intended teaching, the contractile vacuole (an osmoregulatory/excretory organelle) is grouped with the endoplasmic structures rather than the ectoplasm-derived surface organelles the key is testing here.

## answer_d
Foreign body.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 'Foreign body' is not a defined protozoan organelle with an ectoplasm origin.

## answer_e
Cilia.

## explanation_e
Correct. The ectoplasm is the clear, gel-like outer layer of protozoan cytoplasm, immediately under the cell membrane/pellicle, and it is this layer that gives rise to the surface locomotor and protective organelles -- cilia (as in Ciliophora), flagella and pseudopodia -- as well as the pellicle itself. Cilia are the option here that is unambiguously an ectoplasm-derived surface structure, consistent with the ectoplasm's role as the organelle-bearing outer cytoplasmic zone.

QST-MANSPPPM-PPPMBANK5-PPPMBANK5-Q37

## title
Function of the protozoan cytopyge

## question
Protozoan cytopyge is an organ of:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Locomotion

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Locomotion is the role of surface organelles such as cilia, flagella or pseudopodia, not the cytopyge.

## answer_b
Excretion

## explanation_b
Correct. The cytopyge ('cell anus') is a fixed point on the pellicle, seen classically in ciliates, through which undigested residue from the food vacuole is expelled from the cell -- its function is excretion (more precisely, egestion of solid waste), the counterpart at the 'output' end to the cytostome's role in food intake. Together, a fixed cytostome and cytopyge give ciliates a directional, almost tract-like processing of food from intake to waste expulsion, unlike the more diffuse ingestion and egestion seen in amoebae.

## answer_c
Metabolism

## explanation_c
Incorrect. General metabolism occurs throughout the cytoplasm and organelles, not specifically at the cytopyge, which is a defined egestion site.

## answer_d
Reproduction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Reproduction is directed by the nucleus and carried out by fission of the whole cell, not through the cytopyge.

## answer_e
Secretion

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The cytopyge expels waste rather than secreting a product, so it is not classed as a secretory structure.
