# Item

## title
L/M appearance of the plasma cell

## question
The L/M of plasma cells show:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Negative golgi image

## explanation_a
Correct. The plasma cell is packed with rough endoplasmic reticulum for antibody synthesis, which makes its cytoplasm deeply basophilic, and its large Golgi apparatus sits as a pale, unstained area to one side of the nucleus. This clear juxtanuclear zone is described as the "negative Golgi image" because the Golgi excludes the basophilic ribosomes around it and so stains as a hole. Together with the eccentric clock-face (cartwheel) nucleus, this is the classic light-microscope signature of the plasma cell.

## answer_b
Few r-ER

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Plasma cells are exceptionally rich in rough endoplasmic reticulum, not poor in it — the abundant rER is exactly what gives their cytoplasm its strong basophilia.

## answer_c
Metachromatic granules

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cytoplasmic metachromatic granules are the hallmark of the mast cell, whose heparin stains a different colour from the dye; the plasma cell has no such granules.

## answer_d
Kidney shaped nucleus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A kidney- (or horseshoe-) shaped nucleus points to the monocyte; the plasma cell's nucleus is round and eccentric with a clock-face chromatin pattern.

## answer_e
Numerous phagosomes

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Numerous phagosomes belong to the actively phagocytic macrophage. The plasma cell is a secretory, not a phagocytic, cell.

## title
Functional property of the axon

## question
The axon of nerve fiber:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Show decreased diameter gradually towards their end

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The axon keeps a fairly uniform diameter along its length; it does not taper progressively towards its end the way a dendrite's branches do.

## answer_b
Show extensive branching along its length

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The axon gives off only occasional collateral branches and branches mainly at its terminal; it is the dendrites that branch extensively and repeatedly.

## answer_c
Has Nissel granules in its content for protein synthesis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Nissl granules (rough ER) are confined to the cell body and dendrites and are absent from the axon and the axon hillock — the axon has no protein-synthetic machinery of its own.

## answer_d
Responsible for nerve conduction away from cell body

## explanation_d
Correct. The axon is the single, long process that conducts the nerve impulse away from the neuron's cell body towards the next cell (efferent conduction). It arises from the axon hillock, lacks Nissl bodies, and keeps a constant diameter, distinguishing it from the multiple, tapering, Nissl-containing dendrites that carry impulses towards the cell body. This one-way, away-from-the-soma direction of conduction is the axon's defining functional property.

## answer_e
Short and thick

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The axon is characteristically long and slender, not short and thick; length is one of the features that separates it from the shorter dendrites.

## title
Feature of the dorsal root ganglion

## question
As regard dorsal root ganglia:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Cell bodies are mostly of the small uniform size

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Dorsal root ganglion cell bodies are large and of fairly uniform large size; it is the autonomic ganglion whose neurons are smaller and more varied in size.

## answer_b
They contain spinal motor neurons

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The dorsal root ganglion holds the pseudounipolar cell bodies of primary sensory neurons, not motor neurons — spinal motor neurons lie in the ventral horn of the cord.

## answer_c
They are sympathetic motor neurons

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Its neurons are sensory, not sympathetic motor; sympathetic motor cell bodies sit in the paravertebral and prevertebral autonomic ganglia instead.

## answer_d
They have incomplete capsule of satellite cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Each sensory cell body is wrapped by a complete capsule of satellite cells; an incomplete satellite capsule is a feature of autonomic, not dorsal root, ganglia.

## answer_e
They have poor blood supply

## explanation_e
Correct. Among the options offered, the poor (relatively sparse) blood supply is the true statement about the dorsal root ganglion. The other features listed all describe the autonomic ganglion instead — small, variably sized multipolar cell bodies with an incomplete satellite-cell capsule — whereas the dorsal root ganglion has large, uniform pseudounipolar sensory cell bodies with a complete capsule. Reading the ganglion by these paired contrasts is exactly what the question tests.

## title
Content of the granular layer of epidermis

## question
The cells of granular layer of epidermis contain:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Melanin pigment

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Melanin is made by melanocytes and taken up mainly by the basal keratinocytes; it is not the defining content of the granular layer.

## answer_b
Carotene pigment

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Carotene is an exogenous dietary pigment deposited in the subcutaneous fat and horny layer, not a granule of the stratum granulosum.

## answer_c
Eleidin granules

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Eleidin is the refractile material of the stratum lucidum (clear layer) of thick skin, formed one layer more superficially, from the breakdown of keratohyalin.

## answer_d
Keratohyaline granules

## explanation_d
Correct. The stratum granulosum is named for the keratohyalin granules that fill its cells — intensely basophilic, irregular granules of profilaggrin and associated proteins. These granules mark the start of the keratinisation programme: their contents help aggregate the keratin filaments as the cell dies and flattens into the horny layer above. Keratohyalin is therefore the identifying content of the granular layer specifically.

## answer_e
Trichohyaline granules

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Trichohyaline granules belong to the inner root sheath of the hair follicle, not to the epidermal granular layer.

## title
Function of the Langerhans cell

## question
The function of Langerhans cell in skin is:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Give skin its color

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Skin colour comes from melanin made by the melanocyte, a different dendritic cell of the epidermis; the Langerhans cell has no pigment role.

## answer_b
Unknown

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The Langerhans cell's function is well established — this was once uncertain historically, but it is now firmly known to be immunological.

## answer_c
Antibody secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Antibody secretion is the plasma cell's job; the Langerhans cell processes and presents antigen but does not itself secrete antibody.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Eleidin is formed by keratinocytes of the clear layer, not by the Langerhans cell.

Antigen presenting cell (APC)

## explanation_e
Correct. The Langerhans cell is the resident antigen-presenting (dendritic) cell of the epidermis. It is a bone-marrow-derived cell scattered among the keratinocytes of the stratum spinosum, where it captures antigen entering through the skin, processes it, and migrates to the regional lymph node to present it to T lymphocytes. This makes it the sentinel of cutaneous immune surveillance, a member of the mononuclear phagocyte / dendritic-cell system.

The papillary layer of the skin contains:-

## subject

Meissner's corpuscle

## explanation_a
Correct. The papillary layer is the thin, superficial layer of the dermis that raises the dermal papillae into the epidermis. Its papillae house Meissner's (tactile) corpuscles, the encapsulated touch receptors that sit just beneath the epidermis where fine touch is sampled. Pacinian corpuscle

Incorrect. The large, onion-layered Pacinian corpuscle sits deep in the reticular dermis and subcutaneous tissue, far below the papillary layer.

## answer_e
Few cells

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The papillary layer is comparatively rich in cells; it is the deeper reticular layer that is dense and relatively cell-poor.

## question
The epidermis of thin skin does NOT show:-

## subject
fnd

Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Basal layer

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The basal layer (stratum basale) is present in all skin, thin and thick alike — it is the germinative layer that renews the epidermis.

## answer_b
Clear layer

## explanation_b
Correct. Thin (hairy) skin lacks the stratum lucidum, the clear layer, which is found only in the thick, hairless skin of the palms and soles. Thin skin keeps the other four layers — basal, spinous, a thin granular layer and a thin horny layer — but its granular and horny layers are much reduced and the clear layer is absent altogether. The presence of a clear layer is therefore a marker of thick skin, and its absence a marker of thin skin.

Granular layer

## answer_d
Horny layer

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A horny layer (stratum corneum) is present in thin skin; it is simply much thinner than in thick skin.

## answer_e
Spinous layer

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The spinous layer (stratum spinosum) is present in all epidermis; it is not a distinguishing feature between thin and thick skin.

## title
Fibres of the autonomic ganglion

## question
Regarding the autonomic ganglia, their fibers:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Are thick myelinated

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The preganglionic fibres reaching an autonomic ganglion are thin and lightly myelinated, and the postganglionic fibres leaving it are unmyelinated — not thick and heavily myelinated.

## answer_b
Synapse with the ganglion cells

## explanation_b
Correct. The defining feature of an autonomic ganglion is that it is a synaptic relay: the incoming preganglionic axons enter and synapse on the cell bodies (multipolar neurons) of the ganglion, and new postganglionic axons then leave. This is unlike the dorsal root (sensory) ganglion, where the fibres merely pass through and no synapse occurs. The presence of synapses on the ganglion cells is exactly what marks a motor (autonomic) ganglion.

## answer_c
Are widely separated

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The nerve fibres run among the closely arranged ganglion cells rather than being widely separated from them.

## answer_d
Are parallel to the capsule

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The fibres weave among the cell bodies to reach their synapses; they are not simply laid parallel to the capsule.

## answer_e
Have poor blood supply

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Blood supply is not the point being tested here, and it is not the feature that identifies the autonomic ganglion — the synaptic relay is.

## title
Mitotic stage of nuclear-envelope breakdown

## question
The nuclear membrane dissolves during which stage of mitosis:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Prophase

## explanation_a
Correct. The nuclear envelope breaks down at the end of prophase (the transition sometimes called prometaphase), allowing the forming spindle microtubules to reach and attach to the chromosomes. Prophase is also when the chromosomes condense, the nucleolus disappears and the centrosomes move to opposite poles. Envelope breakdown is thus the event that ends prophase and lets metaphase alignment begin.

## answer_b
Metaphase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. By metaphase the envelope has already gone; metaphase is defined by the chromosomes aligning on the equatorial plate, not by envelope breakdown.

## answer_c
Anaphase A

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Anaphase is when sister chromatids separate and move to the poles; the envelope is absent throughout, having broken down two stages earlier.

## answer_d
Telophase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Telophase does the opposite — the nuclear envelope re-forms around each set of daughter chromosomes as they decondense.

## answer_e
Cytokinesis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cytokinesis is the division of the cytoplasm by the contractile ring; it does not involve the nuclear envelope, which has already reassembled in telophase.

# Item

## id
QST-MANSHBG-HISTOPREV4-D59-Q36

## title
Mitotic stage of chromosome alignment

## question
In which stage of mitosis chromosomes align in the center of the cell:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Prophase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Prophase is condensation and spindle formation with envelope breakdown; the chromosomes are not yet aligned in the middle.

## answer_b
Anaphase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. In anaphase the aligned chromatids have already separated and are moving apart to the two poles, not sitting at the centre.

## answer_c
Anaphase 1

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Anaphase I is a meiotic, not a mitotic, stage; it also describes separation, not central alignment.

## answer_d
Metaphase

## explanation_d
Correct. Metaphase is the stage at which the chromosomes line up along the equator (the metaphase plate) in the centre of the cell, each held by spindle fibres from both poles. ## title
Site of rRNA synthesis

## question
Which of the followings is responsible for synthesis of rRNA:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Nuclear membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The nuclear membrane (envelope) controls nucleocytoplasmic transport through its pores; it does not make ribosomal RNA.

## answer_b
Nucleolus

## explanation_b
Correct. The nucleolus is the site where ribosomal RNA is transcribed (from the nucleolar-organiser regions of certain chromosomes) and where the rRNA is assembled with proteins into the small and large ribosomal subunits. It is a dense, unbounded, strongly basophilic body within the nucleus, prominent in cells actively making protein. Synthesis of rRNA and assembly of ribosomal subunits is its defining function.

## answer_c
Nuclear sap

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The nuclear sap (nucleoplasm) is the fluid medium of the nucleus; rRNA synthesis is localised to the nucleolus within it.

## answer_d
rER

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Rough ER is the site where ribosomes translate mRNA into protein, not where rRNA is synthesised.

## answer_e
Peroxisomes

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Peroxisomes carry out oxidative reactions such as detoxification of hydrogen peroxide; they have no role in rRNA synthesis.

## title
Character of epithelial tissue

## question
Which of the followings is a character of epithelial tissue:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cells do not rest on basement membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A defining feature of epithelium is that its cells do rest on a basement membrane separating them from the underlying connective tissue.

## answer_b
There is continuous degeneration and regeneration

## explanation_b
Correct. Epithelium is a continuously renewing tissue: its cells are steadily lost from the surface and replaced by mitosis of the deeper (basal) cells, giving a constant turnover of degeneration and regeneration. This high renewal capacity, together with being avascular, resting on a basement membrane, and having closely packed cells with little intercellular substance, is what characterises epithelium. Continuous regeneration is the true statement among the options.

## answer_c
Widely separated cells with little intercellular substance

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Epithelial cells are closely packed with very little intercellular substance — 'widely separated' is the opposite of the truth and describes connective tissue instead.

## answer_d
There are blood vessels between cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Epithelium is avascular; it has no blood vessels between its cells and is nourished by diffusion from the vessels of the underlying connective tissue.

## answer_e
Nerves can't enter between cells

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Epithelium is innervated — sensory nerve endings can and do penetrate between epithelial cells, as in the epidermis and cornea.
