## title
Cyclosporine's immunosuppressant mechanism

## question
Which of the following most accurately describes the immunosuppressant action of cyclosporine?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Activation of NK cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cyclosporine suppresses, rather than activates, immune cell function; activating NK cells would work against, not produce, an immunosuppressant effect.

## answer_b
Blockade of tissue responses to inflammatory mediators

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cyclosporine does not act by blocking tissue responses to already-released inflammatory mediators; its action is upstream, preventing the T cell from producing those mediators (interleukins) in the first place.

## answer_c
Increased catabolism of IgG antibodies

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Increased IgG catabolism is not cyclosporine's mechanism; that description does not match calcineurin inhibition or any recognised cyclosporine action.

## answer_d
Inhibition of calcineurin mediated gene transcription of interleukins

## explanation_d
Correct. Cyclosporine binds cyclophilin, and this complex inhibits calcineurin, a phosphatase required to activate the transcription factor NFAT. Without active NFAT, T lymphocytes cannot transcribe the interleukin-2 gene and other cytokine genes. This blocks T-cell activation and proliferation, the basis of its immunosuppressant effect.

## answer_e
Interference with MHC II-peptide activation of T cells

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cyclosporine does not interfere with MHC class II-peptide presentation to T cells; its target is intracellular, downstream of antigen recognition, at the calcineurin-NFAT signalling step within the T cell.

## title
Drug blocking soluble receptors of TNF-alpha

## question
Which of the following drugs acts by blocking soluble receptors of TNF-α?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Anakinra

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Anakinra is an IL-1 receptor antagonist, blocking interleukin-1 signalling, not a TNF-alpha soluble receptor construct.

## answer_b
Sulfasalazine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sulfasalazine is a non-biologic prodrug DMARD cleaved by gut bacteria into 5-aminosalicylic acid and sulphapyridine; it has no TNF-receptor-blocking mechanism.

## answer_c
Gold salts

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gold salts are an older conventional DMARD with a mechanism unrelated to TNF-alpha receptor blockade.

## answer_d
Methotrexate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Methotrexate is a dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor with broad antiproliferative and anti-inflammatory effects, not a TNF-alpha soluble receptor blocker.

## answer_e
Etanercept

## explanation_e
Correct. Etanercept is a fusion protein built from the extracellular domain of the TNF receptor joined to an antibody Fc region. It acts as a soluble decoy receptor that binds circulating TNF-alpha. This blocks TNF-alpha from reaching its true cell-surface receptors, dampening the inflammatory cascade.

## title
Tofacitinib's mechanism of action

## question
Which statement is the mechanism of action of tofacitinib that used for treatment of rheumatoid arthritis?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Inhibitor of TNFa

## explanation_a
Incorrect. TNF-alpha inhibition describes drugs such as etanercept, infliximab or adalimumab, which act extracellularly on TNF-alpha or its receptor, not tofacitinib's intracellular mechanism.

## answer_b
New JAK inhibitor

## explanation_b
Correct. Tofacitinib is an oral small-molecule Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor. It blocks intracellular JAK enzymes, interrupting the JAK-STAT pathway that carries cytokine receptor signals to the nucleus. This intracellular, downstream action distinguishes it from the extracellular biologic DMARDs.

## answer_c
Inhibitor of IL-6

## explanation_c
Incorrect. IL-6 receptor inhibition describes tocilizumab, not tofacitinib, which works one step further downstream, inside the cell, rather than blocking a specific cytokine receptor.

## answer_d
Inhibitor of IL-1

## explanation_d
Incorrect. IL-1 inhibition describes anakinra, not tofacitinib; tofacitinib's target is intracellular JAK signalling common to multiple cytokine receptors, not one specific interleukin's receptor.

## answer_e
CD80/86 inhibitor

## explanation_e
Incorrect. CD80/86 inhibition (blocking T-cell costimulation) describes abatacept's mechanism, not tofacitinib's intracellular JAK-STAT-blocking action.

## title
Drug used as bridging therapy in arthritis

## question
Which of the following drug is used in bridging therapy in patient with arthritis?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Leflunomide

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Leflunomide is itself a conventional DMARD with its own delayed onset of action; it is not the fast-acting agent used to bridge the gap while a DMARD takes effect.

## answer_b
Corticosteroids

## explanation_b
Correct. Corticosteroids provide rapid symptomatic relief and inflammation control. A slower-onset DMARD, such as methotrexate, takes weeks to months to reach its full disease-modifying effect. The steroid is then tapered once the DMARD has taken hold, the defining bridging-therapy role.

## answer_c
Sulfasalazine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sulfasalazine is a conventional DMARD, itself slow-onset, not the rapid bridging agent used alongside it.

## answer_d
Celecoxib

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Celecoxib provides symptomatic NSAID-type relief but is not specifically the bridging agent used to cover a DMARD's delayed onset in the way corticosteroids are.

## answer_e
Anakinra

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Anakinra is a biologic DMARD (IL-1 receptor antagonist), not the short-term bridging agent used while a conventional DMARD's effect is awaited.

## title
Reason to avoid celecoxib in a rheumatoid arthritis patient

## question
Which of the following patient characteristics is the most compelling reason for avoiding celecoxib in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
History of alcohol abuse

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A history of alcohol abuse is not the specific, compelling contraindication being tested for celecoxib; celecoxib's most concerning risk relates to cardiovascular events, not hepatic effects from alcohol history.

## answer_b
History of gout

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A history of gout is not a contraindication to celecoxib; NSAIDs including COX-2 selective agents can be used in gout management, so this history does not argue against celecoxib specifically.

## answer_c
History of myocardial infarction

## explanation_c
Correct. Selective COX-2 inhibitors like celecoxib carry an increased cardiovascular (thrombotic) risk. This comes from sparing platelet thromboxane while still inhibiting vascular prostacyclin, tilting the balance toward clotting. A history of myocardial infarction makes this cardiovascular risk the most compelling reason to avoid celecoxib.

## answer_d
History of osteoporosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Osteoporosis history is not a specific contraindication to celecoxib; it is unrelated to celecoxib's known risk profile.

## answer_e
History of peptic ulcer disease

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Peptic ulcer disease history would actually favour celecoxib over non-selective NSAIDs, since celecoxib's COX-1-sparing profile reduces gastric ulcer risk; this option describes a reason to prefer, not avoid, celecoxib.

## title
Drug inhibiting phagocytic function and stabilising lysosomes

## question
Which of the following inhibit phagocytic function and stabilize lysosomes:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Abatacept

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Abatacept blocks T-cell costimulation by binding CD80/86, an entirely different mechanism from lysosomal stabilisation or phagocyte inhibition.

## answer_b
Anakinra

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Anakinra is an IL-1 receptor antagonist, working extracellularly on cytokine receptor binding, not through lysosomal membrane effects.

## answer_c
Leflunomide

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Leflunomide inhibits dihydroorotate dehydrogenase, blocking pyrimidine synthesis, a metabolic mechanism unrelated to lysosomal stabilisation or phagocyte function.

## answer_d
Chloroquine

## explanation_d
Correct. Chloroquine raises lysosomal pH and stabilises lysosomal membranes. This inhibits phagocytic cell function and interferes with antigen processing and presentation. That interference is the mechanism behind its antirheumatic action.

## answer_e
Methotrexate

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, a folate-antagonist mechanism affecting cell proliferation, not a lysosome-stabilising or phagocyte-inhibiting one.

## title
DMARD responsible for retinopathy

## question
Which of the following DMARDs is responsible for retinopathy?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Sulfasalazine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sulfasalazine's characteristic risks are gastrointestinal and haematological (such as agranulocytosis), not retinopathy.

## answer_b
Leflunomide

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Leflunomide's characteristic risks are hepatotoxicity and teratogenicity, not an ocular retinal toxicity.

## answer_c
Hydroxychloroquine

## explanation_c
Correct. Hydroxychloroquine accumulates in the retinal pigment epithelium over years of use. This accumulation can cause irreversible retinopathy if unrecognised. That risk is why long-term users require baseline and periodic ophthalmologic screening.

## answer_d
Cyclosporine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cyclosporine's characteristic risk is nephrotoxicity (along with hypertension and tremor), not retinopathy.

## answer_e
Etanercept

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Etanercept's characteristic risks relate to immunosuppression, chiefly infection and malignancy risk, not a retinal toxicity.
