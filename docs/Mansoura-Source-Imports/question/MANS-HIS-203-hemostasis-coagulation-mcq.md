## title
Chemical that initiates the extrinsic phase of clotting

## question
Which chemical initiates the extrinsic phase of clotting?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Fibrin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fibrin is the end product of the cascade, formed from fibrinogen by thrombin, not the substance that starts the extrinsic pathway.

## answer_b
Thromboplastin

## explanation_b
Correct. Tissue thromboplastin (tissue factor, factor III), released from damaged tissue at the site of vessel injury, is what triggers the extrinsic coagulation pathway. It activates factor X to factor Xa, which then converts prothrombin to thrombin, setting the whole cascade in motion from outside the blood itself — hence "extrinsic".

## answer_c
Prothrombin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Prothrombin is converted into thrombin further down the cascade; it does not itself initiate the extrinsic pathway.

## answer_d
Christmas factor

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Christmas factor (factor IX) is a component of the intrinsic pathway, not the extrinsic pathway's trigger.

## answer_e
Fibrinogin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Fibrinogen is the precursor of fibrin, converted late in the cascade by thrombin; it plays no role in initiating the extrinsic pathway.

## question
Which factor activates the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin?

haem

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Correct. Factor Xa is the activated clotting factor that directly converts prothrombin into active thrombin, the step common to both the extrinsic and intrinsic pathways once they converge. This conversion also requires calcium ions and phospholipid as cofactors, forming the prothrombinase complex.

Factor IXa

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Factor IXa acts upstream in the intrinsic pathway, together with factor VIIIa, to activate factor X — a step before prothrombin conversion, not the conversion itself.

## answer_e
Factor XIa

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Factor XIa acts further upstream still in the intrinsic pathway, activating factor IX; it is two steps removed from prothrombin conversion.

QST-MANSHIS203-HEMOSTASIS-COAGULATION-CALCIUM-CLOTTING-COFACTOR

## title
Ion required for conversion of prothrombin into active thrombin

## question
Which of the following ions is required for conversion of prothrombin into active thrombin?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Ca++

## explanation_a
Correct. Calcium ions (Ca++) are required as a cofactor for the conversion of prothrombin into active thrombin by factor Xa. They are also needed more broadly at multiple other steps of the coagulation cascade, so the cascade cannot proceed without calcium even when every clotting factor is present. This is why citrate (a calcium chelator) is used as an anticoagulant to prevent stored blood from clotting.

## answer_b
Fe++

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ferrous iron (Fe++) is central to haemoglobin's oxygen-binding function, not a coagulation cofactor.

## answer_c
Mg++

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Magnesium ions participate in many enzymatic reactions in the body but are not the specific cofactor required for prothrombin-to-thrombin conversion.

## answer_d
Mn++

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Manganese ions are not the coagulation cascade's required cofactor for this step.

## answer_e
Cl-

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Chloride is the major extracellular anion involved in osmotic and acid-base balance, not a coagulation cofactor.

A condition of heredity deficiency of coagulation is called:

Hemophilia

## explanation_b
Correct. Haemophilia is the general name for a hereditary (inherited) deficiency of a specific coagulation factor, producing a lifelong bleeding tendency from otherwise minor injury or spontaneously into joints and soft tissue. Incorrect. Leukaemia is malignant, uncontrolled white cell proliferation, not a hereditary coagulation-factor deficiency.

## answer_e
Polycythemia

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Polycythemia is an increased red cell count, unrelated to coagulation-factor deficiency.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Plasmin degrades fibrin during fibrinolysis, the clot-breakdown process, and plays no role in vasoconstriction.

## title
Correct order of the steps of hemostasis

## question
Choose the correct order for the steps of hemostasis:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Blood coagulation, platelet plug formation, blood vessel spasm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This reverses the true order; blood vessel spasm is the fastest, most immediate response and comes first, not last.

## answer_b
Platelets plug formation, blood coagulation, blood vessel spasm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This places blood vessel spasm last, when it is actually the immediate, first response to injury.

## answer_c
Blood vessel spasm, platelet plug formation, blood coagulation

## explanation_c
Correct. Normal haemostasis follows a fixed sequence: blood vessel spasm (vasoconstriction) narrows the injured vessel first to reduce flow; platelet plug formation then seals the breach as platelets adhere, activate and aggregate; blood coagulation follows, laying down fibrin that stabilises the platelet plug into a definitive clot. Each step builds on the one before it, and vessel spasm's speed (occurring within seconds) is what buys time for the slower platelet and coagulation responses to develop.

## answer_d
Blood vessel spasm, blood coagulation, platelet plug formation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This places platelet plug formation after coagulation, when platelets actually form the initial plug that the coagulation cascade then stabilises.

## answer_e
Platelet plug formation, blood vessel spasm, blood coagulation

## explanation_e
Incorrect. This places vessel spasm after the platelet plug, when vessel spasm is actually the fastest, first response.

# Item

## id
QST-MANSHIS203-HEMOSTASIS-COAGULATION-PLATELET-CLOT-RETRACTION

## title
Cells necessary for clot retraction

## question
------------- are necessary for clot retraction to occur.

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Red blood cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Red blood cells are passively trapped within the fibrin meshwork of a clot but play no active contractile role in clot retraction.

## answer_b
Lymphocytes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lymphocytes mediate adaptive immunity and have no role in the mechanical process of clot retraction.

## answer_c
Platelets

## explanation_c
Correct. Platelets are necessary for clot retraction: after the fibrin clot has formed, the platelet cytoskeleton contracts, pulling the fibrin meshwork tighter and squeezing out serum. This draws the wound edges together and makes the clot more effective at sealing the vessel, and clot retraction is impaired in conditions of severe thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction.

## answer_d
Basophils

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Basophils mediate immediate hypersensitivity reactions and have no role in clot retraction.

## answer_e
Monocytes

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Monocytes are phagocytic precursor cells with no contractile role in clot retraction.

QST-MANSHIS203-HEMOSTASIS-COAGULATION-VITAMIN-K-DEPENDENT-FACTORS

## title
Vitamin K dependent coagulation factors

## question
Vitamin K dependent coagulation factors are:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Factor II, VII, IX and X

## explanation_a
Correct. Factors II (prothrombin), VII, IX and X, along with proteins C and S, require vitamin K for their hepatic synthesis — vitamin K is a cofactor for the enzyme that carboxylates specific glutamate residues on these factors, activating them for calcium binding. Vitamin K deficiency (or vitamin K antagonists such as warfarin) lowers the functional level of all four factors together, prolonging coagulation time.

## answer_b
Factor XII and XI

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Factors XII and XI are contact-activation factors of the intrinsic pathway, and their synthesis is not vitamin K dependent.

## answer_c
Factor VIII and XI

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Factors VIII and XI are also not vitamin K dependent; factor VIII in particular is synthesised independently of vitamin K status.

## answer_d
Factor III, VIII and XI

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Factor III (tissue thromboplastin) and factors VIII and XI are not among the vitamin-K-dependent factors.

## answer_e
Factor XI and XII

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Factors XI and XII are contact-activation factors, not part of the vitamin-K-dependent group.

## id
QST-MANSHIS203-HEMOSTASIS-COAGULATION-OBSTRUCTIVE-JAUNDICE-HEMORRHAGIC-TENDENCY

## title
Cause of hemorrhagic tendency in obstructive jaundice

## question
Hemorrhagic tendency in obstructive jaundice is due to:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Deficiency of platelets

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Obstructive jaundice's bleeding tendency is not primarily from a platelet deficiency; platelet counts are typically normal.

## answer_b
Increased serum bile salt concentration

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Increased serum bile salts cause pruritus (itching) in obstructive jaundice, not the bleeding tendency, which has a separate mechanism.

## answer_c
Deficiency of factor VIII

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Factor VIII synthesis is not vitamin K dependent and is not the mechanism behind obstructive jaundice's bleeding tendency.

## answer_d
Lack of factors II, VII, IX and X

## explanation_d
Correct. In obstructive jaundice, blocked bile flow into the intestine prevents normal fat (and fat-soluble vitamin) absorption, including vitamin K, because bile salts are needed to emulsify and absorb it. The resulting vitamin K deficiency lowers hepatic synthesis of factors II, VII, IX and X, prolonging coagulation time and producing a hemorrhagic tendency — the same underlying factor group tested directly elsewhere in this cluster, here reached through a malabsorption mechanism rather than dietary deficiency alone.

## answer_e
Deficiency of fibrinogen

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Fibrinogen deficiency is not the mechanism of obstructive jaundice's bleeding tendency; fibrinogen synthesis is not vitamin K dependent.
