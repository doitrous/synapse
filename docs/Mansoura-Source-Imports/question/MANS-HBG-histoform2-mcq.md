## title
Identifying a phase of mitosis from a mixed list

## question
Which of the followings is a phase of mitosis:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Diakinesis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diakinesis is the last substage of meiotic prophase I, not a phase of mitosis at all.

## answer_b
Interphase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Interphase (G1, S, G2) is the period between successive mitotic divisions, when the cell grows and replicates its DNA — it precedes mitosis but is not itself one of its phases.

## answer_c
Prophase

## explanation_c
Correct. Mitosis proper is divided into prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase. Prophase is the first of these: chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes, the mitotic spindle begins to form, and the nuclear envelope starts to break down.

## answer_d
S phase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. S (synthesis) phase is part of interphase, when the cell replicates its DNA before mitosis begins — it is not one of mitosis's own phases.

## answer_e
Zygotene

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Zygotene is a substage of meiotic prophase I, the stage of homologous chromosome pairing (synapsis) — it belongs to meiosis, not mitosis.

The cell type in which meiosis occurs

## question
Meiosis occurs in the following cells:-

## subject
fnd

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Liver cells are somatic cells; when they divide (or regenerate), they do so by mitosis, not meiosis.

## answer_b
Intestinal cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Intestinal epithelial cells renew constantly by mitosis, a somatic process — meiosis is not part of their turnover.

## answer_c
Sex cells

## explanation_c
Correct. Meiosis is the reduction division restricted to the germ (sex) cells of the gonads as part of gametogenesis — oogenesis in the ovary, spermatogenesis in the testis — halving the chromosome number from diploid to haploid ahead of fertilisation.

Nerve cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mature neurons are permanently postmitotic; they neither divide by mitosis nor undergo meiosis.

## answer_e
Skin cells

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The epidermis renews itself by ongoing mitotic division of its basal cells, a somatic process, not meiosis.

## title
The broad stage of meiosis at which crossing over occurs

## question
Crossing over occurs at which stage of meiosis:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Prophase 1

## explanation_a
Correct. Crossing over — the reciprocal exchange of segments between non-sister chromatids of paired homologous chromosomes — takes place during prophase of the first meiotic division, specifically at its pachytene substage, after the homologs have synapsed.

## answer_b
Metaphase 2

## explanation_b
Incorrect. By metaphase II the first meiotic (reduction) division is already complete and homologs have separated; there are no paired homologs left to exchange material with.

## answer_c
Anaphase 1

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Anaphase I is when the already-recombined homologous chromosomes separate and move to opposite poles — the exchange itself happened earlier, in prophase I.

## answer_d
Prophase 2

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prophase II precedes the second meiotic division, which separates sister chromatids, not homologs; there is no homologous pairing or crossing over at this stage.

## answer_e
Anaphase 2

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Anaphase II is when sister chromatids separate in the second meiotic division, long after crossing over occurred in prophase I.

The connective tissue cell with metachromatic cytoplasmic granules

## question
Which of the following CT cells have metachromatic granules in cytoplasm:-

## subject
fnd

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The plasma cell's cytoplasm is agranular and intensely basophilic (rich rough ER), not filled with metachromatic granules; that is the mast cell's feature.

## answer_b
Mast cells

## explanation_b
Correct. The mast cell's cytoplasm is packed with basophilic secretory granules containing heparin and histamine. Incorrect. The adipose cell is dominated by a single large lipid droplet with minimal other cytoplasmic content; it has no metachromatic granules.

Macrophages

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Macrophages are rich in lysosomes and phagocytic vacuoles, not the metachromatic secretory granules that identify the mast cell.

## answer_e
Fibroblasts

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The fibroblast's cytoplasm is dominated by rough ER for matrix synthesis; it carries no metachromatic granules.

## question
As regard neurons:-

## subject

## answer_a
Cell body doesn't contain mitochondria

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The neuron's cell body is metabolically very active and is well supplied with mitochondria, alongside its abundant Nissl substance.

## answer_b
Nucleus is large rounded central vesicular

## explanation_b
Correct. The typical neuron's cell body holds one large, rounded, centrally placed nucleus with finely dispersed (vesicular, pale) chromatin and a prominent nucleolus, reflecting its ongoing high transcriptional activity.

Nissel granules are distributed all over the neuron including axon

Incorrect. Nissl granules are basophilic (not acidophilic) with H&E, staining deep blue-purple because of their high RNA content in the rough ER and free ribosomes.

## answer_e
Centrioles are important for protein synthesis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Centrioles are microtubule-organising structures; protein synthesis is the job of ribosomes and rough ER (Nissl substance), not the centrioles.

## id
QST-MANSHBG-HISTOFORM2-Q96

## title
A correct statement about the ganglia

## question
As regard the ganglia, which of the following is correct:-

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Collection of nerve cells & fibers covered by CT capsule inside CNS

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A ganglion is, by definition, a collection of nerve cell bodies outside the central nervous system, in the peripheral nervous system, not inside the CNS — a comparable cell-body cluster inside the CNS is called a nucleus, not a ganglion.

## answer_b
The nerve cell of spiral (dorsal root) ganglion is multipolar

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The dorsal root (sensory) ganglion's cell bodies are pseudo-unipolar, a single process splitting into peripheral and central branches, not multipolar.

## answer_c
The nerve cell of sympathetic ganglion is pseudo-unipolar

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sympathetic (autonomic) ganglion neurons are multipolar; pseudo-unipolar is the dorsal root ganglion's sensory neuron shape instead.

## answer_d
The nerve fiber of sympathetic ganglion is thickly myelinated and parallel to capsule

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Autonomic ganglion fibres (thin, lightly myelinated preganglionic and unmyelinated postganglionic) weave among the cell bodies to synapse on them, rather than running thickly myelinated and parallel to the capsule.
