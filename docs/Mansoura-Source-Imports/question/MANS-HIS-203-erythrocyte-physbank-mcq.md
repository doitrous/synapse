## title
Main site of blood cell destruction

## question
Blood cell destruction occurs mainly in the:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Bone marrow

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The bone marrow is where blood cells are made, not where they are destroyed; production and destruction happen at different sites.

## answer_b
Kidneys

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The kidneys filter plasma and regulate fluid and electrolyte balance; they are not the main site of blood cell removal.

## answer_c
Heart

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The heart pumps blood around the circulation; it plays no role in removing aged blood cells from it.

## answer_d
Spleen

## explanation_d
Correct. The spleen is the main site of blood cell destruction. Aged, fragile red cells are squeezed through its narrow splenic cords and sinusoids, and macrophages lining the red pulp phagocytose those that can no longer deform enough to pass through, alongside worn platelets and other spent cells.

## title
Erythrocytes and blood viscosity

## question
About erythrocytes:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
They contain no enzymes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Erythrocytes do contain enzymes — carbonic anhydrase in particular, which speeds the interconversion of carbon dioxide and bicarbonate — so this blanket denial is false.

## answer_b
They are responsible for the major part of blood viscosity

## explanation_b
Correct. Erythrocytes are responsible for the major part of blood's viscosity. Whole blood is about three times as viscous as water while plasma alone is only about 1.5 times as viscous, and that extra viscosity comes overwhelmingly from the suspended red cells, which make up the great majority of blood's cellular volume.

## answer_c
Their life span is about 2 months

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The erythrocyte's life span is about four months (120 days), not two.

## answer_d
Their Hb content carries only O2

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Haemoglobin carries more than oxygen alone — it also carries some carbon dioxide as carbamino-haemoglobin and buffers hydrogen ions — so "only O2" understates its function.

QST-MANSHIS203-ERYTHROCYTE-PHYSBANK-PHYS-5

## title
Advantage of the biconcave erythrocyte shape

## question
The biconcave shape of erythrocytes has the following advantage:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Produces a smaller surface area than a spherical cell of the same volume

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Biconcavity does the opposite: for the same enclosed volume it gives a LARGER surface area than a sphere would, not a smaller one, which is part of why it helps gas exchange rather than limiting it.

## answer_b
Allows erythrocytes to be squeezed through small capillaries without rupture

## explanation_b
Correct. The biconcave shape's key advantage is flexibility: it lets the erythrocyte fold and deform as it is squeezed through capillaries narrower than its own resting diameter, without its membrane rupturing. That reversible deformability is what lets the cell survive repeated passage through the microcirculation for its full lifespan.

## answer_c
Results in high tension on the membrane when the volume of the cell changes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The biconcave shape's slack membrane surface area actually minimises tension when the cell's volume changes, rather than producing high tension; that slack is part of the same flexibility this shape provides.

## answer_d
Allows a small surface area for gas exchange

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Biconcavity increases the surface area available for gas exchange relative to a sphere of the same volume, not reduces it.

## answer_e
It allows free diffusion of ions

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Free ion diffusion is a property of the membrane's ion channels and transporters, not a consequence of the cell's overall biconcave shape.

QST-MANSHIS203-ERYTHROCYTE-PHYSBANK-PHYS-6

## title
Erythrocyte structure — the false statement

## question
About the structure of erythrocytes, which of the following is FALSE?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Are surrounded with plastic semipermeable membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the false statement. The erythrocyte membrane genuinely is an elastic ("plastic"), semipermeable lipid bilayer, consistent with the deformability the cell needs to cross narrow capillaries.

## answer_b
Hemoglobin is the main constituent of the erythrocytes

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the false statement. Haemoglobin genuinely is the main constituent of the erythrocyte's cytoplasm, occupying most of its internal volume and giving the cell its oxygen-carrying function.

## answer_c
K is the chief intracellular cation

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the false statement. Potassium genuinely is the chief intracellular cation of the erythrocyte, as it is for cells generally, maintained by the membrane's sodium-potassium pump.

## answer_d
It is a nucleated biconcave disc

## explanation_d
Correct — this is the false statement. The mature erythrocyte is anucleated, not nucleated: it extrudes its nucleus during maturation before entering the circulation. Calling it a "nucleated biconcave disc" is false on the nucleated part even though the biconcave-disc shape itself is accurate. This nucleus-extrusion fact is one of the most repeatedly tested facts in this bank.

## answer_e
It has no mitochondria

## explanation_e
Incorrect as the false statement. The erythrocyte genuinely has no mitochondria, which is exactly why it depends on anaerobic glycolysis for its energy rather than oxidative phosphorylation.

## title
Removal of old or damaged erythrocytes

## question
Old or damaged erythrocytes are removed from the blood by:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Eosinophils

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Eosinophils are specialised against large parasites such as helminths; removing worn-out erythrocytes is not their role.

## answer_b
Lymphocytes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lymphocytes mediate humoral and cell-mediated immunity; they are not phagocytic cells and do not remove aged red cells.

## answer_c
Macrophages

## explanation_c
Correct. Macrophages, mainly in the spleen and to a lesser extent the liver and bone marrow, phagocytose old or damaged erythrocytes. They recognise red cells that have become too fragile or altered to keep circulating and remove them, recycling the iron and breaking down the haemoglobin into bilirubin.

## answer_d
Monocytes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Monocytes are the circulating precursor of tissue macrophages, but it is the differentiated tissue macrophage, not the circulating monocyte itself, that carries out red cell removal in the spleen.

## answer_e
Neutrophils

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Neutrophils are the first line of defence against bacteria; clearing senescent red cells is not their function.
