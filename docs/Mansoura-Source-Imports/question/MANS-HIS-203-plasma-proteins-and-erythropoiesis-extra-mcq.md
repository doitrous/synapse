# Item

## id
QST-MANSHIS203-PLASMA-PROTEINS-AND-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-INTRINSIC-FACTOR-SOURCE

## title
Source of the intrinsic factor

## question
The intrinsic factor:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Is found in the liver

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Intrinsic factor is not stored in or produced by the liver; the liver's role in B12 physiology is instead storing several years' worth of vitamin B12 once absorbed.

## answer_b
Is produced by the gastric parietal cells

## explanation_b
Correct. Intrinsic factor is a glycoprotein produced and secreted by the gastric parietal cells (the same cells that secrete hydrochloric acid). It combines with vitamin B12, protecting it from digestion, and carries it to a specific receptor on the brush border of the mucosal cells of the lower (terminal) ileum, where absorption occurs. Loss of the gastric parietal cells (as in pernicious anaemia's autoimmune gastritis) removes the source of intrinsic factor and causes B12 malabsorption even with adequate dietary intake.

## answer_c
Is secreted by the terminal ileum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The terminal ileum is where intrinsic-factor-bound B12 is absorbed, not where intrinsic factor itself is secreted.

## answer_d
Aids absorption of folic acid

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Intrinsic factor is specific to vitamin B12 absorption; folic acid absorption does not require it.

## answer_e
Is essential for aplastic anemia

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Intrinsic factor's absence causes pernicious anaemia (a megaloblastic anaemia), not aplastic anaemia, which is a distinct marrow-failure condition.

# Item

## id
QST-MANSHIS203-PLASMA-PROTEINS-AND-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-SICKLE-CELL-BETA-GLOBIN-MUTATION

## title
Molecular basis of sickle cell anemia

## question
Sickle cell anemia may be caused by:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Deficiency in the amount or in the structure of spectrin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Spectrin deficiency or structural abnormality is the mechanism behind hereditary spherocytosis, a different red-cell membrane disorder, not sickle cell anaemia.

## answer_b
Mutation at the 6th codon of the beta globin gene where glutamic acid is replaced by valine

## explanation_b
Correct. Sickle cell anaemia is caused by a point mutation at the 6th codon of the beta globin gene, replacing glutamic acid with valine. This single amino acid substitution produces haemoglobin S (HbS), which polymerises under low-oxygen conditions, deforming the red cell into the characteristic sickle shape. This is a structural haemoglobinopathy — the globin chain is made in normal amount but with an abnormal structure — distinct from the thalassaemias, where structurally normal globin chains are made in reduced amount.

## answer_c
Mutations in genes encoding red cell membrane proteins

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mutations in red cell membrane protein genes (such as spectrin or ankyrin) cause hereditary spherocytosis or elliptocytosis, not sickle cell anaemia.

## answer_d
Absence of one or more of the genes responsible for synthesis of alpha or beta globin chains

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Absence of globin genes describes the mechanism of thalassaemia (reduced amount of a structurally normal chain), the other major category of haemoglobinopathy, not sickle cell anaemia's structural defect.

## answer_e
Pyruvate kinase deficiency

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Pyruvate kinase deficiency is a red-cell enzyme defect causing a separate haemolytic anaemia, unrelated to haemoglobin structure.

## title
Site of albumin synthesis

## question
Albumin is synthesized in:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
RBCs

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Red blood cells are anucleate and have no protein-synthesis machinery once mature; they do not synthesise plasma proteins.

## answer_b
Liver

## explanation_b
Correct. Albumin, the most abundant plasma protein, is synthesised in the liver. This is why chronic liver disease characteristically lowers plasma albumin, contributing to reduced plasma oncotic pressure and oedema in conditions such as cirrhosis.

## answer_c
Plasma cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Plasma cells synthesise immunoglobulins (antibodies), not albumin.

## answer_d
Lymphoid tissues

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lymphoid tissues are the site of lymphocyte development and antibody production, not albumin synthesis.

## answer_e
Kidneys

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The kidneys filter and reabsorb plasma proteins but do not synthesise albumin.

QST-MANSHIS203-PLASMA-PROTEINS-AND-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-TRANSFERRIN-IRON-CARRIER

## title
Form in which plasma protein carries iron

## question
The iron that carried by plasma protein circulate with blood in the form of:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Elemental iron

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Iron does not circulate free (elemental) in plasma, since free iron generates toxic reactive oxygen species; it must be bound to a carrier protein.

## answer_b
Apo transferrin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Apotransferrin is the iron-free form of the carrier protein; once it binds iron it becomes (holo-)transferrin, the form that actually carries iron in the plasma.

## answer_c
Transferrin

## explanation_c
Correct. Transferrin is the plasma glycoprotein that binds and carries iron through the circulation, delivering it to tissues (especially the bone marrow for erythropoiesis) via transferrin receptors. Transferrin saturation is used clinically as a marker of iron status, distinct from ferritin, which is the intracellular iron-storage protein.

## answer_d
Ferritin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ferritin is the intracellular iron-storage protein, found mainly in the liver, spleen and bone marrow, not the plasma iron-transport protein.

## answer_e
Apoferritin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Apoferritin is the iron-free storage-protein shell before it has bound iron to become ferritin; it is intracellular, not a plasma carrier.

## title
Plasma protein that carries vitamin B12

## question
Which of the following plasma proteins responsible for carry vitamin B12 in the blood?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Albumin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Albumin carries many small molecules and drugs non-specifically, but it is not the dedicated carrier for vitamin B12.

## answer_b
Ceruloplasmin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ceruloplasmin is the plasma protein that carries copper, not vitamin B12.

## answer_c
Transferrin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Transferrin carries iron, not vitamin B12 — a repeated distractor pairing in this bank's plasma-carrier questions.

## answer_d
Transcobalamin

## explanation_d
Correct. Transcobalamin (specifically transcobalamin II) is the plasma protein that carries vitamin B12 in the blood after its absorption via intrinsic factor at the terminal ileum. It delivers B12 to tissues throughout the body, including the bone marrow, where the vitamin supports normal erythropoiesis. This is a separate, named carrier role from albumin's non-specific transport and from transferrin's dedicated iron-carrying role.

## answer_e
Transthyretin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Transthyretin carries thyroxine and retinol-binding protein, not vitamin B12.

## id
QST-MANSHIS203-PLASMA-PROTEINS-AND-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-ALPHA1-ANTITRYPSIN-EMPHYSEMA

## title
Plasma protein deficiency that causes emphysema

## question
Deficiency of one of the following plasma proteins cause Emphysema:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Alpha globulin

## explanation_a
Correct. Alpha-1 antitrypsin, an alpha globulin, normally inhibits neutrophil elastase, an enzyme that would otherwise digest lung elastin during ordinary neutrophil activity. When alpha-1 antitrypsin is deficient (a hereditary condition), unchecked elastase activity destroys lung elastin over time, producing early-onset emphysema even in non-smokers, and more severe, earlier disease in smokers.

## answer_b
Albumin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Albumin deficiency causes oedema from reduced plasma oncotic pressure, not emphysema.

## answer_c
Beta-globulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Beta-globulins include transferrin and other transport proteins; their deficiency is not linked to emphysema.

## answer_d
Fibrinogen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fibrinogen deficiency causes a bleeding tendency from impaired clot formation, not emphysema.

## answer_e
Gamma globulin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Gamma globulin (immunoglobulin) deficiency causes recurrent infections from impaired antibody-mediated immunity, not emphysema.

QST-MANSHIS203-PLASMA-PROTEINS-AND-ERYTHROPOIESIS-EXTRA-CRP-ACUTE-PHASE-PROTEIN

## title
Example of an acute phase protein

## question
Example of acute phase proteins:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Transferrin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Transferrin is actually a negative acute-phase protein — its plasma level falls, not rises, during the acute-phase response.

## answer_b
Ceruloplasmin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ceruloplasmin is the copper-carrying plasma protein; while it can rise modestly in inflammation, it is not the classic textbook example this bank tests here.

## answer_c
Albumin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Albumin is also a negative acute-phase protein, falling during inflammation rather than rising.

## answer_d
C-reactive protein

## explanation_d
Correct. C-reactive protein (CRP) is the classic acute-phase plasma protein, rising sharply during inflammation, infection or tissue injury, driven mainly by liver synthesis under cytokine (especially IL-6) stimulation. CRP is used clinically as a routine marker of active inflammation because of how reliably and rapidly it rises.

## answer_e
Myoglobin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Myoglobin is a muscle oxygen-storage protein, released into plasma with muscle injury (rhabdomyolysis), not a liver-synthesised acute-phase protein.
