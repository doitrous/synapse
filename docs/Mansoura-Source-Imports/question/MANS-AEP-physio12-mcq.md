## title
Removal of noradrenaline at sympathetic nerve endings

## question
Which of the following is the most important mechanism for removing noradrenaline from the synaptic cleft?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Diffusion to surrounding tissues

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diffusion away from the nerve ending accounts for only a minor fraction of noradrenaline's removal, not the dominant route.

## answer_b
Excretion in urine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Renal excretion is not how noradrenaline is cleared from the synaptic cleft within the few seconds after release.

## answer_c
Extra-neuronal reuptake

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The dominant reuptake route is back into the releasing (neuronal) ending itself, not into extra-neuronal tissue.

## answer_d
Hydrolysis by pseudocholinesterase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pseudocholinesterase hydrolyses acetylcholine-like esters; noradrenaline is a catecholamine and is cleared by reuptake and by MAO/COMT, not by cholinesterase.

## answer_e
Neuronal reuptake

## explanation_e
Correct. 50 to 80% of released noradrenaline is taken back up, intact, into the adrenergic nerve ending that released it — the dominant clearance route, with diffusion and enzymatic breakdown (MAO, COMT) accounting for the rest.

## title
Adrenal medulla epinephrine and liver glycogen

## question
Increased release of epinephrine from the adrenal medulla during muscular exercise:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Stimulates glycogenolysis in liver

## explanation_a
Correct. Epinephrine raises cAMP via adenylyl cyclase, which activates protein kinase A and, through it, glycogen phosphorylase kinase — driving glycogen breakdown (glycogenolysis) in the liver to release glucose for exercising muscle.

## answer_b
Stimulates glycogenesis in liver

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Epinephrine's cAMP cascade inactivates glycogen synthase, opposing glycogenesis rather than stimulating it; glycogenesis is favoured by insulin, which lowers cAMP.

## answer_c
Causes vasoconstriction of arterioles all over the body

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Epinephrine acts on both alpha (vasoconstrictor) and beta-2 (vasodilator) receptors; skeletal-muscle and coronary vessels carry beta-2 receptors and dilate, so the effect is not uniform vasoconstriction everywhere.

## answer_d
Contracts ciliary muscle in the eye

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sympathetic stimulation relaxes the ciliary muscle (for far vision) and contracts the pupil's dilator muscle for mydriasis; it does not contract the ciliary muscle, which is a parasympathetic (near-vision) action.

## answer_e
Decrease heart rate

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Epinephrine acting on cardiac beta-1 receptors accelerates the heart, the opposite of this option.

## title
Location of beta-1 adrenergic receptors

## question
Which of the following is correct about beta-1 adrenergic receptors?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Their stimulation increases insulin secretion and liver glycogenolysis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Beta-1 receptors are concentrated in the heart and kidney, not in tissue that governs insulin secretion; that effect is not a beta-1 action.

## answer_b
They act by decreasing cAMP

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Beta-1 receptors, like beta-2, stimulate adenylyl cyclase and raise cAMP — the opposite of this option.

## answer_c
They act via increasing intracellular calcium ions

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Raising intracellular calcium is the alpha-1 second-messenger mechanism, not beta-1's.

## answer_d
They are located in heart and kidney

## explanation_d
Correct. Beta-1 receptors are mainly excitatory and are concentrated in the heart (where they accelerate rate and increase contractile force) and the kidney.

## answer_e
They are present in presynaptic membrane and act as auto receptors for regulation of NE release

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Presynaptic autoreceptors that regulate noradrenaline release exist for both alpha (decrease release) and beta (increase release) subtypes generally, but this is not the defining postsynaptic location fact for beta-1.

Receptor mediating adrenal medullary secretion

## question
Which autonomic receptors mediate secretion of epinephrine by the adrenal medulla?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cholinergic muscarinic receptors

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Muscarinic receptors sit on effector cells of parasympathetic and cholinergic-sympathetic postganglionic fibres, not on the adrenal medulla's preganglionic input.

## answer_b
Adrenergic alpha receptors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The adrenal medulla is itself the source of catecholamines and is driven by its cholinergic preganglionic input, not by adrenergic receptors on itself.

## answer_c
Cholinergic nicotinic receptors

## explanation_c
Correct. The adrenal medulla is a modified sympathetic ganglion, supplied by preganglionic cholinergic fibres acting on nicotinic receptors — the same receptor that transmits any ganglionic synapse.

## answer_d
Adrenergic beta 1 receptor

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Beta-1 receptors are postsynaptic effector receptors (e.g. in the heart), not the ganglionic-type receptor driving adrenal medullary secretion.

## answer_e
Adrenergic beta 2 receptor

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Beta-2 receptors are inhibitory effector-organ receptors, not the receptor that drives adrenal medullary secretion.

## title
Effect NOT produced by epinephrine

## question
Epinephrine does not produce which of the following effects?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Bronchoconstriction

## explanation_a
Correct — this is NOT an epinephrine effect. Epinephrine relaxes bronchial smooth muscle via beta-2 receptors, producing bronchodilatation, the opposite of bronchoconstriction.

## answer_b
Coronary vasodilatation

## explanation_b
Incorrect — epinephrine does produce this. Beta-2 stimulation dilates coronary vessels, so this is a genuine epinephrine effect and not the answer to "does not produce."

## answer_c
Glycogenolysis

## explanation_c
Incorrect — epinephrine does produce this. Its cAMP cascade drives hepatic glycogen breakdown, so this is a genuine effect.

## answer_d
Increased cardiac output

## explanation_d
Incorrect — epinephrine does produce this. Beta-1 stimulation raises heart rate and contractile force, increasing cardiac output, so this is a genuine effect.

## answer_e
Mydriasis

## explanation_e
Incorrect — epinephrine does produce this. Alpha-1 contraction of the dilator pupillae produces mydriasis, so this is a genuine effect.

Norepinephrine release and heart rate

## question
Release of norepinephrine from the sympathetic nerves of the heart will cause which of the following?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Increased heart rate

## explanation_a
Correct. Noradrenaline released by cardiac sympathetic fibres acts on beta-1 receptors, which are mainly excitatory and accelerate the heart.

## answer_b
Decreased heart rate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This is the vagal (parasympathetic, muscarinic) effect, the opposite of sympathetic noradrenergic action on the heart.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Coronary vasodilatation is a beta-2 action; the direct rate effect on the sinoatrial node in this question is the beta-1 acceleration, not this vascular effect.

Decreased force of cardiac contraction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Beta-1 stimulation increases, not decreases, the force of myocardial contraction.

## answer_e
Decreased excitability of the cardiac muscle

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Sympathetic noradrenergic stimulation increases cardiac excitability, not decreases it.
