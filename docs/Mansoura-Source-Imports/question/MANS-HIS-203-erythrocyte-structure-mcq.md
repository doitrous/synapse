## title
Mature erythrocyte life span

## question
Which of the following about mature erythrocytes is correct?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Their life span is about 4 months

## explanation_a
Correct. A mature erythrocyte's average circulating life span is about 120 days, which is roughly four months. It survives that long by relying on anaerobic glycolysis for energy, since it has no mitochondria, and by folding through capillaries narrower than its own diameter because of its flexible biconcave shape. Only once it becomes too fragile to survive that repeated mechanical stress is it removed, mainly by the spleen.

## answer_b
Have a diameter of about 12 picometer

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A picometre is a thousand times smaller than a micrometre; the erythrocyte's true diameter is about 7-7.5 micrometres, not picometres, so this option is off by three orders of magnitude as well as citing the wrong unit's typical value.

## answer_c
Have nuclei and cell organoids

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The defining feature of a mature erythrocyte is the opposite: it extrudes its nucleus and loses its other organelles (mitochondria, ribosomes) during maturation, becoming a nucleus-free, organelle-free cell built purely to carry haemoglobin.

## answer_d
Are rounded shaped cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The mature erythrocyte is specifically a biconcave disc, not simply rounded; the biconcavity is what gives it its large surface-area-to-volume ratio and its flexibility through narrow capillaries.

## answer_e
Have segmented nucleus

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A segmented nucleus describes the neutrophil, not the erythrocyte; the mature erythrocyte has no nucleus at all.

# Item

## title
RBC adaptability to function — the exception

## question
All of the following are adaptations of the RBC to its function EXCEPT:

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cell membrane is highly selective

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the exception. A highly selective membrane genuinely is one of the erythrocyte's adaptations, controlling what crosses into and out of the cell, so it belongs with the other true adaptations, not against them.

## answer_b
Absence of nucleus and organoids

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the exception. Losing the nucleus and organelles during maturation is a genuine adaptation: it frees up internal volume for haemoglobin and removes machinery a short-lived, function-specific cell no longer needs.

## answer_c
Biconcave surface of RBC

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the exception. The biconcave surface genuinely is an adaptation — it increases the surface-area-to-volume ratio for gas exchange and lets the cell fold through narrow capillaries — so it belongs with the true adaptations.

## answer_d
Cytoplasm contains large amount of granules

## explanation_d
Correct — this is the exception. The mature erythrocyte's cytoplasm contains no granules at all; it is a clear, homogeneous cytoplasm packed with haemoglobin, not granular contents. A cytoplasm full of granules describes cells such as the neutrophil or eosinophil, not the erythrocyte, which is exactly why this statement is the false one among the five.

## answer_e
Elasticity of cell membrane

## explanation_e
Incorrect as the exception. Membrane elasticity genuinely is an adaptation, letting the erythrocyte deform reversibly as it squeezes through capillaries narrower than its own resting diameter.

## title
Biconcave disc shape by cell type (past-exam block, دفعة 62)

## question
Which of the blood cells has the shape of a biconcave disc?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Lymphocytes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Lymphocytes are small, round, mostly nucleus-filled cells; they are not biconcave discs.

## answer_b
Platelets

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Platelets are oval, non-nucleated fragments, not biconcave discs; their shape is a separate distractor commonly paired with the erythrocyte's in this bank.

## answer_c
Red blood cells

## explanation_c
Correct. The red blood cell (erythrocyte) is the biconcave disc among the formed elements of blood. Its two concave faces increase its surface-area-to-volume ratio for gas exchange and let it deform to pass through capillaries narrower than its own diameter.

## answer_d
Neutrophils

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Neutrophils are round leukocytes with a multilobed nucleus; they are not shaped as discs.

## answer_e
Eosinophils

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Eosinophils are round leukocytes with a bilobed nucleus and prominent granules; they are not biconcave discs either.

## title
RBC life span (past-exam block, دفعة 63)

## question
Which of the following is the approximate life span of RBCs?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
4 weeks

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 4 weeks (about 28 days) is far shorter than the erythrocyte's true circulating life span.

## answer_b
5 days

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 5 days is close to a granulocyte's short circulating time, not the much longer-lived erythrocyte.

## answer_c
120 days

## explanation_c
Correct. The average circulating erythrocyte survives about 120 days before it is removed, mainly by the spleen, once accumulated mechanical stress makes it too fragile to keep deforming through the microcirculation. This roughly four-month life span is far longer than the platelet's 7-10 days or a granulocyte's few days in circulation.

## answer_d
1 year

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 1 year substantially overstates the erythrocyte's life span; no normal circulating blood cell in this bank has a lifespan that long.

## answer_e
20 days

## explanation_e
Incorrect. 20 days is well short of the true 120-day figure.
