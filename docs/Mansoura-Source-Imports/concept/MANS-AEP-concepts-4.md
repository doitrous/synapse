The three major salivary glands' ducts open at different sites — the parotid duct opens into the vestibule of the mouth opposite the upper second molar, while the submandibular and sublingual ducts open onto the floor of the mouth

## definition
The three paired major salivary glands drain through distinct ducts that open at different points inside the mouth. The parotid gland's duct (Stensen's duct) crosses the cheek and pierces buccinator to open into the vestibule of the mouth, the space between the cheek/lips and the teeth, typically opposite the upper second molar tooth. The submandibular gland's duct (Wharton's duct) and the sublingual gland's ducts both open onto the floor of the mouth proper, on either side of the lingual frenulum, rather than into the vestibule. A question naming "vestibule of the mouth" as a duct's termination is describing the parotid duct, while one naming the floor of the mouth or oral cavity proper is describing the submandibular or sublingual duct.

## explicit_objective
State that the parotid duct opens into the vestibule of the mouth while the submandibular and sublingual ducts open onto the floor of the mouth (oral cavity proper).

## pitfalls
Swapping the parotid duct's vestibule termination with the submandibular duct's floor-of-mouth termination, since both are "openings into the mouth" and only the specific site (vestibule vs floor) distinguishes them.

The large intestine's named parts occupy characteristic abdominal quadrants — the cecum and ascending colon on the lower-to-upper right, the transverse colon crossing above, the descending colon (beginning at the left colic/splenic flexure) down the upper-to-left side, and the sigmoid colon in the lower left

## definition
The large intestine begins at the cecum in the right iliac fossa (lower right of the abdomen), continues upward as the ascending colon, turns left at the right colic (hepatic) flexure into the transverse colon, turns down at the left colic (splenic) flexure — in the upper left part of the abdominal cavity — into the descending colon on the left side, and ends as the sigmoid colon in the lower left before the rectum. A tumour or cancer localised to the upper left abdominal cavity therefore points to the left colic flexure or the start of the descending colon, one confined to the left side generally points to the descending colon, and one in the lower right points to the cecum.

## explicit_objective
Place the cecum in the lower right, the left colic (splenic) flexure and descending colon on the upper-to-left side, and use a tumour's quadrant to identify the affected colon segment.

## pitfalls
Confusing the left colic (splenic) flexure, specifically at the upper left, with the descending colon's more general left-sided course, or misplacing the cecum outside the lower right iliac fossa.

Sacculations (haustra), produced by the three taeniae coli pulling the wall into pouches, are a gross feature of the large intestine and are absent from the small intestine

## definition
The large intestine's outer longitudinal muscle is gathered into three bands, the taeniae coli, which are shorter than the gut tube itself and so pucker the wall into a series of outpouchings called sacculations or haustra, visible along most of the colon. The small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum), the vermiform appendix and the rectum do not have taeniae coli and so lack this sacculated appearance, giving haustra diagnostic value for telling large from small bowel on imaging or at operation.

## explicit_objective
Identify sacculations (haustra) as a feature of the large intestine (colon), produced by the taeniae coli, and absent from the small intestine.

## pitfalls
Attributing sacculations to a small-intestine segment (jejunum, ileum) or to the appendix or rectum, structures that lack taeniae coli.

## definition
Chyme leaves the stomach into the duodenum, the fixed, largely retroperitoneal first part of the small intestine, then passes into the freely mobile jejunum and finally the ileum before reaching the cecum. Between the jejunum and ileum, the jejunum forms the proximal roughly 2/5 of the mobile small bowel and the ileum the distal roughly 3/5, making the ileum both the more distal and the longer of the two. A question asking for the "longest part of the small intestine" or the segment affected by a lesion in its "distal part" is naming the ileum.

## explicit_objective
State that the ileum is both the most distal and the longest of the small intestine's mobile segments (jejunum and ileum), each about 2/5 and 3/5 of their combined length respectively.

## pitfalls
Naming the jejunum instead of the ileum as the longest or most distal segment, or forgetting that the duodenum is the small intestine's fixed, most proximal part.

## definition
Bile is produced continuously by hepatocytes in the liver, then concentrated and stored in the gallbladder between meals. The common bile duct carries bile from the liver and gallbladder down to join the main pancreatic duct, and the combined hepatopancreatic duct (of Vater) opens into the second part of the duodenum at the major duodenal papilla. Because the duodenum, not the stomach, jejunum or ileum, is where the bile duct terminates, it is the duodenum that receives both bile and pancreatic secretions together.

## pitfalls

## definition
The pharynx is a muscular tube extending from the base of the skull to the level of the cricoid cartilage (C6), divided into nasopharynx, oropharynx and laryngopharynx. Because both the respiratory and digestive routes pass through it, air travelling from the nose or mouth to the larynx and trachea, and food or drink travelling from the mouth to the esophagus, cross paths in the pharynx, making it the one structure that serves as a common conduit for both food and air, unlike the larynx, trachea, esophagus or bronchi, each dedicated to only one of the two functions.

State that the pharynx is the shared pathway for both food and air.

## pitfalls
Naming the larynx, trachea, esophagus or bronchi instead of the pharynx as the shared food-and-air pathway, when in fact each of those is dedicated to only one function.

Each kidney contains roughly a million nephrons, the basic structural and functional units responsible for forming urine. ## pitfalls
Naming only a part of the nephron (such as the proximal convoluted tubule) or an unrelated structure (the renal pelvis, the distal convoluted tubule alone) instead of the whole nephron as the kidney's functional unit.

## definition
The female urethra is a short, roughly 4 cm muscular tube running from the internal urethral orifice at the bladder neck, through the pelvic floor, to open at the external urethral orifice in the vestibule of the vagina — the region between the labia minora — positioned anterior to (in front of) the vaginal opening. Its short length, in contrast to the much longer male urethra, is clinically relevant as a factor in women's greater susceptibility to ascending urinary tract infection.

## explicit_objective
State that the female urethra is approximately 4 cm long and opens at the vestibule anterior to the vaginal opening.

## pitfalls
Selecting a longer distractor length for the female urethra, or placing its opening posterior to (behind) the vagina or within the vagina itself instead of anterior to it in the vestibule.
