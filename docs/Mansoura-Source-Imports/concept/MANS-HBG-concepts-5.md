# Item

## label
The hair bulb is the expanded, bulbous end of the hair follicle at its base, housing the hair papilla and the surrounding hair matrix

## id
CON-DER-E847FB7EA4367D

## canonical_key
hair.bulb-is-expanded-base-of-follicle

## aliases
Hair follicle bulb
Base of the hair follicle
Expanded end of the hair

## arabic_label
بصيلة الشعرة هي النهاية المنتفخة المتوسّعة لجريب الشعر عند قاعدته، وتحوي الحليمة الشعرية ومصفوفة الشعرة المحيطة بها

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
The hair bulb is the swollen, onion-shaped expansion at the very bottom of a growing (anagen) hair follicle. It caps the dermal hair papilla, a knot of vascular connective tissue that nourishes the follicle, and its epithelial walls are the hair matrix — the actively mitotic cell population that proliferates and differentiates to build the hair shaft and the inner root sheath as they push upward. The bulb is therefore a structural landmark (the expanded base), distinct from the hair matrix, which is the specific epithelial cell layer lining it.

## explicit_objective
Identify the hair bulb as the expanded, bulbous terminal end of the hair follicle at its base, as distinct from the hair root, shaft, or the sheaths that surround the follicle above it.

## pitfalls
Confusing the hair bulb (the expanded structural end of the follicle) with the hair matrix (the specific proliferating epithelial cells lining the bulb) or the hair papilla (the dermal connective-tissue core the bulb caps) — related but distinct terms for adjacent structures at the follicle's base.

## label
The mesencephalic nucleus of the trigeminal nerve is the one site where unipolar primary sensory neuron cell bodies sit inside the CNS rather than in a peripheral ganglion

## id
CON-NEU-08C823857B4F1A

## canonical_key
neuron.mesencephalic-trigeminal-nucleus-unipolar-cns-sensory

## aliases
Mesencephalic nucleus of CN V

## arabic_label
النواة المتوسطة الدماغية للعصب مثلث التوائم هي الموضع الوحيد الذي تقع فيه أجسام الخلايا العصبية الحسية الأولية أحادية القطب داخل الجهاز العصبي المركزي بدلاً من عقدة عصبية محيطية

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
Primary sensory (afferent) neuron cell bodies almost always sit outside the central nervous system, in a peripheral sensory ganglion (such as the dorsal root or trigeminal ganglion), as pseudounipolar cells. The mesencephalic nucleus of the trigeminal nerve is the classic exception taught in histology: it carries proprioceptive input from the muscles of mastication and the temporomandibular joint, and its unipolar primary sensory cell bodies lie inside the brainstem (midbrain) itself, making it the only nucleus of unipolar neurons found within the CNS.

## explicit_objective
Identify the mesencephalic nucleus of the trigeminal nerve as containing unipolar neurons, the one exception to primary sensory neuron cell bodies normally residing in a peripheral ganglion.

## pitfalls
Assuming every primary sensory neuron cell body sits in a peripheral ganglion (pseudounipolar/bipolar) and never inside the CNS itself — the mesencephalic trigeminal nucleus is the specific, commonly tested exception, and its cells are described as unipolar.

## label
The synaptic cleft at a chemical synapse measures about 20-30 nm across

## id
CON-NEU-4F547A706D46DF

## canonical_key
synapse.chemical.cleft-width-20-30nm

## aliases
Synaptic cleft width
Width of the synaptic gap

## arabic_label
يبلغ عرض الشق المشبكي في المشبك الكيميائي حوالي 20-30 نانومتر

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
At a chemical synapse, the presynaptic and postsynaptic membranes are separated by a narrow extracellular gap, the synaptic cleft, of roughly 20-30 nm. This gap is wide enough to prevent direct cytoplasmic continuity between the two neurons (unlike the ~2-4 nm gap-junction channel of an electrical synapse) but narrow enough for neurotransmitter released from presynaptic vesicles to diffuse across quickly and bind postsynaptic receptors.

## explicit_objective
State that the synaptic cleft of a chemical synapse is approximately 20-30 nm wide.

## pitfalls
Confusing the chemical synapse's wider cleft (~20-30 nm) with the much narrower gap-junction channel of an electrical synapse (~2-4 nm), or picking a value scaled to a different structure (such as the unit membrane's own ~7.5-10 nm thickness) instead of the cleft itself.

# Item

## label
A chemical synapse is defined by a true synaptic cleft and neurotransmitter release, not by the direct gap-junction coupling of an electrical synapse

## id
CON-NEU-F57B24BA6B7F42

## canonical_key
synapse.chemical-vs-electrical.gap-junctions-are-electrical-not-chemical

## aliases
Chemical synapse features
Chemical vs electrical synapse
Not true about chemical synapse

## arabic_label
يتميز المشبك الكيميائي بوجود شق مشبكي حقيقي وإطلاق ناقلات عصبية، وليس بالاتصال المباشر عبر الوصلات الفجوية الخاص بالمشبك الكهربائي

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
Chemical synapses are the most common type of synapse in the nervous system. They possess a true synaptic cleft separating the pre- and postsynaptic membranes, and they signal by releasing a neurotransmitter (such as acetylcholine) from presynaptic vesicles, which diffuses across the cleft and binds postsynaptic receptors — a process that is inherently slower than the direct, near-instantaneous ionic coupling of an electrical synapse. Electrical synapses, by contrast, are the type connected by gap junctions, which allow ions to pass directly between neurons without any neurotransmitter step; a chemical synapse is specifically NOT gap-junction-connected.

## explicit_objective
Distinguish chemical synapses (cleft, neurotransmitter release, slower conduction, most common type) from electrical synapses (gap-junction coupling, no cleft, faster conduction), and identify gap-junction connection as the one statement that is false of a chemical synapse.

## pitfalls
Attributing gap-junction connection to the chemical synapse — gap junctions are the defining feature of the electrical synapse instead, making "connected by gap junctions" the false statement in a chemical-synapse list.

## label
Axo-dendritic is the commonest synapse type when synapses are classified by structure

## id
CON-NEU-EA55761DE37B21

## canonical_key
synapse.structural-classification.axo-dendritic-most-common

## aliases
Commonest synapse type
Axo-dendritic synapse
Synapse classification by structure

## arabic_label
المشبك المحوري التغصني هو أكثر أنواع المشابك شيوعاً عند تصنيفها حسب البنية

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
Synapses can be classified structurally by which parts of the two neurons meet: axon-to-dendrite (axo-dendritic), axon-to-cell-body (axo-somatic), axon-to-axon (axo-axonic), or dendrite-to-dendrite (dendro-dendritic). Of these, the axo-dendritic synapse — the presynaptic axon terminal contacting a postsynaptic dendrite, often on a dendritic spine — is the most common arrangement in the nervous system, reflecting how heavily dendrites are used to receive convergent synaptic input.

## explicit_objective
Identify the axo-dendritic synapse as the commonest synapse type when synapses are classified structurally by which neuronal parts meet.

## pitfalls
Assuming axo-somatic (axon-to-cell-body) is the commonest arrangement because it sounds like the most "direct" contact — dendrites, not the cell body, receive the bulk of synaptic input, making axo-dendritic the more frequent type.

## label
The clear layer (stratum lucidum) of thick skin is the epidermal layer whose cells contain eleidin granules

## id
CON-DER-35201AA3853BBD

## canonical_key
epidermis.clear-layer.contains-eleidin-granules

## aliases
Stratum lucidum
Clear layer of the epidermis

## arabic_label
الطبقة الشفافة (المتحدة) من البشرة هي الطبقة التي تحتوي خلاياها على حبيبات الإيليدين

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
The clear (lucid) layer, stratum lucidum, is a thin, translucent epidermal layer found only in thick (hairless, glabrous) skin such as the palms and soles, sitting between the granular and horny layers. Its flattened cells contain eleidin, a refractile, keratohyalin-derived precursor substance formed as keratohyalin granules break down one layer below; eleidin is the identifying content of this specific layer, distinguishing it from prekeratin in the basal/spinous layers, keratohyalin in the granular layer, and soft keratin in the horny layer above.

## explicit_objective
Identify the clear layer (stratum lucidum) as the epidermal layer, present only in thick skin, whose cells contain eleidin granules.

## pitfalls
Confusing eleidin (clear layer) with keratohyalin (granular layer, one layer below) or with prekeratin/tonofilaments (basal and spinous layers, the deeper living layers) — each is the identifying granule/filament content of a different epidermal layer in sequence.

## label
The basal layer of the epidermis is where keratinocytes first contain prekeratin (tonofilament) intermediate filaments

## id
CON-DER-F8C11EFB3C6DB6

## canonical_key
epidermis.basal-layer.contains-prekeratin-filaments

## aliases
Prekeratin filaments
Tonofilaments
Stratum basale keratin precursor

## arabic_label
الطبقة القاعدية من البشرة هي أول طبقة تحتوي خلاياها الكيراتينية على خييطات ما قبل الكيراتين

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
Prekeratin (tonofilament) intermediate filaments are the keratin precursor material of the deeper, still-living, still-dividing epidermal layers — the basal (germinative) layer and the spinous (prickle-cell) layer above it — before the cell undergoes full keratinisation. The basal layer, the single row of cuboidal-to-columnar mitotically active stem cells resting on the basement membrane, is where these tonofilaments first appear as the keratinocyte's cytoskeletal keratin content, ahead of the keratohyalin granules of the granular layer and the eleidin of the clear layer that mark the later steps of the same keratinisation programme.

## explicit_objective
Identify the basal layer of the epidermis as the layer whose keratinocytes contain prekeratin (tonofilament) intermediate filaments, the earliest step of the keratinisation sequence.

## pitfalls
Placing prekeratin filaments in a later layer of the keratinisation sequence — the tonofilaments are laid down early, in the basal (and spinous) layer, before keratohyalin (granular layer) and eleidin (clear layer) mark the subsequent stages toward full keratinisation.

The (eccrine) sweat gland is structurally a simple coiled tubular gland

## id

## definition
Glands are classified structurally by the shape of their secretory portion and duct: simple (unbranched duct) versus compound (branched duct), and tubular versus alveolar (acinar) versus tubulo-alveolar secretory units. The (eccrine) sweat gland is a simple coiled tubular gland — a single, unbranched duct connects to a long, coiled secretory tubule deep in the dermis, with no branching of either the duct or the secretory portion — distinguishing it from alveolar, branched-alveolar, or compound glands elsewhere in the skin and body.

## explicit_objective
Classify the (eccrine) sweat gland as a simple coiled tubular gland within the standard simple/compound, tubular/alveolar gland classification scheme.

## pitfalls
Mislabelling the sweat gland's shape by branching (calling it "compound" or "branched") or by secretory-unit shape (calling it "alveolar") — both its duct and its coiled secretory portion are unbranched, and the secretory unit is a coiled tube, not an acinus.

## label
The outer root sheath of the hair follicle is continuous with, and histologically identical to, the Malpighian layer of the epidermis

## id
CON-DER-F7AD04B1A5AF5F

## canonical_key
hair-follicle.outer-root-sheath-identical-to-epidermal-malpighian-layer

## aliases
Outer root sheath
Malpighian layer continuity

## arabic_label
الغمد الجذري الخارجي لجريب الشعر يتصل ببشرة الجلد ويطابقها نسيجياً في طبقتها المالبيغية

## arabic_aliases
[clear]

## definition
The hair follicle is an epidermal downgrowth into the dermis, and its outer root sheath is directly continuous, at the follicle's mouth, with the epidermis proper. Histologically the outer root sheath is identical to the Malpighian layer of the epidermis (the living basal and spinous layers together, the epidermis's proliferative and differentiating compartment) — it thins as it descends but keeps the same layered structure — which is why it, rather than the inner root sheath, hair root, bulb, or papilla, is described as identical to the epidermal Malpighian layer.

## explicit_objective
Identify the outer root sheath of the hair follicle as the structure that is continuous with, and histologically identical to, the Malpighian (basal + spinous) layer of the epidermis.

## pitfalls
Attributing epidermal-Malpighian-layer identity to the inner root sheath, hair bulb, or hair papilla instead of the outer root sheath — only the outer root sheath is the direct downward continuation of the epidermis's own living layers; the inner root sheath and hair itself are products built by the matrix, not epidermal continuations.

## definition
The arrector pili is a small, obliquely-running smooth muscle bundle running from the papillary dermis to the hair follicle's outer root sheath, below the sebaceous gland. Its contraction (triggered by sympathetic stimulation, e.g. cold or fright) pulls the follicle into a more vertical position and dimples the skin surface immediately above it, producing the visible bumps of goose skin (cutis anserina); the same contraction also compresses the adjacent sebaceous gland, aiding sebum release.

## explicit_objective
Identify contraction of the arrector pili muscle as the cause of goose skin, by pulling the hair follicle upright and dimpling the overlying skin.

## pitfalls
Attributing goose skin to a gland (sweat or sebaceous) or duct instead of the arrector pili muscle — glands secrete but do not move the follicle or dimple the skin surface; only the arrector pili's smooth-muscle contraction produces the visible goose-skin effect.
