## label
Primary hyperaldosteronism classically presents with hypertension and hypokalemia

## canonical_key
endo.primary-hyperaldosteronism.hypertension-hypokalemia

## definition
A patient with high circulating aldosterone and severe hypertension (e.g. 175/110 mmHg) is presenting with the classic biochemical signature of primary hyperaldosteronism: aldosterone acts on the principal cells of the renal collecting duct to increase Na+ reabsorption (driving the hypertension via volume expansion) in exchange for increased K+ and H+ secretion, producing hypokalemia (and typically a mild metabolic alkalosis, not acidosis, from the associated H+ loss). Cardiac output is not decreased in this state — the volume expansion, if anything, tends to raise it — and hypercalcemia is not a feature of aldosterone excess.

## explicit_objective
State that hypokalemia, from aldosterone-driven renal K+ secretion, is the expected finding in a hypertensive patient with high circulating aldosterone, distinguishing it from decreased cardiac output, acidosis and hypercalcemia.

## label
Insulin deficiency in type I diabetes mellitus inhibits glycogenesis

## canonical_key
endo.diabetes-mellitus-type-1.glycogenesis-inhibited

## definition
Insulin is the principal anabolic hormone driving glycogenesis (glycogen synthesis) in liver and muscle. In type I diabetes mellitus, absolute insulin deficiency removes this drive, so glycogenesis is inhibited — the one process among ketogenesis, gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis that insulin deficiency shuts down rather than enhances. The other three are all disinhibited/enhanced in insulin deficiency: unopposed glucagon and low insulin promote glycogenolysis (glycogen breakdown) and gluconeogenesis (raising blood glucose further), while the resulting reliance on fatty-acid oxidation for fuel drives ketogenesis (producing the ketoacidosis characteristic of untreated type I diabetes).

## explicit_objective
Identify glycogenesis as the process inhibited (not enhanced) by insulin deficiency in type I diabetes mellitus, distinguishing it from ketogenesis, gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis, which are all enhanced instead.

## label
The bicarbonate buffer system's power comes from its components being independently regulated

## canonical_key
renal.acid-base.bicarbonate-buffer-independently-regulated-open-system

## definition
The bicarbonate/carbonic-acid buffer pair (HCO3-/H2CO3) is physiologically the most important extracellular buffer not because its pKa (about 6.1) sits close to blood pH (7.4) — it does not — but because it is an "open system": the concentration of each component can be independently regulated by a different organ, CO2 (via H2CO3) by the lungs through ventilation, and HCO3- by the kidneys through tubular reabsorption/regeneration. This independent, organ-level regulation lets the buffer pair be actively adjusted rather than merely titrated, and the system responds to an acute pH change within minutes (respiratory) to hours-to-days (renal) rather than requiring 1-2 days uniformly, and it does not release "more" protons as pH drops in the way the option implies — buffering consumes free H+ rather than releasing it further.

## explicit_objective
State that the bicarbonate buffer system's defining physiological feature is that its two components (CO2/lungs, HCO3-/kidneys) can be independently regulated by different organs, distinguishing this "open system" property from its pKa or its response time.

This source's key attributes ADH-deficiency water loss to the distal convoluted tubule

## canonical_key
renal.adh-deficiency.distal-convoluted-tubule-water-loss

## definition
Antidiuretic hormone (ADH/vasopressin) acts on V2 receptors of the distal tubule and collecting duct principal cells to insert aquaporin-2 water channels, allowing water reabsorption along the medullary osmotic gradient; without ADH, this segment stays relatively water-impermeable and the filtrate reaching it is not concentrated, producing the polyuria of diabetes insipidus. Most physiology texts describe the ADH-sensitive segment as the late distal tubule together with the collecting duct as one continuous functional unit; this concept records the source's own distal-tubule-specific phrasing as its teaching point rather than silently substituting the more commonly cited "collecting duct" answer.

## label
Alpha-1 adrenergic receptors signal through calcium/phosphatidylinositol, not cAMP

## canonical_key
endo.alpha1-adrenergic-receptor.calcium-phosphatidylinositol-second-messenger

## definition
Alpha-1 (a1) adrenergic receptors are Gq-protein-coupled: catecholamine binding activates phospholipase C, generating inositol trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG) from membrane phosphatidylinositol, which in turn raises intracellular calcium — making calcium/phosphatidylinositol turnover the a1 receptor's intracellular messenger system, not cAMP. This is distinct from beta-adrenergic receptors (Gs-coupled, signal via increased cAMP through adenylate cyclase) and alpha-2 receptors (Gi-coupled, signal via decreased cAMP); cGMP and tyrosine kinase cascades are not the a1 receptor's signalling route at all.

## explicit_objective
Identify calcium/phosphatidylinositol turnover as the intracellular messenger system of alpha-1 adrenergic receptors, distinguishing it from the cAMP-based signalling of beta (increased) and alpha-2 (decreased) adrenergic receptors.

## label
The vasa recta maintain, rather than create, the medullary interstitial osmotic gradient

## canonical_key
renal.vasa-recta.maintains-not-creates-medullary-gradient

## definition
The vasa recta are the hairpin-shaped peritubular capillaries running alongside the loop of Henle into the renal medulla. Through countercurrent exchange (passive equilibration with the surrounding hypertonic interstitium along their descending and ascending limbs), the vasa recta preserve the steep corticomedullary osmotic gradient that the loop of Henle's countercurrent multiplier mechanism establishes — they maintain the gradient rather than creating it themselves, and rather than dissipating or distributing it evenly across the medulla. Because they are relatively slow, low-flow vessels with freely permeable walls, they remove reabsorbed water and solute from the medulla without washing out the gradient the loop of Henle built.

## explicit_objective
State that the vasa recta's countercurrent-exchange function is to maintain the medullary interstitial osmotic gradient established by the loop of Henle, not to create or actively distribute it.

Diabetic ketoacidosis with low pH-normal-side, low HCO3 and low PaCO2 is compensated metabolic acidosis

## canonical_key
renal.acid-base.dka-compensated-metabolic-acidosis

## definition
A diabetic patient in ketoacidosis with blood gas values pH 7.35, PaCO2 30 mmHg and HCO3- 18 mEq/L is showing a low bicarbonate (the primary disturbance: metabolic acidosis, from accumulating ketoacid anions) alongside a pH that remains within the low-normal range rather than frankly acidotic, and a low PaCO2 reflecting respiratory compensation (hyperventilation/Kussmaul breathing, matching the vignette's "deep and rapid breathing") that has partially normalised the pH. This combination — primary metabolic disturbance (low HCO3-) with a directionally-appropriate respiratory response (low PaCO2) and a pH pulled back toward but not fully into the normal range — is the definition of compensated metabolic acidosis, distinct from an uncompensated disturbance (pH would remain frankly abnormal) or a primary respiratory disturbance (which would show the opposite direction of PaCO2 change relative to the primary problem).

## explicit_objective
Classify a blood gas showing low HCO3-, low-normal pH and low PaCO2 (with clinical hyperventilation) as compensated metabolic acidosis, distinguishing it from uncompensated metabolic alkalosis, compensated respiratory alkalosis, and uncompensated respiratory acidosis.
