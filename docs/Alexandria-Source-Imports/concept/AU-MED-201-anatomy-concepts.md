## label
The perineal body is the point of convergence for the bulbospongiosus and superficial transverse perineal muscles

## canonical_key
gyn.perineal-body.bulbospongiosus-superficial-transverse-perineal-convergence

## definition
The perineal body (central tendon of the perineum) is a fibromuscular node midway between the anus and the vaginal/vulval introitus into which several perineal muscles converge and interlace: the bulbospongiosus and superficial transverse perineal muscles from the superficial perineal pouch, together with the external anal sphincter and part of pubococcygeus (levator ani). Because these superficial muscles physically insert into it, a laceration or surgical incision (episiotomy) through the perineal body during labor risks their function specifically — not the deep transverse perineal muscle or the external urethral (sphincter urethrae) muscle, which sit in the deep perineal pouch above the perineal membrane and are not perineal-body insertions, and not ischiocavernosus, which attaches instead to the ischiopubic ramus and crus of the clitoris/penis.

## explicit_objective
Identify the bulbospongiosus and superficial transverse perineal muscles as the two muscles whose function is put at risk by perineal-body damage, distinguishing them from the deep-pouch muscles (deep transverse perineal, sphincter urethrae) and from ischiocavernosus.

## label
The membranous urethra is the narrowest and least dilatable part of the male urethra

## canonical_key
renal.male-urethra.membranous-part-narrowest-least-dilatable

## definition
The male urethra is conventionally divided into four parts — prostatic, membranous, bulbar and penile (spongy) — and of these the membranous urethra, the short segment that passes through the deep perineal pouch surrounded by the external urethral sphincter (sphincter urethrae), is the narrowest and least dilatable part of the whole urethra. This is distinct from the prostatic urethra (the widest, most dilatable part, surrounded by prostatic tissue) and from the penile urethra (dilatable along most of its length, with the external meatus itself being the narrowest point of the penile portion specifically).

## explicit_objective
Identify the membranous urethra as the narrowest and least dilatable part of the male urethra, distinguishing it from the wider, more dilatable prostatic and penile parts.

## label
The ureter is the structure most at risk of accidental ligation during hysterectomy

## canonical_key
gyn.hysterectomy.ureter-accidental-ligation-risk

## definition
The ureter runs retroperitoneally along the pelvic sidewall and, close to the cervix, passes directly beneath the uterine artery near the lateral fornix of the vagina ("water under the bridge") before continuing to the bladder. During hysterectomy, when the uterine artery is clamped and ligated at the level of the internal os, the ureter's proximity to this vessel — roughly 1-2 cm lateral to the cervix — makes it the structure most at risk of accidental ligation or transection, more so than the ovarian vessels (which run higher, in the infundibulopelvic ligament), or the external/internal iliac arteries (which lie further lateral, outside the standard operative field for uterine vessel ligation).

## explicit_objective
Identify the ureter, by virtue of passing beneath the uterine artery near the cervix, as the structure most at risk of accidental ligation during hysterectomy.

## label
The pudendal nerve arises from the anterior rami of S2, S3 and S4

## canonical_key
gyn.pudendal-nerve.root-value-s2-s3-s4

## definition
The pudendal nerve, the principal somatic nerve of the perineum, arises from the anterior (ventral) rami of the second, third and fourth sacral spinal nerves (S2, S3, S4). It leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, hooks around the ischial spine/sacrospinous ligament, and re-enters through the lesser sciatic foramen into the pudendal (Alcock's) canal to supply the external anal sphincter, the muscles and skin of the perineum, and the external urethral sphincter — the basis of the pudendal nerve block used to anaesthetise the perineum for episiotomy and instrumental delivery.

## explicit_objective
State that the pudendal nerve's root value is S2, S3, S4, distinguishing it from adjacent but incorrect sacral root combinations.

## label
The liver is the anterior relation of the right suprarenal gland

## canonical_key
endo.right-suprarenal-gland.anterior-relation-liver

## definition
The right suprarenal (adrenal) gland is a pyramidal structure capping the upper pole of the right kidney, wedged between the liver and the diaphragm posteriorly and medial to the inferior vena cava. Its anterior surface relates directly to the bare area of the liver (with the peritoneum of the lesser sac separating a small superomedial part from the IVC), distinguishing it from the left suprarenal gland, whose anterior relations instead include the stomach, the pancreas (tail/body) and the splenic vessels — the pancreas and stomach are left-sided anterior relations, not right-sided ones.

## explicit_objective
State that the liver (bare area) is the anterior relation of the right suprarenal gland, distinguishing it from the stomach and pancreas, which relate anteriorly to the left suprarenal gland instead.
