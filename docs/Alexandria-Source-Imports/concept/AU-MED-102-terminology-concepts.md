## label
A medical term is built from a word root plus an optional prefix and suffix, joined by a combining vowel when needed

## canonical_key
medterm.structure.prefix-root-vowel-suffix

## definition
Every medical word is assembled from a small set of parts: a word root (the core meaning), an optional prefix in front and suffix behind, and a combining vowel — usually "o" — inserted only when it is needed to join two consonants smoothly. "Cardiomegaly" is the root cardi(o) joined by its combining vowel to the word "megaly" (enlargement); "cardiac" needs no combining vowel because the suffix -ac already begins with a vowel.

## explicit_objective
Break an unfamiliar medical word into its root, prefix, suffix and combining vowel, and state what each part contributes to the meaning.

## label
Latin and Greek medical nouns pluralise by changing their ending, not by adding an English "-s"

## canonical_key
medterm.pluralisation.latin-greek-endings

## definition
A medical noun borrowed from Latin or Greek keeps that language's own plural ending: -x becomes -ces (thorax → thoraces), -is becomes -es (testis → testes), -um becomes -a (bacterium → bacteria), -a becomes -ae (vertebra → vertebrae), and -on becomes -a (ganglion → ganglia). Adding an English "-s" to the singular is wrong for every one of these patterns.

## explicit_objective
Produce the correct plural of a Latin- or Greek-derived medical noun from its singular ending, and recognise the wrong form when it is offered as a distractor.

## label
Gravida counts pregnancies and para counts births reaching viability, and the two numbers can differ

## canonical_key
obstetric.history.gravida-para-count

## definition
Gravida is the total number of times a woman has been pregnant, counting the current pregnancy and any that ended in miscarriage or termination. Para is the number of those pregnancies that reached a viable gestation, regardless of whether the child was born alive. A woman with two children after four pregnancies is charted "gravida 4, para 2" — the two numbers are not interchangeable and a chart with them reversed misreads her obstetric history.

## explicit_objective
Given a woman's pregnancy and birth history, state her correct gravida and para, and given a gravida/para notation, state what it means.

## label
A medical term is either descriptive of what it names or an eponym naming a person, and both systems are in active use

## canonical_key
medterm.naming.descriptive-vs-eponymous

## definition
Most medical terms are descriptive: they are built from roots, prefixes and suffixes that state what the thing is or does, so "cardiomegaly" literally says "enlarged heart". Some terms are eponyms instead, naming the person credited with first describing the structure or condition, such as the "fallopian tube" (named for Gabriele Falloppio) — an eponym carries no internal clue to its meaning and must simply be learned.

## explicit_objective
Classify a given medical term as descriptive or eponymous, and explain why an eponym cannot be decoded the way a descriptive term can.
