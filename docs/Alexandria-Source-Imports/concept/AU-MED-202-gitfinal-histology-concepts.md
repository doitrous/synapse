## label
Hepatocytes are polyhedral cells

## canonical_key
hepatocyte.shape.polyhedral

## definition
Hepatocytes, the parenchymal cells that make up roughly 80% of liver volume, are polyhedral (many-sided) cells arranged in one-cell-thick plates radiating from the central vein toward the portal tracts. Their polyhedral shape gives each hepatocyte multiple contact surfaces: a sinusoidal (basal) surface facing the space of Disse for exchange with blood, and lateral surfaces forming bile canaliculi with neighbouring hepatocytes.

## explicit_objective
Identify the polyhedral shape of hepatocytes, distinguishing it from the columnar, cuboidal and squamous shapes used elsewhere in the GI epithelium.

The centre of the liver acinus is the vascular core

## canonical_key
liver.acinus.centre-vascular-core

## definition
The liver acinus (Rappaport's functional unit) is centred not on a central vein but on the small vascular core formed by terminal branches of the hepatic artery, portal vein and bile duct as they enter the parenchyma from the portal tract; two adjacent classic lobules' central veins mark the acinus's periphery. This vascular-core orientation is why the acinus, rather than the classic hexagonal lobule, better predicts the zonal pattern of hepatocyte injury seen with ischaemia or toxins.

The small intestine's absorptive surface is amplified in three tiers: plicae circulares (valves of Kerckring) increase it about 3-fold, villi about 10-fold, and microvilli (the brush border) about 20-fold, multiplying together to a combined amplification of roughly 400-600 fold over a flat tube of the same length and diameter. This tiered amplification is what lets the several-metre small intestine achieve the absorptive area of a much larger flat surface.

## explicit_objective
State that the small intestine's combined luminal-surface modifications (plicae, villi, microvilli) amplify absorptive surface area by roughly 400-600 fold.

## definition
Peyer's patches, lymphoid follicles concentrated in the ileal submucosa/lamina propria, are the main site of mucosal B-cell activation for the gut. Their germinal-centre B cells differentiate into IgA-secreting plasma cells, and the secreted dimeric IgA is transcytosed across the overlying epithelium into the lumen, where it neutralises luminal antigens without triggering inflammation.
