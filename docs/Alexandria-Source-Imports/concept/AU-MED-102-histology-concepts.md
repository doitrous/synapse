## label
Cardiolipin sits in the mitochondrial inner membrane, not the outer one

## canonical_key
mitochondria.cardiolipin.inner-membrane-location

## aliases
Cardiolipin location
Diphosphatidylglycerol

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Cardiolipin is a phospholipid unique to mitochondria. It is concentrated in the inner mitochondrial membrane, where it stabilises the respiratory-chain protein complexes embedded in that membrane, rather than in the smooth outer membrane.

## explicit_objective
State which mitochondrial membrane carries cardiolipin, and distinguish it from the outer membrane's porins.

## pitfalls
Placing cardiolipin in the outer membrane because 'outer' sounds like the default location for a membrane lipid. Cardiolipin is an inner-membrane marker precisely because the inner membrane, not the outer one, houses the electron-transport chain it stabilises.

## definition
Autophagy is the regulated, lysosome-dependent process by which a cell encloses its own worn-out or malfunctioning organelles in a membrane-bound vacuole and digests them, recycling their components rather than letting damaged organelles accumulate.

## explicit_objective
Distinguish autophagy (the cell degrading its own organelles) from phagocytosis and heterophagy (the cell degrading material taken in from outside).

## pitfalls
Confusing autophagy with phagocytosis. Phagocytosis brings in material from outside the cell; autophagy is the cell disposing of its own internal, worn-out components — the material never crossed the plasma membrane from outside.

## label
The nucleosome's central core is eight histone molecules, not the DNA itself

## canonical_key
nucleus.nucleosome.eight-histone-core

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A nucleosome is the basic packaging unit of chromatin: an octamer of eight histone proteins (two each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4) forms the central core, and roughly two turns of DNA wind around that core, with linker DNA and histone H1 between nucleosomes.

## explicit_objective
Name what forms the nucleosome's central core, and distinguish the core histones from the DNA wound around them and the linker DNA between nucleosomes.

## pitfalls
Calling the DNA itself the nucleosome's 'core'. The DNA wraps around the core; the core is the eight-histone octamer. A student who reverses this cannot then correctly place H1 or the linker DNA either.

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The restriction checkpoint (the G1 checkpoint) is the point late in G1 at which the cell checks its size, its nutrient supply and for DNA damage before committing to complete the cycle; once passed, the cell is committed to divide even without further external growth signals.

## explicit_objective
Place the restriction checkpoint in G1, and distinguish it from the other cell-cycle checkpoints (G2/M and the spindle-assembly checkpoint).

## pitfalls
Placing the restriction checkpoint in S phase or at the G2/M boundary. It is specifically the G1 checkpoint — the 'go/no-go' decision made before DNA synthesis begins, not the checks made afterwards.

## definition
Mitosis is divided into stages each defined by one specific, observable event: prophase by chromosome condensation and nuclear-envelope breakdown, metaphase by alignment on the equatorial plate, anaphase by centromere splitting and sister-chromatid separation, and telophase by chromosome decondensation, nuclear-envelope reformation and cytokinesis.

## explicit_objective
Given a described or diagrammed event (e.g. nuclear-envelope breakdown, centromere division, maximal chromosome condensation), name the mitotic stage it identifies.

## pitfalls
Treating 'condensation' and 'maximal condensation' as belonging to the same stage as 'nuclear envelope breakdown'. They are sequential, distinguishable events within prophase/prometaphase, and an exam diagram question tests telling them apart, not lumping them together.

Crossing-over between homologous chromosomes occurs in prophase I of meiosis

cell.division.meiosis-crossing-over-in-prophase-1

## aliases
Crossing over
Chiasma formation
Genetic recombination

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Crossing-over — the exchange of segments between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes — takes place during prophase I of meiosis I, while the homologues are paired (synapsed) as bivalents. It is the mechanism that generates genetic recombination in the gametes.

## explicit_objective
Place crossing-over in prophase I of meiosis, and distinguish it from the equational (non-recombining) division of meiosis II.

## pitfalls
Placing crossing-over in meiosis II or in mitosis. Meiosis II has no homologous pairing to exchange material between, and mitosis never pairs homologues at all — crossing-over is unique to prophase I.
