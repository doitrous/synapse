## label
The multiform layer is the deepest and broadest layer of the six-layered cerebral cortex

## canonical_key
histology.cerebral-cortex.multiform-layer-deepest-broadest

## definition
The neocortex has six histological layers: (I) molecular/plexiform, (II) external granular, (III) external pyramidal, (IV) internal granular, (V) internal pyramidal (containing giant Betz cells in the motor cortex), and (VI) multiform (polymorphic) layer, which borders the subcortical white matter. Layer VI, the multiform layer, is both the deepest layer of the cortex and, in most cortical areas, its broadest layer by thickness, containing a heterogeneous mix of spindle-shaped, pyramidal and other cell shapes whose axons chiefly project to the thalamus.

## explicit_objective
Identify the multiform (layer VI) as the deepest and broadest layer of the six-layered neocortex.

## label
Neuropil, seen by electron microscopy, contains neuronal and glial processes but not cell bodies

## canonical_key
histology.neuropil.composition-excludes-cell-bodies

## definition
Neuropil is the dense feltwork of unmyelinated axons, dendrites, axon terminals, dendritic spines and glial (astrocytic and microglial) processes that fills the space between neuronal and glial cell bodies (perikarya) in grey matter; synapses are largely formed within it. By electron microscopy, neuropil shows abundant cell processes of both neurons and neuroglia, but by definition it excludes the perikarya themselves — the cell bodies of neurons (and of neuroglia) sit outside the neuropil proper, surrounded by it.

## explicit_objective
State that neuropil comprises neuronal and glial processes, not neuronal or glial cell bodies, distinguishing the two compartments of grey matter by electron microscopy.

The retina is structurally 'inverted': the photoreceptors (rods and cones) lie at the back, against the retinal pigment epithelium, while light must first cross the inner retinal layers before reaching them. ## label
The basilar membrane's apex responds better to low frequencies than its base does, being wider and less stiff there

## canonical_key
cochlea.basilarmembrane.tonotopic-organization-apex-base

## definition
The basilar membrane of the cochlea is tonotopically organised: near the base (close to the oval window) it is narrow and relatively stiff, giving it maximal displacement in response to high-frequency sounds, while toward the apex it becomes progressively wider and more compliant (less stiff), giving it maximal displacement and better responsiveness to low-frequency sounds. This base-to-apex, high-to-low frequency gradient (tonotopy) is the physical basis for frequency discrimination along the length of the cochlear duct, and is preserved centrally as tonotopic maps in the auditory pathway up to the cortex.

## explicit_objective
State that the basilar membrane's apex is wider and less stiff than its base, giving it better sensitivity to low frequencies while the base favours high frequencies.

A cerebellar glomerulus, in the granular layer of the cerebellar cortex, is a synaptic complex formed by a central mossy fiber rosette surrounded by granule cell dendrites (which it excites) and Golgi cell axon terminals (which provide inhibitory feedback onto the same granule cell dendrites), all wrapped by a glial capsule. State that cerebellar glomeruli are formed by mossy fiber terminals, granule cell dendrites and Golgi cell axon terminals, while Golgi cell dendrites lie outside the glomerulus in the molecular layer.

The stria vascularis produces the characteristic ionic composition of endolymph

## canonical_key
cochlea.striavascularis.endolymph-ionic-composition-formation

## definition
The stria vascularis is a specialised, highly vascular epithelium on the lateral wall of the cochlear duct (scala media), unusual among body epithelia for containing capillaries within an epithelial layer. It actively secretes and maintains endolymph's distinctive ionic composition — high potassium, low sodium, the reverse of typical extracellular fluid — and generates the endocochlear potential, both of which are essential for normal mechanotransduction by the hair cells of the organ of Corti; this is distinct from the basilar and tectorial membranes, which are mechanical/structural rather than secretory, and from the otolithic membrane, which belongs to the vestibular (not cochlear) apparatus.

## explicit_objective
State that the stria vascularis produces endolymph's characteristic high-potassium, low-sodium ionic composition and the endocochlear potential.

synapse.synapticknob.neurotransmitter-synthesis-site

## definition
The synaptic knob (bouton), the terminal expansion of an AXON (not a dendrite) at a chemical synapse, contains the machinery to synthesise (or, for some transmitters, package precursor already synthesised in the cell body) different types of neurotransmitters, store them in synaptic vesicles, and release them by calcium-triggered exocytosis when voltage-gated calcium channels open on depolarisation — not by simple diffusion across the presynaptic membrane. This exocytotic, calcium-dependent release mechanism, together with the knob's location at axon terminals rather than dendrites, are the two features most often tested against distractors describing the opposite.

## explicit_objective
State that synaptic knobs, located at axon terminals, synthesize varied neurotransmitters and release them by calcium-triggered exocytosis rather than diffusion.
