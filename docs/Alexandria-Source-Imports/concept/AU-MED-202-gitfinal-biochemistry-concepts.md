## definition
As a group, the water-soluble vitamins (B-complex and vitamin C) share three properties: they are readily excreted in urine with little accumulation, so rarely cause toxicity; they are generally not stored in the body in significant amounts (folate and B12 are the main exceptions); and most function once converted to a coenzyme form (for example thiamine to thiamine pyrophosphate, riboflavin to FAD/FMN). This contrasts with the fat-soluble vitamins, which are stored in adipose tissue and liver and can accumulate to toxic levels.
