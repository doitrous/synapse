## label
The resting membrane potential rests on opposite Na+/Cl- and K+/protein gradients across the membrane

## canonical_key
membrane.potential.foundational-ionic-basis

## aliases
Resting membrane potential
Ionic basis of membrane potential

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Every cell's resting membrane potential depends on an asymmetric distribution of ions maintained across the plasma membrane: sodium and chloride are concentrated outside the cell, while potassium and the cell's own non-diffusible proteins are concentrated inside — a gradient the Na+/K+ pump maintains and which the membrane's selective permeability converts into a voltage.

## explicit_objective
State which ions are concentrated outside versus inside the cell at rest, as the foundation for any later account of a specific membrane potential.

## pitfalls
Reversing the gradient — putting potassium and protein outside and sodium and chloride inside. Every subsequent statement about depolarisation or hyperpolarisation depends on getting this baseline distribution the right way round.

A negative-feedback loop needs a detector, a control centre and an effector

## canonical_key
homeostasis.negative-feedback.detector-center-effector

## aliases
Negative feedback loop
Detector-centre-effector

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Physiological negative feedback runs through three functional components in sequence: a detector (receptor) senses a deviation from the set point, a control centre compares it against the set point and computes a response, and an effector carries out the response that opposes the original deviation and restores the set point.

## explicit_objective
Name the three components of a negative-feedback loop and the role each plays, as the template every specific homeostatic reflex is built from.

## pitfalls
Substituting 'amplifier' or 'annunciator' for the control centre. The middle component's job is to compare the detected signal against a set point and command a corrective response, not to amplify or merely announce the deviation.

## definition
Osmosis is the net movement of solvent (water) molecules across a semipermeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration, driven by the membrane's selective permeability rather than by bulk flow of the solute itself.

Homeostasis keeps the internal environment within narrow limits, not fixed and unchanging

## canonical_key
physiology.homeostasis.definition-narrow-limits

## aliases
Homeostasis definition
Internal environment regulation

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Homeostasis is the dynamic regulation of the body's internal environment within narrow, physiologically acceptable limits around a set point, achieved through continuous corrective responses to change — it is not a state in which internal conditions are held perfectly fixed and unaltered.

## explicit_objective
Define homeostasis as regulation within narrow limits, distinguishing it from an unattainable idea of a perfectly fixed internal state.

## pitfalls
Describing homeostasis as keeping the body 'fixed and unaltered'. Real physiological variables (temperature, glucose, pH) fluctuate continuously within a narrow band; homeostasis is the process that keeps the fluctuation narrow, not one that abolishes it.
