## label
Monosaccharides sharing a molecular formula are told apart by four distinct kinds of isomerism: D/L by the configuration at the reference carbon, epimers by one non-anomeric carbon, anomers by the anomeric carbon alone, and aldose-ketose by the functional group itself

## canonical_key
carbohydrate.isomerism.anomers-epimers-dl-aldose-ketose

## definition
An asymmetric carbon atom gives a monosaccharide stereoisomers. D and L isomers are mirror images differing at the highest-numbered chiral centre; epimers differ in configuration at one specific carbon that is not the anomeric one; anomers differ only at the anomeric carbon; aldose and ketose isomers share a formula but carry different functional groups (aldehyde vs ketone), e.g. glucose and fructose.

## explicit_objective
Classify a pair of monosaccharides as D/L isomers, epimers, anomers, or aldose-ketose isomers from the carbon at which they differ.

## label
A glycoprotein's carbohydrate is attached either O-linked, through serine or threonine's hydroxyl, or N-linked, through asparagine's amide nitrogen

## canonical_key
carbohydrate.linkage.glycoprotein-o-linked-n-linked

## definition
Oligosaccharide chains attach to glycoproteins by one of two linkage chemistries: O-glycosidic linkage to the hydroxyl of serine or threonine, or N-glycosidic linkage to the amide nitrogen of asparagine. Carbohydrate in glycoproteins is not always O-linked, and proteoglycan GAG chains are usually O-linked to their core protein, which is the specific contrast the exam draws.

## explicit_objective
Identify whether a glycoprotein's carbohydrate attachment is O-linked (Ser/Thr) or N-linked (Asn), and state which linkage predominates in proteoglycans specifically.

Myoinositol is classified as a sugar alcohol, not a deoxy sugar, sugar acid, or amino sugar
