## sections
### Definition
Hepatocytes are polyhedral parenchymal cells arranged in one-cell-thick plates; the gallbladder mucosa is lined by simple columnar epithelium with a brush border for concentrating bile, and its wall has no muscularis mucosae or submucosa layer distinguishing it from the tubular gut. The exocrine pancreas is a compound tubuloalveolar gland, and its main pancreatic duct is lined by simple columnar epithelium containing goblet cells.

### Mechanism
Ito (hepatic stellate) cells sit quiescently in the space of Disse storing vitamin A as retinyl esters; when chronically injured they transdifferentiate into activated, myofibroblast-like cells that deposit type I collagen, driving liver fibrosis. The canals of Herring, connecting bile canaliculi to interlobular ducts, are lined by hepatic stem cells and act as the liver's regenerative niche. The liver acinus is centred on the vascular core (hepatic artery and portal vein branches entering from the portal tract) rather than on the central vein; its zone I (periportal, best oxygenated) is the site of protein synthesis, while zone III (pericentral) dominates biotransformation and is first to show ischaemic or toxic injury.

### Key determinants
The small intestine amplifies its absorptive surface roughly 400-600 fold through the combined effect of plicae circulares, villi and microvilli. Its surface epithelium includes goblet cells (increasing distally) alongside absorptive enterocytes, and its intestinal glands (crypts of Lieberkuhn) open onto the mucosa between the bases of adjacent villi, not on the villi themselves.

### Clinical significance
Peyer's patches, concentrated in the ileal submucosa, are the gut's main site of mucosal B-cell activation and secrete IgA, which is transcytosed into the lumen to neutralise antigens without triggering inflammation — the basis of the gut's tolerogenic, non-inflammatory local immunity.

## hold_these
Hepatocytes are polyhedral cells arranged in one-cell-thick plates radiating from the central vein.
Quiescent Ito (hepatic stellate) cells store vitamin A as retinyl esters, the body's main vitamin A reservoir.
Activated hepatic stellate cells, not Kupffer cells or the sinusoid itself, deposit type I collagen in liver fibrosis.
Hepatic stem cells line the canals of Herring, the liver's regenerative stem-cell niche.
The gallbladder's mucosa is simple columnar epithelium.
The liver acinus's centre is the vascular core (hepatic artery and portal vein branches), not the central vein.
Zone I (periportal) of the liver acinus is responsible for protein synthesis; zone III (pericentral) dominates biotransformation.
The gallbladder wall lacks a muscularis mucosae, unlike the tubular gut.
The exocrine pancreas is a compound tubuloalveolar gland.
The main pancreatic duct is lined by simple columnar epithelium with goblet cells.
The small intestine's plicae, villi and microvilli together amplify absorptive surface area by roughly 400-600 fold.
Goblet cells are a normal, abundant component of the small-intestinal epithelium alongside absorptive enterocytes.
Intestinal glands (crypts of Lieberkuhn) open onto the mucosa between the bases of adjacent villi.
Peyer's patches secrete IgA, transcytosed into the gut lumen.

## lose_the_mark
Describing hepatocytes as columnar, cuboidal or squamous instead of polyhedral.
Attributing vitamin A storage to Kupffer cells or to activated (rather than quiescent) hepatic stellate cells.
Naming the sinusoid, Kupffer cell or hepatic stem cell as the collagen-depositing cell in liver fibrosis instead of the activated hepatic stellate cell.
Assigning the canals of Herring's lining to mature hepatocytes instead of hepatic stem cells.
Choosing simple cuboidal, pyramidal or stratified cuboidal for the gallbladder's epithelium instead of simple columnar.
Placing the acinus's centre at the central vein rather than the vascular core.
Assigning protein synthesis to zone III (pericentral) instead of zone I (periportal).
Naming the serosa or musculosa as the layer absent from the gallbladder wall instead of the muscularis mucosae.
Classifying the exocrine pancreas as simple tubular or simple alveolar instead of compound tubuloalveolar.
Choosing simple cuboidal without goblet cells for the main pancreatic duct's epithelium.
Selecting 10-50 folds or the 1000-2000 fold range instead of 400-600 fold for total surface area amplification.
Omitting goblet cells from the small intestine's normal epithelial cell population.
Placing the intestinal gland's opening in the brush border or superficial villus surface instead of between villi bases.
Selecting IgM or IgG instead of IgA as the antibody class secreted by Peyer's patches.
