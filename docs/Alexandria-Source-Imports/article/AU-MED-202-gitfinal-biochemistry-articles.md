## sections
### Definition
Gout results from either overproduction or underexcretion of uric acid, presenting with rapid joint pain, warmth, swelling and marked tenderness (classically the big toe), and is treated with allopurinol, which blocks xanthine oxidase and so lowers uric acid — but, precisely because it blocks that final step, raises urinary xanthine (and hypoxanthine) as purine catabolism backs up upstream of uric acid. HGPRT deficiency (crippled salvage, forcing more de novo synthesis) and renal failure (reduced excretion) are each independently valid causes of gout.

### Mechanism
Pyrimidine ring synthesis runs almost entirely in the cytosol: carbamoyl phosphate synthetase II (CPS-II) combines glutamine, CO2 and ATP to form carbamoyl phosphate, which condenses with aspartate and proceeds through dihydroorotate — oxidised to orotate by dihydroorotate dehydrogenase, the pathway's one mitochondrial step — to orotic acid. Orotate phosphoribosyltransferase then attaches ribose-5-phosphate (from PRPP) to form orotidine monophosphate (OMP), which is decarboxylated to UMP, the first true pyrimidine ribonucleotide. UMP is phosphorylated to UDP, reduced by ribonucleotide reductase to dUDP, and dephosphorylated to dUMP en route to dTMP; CTP synthase, like CPS-II, uses glutamine (not free ammonia) to aminate UTP to CTP. A block in UMP synthase, the bifunctional enzyme for the pathway's last two steps, causes hereditary orotic aciduria — megaloblastic anaemia and growth/mental retardation from a pyrimidine shortage, corrected with a uridine-containing drug that bypasses the block.

### Key determinants
Purine de novo synthesis is largely restricted to the liver; brain cells, red blood cells and white blood cells instead depend on salvage (HGPRT, and a secondary route phosphorylating intact ribonucleosides directly with ATP and a kinase enzyme). Purine catabolism proceeds through hypoxanthine and xanthine (guanosine's own first catabolic step is performed by purine nucleoside phosphorylase) to end at uric acid — poorly soluble and prone to crystallise — in contrast to pyrimidine catabolism, which ends in freely soluble beta-alanine, CO2 and beta-aminoisobutyrate.

### Clinical significance
Vitamins are organic micronutrients essential for body function in small amounts: the four fat-soluble vitamins are A, D, E (as tocopherol) and K, while the water-soluble B-complex and vitamin C are readily excreted, generally not stored, and act once converted to coenzymes (thiamine to TPP, riboflavin to FAD/FMN feeding NADP+-dependent enzymes such as glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase). Thiamine deficiency impairs pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and transketolase together, so their substrates accumulate; riboflavin deficiency causes photophobia alongside its mucocutaneous features.

HGPRT deficiency and renal failure are each independently valid causes of gout.
Hereditary orotic aciduria (UMP synthase deficiency) causes megaloblastic anaemia and is treated with a uridine-containing drug.
Pyrimidine catabolism ends in beta-alanine, CO2 and beta-aminoisobutyrate, all freely soluble.
Dihydroorotate dehydrogenase is the sole mitochondrial enzyme among the otherwise cytosolic pyrimidine synthesis pathway.
UMP is the first true pyrimidine ribonucleotide.
CPS-II and CTP synthase both use glutamine, not free ammonia, as their amino-group source.
UMP becomes dUMP by phosphorylation, reduction (ribonucleotide reductase) and dephosphorylation.
Carbamoyl phosphate for pyrimidine synthesis is formed from glutamine, CO2 and ATP by CPS-II.
Orotate phosphoribosyltransferase adds ribose-5-phosphate onto the pyrimidine ring to form OMP.
Glutamine is the nitrogen source for pyrimidine synthesis.
Only hepatic cells are fully capable of purine de novo synthesis; brain, RBCs and WBCs rely on salvage.
The secondary purine salvage pathway phosphorylates ribonucleosides directly using ATP and a kinase enzyme.
Uric acid is the end product of human purine catabolism.
Purine nucleoside phosphorylase performs guanosine's first catabolic step.
Riboflavin deficiency causes photophobia.
Vitamins are organic molecules essential for body function, needed only in small amounts.
Tocopherol (vitamin E) is fat-soluble, not water-soluble.
Water-soluble vitamins are readily excreted, generally not stored, and act as coenzymes.
Pyruvate, alpha-ketoglutarate and pentoses can each accumulate in thiamine deficiency.
NADP+ is the coenzyme of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase.

## lose_the_mark
Diagnosing a condition other than gout, or choosing a treatment other than allopurinol, for a classic acute monoarticular presentation with reduced renal function.
Naming urate (which falls) or adenosine instead of xanthine as the component that rises in urine after allopurinol.
Selecting only one of HGPRT deficiency or renal failure as a valid cause of gout instead of both.
Choosing a non-uridine treatment (weight loss, diet, a carbamoyl-phosphate drug) for orotic aciduria.
Attributing a poorly soluble, crystallising end product to pyrimidine catabolism instead of its freely soluble products.
Naming a cytosolic enzyme (dihydroorotase, CTP synthase, thymidylate synthase) as the pathway's mitochondrial exception instead of dihydroorotate dehydrogenase.
Naming OMP, UDP or CTP instead of UMP as the first true pyrimidine ribonucleotide.
Including CPS-I (ammonia-dependent) among the glutamine-dependent pyrimidine enzymes.
Substituting oxidation or decarboxylation for reduction in the UMP-to-dUMP pathway.
Naming ammonia/H2O or GTP/orotic acid instead of glutamine/CO2/ATP as carbamoyl phosphate's own precursors in pyrimidine synthesis.
Naming ribonucleotide reductase or thymidylate synthase instead of orotate phosphoribosyltransferase for adding ribose-5-phosphate.
Naming ammonia, aspartate alone or PRPP instead of glutamine as pyrimidine synthesis's nitrogen source.
Including hepatic cells among the tissues incapable of purine de novo synthesis.
Naming a phosphorylase enzyme instead of a kinase for the secondary purine salvage pathway.
Naming xanthine or inosine (intermediates) instead of uric acid as purine catabolism's end product.
Naming guanase or HGPRT instead of purine nucleoside phosphorylase for guanosine's first catabolic step.
Attributing photophobia to niacin deficiency instead of riboflavin deficiency.
Describing vitamins as inorganic, absent from food, or needed in large amounts.
Including tocopherol or vitamin B among the water-soluble or fat-soluble lists respectively where they do not belong.
Attributing significant storage or high toxicity risk to water-soluble vitamins instead of fat-soluble ones.
Naming only one of pyruvate, alpha-ketoglutarate or pentoses instead of all three as thiamine-deficiency accumulations.
Naming lactate or malate dehydrogenase (NAD+-dependent) instead of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase as NADP+'s coenzyme partner.
