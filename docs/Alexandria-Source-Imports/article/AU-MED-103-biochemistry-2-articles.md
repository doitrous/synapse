## summary
PFK-1 is glycolysis's committed, rate-limiting step, and the cell paces the whole pathway
by controlling this one enzyme allosterically. A falling energy charge (rising AMP) and a
hormonal go-ahead (fructose 2,6-bisphosphate) switch PFK-1 on; a full energy tank (ATP) and
a citric acid cycle that is already well supplied (citrate) switch it off. Knowing this one
enzyme's regulators explains why glycolysis speeds up exactly when the cell needs ATP and
slows down exactly when it does not.

## sections
### Definition
Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1) catalyses the phosphorylation of fructose-6-phosphate to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate, glycolysis's committed and irreversible step. Unlike hexokinase's product-inhibition or pyruvate kinase's later regulation, PFK-1 is controlled almost entirely by allosteric effectors rather than by covalent modification or gene induction.

### Mechanism
PFK-1 is activated by AMP and by fructose 2,6-bisphosphate, and inhibited by ATP and by citrate. AMP rises steeply as ATP starts to fall, because the adenylate kinase reaction (2 ADP to ATP + AMP) amplifies small drops in ATP into much larger relative rises in AMP; this makes AMP a highly sensitive low-energy alarm that switches glycolysis on well before ATP itself runs critically low. Fructose 2,6-bisphosphate is made from fructose-6-phosphate by a separate bifunctional enzyme (PFK-2/FBPase-2) whose activity is set hormonally — insulin raises it in the fed liver, glucagon lowers it in the fasted liver — and it is PFK-1's single most powerful activator, strong enough to override a fairly high ATP level. On the inhibitory side, ATP itself binds a second, lower-affinity allosteric site on PFK-1 (distinct from its catalytic site) once energy charge is high, and citrate, which accumulates when the citric acid cycle is already running well supplied with acetyl-CoA, adds a second brake that links glycolysis's pace to the cycle's own downstream capacity.

### Key determinants
Energy charge (ATP versus AMP) is the fastest-acting determinant, changing within seconds of a shift in ATP demand. Hormonal state, acting through fructose 2,6-bisphosphate, is the slower-acting determinant that sets the liver's baseline glycolytic rate across the fed-fasted cycle. Citrate ties PFK-1's pace to the citric acid cycle's own supply state, preventing glycolysis from outrunning the mitochondria's capacity to use its product.

### Clinical significance
A question that lists activators and asks which one is the exception is testing exactly this two-signal, two-brake system: AMP and fructose 2,6-bisphosphate belong on the activator side, ATP and citrate on the inhibitor side, and any other listed molecule (fluoride, biotin, or a molecule from an unrelated pathway) is the odd one out precisely because it plays no role in PFK-1's own regulation.
