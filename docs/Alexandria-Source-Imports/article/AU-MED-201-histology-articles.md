## summary
Alexandria's EGU Mock exam pairs each histological structure with the specific alternative its own answer table tests it against: what distinguishes an alpha cell within the pancreatic islet, what distinguishes zona glomerulosa from zona reticularis in the adrenal cortex, what the ovarian "germinal" epithelium actually is (and is not), and which organ lies anterior to the right suprarenal gland.

## sections
### Definition
Within a pancreatic islet of Langerhans, alpha (A) cells (glucagon-secreting) sit at the periphery, forming a rim around the more numerous, centrally-placed beta (B) cells (insulin-secreting); delta (D) cells (somatostatin-secreting) are the least numerous islet cell type. The adrenal cortex has three zones: the outer zona glomerulosa (mineralocorticoids), arranged in closely-packed arched clusters beneath the capsule; the middle zona fasciculata (glucocorticoids), in parallel cords; and the inner zona reticularis (androgens), an anastomosing network of cords whose cells accumulate more cytoplasmic lipofuscin pigment than the other two zones. The ovary's surface carries a single layer of simple cuboidal-to-low-columnar "germinal epithelium," lying external (superficial) to the tunica albuginea, the ovary's dense connective-tissue capsule. The right suprarenal gland's anterior relation is the bare area of the liver.

### Mechanism
Alpha cells' distinguishing histological feature on a stained section is their peripheral position within the islet, not a proportion (numerousness) claim — that distinction (least numerous) instead belongs to delta cells, and alpha cells are not characteristically small. The zona glomerulosa's arched-cluster architecture is what differentiates it from the zona reticularis, whose own distinguishing feature is greater lipofuscin content rather than a distinctive arrangement; neither zone gives a positive chromaffin reaction (a medullary chromaffin-cell feature) or shows classic protein-secreting-cell ultrastructure (all three cortical zones are steroidogenic, with abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum and lipid droplets, not protein-secreting granules). Despite its name, the ovarian germinal epithelium does not give rise to the primordial germ cells — those originate from yolk sac endoderm and migrate into the developing gonad during embryogenesis; the germinal epithelium is simply the ovary's mesothelium-derived surface layer, external to the tunica albuginea, and is not continuous with the tunica vaginalis mesothelium (a testicular structure) or a sheet of ciliated columnar epithelium. The right suprarenal gland is wedged between the liver and diaphragm and medial to the inferior vena cava, so its anterior relation is the liver's bare area — the stomach and pancreas are instead anterior relations of the LEFT suprarenal gland.

### Key determinants
Alpha-cell peripheral location, zona glomerulosa's arched-cluster arrangement, the germinal epithelium's position external to the tunica albuginea, and the right suprarenal gland's liver relation are each tested against a specific, closely related alternative from the same structure (a different islet cell's proportion fact, the zona reticularis's own lipofuscin feature, the primordial-germ-cell misnomer, and the left suprarenal gland's stomach/pancreas relations respectively) — the discriminating skill in each item is keeping the paired structures' distinguishing features straight rather than swapping them.

### Clinical significance
Distinguishing islet cell types by location underlies interpreting immunohistochemical staining of pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (insulinoma vs glucagonoma vs somatostatinoma) by their cell of origin. Recognising zona glomerulosa's distinct architecture and its isolated aldosterone-secreting role (rather than the zona reticularis's androgen-secreting role) is relevant to understanding primary hyperaldosteronism's site of origin within the adrenal cortex. Knowing that the germinal epithelium is not a source of primordial germ cells corrects a common naming-based misconception relevant to ovarian surface epithelial tumour biology (most epithelial ovarian cancers arise from this surface layer). And knowing the right suprarenal gland's liver relation (versus the left gland's splenic/pancreatic/gastric relations) matters for correctly interpreting cross-sectional imaging and for surgical adrenalectomy planning.

## hold_these
Alpha cells = peripherally located in the islet (not "least numerous" — that is delta cells).
Zona glomerulosa = closely-packed arched clusters beneath the capsule; zona reticularis = more lipofuscin pigment, NOT the glomerulosa.
Germinal epithelium lies OUTER to the tunica albuginea; despite its name it does NOT give rise to primordial germ cells (those come from yolk sac endoderm).
Right suprarenal gland's anterior relation = liver; the stomach and pancreas are LEFT suprarenal gland relations instead.

## lose_the_mark
Calling alpha cells "the least numerous" islet cell type — that description fits delta cells.
Attributing lipofuscin accumulation, a chromaffin reaction, or protein-secreting features to the zona glomerulosa instead of correctly to the zona reticularis (lipofuscin) or the medulla (chromaffin).
Taking "germinal epithelium" literally as a source of primordial germ cells.
Assigning the stomach or pancreas (left-sided relations) to the right suprarenal gland instead of the liver.
