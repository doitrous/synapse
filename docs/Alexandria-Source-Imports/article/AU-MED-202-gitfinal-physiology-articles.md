## sections
### Definition
Brunner's glands, compound tubuloalveolar glands in the submucosa of the first part of the duodenum (the duodenal cap), secrete an alkaline mucus that neutralises acidic chyme entering from the stomach. Pooled intestinal digestive juice (succus entericus, mixed with pancreatic and biliary secretions) runs near-neutral to mildly alkaline at roughly pH 7-7.5, protecting the duodenal mucosa and giving pancreatic and brush-border enzymes their working pH range.

### Mechanism
Although luminal enzymes carry out the initial bulk breakdown of a meal, the final digestive steps for carbohydrates and proteins happen by membrane (contact) digestion at the enterocyte's brush border itself — this, not the lumen or the crypts of Lieberkuhn, is the main site of intestinal digestion. The brush-border enzyme trehalase hydrolyses the disaccharide trehalose into two free glucose molecules (not one), consistent with the intestinal mucosal cell's short 3-5 day lifespan and the intracellular hydrolysis of some di- and tripeptides to amino acids by cytoplasmic peptidases.

### Key determinants
Vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP), released by enteric neurons, dilates peripheral blood vessels (it does not cause vasoconstriction), relaxes gastrointestinal smooth muscle, potentiates acetylcholine's effect on salivary secretion, and stimulates intestinal electrolyte secretion.

### Clinical significance
Brunner's gland hyperplasia or hamartoma, arising from this same first-part-of-duodenum location, is a recognised (if uncommon) cause of upper GI bleeding or obstruction, making correct localisation of these glands clinically as well as anatomically relevant.

## hold_these
Brunner's glands are located in the submucosa of the first part of the duodenum (the duodenal cap).
Pooled intestinal digestive juice runs at roughly pH 7-7.5, distinct from the strongly acidic gastric juice.
The brush border, not the lumen or crypts of Lieberkuhn, is the main site of intestinal digestion.
Trehalase hydrolyses trehalose into two glucose molecules, not one.
VIP dilates (does not constrict) peripheral blood vessels, alongside relaxing GI smooth muscle, potentiating salivary acetylcholine action, and stimulating intestinal electrolyte secretion.

## lose_the_mark
Placing Brunner's glands in the third part of the duodenum, jejunum or ileum instead of the duodenal cap.
Selecting 8-8.5 or 2.5 instead of 7-7.5 for the pH of pooled intestinal digestive juice.
Placing the main site of intestinal digestion in the lumen, cytoplasm or crypts of Lieberkuhn instead of the brush border.
Accepting 'trehalase yields one glucose molecule' as true instead of recognising it as the bank's marked false statement.
Listing vasoconstriction as one of VIP's actions instead of recognising it as the one action VIP does NOT perform.
