## sections
### Definition
The pancreas is a retroperitoneal gland except for its tail, which lies within the two layers of the lienorenal (splenorenal) ligament and so is the only part covered by peritoneum both anteriorly and posteriorly; the rest (head, neck, body) is covered by peritoneum on its anterior surface only. The transverse mesocolon and the greater omentum's posterior layer attach along the pancreas's anterior border, carrying the left gastroepiploic vessels within that attachment. The lesser sac (omental bursa) lies behind the stomach and in front of the pancreas, and its posterior wall is formed by peritoneum draped over the coeliac trunk, the left kidney/suprarenal gland and the upper abdominal aorta before reflecting forward over the pancreas. The epiploic foramen (of Winslow), the sole communication between the greater and lesser sacs, has the portal vein, hepatic artery and bile duct in its anterior boundary, the IVC posteriorly, the liver's inferior surface superiorly and the first part of the duodenum inferiorly.

The portal vein forms posterior to the neck of the pancreas by the union of the splenic vein and superior mesenteric vein, then terminates at the porta hepatis, where it divides into right and left branches and where the free edge of the lesser omentum attaches. The inferior mesenteric vein most commonly drains into the splenic vein (not directly into the portal vein), so splenic vein thrombosis can cause isolated left-sided portal hypertension. Three portosystemic anastomoses recur across this bank: at the gastro-oesophageal junction (left gastric vein with oesophageal/azygos veins, producing oesophageal varices), at the umbilicus (paraumbilical veins, running with the ligamentum teres from the left portal branch, with anterior abdominal wall veins, producing caput medusae), and at the anorectal junction (superior rectal vein with middle and inferior rectal veins, producing internal haemorrhoids in portal hypertension).

### Mechanism
The pancreatic head receives a dual arterial supply reflecting its foregut/midgut position: the superior pancreaticoduodenal artery (via the gastroduodenal artery, from the coeliac trunk) and the inferior pancreaticoduodenal artery (from the superior mesenteric artery) form an anastomosing arcade around the head. The body and tail are supplied by pancreatic branches of the splenic artery. The superior mesenteric artery itself passes anterior to the uncinate process rather than posterior to the head, distinguishing it from the head's true posterior relations (IVC, right renal vessels, common bile duct). The accessory pancreatic duct (of Santorini) opens on the minor duodenal papilla in the second part of the duodenum, proximal to the major papilla where the main pancreatic duct and bile duct open together; lymphatics from the upper head follow the superior pancreaticoduodenal vessels to coeliac nodes, while the lower head/uncinate process drain along the SMA-derived vessels to superior mesenteric nodes.

The jejunum and ileum are distinguished by a consistent set of features: the jejunum has a thicker wall, wider lumen, sparser and more translucent ('windowed') mesenteric fat, taller and more numerous circular mucosal folds, and few or no Peyer's patches, while the ileum has a thinner wall, narrower lumen, fattier mesentery, fewer mucosal folds, numerous short complex vascular arcades, and numerous Peyer's patches (especially in the terminal ileum). The root of the small bowel mesentery runs obliquely, right-sided, from the duodenojejunal flexure to the ileocaecal junction, crossing the third part of the duodenum, the aorta, the IVC, the right ureter, the right gonadal vessels, the right genitofemoral nerve and right psoas major — the left ureter is not crossed.

### Key determinants
The caecum lies in the right iliac fossa/groin, is usually completely invested by peritoneum and freely mobile, and is related posteriorly to psoas major, iliacus and the femoral nerve (not the medial cutaneous nerve of the thigh, which arises from the femoral nerve within the thigh rather than relating to the caecum directly) — the lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh is the one that runs on iliacus behind the caecum. The appendix opens into the caecum's posteromedial wall, inferior to the ileocaecal opening (not superior to it), and the caecum is supplied by the anterior and posterior caecal arteries, branches of the ileocolic artery. The appendicular artery, an end artery, is itself a branch of the ileocolic artery, which is why post-appendectomy haemorrhage from a slipped appendicular stump is controlled by securing the ileocolic artery.

Among the named parts of the large intestine, the transverse colon and sigmoid colon are each suspended on their own mesocolon and so are completely covered by peritoneum (mobile, intraperitoneal), while the ascending and descending colon are secondarily retroperitoneal, covered by peritoneum only on their anterior surface and sides, with a bare posterior area fixed to the posterior abdominal wall.

### Clinical significance
Head-of-pancreas carcinoma commonly compresses the intrapancreatic common bile duct (producing obstructive/post-hepatic jaundice) and can compress the adjacent portal vein as it enlarges, since the vein lies immediately posterior to the pancreatic neck — a relation used in staging pancreatic cancer for resectability. Early visceral appendiceal pain is referred to the periumbilical region because the appendix's visceral afferents travel with sympathetic fibres back to the T10 spinal segment, which also supplies the umbilical dermatome; a positive psoas sign (pain on resisted hip flexion) then points to a retrocaecal appendix irritating psoas major, and the pain shifts to the right iliac fossa (or, with a low-lying/pelvic appendix, to the right groin) as somatic parietal peritoneal pain takes over. The combination of fever, McBurney's point tenderness and leukocytosis is the classic bedside diagnostic pattern for acute appendicitis, distinguishing it from cholecystitis (right hypochondrium, Murphy's sign), renal colic (loin-to-groin) or duodenal perforation (sudden epigastric peritonitis).

## hold_these
Left gastroepiploic vessels run in the transverse mesocolon/greater omentum attachment to the pancreas's anterior border.
Paraumbilical vein dilatation in cirrhosis reflects obstruction/hypertension at the left branch of the portal vein (ligamentum teres pathway), producing caput medusae.
The epiploic foramen (of Winslow)'s inferior boundary is the first part of the duodenum; anterior boundary carries the portal vein, hepatic artery and bile duct; posterior boundary is the IVC; superior boundary is the liver's inferior surface.
The portal vein terminates at the porta hepatis, where the free edge of the lesser omentum attaches.
The lesser sac (omental bursa) separates the stomach from the pancreas; its posterior wall is peritoneum over the coeliac trunk, left kidney/suprarenal and upper abdominal aorta.
The gastroduodenal junction (pylorus) lies anterior to the neck of the pancreas.
The SMA passes anterior to the uncinate process, not posterior to the pancreatic head (unlike the IVC, renal vessels and bile duct, which are true posterior relations).
The pancreas is supplied by both the coeliac trunk (splenic artery branches, superior pancreaticoduodenal artery) and the superior mesenteric artery (inferior pancreaticoduodenal artery).
The inferior mesenteric vein most commonly drains into the splenic vein, not directly into the portal vein.
The anorectal portosystemic anastomosis is between the superior rectal vein and the middle/inferior rectal veins.
Only the tail of the pancreas is covered by peritoneum on both surfaces (within the lienorenal ligament); head/neck/body are covered anteriorly only.
Head-of-pancreas carcinoma causes both obstructive jaundice (bile duct compression) and portal vein pressure (adjacent compression).
The umbilical portosystemic anastomosis is between paraumbilical veins (from the left portal branch) and anterior abdominal wall veins.
The upper part of the pancreatic head drains to coeliac lymph nodes, following its coeliac-derived blood supply.
The superior pancreaticoduodenal artery (from the gastroduodenal artery, coeliac-derived) supplies the pancreatic head.
The accessory pancreatic duct opens at the minor duodenal papilla in the second part of the duodenum.
The sympathetic chain is a posterior relation of the pancreas, not an inferior-surface relation (duodenojejunal flexure, jejunal loops, transverse colon are).
The portal vein forms posterior to the neck of the pancreas, by union of the splenic vein and SMV.
The ileum has numerous, short, complex mesenteric vascular arcades, distinguishing it from the jejunum's fewer, longer, simpler arcades.
The root of the mesentery crosses the right ureter, right gonadal vessels and right genitofemoral nerve — not the left ureter.
The caecum's posterior relations are psoas major, iliacus, femoral nerve and the lateral (not medial) cutaneous nerve of the thigh.
Early appendicitis pain is referred to the umbilical region via T10 visceral afferents, later localising to the right iliac fossa as parietal peritoneum is irritated.
Fever, McBurney's point tenderness and leukocytosis together indicate acute appendicitis.
The appendicular artery is a branch of the ileocolic artery, which must be secured to control post-appendectomy bleeding.
The jejunum lacks numerous Peyer's patches (an ileal feature), despite sharing thick wall/wide lumen/sparse mesenteric fat/numerous mucosal folds with the rest of the jejunal description.
The appendix opens into the caecum's posteromedial wall inferior to (not superior to) the ileocaecal opening.
The caecum, in the right iliac fossa, is the most likely source of pain localised to the right groin among the parts of the large intestine.
The transverse and sigmoid colon are completely peritoneum-covered (own mesocolon); the ascending and descending colon are covered anteriorly and laterally only (secondarily retroperitoneal).

## lose_the_mark
Assigning the right gastric vessels or hepatic artery (lesser omentum contents) to the pancreas's anterior-border attachment instead of the left gastroepiploic vessels.
Attributing paraumbilical vein dilatation to the right portal branch or to the azygos/left gastric veins instead of the left portal branch pathway.
Placing the common bile duct, head of pancreas or liver's inferior surface as the epiploic foramen's inferior boundary instead of the first part of the duodenum.
Naming the lienorenal or gastrophrenic ligament, or the greater omentum, as the structure attached at the porta hepatis instead of the lesser omentum.
Treating only one of the coeliac trunk / left kidney / aorta as the lesser sac's posterior wall rather than all three together.
Placing the gastroduodenal junction relation at the pancreatic body, head or tail rather than the neck.
Listing the SMA as a true posterior relation of the pancreatic head alongside the IVC, renal vessels and bile duct.
Naming only the coeliac trunk or only the superior mesenteric artery as the pancreas's blood supply, omitting the dual foregut/midgut contribution.
Sending the inferior mesenteric vein directly to the portal vein or IVC instead of the splenic vein.
Confusing the anorectal portosystemic anastomosis with the oesophageal (left gastric/azygos) or umbilical (paraumbilical) anastomoses.
Attributing peritoneum-both-surfaces coverage to the head, neck or body of the pancreas instead of the tail alone.
Choosing only obstructive jaundice or only portal vein pressure as the consequence of head-of-pancreas carcinoma, omitting the other.
Reversing the umbilical anastomosis's components — confusing it with the superior mesenteric vein/IVC or right colic/right renal vein pairings.
Sending the upper pancreatic head's lymphatic drainage to superior mesenteric or para-aortic nodes instead of coeliac nodes.
Naming the SMA or splenic artery branch directly as the pancreatic head's main supply instead of the superior pancreaticoduodenal artery.
Placing the accessory pancreatic duct's opening in the first, third or fourth part of the duodenum instead of the second.
Treating the sympathetic chain as an inferior-surface relation of the pancreas rather than a posterior one.
Placing portal vein formation anterior to the pancreatic head or at the superior border rather than posterior to the neck.
Describing the ileum as having a thick wall, wide lumen or few/simple arcades — those are jejunal features.
Naming the left ureter (rather than the right) as crossed by the root of the mesentery.
Assigning the medial cutaneous nerve of the thigh as a posterior relation of the caecum instead of the lateral cutaneous nerve.
Attributing early appendiceal pain to a right hypochondrial or epigastric distribution instead of periumbilical (T10).
Diagnosing cholecystitis, renal colic or duodenal perforation instead of appendicitis given fever + McBurney's point tenderness + leukocytosis.
Naming the right colic or superior rectal artery as the vessel controlling post-appendectomy bleeding instead of the ileocolic artery.
Listing Peyer's patches among the jejunum's own features rather than recognising them as the ileal exception.
Placing the appendiceal opening superior to the ileocaecal opening instead of inferior to it.
Choosing the ascending colon, transverse colon or sigmoid colon instead of the caecum as the source of right groin pain.
Reversing the peritoneal-covering pairing — calling the ascending/descending colon completely covered and the transverse/sigmoid colon only partly covered.
