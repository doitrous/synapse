Nuclear bag fiber's non-contractile region

## question
All of the following characterize the nuclear bag fibers EXCEPT:

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true statement, so not the exception). A muscle spindle typically contains 2-4 nuclear bag fibers alongside its more numerous nuclear chain fibers.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect (a true statement, so not the exception). Both intrafusal fibre types have a central, non-contractile (sarcomere-poor) region distinct from their contractile polar ends.

In short: identify that the nuclear bag intrafusal fiber's central region is expanded, not un-expanded, distinguishing it from the nuclear chain fiber.

## answer_d
It contains many aggregated nuclei

## explanation_d
Incorrect (a true statement, so not the exception). The expanded equatorial bag is filled with many aggregated nuclei, in contrast to the chain fibre's nuclei arranged in a single row.

Shape of craniospinal ganglion cell bodies

## question
Which of the following characterizes the craniospinal ganglia?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
They have a thin capsule with thin septa

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Craniospinal (sensory) ganglia have a THICK connective-tissue capsule with septa dividing the ganglion into lobules, not a thin one — a thin capsule better describes an autonomic ganglion.

## answer_b
Ganglion cells are multipolar neurons with eccentric nuclei

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sensory ganglion cells are PSEUDOUNIPOLAR, not multipolar, and their large, round nucleus is CENTRALLY placed, not eccentric — 'multipolar with eccentric nuclei' instead describes autonomic ganglion neurons.

## answer_c
The nerve fibers are unmyelinated

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The central and peripheral processes of craniospinal ganglion neurons are MYELINATED, not unmyelinated, right up to their terminal branches.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Correct. The pseudounipolar cell bodies of a craniospinal (sensory) ganglion are packed at the ganglion's periphery in an OVAL shape, each surrounded by a ring of satellite cells and separated from its neighbours by connective-tissue septa — a distinctive histological feature that, together with the thick capsule, myelinated fibers and centrally-placed round nucleus, distinguishes sensory ganglia from the irregularly-shaped, thin-capsuled autonomic ganglia. In short: state that craniospinal (sensory) ganglion cell bodies are oval, thickly capsulated and myelinated, distinguishing them from autonomic ganglion histology.

It has no motor nerve endings

## explanation_a
Correct. The Golgi tendon organ is a purely sensory receptor: its capsule of interwoven collagen fascicles is threaded only by group Ib sensory afferent terminals, with no motor (efferent) nerve endings at all — a key contrast with the muscle spindle, whose intrafusal fibers receive gamma motor efferents that adjust the receptor's own sensitivity. It is a fusiform structure

Incorrect. While the tendon organ is an elongated, capsulated structure, describing it simply as 'fusiform' does not capture what specifically characterizes it among these options, and the muscle spindle is also commonly described as fusiform, so this is not the discriminating feature being tested.

## answer_d
It has small modified muscle fibers called intrafusal muscle fibers

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Small, modified intrafusal muscle fibers are the defining component of the MUSCLE SPINDLE, not the tendon organ, whose sensory terminals interweave with collagen fascicles rather than modified muscle fibers.

## title
Receptor potential and the refractory period

## question
All of the following are true regarding the receptor potential EXCEPT:

## subject
neuro

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It doesn't obey the 'all-or-none' rule

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true statement, so not the exception). As a graded potential, the receptor potential's amplitude varies with stimulus strength and does not obey the all-or-none rule that governs the action potential.

## answer_b
It has a refractory period

## explanation_b
Correct. Because the receptor potential is a graded, non-propagated, non-regenerative local event rather than a voltage-gated all-or-none impulse, it has NO refractory period — this is the false statement among the four, and is exactly what distinguishes it from the action potential it may go on to trigger. In short: state that the receptor (generator) potential has no refractory period, unlike the action potential.

## answer_c
It can be summated

## explanation_c
Incorrect (a true statement, so not the exception). Graded potentials, including the receptor potential, can summate spatially and temporally, unlike the all-or-none action potential.

## answer_d
It cannot be blocked by local anaesthetics

## explanation_d
Incorrect (a true statement, so not the exception). Because the receptor potential is generated by stimulus-gated (e.g. mechanically-gated) transduction channels rather than the voltage-gated Na+ channels that local anaesthetics block, its formation is not itself blocked by local anaesthetics, even though the downstream propagated action potential is.
