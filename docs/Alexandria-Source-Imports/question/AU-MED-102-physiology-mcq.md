## question
Which is responsible for membrane potential?

## subject

## status

## owner
Alexandria content lane (W1-102-HIST)

## vignette


## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Na & Cl high concentration outside and K& proteins high concentration inside

## explanation_a
Correct. The resting membrane potential rests on sodium and chloride being concentrated outside the cell while potassium and the cell's own non-diffusible proteins are concentrated inside — a gradient the Na+/K+ pump maintains.

## answer_b
K & Cl high concentration outside and Na & protein high concentration inside

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This reverses potassium and sodium's actual positions: potassium is the ion concentrated inside the cell, not outside, and sodium is concentrated outside, not inside.

## answer_c
Na & K high concentration outside and Cl & protein high concentration inside

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Potassium is concentrated inside the cell, not outside alongside sodium — grouping Na and K together on the same side of the membrane misreads the gradient entirely.

## answer_d
K & proteins high concentration outside and Na& Cl high concentration inside

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This is the complete mirror-image of the true distribution: potassium and protein are actually concentrated inside, and sodium and chloride outside, not the reverse.

## question

## explanation_a

Incorrect. G-protein-coupled receptors act through a second-messenger cascade and produce slow, graded effects; the nicotinic receptor's fast, brief action at the motor end-plate is exactly why it cannot be this receptor class.

## answer_d
They are ligand-gated ion channels

## explanation_d
Correct. Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors are ligand-gated ion channels: acetylcholine binding directly opens the integral channel, producing the fast depolarisation of the motor end-plate that myasthenia gravis's autoantibodies disrupt.
