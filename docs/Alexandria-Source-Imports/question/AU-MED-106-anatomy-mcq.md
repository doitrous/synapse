## explanation_a
Incorrect. The costocervical trunk is a branch of the subclavian artery's SECOND part (behind scalenus anterior), not the vessel this image points at.

## answer_b
Arch of aorta

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The arch of the aorta gives the brachiocephalic, left common carotid and left subclavian arteries, not this vessel.

Incorrect. The thyrocervical trunk is itself one of the branches OF the first part of the subclavian artery, not the parent vessel the image is pointing to.

Posterior cricoarytenoid action

## question
Action of this muscle is…

## subject
resp

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Adduction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adduction (closing the rima glottidis) is performed by the lateral cricoarytenoid and the arytenoid muscles, not by the muscle pictured here.

## answer_b
Closing of the laryngeal inlet

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Closing the laryngeal inlet is a sphincteric action of the aryepiglottic and oblique arytenoid fibres during swallowing, a different action from the one this muscle performs.

## answer_c
Stretching the vocal cords

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Stretching (tensing) the vocal cords is the cricothyroid's action, the one intrinsic muscle supplied by the external laryngeal nerve rather than the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

## answer_d
Abduction

## explanation_d
Correct. The posterior cricoarytenoid is the ONLY abductor of the vocal folds — it rotates the arytenoid cartilage to swing the vocal process laterally, opening the airway. Its unique abductor role is why bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury (paralysing this muscle on both sides) is a life-threatening airway emergency, since nothing else can open the rima glottidis. This muscle's isolated action is one of the highest-yield facts in laryngeal anatomy.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The left external jugular vein is not the pairing partner in the standard brachiocephalic-vein union; the standard union is an internal jugular plus a subclavian vein on the same side.

## answer_b
Left internal + left external jugular vein

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Two jugular veins (internal + external) of the same side do not unite to form a brachiocephalic vein; the union is always one internal jugular plus one subclavian vein.

Right subclavian + right internal jugular vein
