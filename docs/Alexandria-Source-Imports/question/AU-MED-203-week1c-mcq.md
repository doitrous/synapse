Testing the spinal accessory nerve

## question
How would you test for the spinal accessory nerve?

## subject
neuro

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Facial sensation is tested for the trigeminal nerve, not the accessory nerve, which carries no general sensory component.

## answer_b
Examine for the power of the muscles of mastication

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The power of the muscles of mastication tests the motor division of the trigeminal nerve (V3), a different cranial nerve from the accessory.

## answer_c
Examine for the sense of smell

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sense of smell tests the olfactory nerve (CN I), unrelated to the accessory nerve's motor function.

Ask the patient to shrug the shoulder and to turn the head against resistance

Correct. The spinal accessory nerve (CN XI) supplies trapezius, tested by asking the patient to shrug the shoulders against the examiner's downward resistance, and sternocleidomastoid, tested by asking the patient to turn the head to each side against resistance while the examiner palpates the muscle. Combining both actions in one bedside test covers both of the nerve's motor targets in a single manoeuvre. In short: state that the spinal accessory nerve is examined by asking the patient to shrug the shoulder and turn the head against resistance.

## title
A true general statement about sensory tracts

## question
Regarding sensory tracts

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Proprioceptive information is carried in the dorsal columns only

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Proprioceptive information also travels unconsciously via the spinocerebellar tracts to the cerebellum, not only via the dorsal columns.

## answer_b
Spino-thalamic tracts carry information from ipsilateral side of the body

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The spinothalamic tracts are crossed pathways, carrying information from the CONTRALATERAL (opposite), not ipsilateral, side of the body.

## answer_c
All affects fibers cross the midline at same level of spinal cord

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Different tracts cross at different spinal levels: the spinothalamic tracts cross within a couple of segments of entry, while the dorsal columns do not cross until the medulla.

## answer_d
Their impulses reach cerebellum and-or somatosensory cortex

## explanation_d
Correct. As a general rule across the ascending sensory tracts, unconscious information (spinocerebellar tracts) terminates in the cerebellum for reflex coordination, while conscious information (dorsal column-medial lemniscus, spinothalamic tracts) relays through the thalamus to the somatosensory cortex.
