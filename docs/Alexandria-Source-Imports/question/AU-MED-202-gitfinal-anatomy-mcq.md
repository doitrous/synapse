QST-AUMED202-GITFINAL-ANATOMY-GITFINAL-Q01

## title
Contents attached to the anterior border of the pancreas

## question
……… is one of the contents of the structure attached to the anterior border of the pancreas.

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Right gastric vessels

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The right gastric vessels run in the free edge of the lesser omentum along the lesser curvature of the stomach, not in the structure that attaches to the pancreas's anterior border.

## answer_b
Left gastroepiploic vessels

## explanation_b
Correct. The transverse mesocolon and the greater omentum's posterior layer attach along the anterior border of the pancreas, and the left gastroepiploic (gastro-omental) vessels run within the layers of that attachment as they pass toward the greater curvature. This is why surgeons dividing the greater omentum off the transverse mesocolon near the pancreas must identify and protect these vessels. Recognising the anterior-border attachment as the transverse mesocolon/greater omentum root, and its contained vessel, is the key teaching point.

## answer_c
Hepatic artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The hepatic artery runs in the free edge of the lesser omentum toward the porta hepatis, well away from the pancreas's anterior border attachment.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect, since only the left gastroepiploic vessels (not the right gastric vessels or hepatic artery) are contained in this specific attachment.

## title
Paraumbilical vein dilatation in liver cirrhosis

## question
A 76-year-old male was followed in the gastroenterology unit because of alcoholic liver cirrhosis (ALC). A computerized tomography of the abdomen showed dilatation of the paraumbilical veins. What is the most likely vein to be obstructed?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Azygous vein

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The azygos vein is a systemic (caval) tributary at the oesophagus, not the vessel whose obstruction dilates the paraumbilical veins.

## answer_b
Right branch of portal vein

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The right branch of the portal vein does not communicate with the paraumbilical veins; the round-ligament (ligamentum teres) route that carries the paraumbilical veins runs from the left branch.

## answer_c
Left branch of portal vein

## explanation_c
Correct. The paraumbilical veins run in the falciform ligament alongside the ligamentum teres (obliterated umbilical vein), draining into the left branch of the portal vein. In portal hypertension from cirrhosis, this portosystemic pathway dilates and can reopen, producing the paraumbilical/periumbilical varices seen on imaging as 'caput medusae' — so left portal-branch obstruction/hypertension is the vessel implicated, not the right branch.

## answer_d
Left gastric vein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The left gastric vein forms a different portosystemic anastomosis (with the oesophageal veins, producing oesophageal varices), not the paraumbilical pathway.

## title
Inferior boundary of the epiploic foramen

## question
The inferior boundary of the structure that separates the portal vein from the IVC is…….

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Common bile duct

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The common bile duct runs in the anterior (right free) margin of the epiploic foramen, alongside the portal vein and hepatic artery — it is a content of the anterior boundary, not the inferior boundary.

## answer_b
1st part of duodenum

## explanation_b
Correct. The epiploic foramen (of Winslow) — the only opening between the greater and lesser peritoneal sacs — has the portal vein, hepatic artery and bile duct in its anterior boundary (free edge of lesser omentum) and the IVC in its posterior boundary. Its inferior boundary is the first (superior) part of the duodenum, which is why this landmark is used surgically to identify the foramen and control bleeding by compressing the structures within the free edge of the lesser omentum (Pringle manoeuvre).

## answer_c
Head of pancreas

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The head of the pancreas lies inferior and to the right of the epiploic foramen but is not itself a named boundary of the foramen.

## answer_d
Inferior surface of liver

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The inferior surface (visceral surface) of the liver forms the superior boundary of the epiploic foramen, not the inferior one.

The posterior wall of the structure separating the stomach from the pancreas is formed of:
