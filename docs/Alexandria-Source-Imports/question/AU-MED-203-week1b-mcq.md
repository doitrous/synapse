## title
Composition of a dural fold

## question
The dural folds formed by:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Inner meningeal layer and periosteum

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The dura mater is a single membrane with two apposed layers (periosteal/outer and meningeal/inner), but a dural fold is not formed by pairing the meningeal layer with the periosteum — the periosteal layer stays fused to the skull's inner table except where it splits off to form a venous sinus wall.

## answer_b
Two outer layers of dura

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Naming the outer (periosteal) layer twice does not describe a dural fold: the periosteal layer is the one that stays applied to bone, so a fold cannot be built from two copies of it.

## answer_c
Two inner layers of dura

## explanation_c
Correct. A dural fold such as the falx cerebri or tentorium cerebelli is formed by a reduplication (in-folding) of the dura's INNER, meningeal layer, which reflects off the skull and projects into the cranial cavity as a double-layered septum partitioning the intracranial compartments — the periosteal layer is left behind, lining the bone, so it plays no part in the fold itself. In short: state that a dural fold is a reduplication of the meningeal (inner), not periosteal, layer of the dura.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Option C correctly names the meningeal layer as the source of a dural fold, so 'none of the above' is not the answer.

Basilar plexus of sinuses

## title
A member of the deep (vertical) lymph node group

## question
The lymph drainage of the vertical deep of the head and neck

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Occipital Lymph node

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The occipital nodes belong to the superficial (transverse) pericervical group, draining the posterior scalp — not the deep vertical chain.

## answer_b
Palatine tonsil lymph node

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 'Palatine tonsil lymph node' is not a standard named deep cervical node; the tonsil itself drains to the jugulodigastric node of the deep chain, but is not itself the answer offered here.

## answer_c
Retro pharyngeal lymph node

## explanation_c
Correct. The retropharyngeal nodes are part of the deep (vertical) cervical lymphatic group, lying behind the pharynx and draining the nasopharynx, paranasal sinuses and middle ear before onward drainage into the deep cervical chain along the internal jugular vein — distinct from the superficial pericervical collar (occipital, preauricular, submental, submandibular). In short: identify the retropharyngeal nodes as belonging to the deep (vertical) cervical lymphatic group.

## answer_d
External jugular vein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The external jugular vein is a vein, not a lymph node, and is unrelated to this lymphatic grouping.
