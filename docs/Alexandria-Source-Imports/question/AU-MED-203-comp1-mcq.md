## title
Composition of the midbrain tectum

## question
The tectum is formed of:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
4 geniculate bodies

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The medial and lateral geniculate bodies are thalamic nuclei (relaying auditory and visual information respectively), not components of the midbrain tectum.

## answer_b
4 cochlear nuclei

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The dorsal and ventral cochlear nuclei sit at the pontomedullary junction, not in the midbrain tectum.

## answer_c
2 salivary nuclei

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior and inferior salivatory nuclei are pontine and medullary parasympathetic nuclei, unrelated to the tectum.

## answer_d
4 colliculi

## explanation_d
Correct. The tectum, the roof (dorsal part) of the midbrain, is formed by the corpora quadrigemina: two superior colliculi, which mediate visual reflexes, and two inferior colliculi, which relay auditory information — four colliculi in total. This composition distinguishes the tectum from the tegmentum and crus cerebri, the other two midbrain subdivisions. In short: identify the tectum as being built from four colliculi (two superior, two inferior), not geniculate bodies, salivatory nuclei or cochlear nuclei.

## title
Mechanism of antagonist relaxation in the stretch reflex

## question
In the stretch reflex, relaxation of antagonist muscles is due to:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Presynaptic inhibition

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Presynaptic inhibition modulates the strength of afferent input generally (e.g. in pain gating), but it is not the mechanism that specifically relaxes the antagonist muscle during a stretch reflex.

## answer_b
Antidromic conduction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Antidromic conduction (an action potential travelling backward along an axon) is not part of the normal stretch reflex circuit.

## answer_c
Ia fiber stimulation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ia fiber stimulation is the afferent (sensory) limb of the stretch reflex arising from the muscle spindle, but by itself it does not explain why the antagonist relaxes — that requires the specific circuit described by reciprocal innervation.

## answer_d
Reciprocal innervation

## explanation_d
Correct. In reciprocal innervation, the same Ia afferent that excites the agonist's alpha motor neurons monosynaptically also excites an inhibitory interneuron in the spinal cord, which in turn inhibits the antagonist muscle's motor neurons (disynaptically). This coordinated excitation of the agonist with inhibition of the antagonist allows the stretched muscle to contract while its opposing muscle relaxes, rather than the two working against each other. In short: identify reciprocal innervation as the spinal circuit responsible for antagonist relaxation during the stretch reflex.

Composition of the tectorial membrane

## question
Which component of the scala media is formed of gelatinous mass with collagen fibrils?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Stereocilia of the hair cells.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Stereocilia are actin-filled cellular processes of the hair cells themselves, not a gelatinous extracellular matrix.

## answer_b
The tectorial membrane.

## explanation_b
Correct. The tectorial membrane is a gelatinous, largely acellular structure made of collagen fibrils embedded in a glycoprotein matrix, overlying the organ of Corti within the scala media. Vibration of the basilar membrane creates a shearing force between it and the tectorial membrane, bending the stereocilia of the outer hair cells embedded within it and generating a receptor potential. In short: identify the tectorial membrane as the gelatinous, collagen-fibril-containing structure of the scala media, not the stereocilia, kinocilium or pillar cells.

## answer_c
Kinocilium of outer hair cells.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mature mammalian outer hair cells lose their functional kinocilium; in any case a kinocilium is a cellular structure, not a gelatinous mass.

## answer_d
The pillar cells.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pillar (rod) cells are cellular structural elements of the organ of Corti that form the tunnel of Corti, not a gelatinous mass.
