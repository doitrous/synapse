## title
Fourth pharyngeal pouch dorsal wing derivative

## question
Which of the following arises from the dorsal wing of the 4th pharyngeal pouch?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Superior parathyroid gland

## explanation_a
Incorrect. In this department's pouch-by-pouch, wing-by-wing grid, the superior parathyroid gland is the fourth pouch's VENTRAL-wing derivative, not the dorsal-wing derivative being asked about here.

## answer_b
Thymus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The thymus derives from the THIRD pharyngeal pouch's ventral wing, not the fourth pouch at all; the third pouch's dorsal wing instead gives the inferior parathyroid gland.

## answer_c
Ultimobranchial body

## explanation_c
Correct. The ultimobranchial body is the dorsal-wing derivative of the fourth pharyngeal pouch; it migrates to fuse with the developing thyroid gland and contributes the parafollicular (C) cells, which secrete calcitonin. This pairs with the third pouch's own dorsal/ventral split (inferior parathyroid, thymus) as a commonly confused four-way grid worth memorising by pouch and wing rather than as a flat list.

## answer_d
Inferior parathyroid gland

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The inferior parathyroid gland is a THIRD pouch (dorsal wing) derivative, not a fourth-pouch derivative; despite its dorsal-wing origin it ends up lower than the superior parathyroid because it travels a shorter migratory distance than the thymus, its third-pouch ventral-wing partner.

## title
Hypoglossal nerve crossing the carotid and lingual arteries

## question
Which of the following nerves crosses the internal carotid artery and the first part of the lingual artery during its course?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Vagus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The vagus nerve runs WITHIN the carotid sheath, in the groove between the internal jugular vein and the carotid artery, rather than crossing superficial to the carotid vessels and the lingual artery on the way to the tongue.

## answer_b
Glossopharyngeal

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The glossopharyngeal nerve passes between the internal and external carotid arteries higher in the neck, on its way to the pharynx and tongue base, a different relationship from crossing the carotid and first-part lingual artery.

## answer_c
Accessory

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The accessory nerve exits the carotid sheath early, crossing the internal jugular vein en route to the posterior triangle and sternocleidomastoid/trapezius, unrelated to the carotid/lingual artery crossing being described.

## answer_d
Hypoglossal

## explanation_d
Correct. The hypoglossal nerve (CN XII) descends in the neck and, on its way to the tongue, crosses superficial to the internal carotid artery and the first (proximal) part of the lingual artery, before looping over the lingual artery's later course and passing deep to the posterior belly of digastric to reach the tongue's musculature. Surgeons rely on this constant crossing relationship to identify the nerve during neck dissections, such as carotid endarterectomy or submandibular gland surgery.

## title
Argyll-Robertson pupil — the incorrect statement

## question
Which of the following statements about Argyll-Robertson pupil is INCORRECT?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Light reflex is absent

## explanation_a
This statement is TRUE, so it is not the answer being asked for: the pupillary light reflex is indeed absent in Argyll-Robertson pupil, the defining half of its light-near dissociation.

## answer_b
Accommodation reflex is present

## explanation_b
This statement is TRUE, so it is not the answer: the near (accommodation) reflex is preserved in Argyll-Robertson pupil, the other half of the dissociation that gives the sign its diagnostic value.

## answer_c
The lesion is in the Edinger-Westphal nucleus

## explanation_c
Correct — this is the FALSE statement being asked for. The lesion is not considered to sit within the Edinger-Westphal nucleus itself, because a true nuclear lesion there would knock out the efferent limb shared by both the light reflex AND the near reflex, abolishing accommodation as well. Instead, the lesion is thought to interrupt the light-reflex pathway further upstream, near the pretectal region, while sparing the separate route by which the cortically-mediated near response reaches the Edinger-Westphal nucleus — which is exactly why accommodation survives while the light reflex does not.

## answer_d
It occurs in neurosyphilis

## explanation_d
This statement is TRUE, so it is not the answer: Argyll-Robertson pupil is a classic sign of neurosyphilis (tabes dorsalis), historically one of its most recognisable ocular findings.

Dopamine — the unrelated statement

## question
Which of the following is NOT related to dopamine?

## subject
neuro

## status

## vignette

## answer_a
It is important in the treatment of renal failure

## explanation_a
Correct — this is the unrelated statement. Low-dose ('renal-dose') dopamine infusion was once used in critically ill patients in an attempt to protect renal perfusion by dilating renal vasculature. However, controlled trials found no meaningful renal protective benefit and the practice is now considered outdated, so it is not an established, evidence-based treatment for renal failure and this claim does not hold up as a genuine dopamine relationship.

## answer_b
It is important for working memory

## explanation_b
This statement IS related to dopamine, so it is not the answer: dopamine is central to prefrontal cortical circuits supporting working memory, and both excess and deficient dopaminergic tone can impair it.

## explanation_c
This statement IS related to dopamine, so it is not the answer: dysregulated dopaminergic transmission underlies schizophrenia's pathophysiology — mesolimbic dopamine excess drives positive symptoms, while mesocortical dopamine deficiency contributes to negative symptoms, so a deficiency-linked mechanism genuinely exists.

## answer_d
Its main metabolite is HVA

## explanation_d
This statement IS related to dopamine, so it is not the answer: homovanillic acid (HVA) is dopamine's principal metabolite, formed via monoamine oxidase and catechol-O-methyltransferase, and is used clinically/in research as a marker of dopamine turnover.

Internal capsule arterial supply — the exception

## question
The internal capsule is NOT supplied by which of the following arteries?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

A

## answer_a
Posterior cerebral artery

## explanation_a
Correct. The posterior cerebral artery chiefly supplies the occipital lobe and, via separate thalamoperforating branches, the thalamus, but it is not counted among the internal capsule's named supplying vessels — despite the thalamus's proximity, the internal capsule and thalamus have distinct arterial suppliers. This is a classically tested exam point precisely because the thalamus and internal capsule sit side by side yet draw on different named vessels.

Anterior choroidal artery

Incorrect (it DOES supply the capsule). The anterior cerebral artery, via its recurrent artery of Heubner, supplies the anterior limb of the internal capsule and the head of the caudate nucleus.

## answer_d
Middle cerebral artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it DOES supply the capsule). Lenticulostriate branches of the middle cerebral artery supply the genu and much of the posterior limb of the internal capsule, and are the vessels most often implicated in hypertensive internal capsule ('lacunar') strokes.

## title
The near response and Argyll-Robertson pupil

## question
Concerning the near response, which of the following is correct?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is abolished in lesions of the pretectal nucleus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The near response survives a pretectal-region lesion — that is precisely the light-near dissociation seen in Argyll-Robertson pupil, where the light reflex (which does depend on the pretectal relay) is lost while the near response is spared.

## answer_b
It is normal (preserved) in Argyll-Robertson pupil

## explanation_b
Correct. The near (triad) response — convergence, accommodation and pupillary constriction — remains normal in Argyll-Robertson pupil even though the separate pupillary light reflex is lost, which is exactly the light-near dissociation that makes the sign clinically useful for localising neurosyphilis. Recognising this preserved near response, contrasted with the absent light reflex, is exactly what makes the sign diagnostically useful at the bedside.

## answer_c
Convergence is important to increase depth of focus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. It is pupillary CONSTRICTION (miosis), the third component of the near triad, that increases depth of focus by narrowing the aperture, not convergence, whose role is to align both visual axes on the near target.

## answer_d
Accommodation occurs by increasing the curvature of the posterior surface of the cornea

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Accommodation increases the curvature of the ANTERIOR surface of the lens (via ciliary muscle contraction relaxing the lens's zonular tension), not the posterior surface of the cornea, which does not change shape during accommodation.

## title
Tuberculum impar and bifid tongue

## question
Which of the following occurs due to abnormal development of the tuberculum impar?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Bifid uvula

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A bifid uvula results from failure of fusion of the two palatine shelves in the midline posteriorly, a palate development anomaly unrelated to the tuberculum impar or tongue development.

## answer_b
Ankyloglossia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ankyloglossia (tongue-tie) is a short, tight lingual frenulum restricting tongue movement, unrelated to fusion of the lateral lingual swellings over the tuberculum impar.

Correct. ## answer_d
Hemiglossia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hemiglossia (a hypoplastic or absent half of the tongue) is a much rarer, more severe developmental field defect, not the classic consequence of isolated tuberculum impar fusion failure.

## title
Optic tract lesion and homonymous hemianopia

## question
Which of the following conditions is caused by a lesion of the optic tract?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Bitemporal hemianopia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bitemporal hemianopia results from a lesion at the optic CHIASM, which selectively damages the crossing nasal retinal fibers from both eyes, not from an optic tract lesion further downstream.

## answer_b
Blindness of both eyes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Bilateral blindness would require damage to both optic nerves (or both post-chiasmal pathways, or the visual cortex bilaterally); a unilateral optic tract lesion does not blind both eyes completely.

## answer_c
Contralateral homonymous hemianopia

## explanation_c
Correct. The optic tract carries fibers representing the CONTRALATERAL visual field from both eyes (the temporal, uncrossed fibers from the ipsilateral retina plus the nasal, crossed fibers from the contralateral retina), running from the chiasm to the lateral geniculate body. A lesion here therefore produces a contralateral homonymous hemianopia — loss of the same half of the visual field in both eyes, on the side opposite the lesion.

## answer_d
Ipsilateral homonymous hemianopia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Optic tract lesions produce a CONTRALATERAL, not ipsilateral, homonymous hemianopia, because the tract already carries a mixed (partially crossed) representation of the opposite visual field by the time it reaches this level.

## title
Corpus callosum is commissural, not a long association fiber

## question
One of the following does NOT belong to the long association fibers:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Corpus callosum

## explanation_a

Tumor

## title
Spinal shock and decreased venous return

## question
Which of the following would occur during the stage of spinal shock?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Marked rise in arterial blood pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Spinal shock causes HYPOtension, not a rise in arterial pressure, from loss of descending sympathetic vasomotor tone below the lesion.

## answer_b
Plantar reflex showing a positive Babinski's sign

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Reflexes, including the plantar response, are typically ABSENT during acute spinal shock; a positive Babinski sign more classically re-emerges as spinal shock RESOLVES, not during its acute phase.

## answer_c
Mass reflex is present

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The mass reflex is a later, exaggerated flexor withdrawal response that only appears once spinal shock has resolved, not during the acute shock phase itself.

## answer_d
Marked decrease in venous return

## explanation_d
Correct. Loss of sympathetic vasomotor tone below a complete spinal cord transection causes marked venous pooling in the now-unopposed capacitance vessels, producing a marked decrease in venous return and consequent hypotension. During this acute phase, tendon and cutaneous reflexes (including the plantar response) are also typically absent, only returning — and eventually becoming exaggerated — once spinal shock resolves.
