# Item

## id
QST-AU105-LL2-LL2-Q01

## title
Muscle that unlocks the knee

## question
Unlocking of the fully extended knee, before flexion can begin, is mainly done by:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Popliteus

## explanation_a
Correct. Popliteus arises from the lateral femoral condyle and inserts on the posterior tibia; contracting, it laterally rotates the femur on the fixed tibia, reversing the screw-home mechanism that locked the knee. It must act first, before the hamstrings can produce any flexion at all. A popliteus that cannot function leaves a knee that cannot begin to bend, however strong the hamstrings are.

## answer_b
Biceps femoris

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Biceps femoris is a hamstring that flexes the already-unlocked knee; it plays no part in the initial unlocking rotation.

## answer_c
Quadratus femoris

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Quadratus femoris is a lateral rotator of the hip joint and has no action at the knee at all.

## answer_d
Gastrocnemius muscle

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gastrocnemius crosses the knee and weakly assists flexion once it is unlocked, and plantarflexes the ankle, but it is not the muscle responsible for the unlocking rotation itself.

## answer_e
Plantaris muscle

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Plantaris is a vestigial muscle contributing weakly to knee flexion and ankle plantarflexion; it has no unlocking role.

## title
Insertion of the superficial posterior compartment

## question
All muscles of the superficial posterior compartment of the leg insert into:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Fibula

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The fibula receives muscles of the lateral and deep posterior compartments (peroneus longus/brevis, part of soleus's origin), not the insertion of the superficial group.

## answer_b
Talus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The talus has no muscular insertions at all — tendons cross it but attach elsewhere, chiefly the calcaneus and navicular.

## answer_c
Calcaneus

## explanation_c
Correct. Gastrocnemius, soleus and plantaris all converge on the tendo calcaneus (Achilles tendon), which inserts on the posterior surface of the calcaneus. This shared insertion is why all three act, directly or indirectly, to plantarflex the ankle at the subtalar/calcaneal lever.

## answer_d
First metatarsal

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The first metatarsal receives tibialis anterior and part of peroneus longus, both from other compartments, not the superficial posterior group.

## answer_e
Cuboid

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The cuboid receives no superficial posterior compartment muscle; peroneus longus's tendon merely grooves it en route to the first metatarsal/medial cuneiform.

## title
Anterior tibial nerve position at the ankle

## question
While passing deep to the superior extensor retinaculum, the anterior tibial (deep peroneal) nerve lies medial to:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Extensor hallucis longus muscle

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Extensor hallucis longus lies medial to the neurovascular bundle at this level, not lateral to it — so the nerve is lateral to, not medial to, extensor hallucis longus.

## answer_b
Extensor digitorum longus muscle

## explanation_b
Correct. From medial to lateral at the ankle the order is: tibialis anterior tendon, extensor hallucis longus tendon, anterior tibial vessels with the deep peroneal nerve, then extensor digitorum longus tendon and peroneus tertius. The deep peroneal nerve therefore lies medial to extensor digitorum longus.

## answer_c
Tibialis anterior muscle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Tibialis anterior's tendon is the most medial structure at this level, well medial to the nerve, not lateral to it.

## answer_d
Anterior tibial artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The anterior tibial artery runs immediately alongside the deep peroneal nerve as a paired neurovascular bundle, not as a separate structure the nerve sits medial to.

## answer_e
Peroneus tertius muscle

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Peroneus tertius is the most lateral tendon at the ankle, well lateral to the nerve — the nerve's medial relation is to extensor digitorum longus, which lies between it and peroneus tertius.

## title
Dorsiflexors of the foot

## question
All of the following muscles are dorsiflexors of the foot EXCEPT:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Peroneus tertius

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it IS a dorsiflexor). Peroneus tertius, part of the anterior compartment, dorsiflexes and everts the ankle.

## answer_b
Tibialis anterior

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it IS a dorsiflexor). Tibialis anterior is the prime mover of dorsiflexion, also inverting the foot.

## answer_c
Extensor hallucis longus

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it IS a dorsiflexor). Extensor hallucis longus dorsiflexes the ankle as it extends the great toe.

Peroneus longus

Correct. Peroneus longus is a lateral compartment muscle, supplied by the superficial peroneal nerve, that plantarflexes and everts the foot. It is not a dorsiflexor, unlike the other three anterior-compartment options. Its tendon crosses the sole to attach near the base of the first metatarsal, powering eversion and supporting the transverse arch, actions unrelated to dorsiflexion.

## title
Tibial nerve supply — the hamstring exception

## question
All of the following muscles are supplied by the tibial (medial popliteal) nerve EXCEPT:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Short head of biceps femoris

## explanation_a
Correct. The short head of biceps femoris is the one hamstring supplied by the common peroneal division of the sciatic nerve rather than the tibial division — it acts only at the knee and is the classic exception that survives a tibial-division-selective sciatic injury. This division-based split is the standard way this bank distinguishes a proximal (undivided) sciatic injury, which paralyses all hamstrings, from a more distal tibial-division lesion, which spares this one muscle.

Gastrocnemius

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it IS tibial-supplied). Gastrocnemius is a superficial posterior compartment muscle, supplied throughout by the tibial nerve.

## answer_c
Soleus

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it IS tibial-supplied). Soleus is likewise supplied by the tibial nerve.

## answer_d
Plantaris

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it IS tibial-supplied). Plantaris, the third superficial posterior compartment muscle, is also tibial-nerve-supplied.

Dorsiflexion and eversion together

## question
Which ONE of the following muscles is responsible for dorsiflexion AND eversion of the foot?

## subject
msk
