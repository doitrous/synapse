Normal arterial pH

## question
What is the normal arterial PH?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
7.40 ± 0.02

## explanation_a
Correct. Arterial blood pH is tightly regulated by the bicarbonate buffer system, respiratory CO2 excretion and renal acid handling to a mean of about 7.40, with a normal range of roughly 7.36-7.44. This value is the reference point against which any acidosis or alkalosis is defined.

## answer_b
7.38 ± 0.02

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 7.38 ± 0.02 sits within the low-normal edge of the physiological range but is not the conventional reference mean taught for normal arterial pH.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 7.36 ± 0.02 is at the lower boundary of the normal range and would already be flagged as borderline acidemia on a blood gas, not the typical reference value.

## answer_d
7.34 ± 0.02

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 7.34 ± 0.02 falls below the normal range altogether and represents mild acidemia, not the normal arterial pH.

Normal glomerular filtration rate

## question
What is the normal GFR?

## subject
renal

## status

## explanation_a
Correct. The average adult glomerular filtration rate is approximately 125 mL/min (roughly 180 L/day), a figure derived from inulin- or creatinine-based clearance studies. This value is the textbook reference for normal renal function and the denominator against which clinical estimated-GFR readings are compared. Incorrect. 110 mL/min is below the conventional 125 mL/min reference value for normal adult GFR.

## answer_d
80

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 80 mL/min is well below normal GFR and would instead suggest at least moderate renal impairment, not a healthy adult's filtration rate.

Genuine anterior pituitary hormone

Which of the following hormones is released from anterior pituitary?

## subject

## title
Effect of sudden loss of the parathyroid glands

## question
What will happen to an adult if he suddenly loses his parathyroid glands?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Skeletal muscles spasm

## explanation_a
Correct. Sudden loss of all parathyroid tissue removes parathyroid hormone's tonic support of plasma calcium, and plasma calcium falls acutely. Acute hypocalcemia increases neuromuscular excitability, producing tetany — skeletal muscle spasm, classically starting as carpopedal spasm (Trousseau's sign) or facial twitching (Chvostek's sign). This acute neuromuscular presentation, not a skeletal or soft-tissue change, is what follows within hours to days of parathyroid loss.

## answer_b
Bone fractures

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Bone fractures from excess resorption are a feature of chronic PTH excess (hyperparathyroidism), not of acute PTH loss, which instead produces acute hypocalcemic tetany.

## answer_c
Overgrowth of soft tissues

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Overgrowth of soft tissues describes acromegaly (growth hormone excess), unrelated to parathyroid hormone loss.

## answer_d
Peripheral neuropathy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peripheral neuropathy is not the acute consequence of parathyroid loss; the acute picture is hypocalcemic tetany from increased neuromuscular excitability, not nerve damage.

## title
Acid-base consequence of aldosterone hypersecretion

## question
Which of the following is caused by hypersecretion of aldosterone?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Hyponatremia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Aldosterone increases renal sodium reabsorption, so it tends to raise, not lower, plasma sodium (or leave it near-normal via volume expansion) — hyponatremia is not the expected finding.

## answer_b
Hyperkalemia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Aldosterone promotes potassium secretion at the collecting duct principal cells, producing hypokalemia, not hyperkalemia — the opposite direction from this option.

## answer_c
Alkalosis

## explanation_c
Correct. Aldosterone's sodium-potassium exchange at the collecting duct is coupled to hydrogen ion secretion into the tubular lumen. Sustained aldosterone excess therefore drives excess H+ loss in the urine alongside the K+ loss, producing a mild metabolic alkalosis. This alkalosis, paired with hypokalemia, is the classic biochemical signature of hyperaldosteronism.

## answer_d
Hypotension

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Aldosterone-driven sodium and water retention expands extracellular volume and raises, not lowers, blood pressure — hyperaldosteronism classically causes hypertension, not hypotension.

## title
Reabsorption in the diluting segment

## question
Which of the following are reabsorbed in the diluting segment?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Sodium and chloride

## explanation_a
Correct. The thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle is called the 'diluting segment' precisely because it reabsorbs sodium and chloride via the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter while remaining essentially impermeable to water. Removing solute without water dilutes the tubular fluid, so what leaves this segment is hypotonic relative to plasma — the defining feature tested here.

## answer_b
Water and sodium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. If water were reabsorbed alongside sodium, the tubular fluid would not become dilute; the diluting segment's defining property is that it stays water-impermeable while removing solute.

## answer_c
Sodium and ammonium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ammonium handling is a proximal tubule and collecting duct acid-base function, not the diluting segment's defining transport.

## answer_d
Water and urea

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Water is not reabsorbed here (the segment is water-impermeable), and urea reabsorption is instead a feature of the inner medullary collecting duct.

## title
Factor that increases glomerular filtration rate

## question
Which of the following increases GFR?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Increased filtration coefficient

## explanation_a
Correct. GFR is the product of net filtration pressure and the filtration coefficient (Kf), which reflects glomerular capillary surface area and permeability. Raising Kf directly raises GFR for any given filtration pressure, making it a straightforward determinant among the four options.

## answer_b
Afferent arteriole vasoconstriction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Afferent arteriole constriction reduces glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure and blood flow into the glomerulus, which lowers, not raises, GFR.

## answer_c
Efferent arteriole vasodilation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Efferent arteriole dilation lowers resistance to outflow, reducing glomerular capillary pressure and GFR; it is efferent constriction (in moderation) that raises GFR.

## answer_d
Sympathetic stimulation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sympathetic stimulation constricts the afferent arteriole (and reduces renal blood flow generally), lowering GFR rather than increasing it.

## answer_d
Sacral plexus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The sacral plexus is the nerve network through which the reflex's efferent and afferent fibres travel, but the reflex itself is described by its spinal cord level, not by the peripheral plexus name.

Role of the vasa recta in the countercurrent mechanism

## question
What is the role of vasa recta in counter current mechanism?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Maintaining the hyperosmolality of the interstitial fluid

## explanation_a
Correct. The vasa recta are hairpin peritubular capillaries that run alongside the loop of Henle into the medulla. Secrete sodium and chloride in renal tubules

Reabsorb sodium and water from renal tubules

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Reabsorption of sodium and water from the tubular lumen back into blood is performed by the tubular epithelium and peritubular capillaries generally; describing the vasa recta's countercurrent role this way misses their gradient-preserving function specifically.

## answer_d
Create a hyperosmolar interstitial fluid

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Creating the medullary hyperosmolar gradient is the loop of Henle's countercurrent multiplier's role; the vasa recta preserve, rather than create, a gradient already established by the loop.
