# Item

## title
Venous openings into the right atrium

## question
The superior and inferior venae cavae open into which part of the right atrium?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The rough atrium proper, anterior to the crista terminalis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The rough atrium proper, lined by pectinate muscles, lies anterior to the crista terminalis and receives the right auricle, not the two venae cavae.

## answer_b
The smooth-walled sinus venarum, posterior to the crista terminalis

## explanation_b
Correct. The crista terminalis, a muscular ridge, divides the right atrium into an anterior, rough-walled atrium proper (derived embryologically from the primitive atrium and lined by pectinate muscles) and a posterior, smooth-walled sinus venarum (derived from the absorbed sinus venosus); the superior vena cava opens into the upper part of the sinus venarum and the inferior vena cava into its lower part, the latter guarded by a vestigial valve. This smooth-versus-rough distinction, with both great veins opening into the smooth part, is exactly what the practical bank's labelled images of the right atrium's interior test. Locating the venous openings in the smooth sinus venarum rather than the rough, pectinate-muscle-lined atrium proper is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_c
The right auricle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The right auricle is a small conical pouch projecting from the rough atrium proper; the venae cavae do not open into it.

## answer_d
The atrial septum, directly into the fossa ovalis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The fossa ovalis is a depression on the atrial septum, the remnant of the foramen ovale, a separate structure from either venous opening.

## title
Contents of the anterior interventricular groove

## question
Which two vessels run together in the anterior interventricular groove of the heart?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The right coronary artery and the small cardiac vein

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The right coronary artery runs the coronary sulcus, not the anterior interventricular groove, and the small cardiac vein accompanies it there, not in the anterior interventricular groove.

## answer_b
The anterior interventricular (left anterior descending) artery and the great cardiac vein

## explanation_b
Correct. The anterior interventricular artery, the left coronary artery's larger terminal branch (commonly the "LAD"), runs down the anterior interventricular groove accompanied by the great cardiac vein, which begins near the apex and ascends alongside it before curving back in the coronary sulcus to become the coronary sinus. Together these two vessels, one arterial and one venous, occupy the same groove and supply/drain much of the anterior interventricular septum and adjacent ventricular walls. Pairing the anterior interventricular artery with the great cardiac vein, rather than the small or middle cardiac veins which lie elsewhere, is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_c
The circumflex artery and the coronary sinus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The circumflex artery runs in the left part of the coronary (atrioventricular) sulcus, not the anterior interventricular groove; the coronary sinus itself lies in the posterior coronary sulcus, receiving the great cardiac vein rather than running alongside the anterior interventricular artery.

## answer_d
The posterior interventricular artery and the middle cardiac vein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The posterior interventricular artery and the middle cardiac vein occupy the POSTERIOR interventricular groove, the mirror-image structure on the diaphragmatic surface of the heart, not the anterior groove.

# Item

## id
QST-AU106ANAT-ADD-ADD-Q07

## title
Nerve relations of the arch of the aorta

## question
As the left vagus nerve crosses the left side of the arch of the aorta, which branch does it give off there, and what other nerve also lies in close relation to the arch at this point?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It gives off the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, which hooks beneath the arch beside the ligamentum arteriosum; the left phrenic nerve also crosses the arch's left side, anterior to the vagus

## explanation_a
Correct. As the left vagus nerve descends across the left side of the arch of the aorta, it gives off the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, which hooks beneath the arch immediately beside the ligamentum arteriosum before ascending in the tracheo-oesophageal groove to supply the larynx; the left phrenic nerve runs down separately, anterior to the vagus, also crossing the arch's left side on its way to the diaphragm. This shared left-sided relation to the arch, vagus and phrenic both crossing it with the recurrent laryngeal nerve branching off and hooking under it, is exactly why the arch of the aorta is a classic site of injury to both nerves in aortic arch aneurysm or surgery, producing hoarseness and/or a paralysed hemidiaphragm. Distinguishing which nerve hooks under the arch (the recurrent laryngeal) from which merely crosses its surface (the phrenic) is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_b
It gives off the left phrenic nerve, which hooks beneath the arch; the recurrent laryngeal nerve lies posterior to it

## explanation_b
Incorrect. It is the recurrent laryngeal nerve, not the phrenic nerve, that hooks beneath the arch; the phrenic nerve instead continues down the arch's surface toward the diaphragm without hooking back.

## answer_c
It gives off the external laryngeal nerve, which hooks beneath the arch; the right phrenic nerve lies anterior to it

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The vagus gives off the recurrent laryngeal nerve at the arch, not the external laryngeal nerve, which branches much higher, from the vagus in the neck; the phrenic nerve relation described is also the LEFT phrenic nerve, not the right.

## answer_d
It gives no branch at the arch; both the phrenic and recurrent laryngeal nerves cross only the pulmonary trunk, not the aorta

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Both nerves do relate directly to the arch of the aorta itself, on its left side, the recurrent laryngeal nerve hooking beneath it and the phrenic nerve crossing its surface, not to the pulmonary trunk.

## title
Coronary sulcus contents and the conducting system

## question
Which vessel runs in the coronary (atrioventricular) sulcus and, in most people, supplies the whole conducting system of the heart?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The circumflex artery

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The circumflex artery, a branch of the LEFT coronary artery, runs the left part of the coronary sulcus and supplies the left atrium and part of the left ventricle; it supplies the conducting system only in the minority of people with left dominance.

## answer_b
The right coronary artery

## explanation_b
Correct. The right coronary artery runs the coronary sulcus between the right atrium and right ventricle, giving off the right marginal artery and, on reaching the posterior/diaphragmatic surface, the posterior interventricular artery; in the majority of people it also supplies the sinoatrial and atrioventricular nodes, making it the dominant vessel for the heart's conducting system as well as its whole right side. The coronary sinus, the venous counterpart running the posterior part of the same sulcus, drains alongside it but does not itself supply the conducting system. Recognising the right coronary artery, not the circumflex artery or the venous system, as the usual supply to the conducting system is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_c
The anterior interventricular artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The anterior interventricular artery runs the anterior interventricular groove, not the coronary sulcus, and mainly supplies the anterior septum and ventricular walls.

## answer_d
The great cardiac vein alone

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The great cardiac vein is a venous structure that drains, rather than supplies, the heart, and does not itself perfuse the conducting system.

## title
Origin of the internal thoracic artery

## question
The internal thoracic artery arises from which vessel?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The first part of the subclavian artery

## explanation_a
Correct. The internal thoracic (internal mammary) artery arises from the first part of the subclavian artery, medial to scalenus anterior, alongside the vertebral artery and the thyrocervical trunk, the first part's three named branches. From its origin it descends behind the upper six costal cartilages, about a fingerbreadth from the sternal edge, to end opposite the sixth intercostal space by dividing into the musculophrenic and superior epigastric arteries. Recognising the first part of the subclavian artery, rather than the thyrocervical trunk (a sibling branch of the same part) or a more distal segment, as its true origin is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_b
The thyrocervical trunk

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The thyrocervical trunk is itself a separate branch of the first part of the subclavian artery, a sibling of the internal thoracic artery, not its parent vessel.

## answer_c
The second part of the subclavian artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The second part of the subclavian artery, behind scalenus anterior, gives only the costocervical trunk, not the internal thoracic artery.

## answer_d
The axillary artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The axillary artery, the subclavian's continuation beyond the first rib, is far too distal and lateral to be this vessel's origin.

QST-AU106ANAT-ADD-ADD-Q12

## title
Formation and termination of the superior vena cava

## question
The superior vena cava is formed by the union of which two veins, and where does it enter the right atrium?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The two brachiocephalic veins, uniting behind the first right costal cartilage; it enters the right atrium behind the third right costal cartilage

## explanation_a
Correct. The superior vena cava is formed by the union of the two brachiocephalic veins behind the lower border of the first right costal cartilage; about 5 cm long, it descends to open into the right atrium behind the third right costal cartilage, receiving the azygos vein, its one named tributary, from behind just before it pierces the fibrous pericardium at the second right costal cartilage. Each brachiocephalic vein is itself formed, in the root of the neck, by the union of an internal jugular and a subclavian vein on the same side, a separate, earlier union from the one that forms the SVC itself. Getting the SVC's own formation (two brachiocephalic veins) and termination level (behind the third right costal cartilage) right, distinct from the brachiocephalic veins' own formation further upstream, is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_b
The two internal jugular veins, uniting behind the manubrium; it enters the right atrium behind the second right costal cartilage

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The two internal jugular veins do not unite to form the SVC; each internal jugular unites with its own side's subclavian vein instead, forming a brachiocephalic vein, one step upstream of the SVC.

## answer_c
The right brachiocephalic vein and the azygos vein, uniting behind the second right costal cartilage; it enters the right atrium behind the fourth right costal cartilage

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The azygos vein is the SVC's tributary, entering it near its termination, not a vessel that unites with the right brachiocephalic vein to form it; the SVC is formed purely by the two brachiocephalic veins.

## answer_d
The two subclavian veins, uniting behind the sternoclavicular joint; it enters the right atrium behind the first right costal cartilage

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The two subclavian veins do not unite directly; each subclavian instead unites with its own side's internal jugular vein to form a brachiocephalic vein, which is the vessel that unites with its pair to form the SVC.

Azygos vein impression on the right lung

## question
On the mediastinal surface of the RIGHT lung, which of the following produces a groove or impression?

resp

## owner

The pulmonary trunk

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pulmonary trunk contributes to the cardiac impression anteriorly on the left lung, not to a distinct right-lung groove.

## title
Tracheal bifurcation level and anterior relations

## question
At what level does the trachea bifurcate into the two principal bronchi, and which structure lies immediately anterior to its thoracic part?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The sternal angle (~T4); the manubrium and left brachiocephalic vein lie anterior

## explanation_a
Correct. The trachea, continuing the larynx from the lower border of C6, descends through the neck and thorax to bifurcate into the two principal bronchi at the sternal angle, roughly the T4 vertebral level; its thoracic part lies with the manubrium and the left brachiocephalic vein anteriorly, the oesophagus and left recurrent laryngeal nerve behind, and the great vessels of the superior mediastinum flanking it on either side. The sternal angle is a useful external landmark precisely because it marks both the tracheal bifurcation internally and the plane separating the superior from the inferior mediastinum. Locating the bifurcation at the sternal angle (T4), with the manubrium and left brachiocephalic vein anterior to the trachea at that level, is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_b
The suprasternal notch (~T2); the thymus alone lies anterior

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The suprasternal notch marks roughly where the trachea enters the thorax from the neck, not where it bifurcates; and the manubrium and left brachiocephalic vein, not the thymus alone, lie anterior to the thoracic trachea.

## answer_c
The xiphisternal junction (~T9); the pericardium lies anterior

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The xiphisternal junction is far too low and marks the inferior end of the sternum, well below the level of tracheal bifurcation.

## answer_d
The cricoid cartilage (~C6); the thyroid isthmus lies anterior

## explanation_d
Incorrect. C6 and the cricoid cartilage mark where the trachea BEGINS, continuing the larynx, not where it bifurcates; the thyroid gland constricts the trachea near its upper end, not at the bifurcation.

## title
The cardiac notch of the left lung

## question
The cardiac notch, a deep concavity on the anterior border of the lung that leaves part of the pericardium uncovered, is a feature of which lung?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The right lung only

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The right lung's anterior border is nearly straight, without a comparable deep notch; the cardiac notch is a left-lung feature.

## answer_b
The left lung

## explanation_b
Correct. The left lung's anterior border carries a deep cardiac notch, which together with the lingula, the left lung's small tongue-shaped projection analogous to the right middle lobe, leaves part of the pericardium and heart uncovered by lung tissue at the front of the chest. The left lung's mediastinal surface, above and behind the cardiac impression, is further grooved by the arch of the aorta and the descending aorta. This left-sided cardiac notch, absent from the right lung's more gently curved anterior border, is the feature this item tests.

## answer_c
Both lungs equally

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The cardiac notch is asymmetric, a left-lung feature only, reflecting the heart's own leftward-shifted position in the thorax.

## answer_d
Neither lung — it is a feature of the pericardium only

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cardiac notch is a feature of the lung's own anterior border, specifically the left lung, not of the pericardium.

## title
Origin and diaphragmatic opening of the oesophagus

## question
At what level does the oesophagus begin, continuing from the pharynx, and where does it leave the thorax through the diaphragm?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Begins at C6 (lower border of the cricoid cartilage); leaves through the diaphragm's oesophageal opening at T10

## explanation_a
Correct. The oesophagus continues the pharynx from the level of the cricoid cartilage's lower border (C6), enters the thorax as the most posterior structure of the superior mediastinum, and, after crossing to the left in the posterior mediastinum, leaves the thorax through the diaphragm's own oesophageal opening at the T10 level, about an inch left of the midline. This is one of the diaphragm's three named openings, distinct from the more posterior and higher aortic opening (T12) and the more anterior, higher caval opening (T8). Pairing the oesophagus's C6 origin with its own T10 diaphragmatic opening, not the aortic or caval openings, is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_b
Begins at C3; leaves through the diaphragm's aortic opening at T12

## explanation_b
Incorrect. C3 is too high for the oesophagus's origin, and the aortic opening at T12 belongs to the aorta and thoracic duct, not the oesophagus.

## answer_c
Begins at T1; leaves through the diaphragm's caval opening at T8

## explanation_c
Incorrect. T1 is not the oesophagus's origin; it begins higher, at C6, continuing the pharynx, and the caval opening at T8 belongs to the inferior vena cava, not the oesophagus.

## answer_d
Begins at C6; leaves through the diaphragm's caval opening at T8

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The origin at C6 is correct, but the oesophagus passes through its OWN oesophageal opening at T10, not through the caval opening at T8, which is reserved for the inferior vena cava.

Diaphragmatic aortic opening: level and contents

## question
The aortic opening of the diaphragm lies at which vertebral level, and which structures pass through it besides the aorta?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
T12; the azygos vein and thoracic duct

## explanation_a
Correct. The aortic opening, the lowest and most posterior of the diaphragm's three major openings, lies at the T12 level, strictly retro-diaphragmatic between the two crura, and transmits the aorta together with the azygos vein and the thoracic duct, both ascending from the abdomen alongside it. This is distinct from the oesophageal opening (T10, transmitting the oesophagus and vagus nerves) and the caval opening (T8, transmitting the inferior vena cava and right phrenic nerve). Pairing the aortic opening's T12 level with its own accompanying structures, the azygos vein and thoracic duct, rather than those of the other two openings, is the discrimination this item tests.

T10; the vagus nerves

## explanation_b
Incorrect. T10 is the oesophageal opening's level, which transmits the oesophagus and vagus nerves, not the aorta.

## answer_c
T8; the right phrenic nerve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. T8 is the caval opening's level, which transmits the inferior vena cava and right phrenic nerve, not the aorta.

## answer_d
T12; the oesophagus and vagus nerves

## explanation_d
Incorrect. T12 is the correct level for the aortic opening, but the oesophagus and vagus nerves pass through the separate oesophageal opening at T10, not through the aortic opening.

## title
Lobes and fissures of the right lung

## question
How many lobes does the right lung have, and which fissure is unique to it (absent from the left lung)?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Three lobes; the horizontal fissure, separating the upper and middle lobes

## explanation_a
Correct. The right lung has three lobes, upper, middle and lower, separated by two fissures: an oblique fissure, present in both lungs, and a horizontal fissure unique to the right lung, which splits off the middle lobe from the upper lobe. The left lung, by contrast, has only two lobes, upper and lower, divided by its own oblique fissure alone, with the lingula standing in as the left lung's small analogue of the right middle lobe rather than a separate lobe. Knowing that the horizontal fissure, not the oblique fissure which is present in both lungs, is the feature unique to the right lung is the discrimination this item tests.

## answer_b
Two lobes; the oblique fissure, separating the upper and lower lobes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Two lobes describes the left lung, not the right, which has three; and the oblique fissure is present in both lungs, not unique to either.

## answer_c
Three lobes; the oblique fissure, unique to the right lung

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Three lobes is correct for the right lung, but the oblique fissure is present in both lungs, not a feature unique to the right.

## answer_d
Two lobes; the horizontal fissure, separating the upper and lower lobes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The right lung has three lobes, not two, and its horizontal fissure separates the upper from the MIDDLE lobe, not the upper from the lower lobe.

Diaphragmatic caval opening: level and companion nerve

## question
The caval opening of the diaphragm lies at which vertebral level, and which nerve accompanies the inferior vena cava through it?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
T8; the right phrenic nerve

## explanation_a
Correct. The caval opening, the highest and most anterior of the diaphragm's three major openings, lies within the central tendon at the T8 level and transmits the inferior vena cava together with the right phrenic nerve, which accompanies it down onto the diaphragm's upper surface. T12; the vagus nerves

## explanation_c
Incorrect. T12 is the aortic opening's level, transmitting the aorta, azygos vein and thoracic duct, not the IVC or vagus nerves.

## answer_d
T8; the splanchnic nerves

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The splanchnic nerves pierce the diaphragm's crura separately, not through the caval opening, and are not the nerve that accompanies the IVC.
