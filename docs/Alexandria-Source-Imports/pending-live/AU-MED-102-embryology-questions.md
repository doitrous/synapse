# Item

## title
The part that gives rise to the embryo

## question
The part that gives rise to the embryo is….

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette


## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inner cell mass

## explanation_a
Correct. The inner cell mass (embryoblast) is the cluster of cells attached to the inner
wall of the trophoblast at the embryonic pole of the blastocyst, and it alone gives rise to
the embryo proper — first splitting into the bilaminar disc (epiblast and hypoblast), then
forming all three germ layers at gastrulation. The trophoblast, by contrast, forms the
fetal side of the placenta and membranes, not the embryo itself.

## answer_b
Outer cell mass

## explanation_b
Incorrect. There is no structure named this way in the blastocyst; this distractor tests
whether a student has confused the inner cell mass with the trophoblast, which is the
single outer layer of the blastocyst wall and does not form the embryo.

## answer_c
Zona pellucida

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The zona pellucida is the acellular glycoprotein coat around the oocyte and
early embryo; it degenerates as the blastocyst hatches and plays no part in forming the
embryo's own tissues.

## answer_d
Yolk sac

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The yolk sac is an extra-embryonic membrane that forms after the embryonic disc
is already established; it contributes the gut endoderm and early blood cells but is not
itself the source of the embryo.

# Item

## id
QST-AU-MED-102-EMBR-002

## title
Origin of the amniotic cavity

## question
The amniotic cavity arises from….

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette


## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hypoblast

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The hypoblast forms the floor of the bilaminar disc and later lines the primary
yolk sac; the space that becomes the amniotic cavity opens above the epiblast, not the
hypoblast, which is the trap this distractor is built to catch.

## answer_b
Epiblast

## explanation_b
Correct. On the eighth day, a cavity opens within the remainder of the inner cell mass,
separating two populations: the amnioblasts, which line the roof of the new space, and the
epiblast, tall columnar cells that form its floor. That space is the amniotic cavity, and
because it opens directly above the epiblast layer, the epiblast is the structure students
should name as its point of origin.

## answer_c
Endoderm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Endoderm is one of the three germ layers formed later, at gastrulation in the
third week; the amniotic cavity is already present a week earlier, before endoderm exists.

## answer_d
Mesoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mesoderm, like endoderm, is a third-week gastrulation product and does not yet
exist at the second-week stage when the amniotic cavity forms.

# Item

## id
QST-AU-MED-102-EMBR-003

## title
Site of normal implantation

## question
The site of normal implantation of the blastocyst is…..

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette


## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Upper segment of uterus

## explanation_a
Correct. Implantation normally occurs in the endometrium of the upper posterior (or,
less often, anterior) wall of the uterus, near the fundus — the upper segment — where the
secretory-phase endometrium is thickened and richly vascular enough to support the
syncytiotrophoblast as it erodes into it. Implantation outside this normal site, whether
lower in the uterus or entirely outside the uterine cavity, is what defines the abnormal
implantations (placenta previa, ectopic pregnancy) covered elsewhere in this chapter.

## answer_b
Corn of uterus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cornu (horn) of the uterus is an abnormal, interstitial site of ectopic
implantation, not the normal one — this distractor catches a student who has the right
general area (uterus) but the wrong specific site.

## answer_c
Fallopian tube

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Tubal implantation is the commonest form of ectopic pregnancy, not the normal
site; it results from a delay in the fertilised ovum's transport to the uterine cavity.

## answer_d
Ovary

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ovarian implantation is a rare form of ectopic pregnancy, on the surface of the
ovary itself, and is never the normal site.

Structure of the tertiary chorionic villi

## question
What is the structure of the tertiary chorionic villi?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette


## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This describes the primary villus, which is a solid core of proliferating
cytotrophoblast pushing the syncytiotrophoblast, before any mesoderm or vessels enter it.

## answer_b
Cytotrophoblast, syncytiotrophoblast and extraembryonic mesoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This describes the secondary villus, once somatic extra-embryonic mesoderm has
entered the trophoblastic core — the step before the tertiary stage, which additionally
needs fetal blood vessels.

Cytotrophoblast, syncytiotrophoblast, extra embryonic mesoderm, umbilical blood vessels

Correct. A villus becomes tertiary once fetal blood vessels appear within the
extraembryonic mesoderm core, alongside the cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast layers
already present from the primary and secondary stages. This is the stage at which the
villus can actually carry fetal blood, which is what makes it the functional exchange unit
of the placenta — the answer a student should recognise as "everything the earlier stages
had, plus vessels."

## answer_d
Cytotrophoblast and extraembryonic mesoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This omits the syncytiotrophoblast, which is present at every stage from
primary onward and is never lost as the villus matures.

## title
Embryonic origin of a sacrococcygeal mass

## question
After the delivery the baby was found to have a mass in the sacrococcygeal region. What is
the embryonic origin of this mass?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette


## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Primitive streak

## explanation_a
Correct. A sacrococcygeal teratoma arises from incompletely regressed remnants of the
primitive streak — a totipotent population of cells at the caudal end of the embryo. If
this population fails to disappear normally once gastrulation is complete, it can persist
and give rise to a tumour containing tissue from all three germ layers, which is why the
mass presents in exactly this caudal midline site. The thing to remember is that the
streak's totipotency, not any single germ layer, is what explains the tumour's mixed
tissue content.

## answer_b
Ectoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ectoderm alone would not account for the mixed-tissue nature typical of a
sacrococcygeal teratoma; the streak's totipotent cells, not one committed germ layer, are
the origin the question tests.

## answer_c
Notochord

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This distractor catches confusion between two caudal midline structures: the
notochord's own remnant is the nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral disc, an entirely
different fate from a sacrococcygeal mass.

## answer_d
Mesoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. As with ectoderm, mesoderm alone is a committed single germ layer and does not
explain the mixed tissue types that characterise this tumour.

## title
Cause of cephalocaudal folding

## question
Cephalocaudal folding occurs due to rapid growth of the……

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette


## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Central nervous system

## explanation_a
Correct. Cephalocaudal (longitudinal) folding is driven by the rapid growth of the neural
tube, particularly the developing brain, which outpaces the growth of the tissue around
it. This overgrowth forces the head and tail ends of the flat disc to curve ventrally,
carrying the septum transversum and the developing heart with it. The thing to remember is
that this is a distinct mechanism from transverse folding, which is driven by the somites
instead.

## answer_b
Somites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Somite growth drives transverse (lateral) folding, which brings the two lateral
edges of the disc together to close the ventral body wall — a different plane of folding
from the cephalocaudal one this question asks about.

## answer_c
Notochord

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The notochord organises the axial midline and induces the neural plate, but it
is not itself the structure whose overgrowth drives folding.

## answer_d
Primitive streak

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The primitive streak is the site of gastrulation in the third week and has
already done its work of forming the germ layers by the time folding begins; it is not the
driver of folding itself.

The structure found most cranially before folding………..

## subject

## status

## vignette


## correct_answer

## answer_a
Oral membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Folding reverses the disc's cranio-caudal order: the oral (bucco-pharyngeal)

opposite of what this question asks.

## answer_b
Cardiogenic plate

becomes caudal to the developing heart, while the bucco-pharyngeal (oral) membrane —
originally caudal to it — becomes the new most-cranial structure. Holding this reversal in
mind is what lets a student answer either version of the question, before or after
folding.

## answer_d
Pericardial cavity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pericardial cavity develops within the cardiogenic area, which sits caudal
to the septum transversum before folding, not cranial to it.

Folding as the disc-to-cylinder process

## question
What's the process that converts the embryo from a trilaminal disc form into a

## subject

Folding

endometrium; it happens well before the disc exists in trilaminar form.

Fertilization

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fertilisation is the very first step of development, restoring the diploid
chromosome number; it has no direct role in shaping the disc into a cylinder weeks later.

## answer_a
Allantois

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The allantois is a separate diverticulum that buds from the caudal wall of the

Gut tube

## explanation_b

Amnion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The amnion develops separately from the amnioblasts roofing the amniotic
cavity; it has no developmental relationship to the yolk sac.
