## title
Which plasma protein mainly holds blood volume in the vessels?

## question
Which plasma protein mainly holds blood volume in the vessels?

## subject
haem

## status

## owner

## vignette
A student is asked which single plasma protein is mainly responsible for keeping fluid inside the circulation rather than letting it filter out into the tissues.

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Globulin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The globulins carry smaller molecules and provide immune defence (as immunoglobulins), but they are present at a much lower plasma concentration than albumin and contribute far less to the oncotic pressure that holds water in the capillary.

## answer_b
Albumin

## explanation_b
Correct. Albumin is the most abundant plasma protein, and because it is both plentiful and too large to cross the capillary wall easily, it is the main source of the plasma's oncotic (colloid osmotic) pressure — the force that pulls water back into the capillary and opposes the hydrostatic pressure pushing fluid out. A fall in albumin (from liver disease or nephrotic loss) lets fluid leak into the tissues as oedema, which is the clinical signature of this function failing.

## answer_c
Fibrinogen

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fibrinogen is the substrate of the clotting cascade — it polymerises into fibrin during coagulation — not the protein responsible for retaining plasma volume in the vessels.

## answer_d
Prothrombin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prothrombin is a clotting factor, activated to thrombin during coagulation. It is present at a low plasma concentration and plays no meaningful role in maintaining blood volume.
