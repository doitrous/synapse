# Item

## id
QST-DEV-FERTILIZATION-SITE-01

## title
Fertilization occurs in the:

## question
Fertilization occurs in ...............

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Ampulla

## explanation_a
Correct. Fertilisation normally takes place in the ampulla of the uterine tube, the widest and longest part of the tube, where the ovulated oocyte is met by sperm that have travelled up through the cervix, uterus and tube. The ampulla's width gives the sperm and oocyte enough space and time together for the sperm to complete capacitation and the acrosome reaction before penetrating the corona radiata and zona pellucida. If fertilisation does not occur within about 24 hours of ovulation, the oocyte degenerates before reaching this site.

## answer_b
Fundus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The fundus is the upper part of the uterine body; it is where the fertilised egg's blastocyst normally implants roughly a week later, not where fertilisation itself happens.

## answer_c
Cervix

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The cervix is the lower, narrow part of the uterus through which sperm must first pass on their way to the tube; fertilisation happens much further along the sperm's journey.

## answer_d
Vagina

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The vagina is where semen is deposited at intercourse, the very start of the sperm's journey, well before fertilisation occurs in the tube.

## id
QST-DEV-CAPACITATION-DEFINITION-01

## title
Loss of the glycoprotein coat over the sperm head is called:

## question
The process of loss of glycoprotein coat present over the head of the sperm is called

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Capacitation

## explanation_a
Correct. Capacitation is the process, occurring in the female genital tract, that strips the glycoprotein coat and seminal plasma proteins from the plasma membrane overlying the sperm's acrosomal region. It is a prerequisite step, not the fertilising event itself: only a capacitated sperm can go on to bind zona pellucida receptors and undergo the acrosome reaction. Without capacitation first removing this surface coat, a sperm cannot penetrate the corona radiata and zona pellucida around the oocyte.

## answer_b
Acrosomal reaction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The acrosome reaction is the release of acrosomal enzymes (acrosin, hyaluronidase) that happens after capacitation, when the sperm binds the zona pellucida — it is a different, later step, not the coat-loss step itself.

## answer_c
Cortical reaction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The cortical reaction is the oocyte's own response after a sperm has fused with it, releasing cortical granules that block polyspermy; it happens in the egg, not on the sperm's surface.

## answer_d
Implantation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Implantation is the blastocyst embedding into the endometrium about a week after fertilisation, unrelated to the sperm's own surface coat.

## title
The morula consists of how many cells?

## question
The morula consists of ........... cells.

## subject
dev

## status

## owner

A

## answer_a
16

## explanation_a
Correct. After fertilisation the zygote divides mitotically inside the zona pellucida, doubling from 2 blastomeres on the first day to 4 on the second and 8 on the third; at 16 cells the solid ball of blastomeres is named the morula. 4

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 4 cells is the second-day cleavage stage, two divisions before the morula is reached.

## answer_d
64

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 64 cells is well past the morula stage, into a much more advanced blastocyst.

The morula forms on which day after fertilization?

## question
The morula formed at ........... Day after fertilization.

## subject

## status

## explanation_a

## answer_b
Second

## explanation_b
Incorrect. On the second day the embryo is at the 4-cell stage, still two divisions short of the morula.

Third

## explanation_c

Fourth

## explanation_d
Incorrect. By the fourth day the morula has already formed (on day three) and is arriving at the uterine cavity, not forming for the first time.

## title
The cavity present in the embryo at the first week is:

## question
The cavity which presents in the embryo at the first week is ...........

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Chorionic cavity

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The chorionic cavity appears later, in the second week, once extra-embryonic mesoderm has formed around the yolk sac.

## answer_b
Blastocyst

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Blastocyst names the whole structure (trophoblast wall, blastocele and embryoblast together), not the cavity itself.

## answer_c
Blastocele

## explanation_c
Correct. Once the morula reaches the uterine cavity, the zona pellucida begins to degenerate and uterine fluid seeps in between the blastomeres; these fluid-filled spaces run together into a single cavity, the blastocele, completing the blastocyst by the end of the sixth day — still within the first week. The blastocele is the first true cavity to appear in the conceptus, before the amniotic cavity or the chorionic cavity exist. Its appearance is what turns the solid morula into the hollow blastocyst.

## answer_d
Amniotic cavity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The amniotic cavity opens above the epiblast during the second week, after the blastocele and bilaminar disc have already formed.

## title
The part of trophoblast covering the embryoblast is called:

## question
A part of trophoblast that covers the embryo blast is called ...........

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ampulla

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The ampulla is a region of the uterine tube where fertilisation occurs, unrelated to trophoblast anatomy.

## answer_b
Coelom

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A coelom is a body cavity that appears much later in development (extra-embryonic and intra-embryonic coelom), not a part of the trophoblast.

## answer_c
Embryonic pole

## explanation_c
Correct. The blastocyst wall (trophoblast) surrounds the blastocele, and the inner cell mass (embryoblast) lies against one side of that wall, inside it. It is by the embryonic pole that the blastocyst attaches to the endometrium and begins implantation, so this naming is not just descriptive — it marks the side that actually implants first.

## answer_d
Amnioblast

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The amnioblast is a cell layer that appears later, roofing the amniotic cavity above the epiblast, not the trophoblast covering the embryoblast.

## question
The implantation begins at ........... after fertilization.

## subject

## explanation_a

## explanation_d
Incorrect. By day 8 the blastocyst is already partly embedded and forming the bilaminar disc, one day after implantation began.

## question

## subject
dev

## status

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Fallopian tube

## explanation_a
Correct. Abnormal implantation is grouped in two ways: intrauterine but too low (placenta previa) and entirely outside the uterine cavity (ectopic pregnancy). Ectopic pregnancy itself is overwhelmingly tubal — most often in the ampulla, but also the isthmus or intramural part of the tube — making the fallopian tube by far the most common site of abnormal implantation overall. Cervix of the uterus

## answer_c
Ovary

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ovarian implantation is a recognised but rare form of ectopic pregnancy, much less common than tubal implantation.

## answer_d
Peritoneal cavity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peritoneal (abdominal) implantation is the rarest of the ectopic sites, well behind the tube.

## title
Implantation occurs when which structure ruptures?

## question
The implantation occurs when …........... ruptures:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cytotrophoblast

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cytotrophoblast is a cell layer of the trophoblast that forms after implantation has begun, not a structure that ruptures to allow implantation.

## answer_b
Zona pellucida

## explanation_b
Correct. The morula and early blastocyst are held together and protected inside the zona pellucida, an acellular glycoprotein coat, which must first degenerate — beginning at the end of the fifth day — before the blastocyst can hatch out and its trophoblast can make direct contact with, and then invade, the endometrium. Implantation itself cannot begin while the zona pellucida is still intact, since it is a physical barrier between the trophoblast and the uterine lining. This is why the zona pellucida's own breakdown is described as the event that permits implantation to occur.

## answer_c
Chorionic vesicle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The chorionic vesicle is a later structure of the second week, formed well after implantation has already started.

## answer_d
Placenta

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The placenta does not exist yet at the time of implantation; it develops over subsequent weeks from the trophoblast and decidua.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-UMBILICAL-CORD-ATTACHMENT-01

## title
The umbilical cord is attached to the embryo and to:

## question
The umbilical cord attached to the embryo and ..........

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Maternal surface of the placenta

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The maternal surface of the placenta faces the decidua basalis and is where the placenta attaches to the uterine wall, not where the umbilical cord attaches.

## answer_b
Fetal surface of placenta

## explanation_b
Correct. The umbilical cord runs from the embryo (fetus) to the placenta's fetal surface, where the umbilical vessels spread out over the surface covered by amnion before diving into the chorionic plate. The placenta's fetal surface is smooth and amnion-covered, in contrast to the rough, cotyledon-marked maternal surface facing the decidua basalis — and the cord's own attachment point (central, marginal/battledore, or velamentous) is always described relative to this fetal surface. Naming the fetal, not maternal, surface as the cord's attachment is the key distinction this item is testing.

## answer_c
Endometrium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The endometrium (as decidua) surrounds the placenta and pregnancy generally; the cord itself attaches to the placenta's own fetal surface, not directly to the endometrium.

## answer_d
Myometrium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The myometrium is the uterine muscle wall, deep to the endometrium/decidua; the cord has no direct attachment to it.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-DEFINITIVE-CORD-EXCEPT-01

## title
Which is NOT present in the definitive umbilical cord?

## question
All of the following presents in the definitive umbilical cord, except:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Two umbilical arteries

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Two umbilical arteries are genuinely present in the definitive cord, not the exception.

## answer_b
Right umbilical vein

## explanation_b
Correct. As the primitive cord matures into the definitive cord, one of the two original umbilical veins — the right umbilical vein — obliterates, leaving only the left umbilical vein in the definitive cord alongside the two umbilical arteries. The definitive cord therefore holds two umbilical arteries and one umbilical vein (the left), all embedded in Wharton's jelly and sheathed by amnion. Naming the right, not the left, umbilical vein as the one that disappears is the specific fact this exception item tests.

## answer_c
Left umbilical vein

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The left umbilical vein genuinely persists in the definitive cord (only the right vein obliterates), not the exception.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Since only the right umbilical vein is absent, 'all of the above' is not correct — the arteries and the left vein are both genuinely present.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-PLACENTA-FUNCTIONS-EXCEPT-01

## title
Which of the following is NOT a function of the placenta?

## question
All of the followings are functions of placenta, except:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Endocrine function

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Endocrine function (secreting progesterone, oestrogen, hCG and somatomammotropin) is a genuine placental function, not the exception.

## answer_b
Metabolic function

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Metabolic (exchange) function is a genuine placental function, not the exception.

## answer_c
Regulate temperature

## explanation_c
Correct. The placenta's genuine functions are exchange (transport of gases and metabolic products), excretion of fetal waste, transmission of maternal antibodies, and acting as an endocrine gland secreting progesterone, oestrogen, human chorionic gonadotropin and somatomammotropin. Regulating fetal temperature is not among these — that role belongs instead to the amniotic fluid, which acts as a thermal insulator keeping the fetal temperature constant. Splitting these two roles between two different structures (temperature to the fluid, exchange/endocrine/transport to the placenta) is exactly what this exception item is testing.

## answer_d
Transport function

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Transport function is a genuine placental function, not the exception.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-EXOMPHALOS-DEFINITION-01

## title
Loops of small intestine in the umbilical cord is called:

## question
The presence of loops of small intestine in the umbilical cord is called ...........

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Exompholus

## explanation_a
Correct. Normally the herniated intestinal loops of the primitive umbilical cord (a physiological umbilical hernia) return fully to the abdominal cavity by about the tenth week; when they fail to return, the proximal cord persists containing loops of small intestine, and this congenital anomaly is named exomphalos (omphalocele). The department book files this specifically as an anomaly of the cord's contents, distinct from anomalies of its length, vessels or attachment. Naming it exomphalos, rather than the more generic 'congenital hernia', is the precise term this item is testing.

## answer_b
Congenital hernia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 'Congenital hernia' is a generic term; the specific named anomaly of intestinal loops persisting in the umbilical cord is exomphalos.

## answer_c
Battledore's cord

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Battledore's cord is an anomaly of the cord's attachment to the placenta (at its margin), unrelated to intestinal contents.

## answer_d
Velamentous

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Velamentous is an anomaly of the cord's attachment (through the amniotic membrane before reaching the placenta), unrelated to intestinal contents.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-UMBILICAL-CORD-FACTS-EXCEPT-01

## title
Which of the following is NOT true about the umbilical cord?

## question
All of the followings are true about the umbilical cord, except:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Admin team

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Carry the blood from the mother through the umbilical arteries

## explanation_a
Correct. The two umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated blood away from the fetus, toward the placenta — from the fetus, not from the mother — while the single umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood back from the placenta to the fetus. Describing the arteries as carrying blood from the mother reverses both the direction of flow and which vessel does which job, which is exactly the error this exception item is built to catch. The cord's other listed features — allowing the fetus free movement within the amniotic cavity and containing Wharton's jelly for protection — are both genuinely true.

## answer_b
Carry the blood from the fetus through the umbilical arteries

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This is the genuinely true statement (the umbilical arteries do carry blood from the fetus), so it is not the exception.

## answer_c
Allow free movement to the embryo

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Allowing free fetal movement within the amniotic cavity is a genuine, true feature of the umbilical cord, not the exception.

## answer_d
Contains jelly material

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Containing Wharton's jelly (a mucoid connective tissue) is a genuine, true feature of the umbilical cord, not the exception.
