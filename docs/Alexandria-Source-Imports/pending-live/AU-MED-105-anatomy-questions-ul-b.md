## title
Branch of the second part of the axillary artery

## question
One of the following is a branch from the 2nd part of the axillary artery:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Lateral thoracic artery

## explanation_a
Correct. Pectoralis minor crosses the axillary artery and divides it into three parts, each giving a set number of branches (one, two, three): the first part (medial to pectoralis minor) gives the superior thoracic artery; the second part (behind pectoralis minor) gives the thoraco-acromial and lateral thoracic arteries; the third part (lateral to pectoralis minor) gives the subscapular, anterior and posterior circumflex humeral arteries. The lateral thoracic artery, running along serratus anterior, is one of the second part's two branches.

## answer_b
Subscapular artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The subscapular artery is one of the three branches of the third part.

## answer_c
Superior thoracic artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior thoracic artery is the sole branch of the first part.

## answer_d
Anterior circumflex humeral artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The anterior circumflex humeral artery is one of the three branches of the third part.

## title
Muscle forming the medial wall of the axilla

## question
Which muscle forms the medial wall of the axilla?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Serratus anterior

## explanation_a
Correct. Serratus anterior, overlying the upper ribs, forms the medial wall of the axilla. The long thoracic nerve runs along this same muscle's superficial surface, which is why both structures are vulnerable together during axillary surgery or a penetrating chest-wall injury. This pairing — medial wall muscle and the nerve that supplies it lying right on its surface — is a frequently tested spatial relation.

## answer_b
Pectoralis major

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pectoralis major (with pectoralis minor and the clavipectoral fascia) forms the anterior wall, not the medial wall.

## answer_c
Teres major

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Teres major (with latissimus dorsi) forms the posterior wall, not the medial wall.

## answer_d
Subclavius

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Subclavius sits above the axilla proper, contributing to the clavipectoral fascia near the apex, not the medial wall.

The axilla contains all the following EXCEPT:

## subject

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect (true, not the exception). Axillary fat fills the spaces between the named contents.

## answer_b
Trunks of the brachial plexus

## explanation_b
Correct. The trunks of the brachial plexus form higher up, in the posterior triangle of the neck, above the clavicle; only the cords (and their branches) of the plexus have formed by the time the neurovascular bundle reaches the axilla. Incorrect (true, not the exception). The axillary tail of the breast (of Spence) projects into the axilla's anterior wall region.

## answer_d
Axillary vein

## explanation_d
Incorrect (true, not the exception). The axillary vein, formed by the basilic vein and the venae comitantes of the brachial artery, runs through the axilla alongside the artery.

## title
Medial boundary of the apex of the axilla

## question
The medial boundary of the apex of the axilla is formed by:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Acromion process of scapula

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The acromion process is not one of the apex's three boundaries.

## answer_b
Lateral third of clavicle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The clavicle's posterior border forms the apex's anterior boundary, not its medial one, and it is the whole posterior border rather than specifically the lateral third.

## answer_c
Surgical neck of humerus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The surgical neck of the humerus is not related to the axillary apex at all; it is related instead to the axillary nerve and posterior circumflex humeral artery lower down, at the quadrangular space.

## answer_d
Outer border of the 1st rib

## explanation_d
Correct. The apex of the axilla — the cervico-axillary canal through which the neurovascular bundle enters — is bounded by the posterior border of the clavicle (anteriorly), the outer border of the first rib (medially), and the upper border of the scapula (posteriorly). This narrow triangular opening is why axillary vessel and plexus injuries at the apex are functionally equivalent to injuries just below the clavicle.

## answer_b
Radial nerve

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The radial nerve lies at the fossa's lateral boundary, the opposite side from where this question is asking.

## answer_c
Median nerve

## explanation_c
Correct. The median nerve is the most medial structure in the cubital fossa, so surgery on the medial side of the fossa places it at greatest risk — the same lateral-to-medial ordering (biceps tendon, brachial artery, median nerve) that makes this the consistent answer whenever a cubital fossa question specifies the medial side. This is why surgeons approaching the cubital fossa from its medial side take particular care to identify and protect the nerve before proceeding, in contrast to a lateral approach where the biceps tendon is the main structure at risk instead.
