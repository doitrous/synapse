## label
Complete oxidation of one acetyl-CoA through the citric acid cycle yields 12 ATP

## canonical_key
tca.atp-yield.acetyl-coa-complete-oxidation

## aliases
TCA cycle ATP yield
Citric acid cycle energy yield

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
One turn of the citric acid cycle from acetyl-CoA yields 3 NADH, 1 FADH2 and 1 GTP directly. Oxidised through the electron transport chain and conventional P:O ratios, this is taught as a total of 12 ATP equivalents per acetyl-CoA.

## explicit_objective
State the ATP yield from complete oxidation of one acetyl-CoA through one turn of the citric acid cycle.

## pitfalls
Confusing the ATP yield of one acetyl-CoA (12) with the yield of one whole glucose molecule (which enters as two acetyl-CoA after glycolysis and pyruvate dehydrogenase, and so totals far more).

Mature erythrocytes extrude their nucleus and all organelles, including mitochondria, during maturation, leaving them dependent entirely on anaerobic glycolysis for ATP. Identify the mature erythrocyte as the tissue that cannot run the citric acid cycle, because it has no mitochondria.

## pitfalls
Assuming every human cell type runs the citric acid cycle; the anucleate, organelle-free mature erythrocyte is the standard exam exception.

## definition
Beta-oxidation of an odd-chain fatty acid ends with a three-carbon residue, propionyl-CoA, instead of the two-carbon acetyl-CoA that even-chain fatty acids yield. Biotin-dependent propionyl-CoA carboxylase converts it to methylmalonyl-CoA, which is rearranged to succinyl-CoA, a citric acid cycle intermediate that enters before the irreversible pyruvate dehydrogenase step and so can be drawn off into gluconeogenesis.

## pitfalls
Assuming all products of fatty acid breakdown are equally non-glucogenic; the odd-chain-specific three-carbon residue is the exception that enters gluconeogenesis, unlike acetyl-CoA and the ketone bodies built from it.

Anaerobic glycolysis invests 2 ATP in its preparatory phase and generates 4 ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation in its payoff phase, for a net yield of 2 ATP per glucose. State that anaerobic glycolysis yields a net 2 ATP per glucose, with NADH reoxidised via lactate formation rather than the electron transport chain.

## pitfalls
Quoting the higher aerobic glycolysis/full oxidation ATP figures when the question specifically asks about the anaerobic pathway, which stops at lactate and yields only the net glycolytic 2 ATP.

## definition
Glycogen phosphorylase catalyses phosphorolysis (cleavage by inorganic phosphate, not water) of the alpha-1,4 glycosidic bonds linking glucose units in glycogen's linear chains, releasing glucose-1-phosphate from the non-reducing ends. ## pitfalls
Confusing glycogen phosphorylase's alpha-1,4 phosphorolysis with debranching enzyme's separate action at alpha-1,6 branch points, or with glycogen synthase's opposite, synthetic role.

Vitamin E (alpha-tocopherol) sits within the phospholipid bilayer of cell membranes, where its chromanol ring donates a hydrogen atom to neutralise peroxyl radicals generated during lipid peroxidation. Name vitamin E as the major lipid-soluble antioxidant protecting cell membrane phospholipids from peroxidation.

## pitfalls
Confusing vitamin E's membrane-lipid antioxidant role with vitamin C's water-soluble antioxidant role, or with vitamin A/D/K's unrelated fat-soluble functions.

## definition
Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I and ornithine transcarbamylase operate in the mitochondrial matrix of hepatocytes, producing citrulline, which is transported to the cytoplasm where argininosuccinate synthetase, argininosuccinase and arginase complete the cycle. The urea cycle is liver-specific — the kidney lacks a complete set of urea cycle enzymes and cannot synthesise urea de novo.

## explicit_objective
State that the urea cycle's enzymes are split between the mitochondria and cytoplasm of the liver, and no other organ runs the full cycle.

## pitfalls
Assuming the urea cycle is confined to one compartment (all-mitochondrial or all-cytoplasmic), or that another organ such as the kidney can run the complete cycle.

Respiration is briefly inhibited, with glottic closure, during the vomiting reflex

## canonical_key
vomitingreflex.mechanics.respiration-inhibited-glottis-closed

## aliases
Vomiting reflex mechanics
Airway protection during vomiting

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
During vomiting, the vomiting centre coordinates a brief but complete inhibition of respiration, with the glottis closing to protect the airway from aspiration of gastric contents. This respiratory pause is timed with diaphragmatic and abdominal muscle contraction, which raises intra-abdominal and intragastric pressure to drive expulsion, while the lower esophageal (cardiac) sphincter relaxes to allow contents to pass upward.

## explicit_objective
State that respiration is briefly inhibited, with glottic closure, during the vomiting reflex to protect the airway.

## pitfalls
Assuming the diaphragm relaxes during vomiting (it contracts, alongside abdominal muscles, to raise intra-abdominal pressure) or that the cardiac sphincter contracts (it relaxes to allow expulsion).

The hepatopancreatic ampulla is the shared bile-pancreatic duct outlet whose gallstone obstruction causes biliary reflux pancreatitis

## canonical_key

## aliases
Hepatopancreatic ampulla anatomy
Gallstone pancreatitis mechanism

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
The hepatopancreatic ampulla (ampulla of Vater) is the short common channel where the common bile duct and main pancreatic duct join just before opening into the duodenum. A gallstone impacted here obstructs this shared outlet, so rising pressure forces bile to reflux backward into the pancreatic duct, prematurely activating pancreatic enzymes and producing gallstone (biliary reflux) pancreatitis.

## explicit_objective
Identify the hepatopancreatic ampulla as the shared bile-pancreatic duct outlet whose obstruction by a gallstone causes biliary reflux pancreatitis.

## pitfalls
Placing the obstructing stone anywhere upstream of the bile-pancreatic duct confluence (common bile duct, cystic duct or common hepatic duct); a stone there cannot force bile into the pancreatic duct, since the two systems have not yet joined.

## definition
The esophagus is lined along the great majority of its length by stratified squamous non-keratinised epithelium, suited to the mechanical wear of swallowed food. Its short terminal segment, at the junction with the stomach (the cardia), transitions to simple columnar epithelium matching the adjacent gastric mucosa; the upper third of the esophageal wall also contains striated muscle, transitioning to smooth muscle lower down, but this is a separate, muscular feature from the epithelial lining.

## explicit_objective
State that the esophagus is lined predominantly by stratified squamous epithelium, with a short transition to simple columnar epithelium at the gastroesophageal junction.

## pitfalls
Describing the esophagus as uniformly stratified squamous with no columnar segment at all, or, in the other direction, describing it as lined by simple or stratified columnar epithelium throughout, rather than recognising the short gastroesophageal transition zone as the exception within an otherwise stratified squamous tube.
