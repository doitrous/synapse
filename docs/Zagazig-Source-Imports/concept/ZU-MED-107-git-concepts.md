Antimycin blocks the electron transport chain at Complex III

## canonical_key
biochem.etc.antimycin-complex-iii-block

## aliases

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Antimycin A binds cytochrome b within Complex III (ubiquinol–cytochrome c oxidoreductase) and blocks electron transfer from ubiquinol to cytochrome c, stopping oxidative phosphorylation at that point in the chain. It is distinguished from rotenone, which blocks Complex I, and cyanide/azide, which block Complex IV.

## explicit_objective
Name Complex III as antimycin's site of block in the electron transport chain, and distinguish it from the Complex I and Complex IV inhibitor sites.

## pitfalls
Confusing antimycin (Complex III) with rotenone (Complex I) — both are classic exam "which complex does X inhibit" pairs, and mixing up the complex number loses the mark even when the general concept (an ETC blocker) is understood.

## label
Succinyl-CoA synthetase is the site of substrate-level phosphorylation in the TCA cycle

## canonical_key
tca.substrate-level-phosphorylation.succinyl-coa-synthetase

## aliases
Substrate-level phosphorylation in TCA cycle
Succinate thiokinase
GTP generation in Krebs cycle

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Substrate-level phosphorylation — phosphorylation driven directly by the energy of a reaction, without the electron transport chain — occurs once in the TCA cycle, at the succinyl-CoA synthetase (succinate thiokinase) step, where succinyl-CoA is converted to succinate and GTP (readily converted to ATP) is generated. Every other TCA step that yields energy does so by handing electrons to NAD+ or FAD for later oxidative phosphorylation, not by substrate-level phosphorylation directly.

## explicit_objective
Identify succinyl-CoA synthetase as the one TCA-cycle step that performs substrate-level phosphorylation, as distinct from the NAD+/FAD-linked dehydrogenase steps.

## pitfalls
Assuming any energy-yielding TCA step is "substrate-level phosphorylation" — isocitrate dehydrogenase, malate dehydrogenase and aconitase all participate in the cycle but do not phosphorylate a nucleotide directly; only the succinyl-CoA synthetase step does.

## definition
Cytochrome P450 is a haem-containing monooxygenase (mixed function oxidase): it incorporates one atom of molecular oxygen into the substrate (as a hydroxyl group) while reducing the other atom to water, using NADPH as the electron donor. This phase 1 reaction acts on both endogenous compounds (steroids, fatty acids) and exogenous compounds (drugs, toxins) — it is not restricted to exogenous substrates — and it is not formed of 450 amino acids; the name refers to the 450 nm absorbance peak of its reduced, carbon-monoxide-bound form.

## explicit_objective
State that cytochrome P450 acts as a monooxygenase in phase 1 xenobiotic metabolism, and correct the common distractors that it acts only on exogenous compounds or is a di-oxygenase.

## pitfalls
Reading "P450" as a literal amino-acid count, and assuming phase 1 cytochrome P450 metabolism is restricted to drugs and toxins — it also hydroxylates endogenous steroids and fatty acids.

Chymotrypsin is an endopeptidase that cleaves peptide bonds at aromatic amino acids

## canonical_key
chymotrypsin.specificity.aromatic-residue-endopeptidase

## aliases

Aromatic amino acid cleavage
Pancreatic endopeptidases

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Chymotrypsin is a pancreatic endopeptidase — it cleaves peptide bonds in the middle of the polypeptide chain, not at either end — with specificity for the carboxyl side of the bulky aromatic amino acids phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan. It has an alkaline (not acidic) pH optimum and, like trypsin, is secreted as an inactive zymogen (chymotrypsinogen), activated by trypsin rather than in active form.

## explicit_objective
Identify chymotrypsin as the endopeptidase specific for peptide bonds on the carboxyl side of aromatic amino acids, distinct from trypsin (basic residues) and the exopeptidases.

## pitfalls
Confusing chymotrypsin's specificity (aromatic residues, endopeptidase) with trypsin's (basic residues, also endopeptidase) or with the exopeptidases (carboxypeptidase, aminopeptidase), which trim single residues from the chain ends rather than cutting mid-chain.

## label
Acetyl-CoA is not a substrate for gluconeogenesis in mammals

## canonical_key
gluconeogenesis.substrates.acetyl-coa-excluded

## aliases
Gluconeogenic substrates
Non-gluconeogenic substrates
Pyruvate carboxylase bypass

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Lactate, alanine and glycerol are all genuine substrates for gluconeogenesis — lactate and alanine feed in via pyruvate, glycerol via dihydroxyacetone phosphate. Acetyl-CoA cannot be a substrate: the pyruvate dehydrogenase reaction that produces it is irreversible, and mammals have no enzyme to convert acetyl-CoA's two carbons back to a TCA-cycle intermediate net of the two carbons lost as CO2 in one turn of the cycle, so fatty-acid-derived acetyl-CoA cannot yield net new glucose.

## explicit_objective
Name acetyl-CoA as the option that cannot serve as a gluconeogenic substrate, and explain why, as distinct from lactate, alanine and glycerol, which can.

## pitfalls
Assuming that because acetyl-CoA enters the TCA cycle, it can be converted to glucose — the two carbons acetyl-CoA contributes are exactly balanced by two carbons lost as CO2 in one turn of the cycle, so there is no net carbon skeleton available for gluconeogenesis.

## label
Epinephrine and glucagon phosphorylate glycogen phosphorylase into its active form and glycogen synthase into its inactive form at the same time

## canonical_key
glycogen.hormonalcontrol.reciprocal-phosphorylation-phosphorylase-synthase

## aliases
Reciprocal regulation of glycogen metabolism
Glycogen phosphorylase and synthase phosphorylation state
Hormonal control of glycogenolysis

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Epinephrine and glucagon both raise cAMP and activate protein kinase A, which phosphorylates two enzymes in opposite functional directions at once: glycogen phosphorylase is phosphorylated into its active (a) form, driving glycogenolysis, while glycogen synthase is phosphorylated into its inactive form, shutting off glycogen synthesis. The two enzymes are not phosphorylated at "significantly different rates" as a single loose process — the phosphorylation is coordinated so that breakdown and synthesis are switched in opposite directions together, and net glycogen synthesis falls rather than rises under these hormones.

## explicit_objective
State that epinephrine/glucagon signalling phosphorylates glycogen phosphorylase to its active state and glycogen synthase to its inactive state simultaneously, and reject the distractors describing independent rates or increased net synthesis.

## pitfalls
Treating phosphorylation as uniformly "activating" — it activates glycogen phosphorylase but inactivates glycogen synthase, the reciprocal control that makes hormonal signalling switch net glycogen flux in one direction rather than leaving both enzymes running against each other.

## label
The ileocecal sphincter is relaxed by ileal distension, gastrin and gastroileal reflex activity, but overdistension of the cecum keeps it closed

## canonical_key
ileocecalsphincter.relaxation-triggers.gastroileal-reflex

## aliases
Ileocecal sphincter
Gastroileal reflex
Ileocecal valve regulation

## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
The ileocecal sphincter normally stays mildly constricted, slowing the emptying of ileal contents into the caecum. Distension of the ileum, gastrin (released as part of the gastroileal reflex when food enters the stomach), and distension of the stomach itself all promote its relaxation to allow chyme through. Overdistension of the caecum has the opposite effect: it triggers reflex contraction of the sphincter, preventing further filling and backflow of caecal contents into the ileum.

## explicit_objective
Identify caecal overdistension as the factor that closes (not relaxes) the ileocecal sphincter, contrasted with the relaxing effects of ileal distension, gastric distension and gastrin.

## pitfalls
Assuming distension anywhere in the gut has the same effect on the ileocecal sphincter — distension proximal to it (stomach, ileum) relaxes it to allow forward flow, while distension distal to it (caecum) closes it to prevent reflux, a directional logic students often miss.

## definition
Chief (zymogenic) cells, found mainly at the base of the gastric glands, are basophilic cells packed with apical zymogen granules containing pepsinogen (and gastric lipase); this granular, secretory ultrastructure is what identifies them histologically. Parietal cells are instead identified by an extensive intracellular canalicular system and abundant mitochondria, mucous (neck) cells by apical mucinogen droplets, and enteroendocrine cells by small basal secretory granules — none of these carry the apical zymogen granules that mark chief cells.

## explicit_objective
Identify chief cells as the gastric cell type marked by apical zymogen granules, distinct from parietal, mucous neck and enteroendocrine cells.

## pitfalls
Confusing "granules" in general with the specific apical zymogen granules of chief cells — parietal cells and enteroendocrine cells also have distinctive ultrastructural features, but only chief cells show the pepsinogen-containing zymogen granules this question tests.

## definition
Fungiform papillae are mushroom-shaped papillae scattered mainly at the tip and lateral margins of the tongue, in contrast to the much larger, circumvallate papillae that form a V-shaped row at the junction of the anterior two-thirds and posterior third and carry the most numerous taste buds. Fungiform papillae are covered by non-keratinized (not keratinized stratified squamous) epithelium and have relatively few taste buds on their upper surfaces, rather than "numerous".

## explicit_objective
State that fungiform papillae are scattered especially at the tongue's tip, distinguishing their distribution, epithelium and taste-bud density from circumvallate and filiform papillae.

## pitfalls
Attributing filiform papillae's keratinised covering, or circumvallate papillae's numerous taste buds and greatest-numbers status, to fungiform papillae — each of the three main papilla types has its own distinct combination of shape, distribution, covering epithelium and taste-bud density, and this bank tests exactly those distinguishing features.
