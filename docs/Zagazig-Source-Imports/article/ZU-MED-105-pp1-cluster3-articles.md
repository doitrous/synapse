## summary
This chapter works through eight foundational self-directed-learning (SDL) facts from mcq شامل.pdf pages 2-3: the general definition of learning, the narrower definition of SDL itself (initiative-taking, with or without help), the combined definition of learning materials, the three types of learning resources, the one non-function among five genuine functions of learning resources, the three benefits of SDL taken together, the two broad method subdivisions of SDL (facilitated and self-paced learning), and the specific definition of self-paced learning. These eight facts build the vocabulary the rest of this module's SDL material depends on.

## sections
### Definition
Learning is the general process of acquiring new understanding, knowledge, behaviours, skills, values, attitudes and preferences. Self-directed learning (SDL) narrows this: it is a process in which the learner takes the initiative, with or without help from others, rather than an instructor driving the process. Learning materials combine two roles this source treats as inseparable: the curriculum-aligned resources teachers use, and the procedures and devices that make teaching and learning more effective. Learning resources split into three types by sensory channel: audio, visual, and combined visual-audio. Self-paced learning specifically needs the learner to be motivated, oriented towards learning, and competent to choose suitable resources without another person setting the pace.
### Mechanism
Each of these eight facts turns on correctly scoping one SDL-vocabulary term against a plausible neighbour term. Learning and SDL are distinguished by who initiates the process: any instructor or curriculum can drive learning in general, but only the learner's own initiative defines SDL specifically, even where help from others is still involved. Learning materials versus learning resources are distinguished by role (curriculum-alignment and teaching-effectiveness devices) versus channel (audio/visual/visual-audio). Facilitated learning and self-paced learning are distinguished by who sets the pace: a facilitator in the first case, the learner alone in the second, which is exactly why self-paced learning demands the learner's own motivation, orientation and resource-selection competence that facilitated learning does not require to the same degree.
### Key determinants
Each question in this chapter turns on picking the SDL-vocabulary term this source's own key names, most often by combining two partial descriptions into one complete answer rather than accepting either half alone: learning materials (curriculum-alignment description plus procedures-and-devices description, both required), learning-resource functions (five genuine functions against one exception, "increase the hope", that this source does not credit), SDL benefits (soft skills, independence and collaboration together, not any one alone), and SDL methods (facilitated and self-paced learning together, against a "providing a template" distractor).
### Clinical significance
A shared, precise vocabulary for learning, self-directed learning, learning materials and resource types is the foundation every later fact in this module's SDL material depends on: a learner cannot correctly identify which implementation step, competency, or assessment method applies without first knowing what SDL itself means and how it differs from learning in general. Building this vocabulary early, before the process- and plan-level material in the companion chapters, mirrors how this source's own bank sequences the same content across its first ten pages.

## summary
This chapter works through nine self-directed-learning (SDL) implementation and progression facts from mcq شامل.pdf pages 4-6: the implementation step of adjusting and adapting to suit student (not doctor) needs, the goal-setting implementation step, evaluating the effectiveness of learning, the one non-competency among four genuine SDL competencies, the self-referential process of self-directed learning, the mindset factor of belief about intelligence and learning within that process, the 4-stage count of this source's SDL-competency ascendency model, stage 4 of that model (self-directed learner), and the one non-method among four genuine methods for assessing SDL competencies.

## sections
### Definition
This source names four steps to implement self-directed learning: assessing student readiness, setting goals for learning, engaging students in the process, and adjusting and adapting to suit student needs. The last of these is tested from two directions: as the false exception when misdirected toward 'doctor needs', and as the correct match when a stem describes blending the method within classroom education to suit student needs and growth. Genuine SDL competencies are self-awareness, critical appraisal, critical thinking and reflection; 'evaluating the professionalism' is not one of them. The SDL process itself is self-referential, spanning task assessment, self-evaluation of strengths and weaknesses, and practising and monitoring one's own performance, alongside the mindset factor of a student's belief about intelligence and learning. This source's ascendency model has exactly 4 stages, of which stage 4 is reaching the level of a self-directed learner.
### Mechanism
Each of these nine facts turns on correctly scoping one step, competency or stage against a plausible neighbour within the same numbered sequence. The 'adjust and adapt' step is tested twice from opposite directions specifically because this source uses the same underlying fact, that adaptation should serve the student, to build both a false-exception question and a direct-match question. The competencies-except and assessment-methods-except questions both work the same way: naming several genuine members of a category and one plausible-sounding non-member (evaluating professionalism; co-operation), so recognising the category's true scope is what the question actually tests, not memorising which single word is 'the answer'.
### Key determinants
Each question turns on picking the specific step, competency, process element or stage this source's own key names, out of several plausible-sounding neighbours drawn from the same implementation sequence, competency list, process description, or 4-stage ascendency model. Getting the exact step or stage right matters because this source tests each one individually across several consecutive questions rather than testing the sequence only once as a whole.
### Clinical significance
Correctly implementing, evaluating and progressing through self-directed learning is a professional-development skill medical students are expected to carry through their entire career, well past this single module: knowing which implementation step is missing, which competency needs building, or which stage of the ascendency model a learner has reached is exactly the kind of self-monitoring this source's own SDL-competencies material (self-awareness, critical appraisal, reflection) asks a self-directed learner to be able to do for themselves.

## summary
This chapter works through fourteen learning-plan and learning-needs-assessment facts from mcq شامل.pdf pages 7-11: the definition and aims of a learning plan, its cyclical needs-then-goals development process, methods to uncover knowledge gaps, a three-method list for learning-needs assessment, the specific definition of practice reviews, the four-step sequence to prioritize learning needs systematically and fairly (assess, analyze, prioritize, communicate), the SWOT/KSA/ADDIE frameworks that support that process, the importance/feasibility/relevance basis for prioritisation, the identification-of-learning-goals step that follows needs assessment, the people/resources/technology scope of identifying learning resources, and the portfolio/case-notes/role-plays forms evidence of learning can take.

## sections
### Definition
A learning plan is a document for reflecting on and structuring learning toward exam and career success, grounded in self-reflection and objective self-evaluation, and it aims to centre learning on current practice, support informed choices, integrate theory and practice, and enhance motivation, all four aims together. Developing one is a cyclical process that starts by identifying learning needs so that learning goals can be formulated from them. Learning needs themselves are assessed through several methods (360-degree appraisal, critical incident reviews, self-assessment, and practice reviews using existing data or a satisfaction questionnaire), then prioritized through a four-step sequence: assess the situation, analyze the data, prioritize the needs, and communicate the results, using tools such as SWOT, KSA and ADDIE, and weighing importance/urgency, feasibility, and relevance/demand together. Once needs are prioritized, the learning plan proceeds to identifying learning goals, then identifying learning resources (people, resources and technology), then identifying forms of evidence of learning such as a portfolio, case notes, role plays or case studies.
### Mechanism
Each of these fourteen facts turns on correctly placing one step within one of two nested sequences this source tests repeatedly: the four-step needs-prioritisation sequence (assess situation, analyze data, prioritize needs, communicate results) and the broader learning-plan development sequence that needs-prioritisation feeds into (assess needs, identify goals, identify resources, identify evidence of learning). Several questions in this chapter name the same four-option answer set (assess the situation / analyze the data / prioritize the needs / communicate the results) and ask which specific step a given stem describes, so getting the sequence order right, not just recognising the four step names, is what each question actually tests.
### Key determinants
Each question turns on picking the specific step, method, framework or criterion this source's own key names, out of several plausible-sounding neighbours from the same numbered sequence or list. The learning-needs assessment methods (360-degree appraisal, critical incident reviews, self-assessment, practice reviews) are frequently tested against one another as near-identical-sounding distractors, so recognising which method matches a given data-source description (existing data or a questionnaire for practice reviews; direct colleague feedback for 360-degree appraisal) is the specific skill each question is testing.
### Clinical significance
Building, prioritizing and executing a personal learning plan is a professional-development skill medical students are expected to use throughout training and practice, well beyond this single module: knowing how to systematically identify, prioritize and act on one's own learning needs, using structured frameworks like SWOT, KSA or ADDIE rather than an unstructured approach, is directly transferable to continuing professional development after graduation.

## published_sections


## hold_these
A learning plan is a document for structuring learning toward exam and career success, grounded in self-reflection and objective self-evaluation, not merely 'a set of skills that can be taught, learned and acquired'.
Developing a learning plan is a cyclical process that starts by identifying learning needs so learning goals can be formulated from them.
The four-step sequence to prioritize learning needs is: assess the situation, analyze the data, prioritize the needs, and communicate the results, in that order.
After learning needs are assessed, the next step in developing a learning plan is identifying learning goals, before identifying resources or forms of evidence of learning.

Assuming only one of the SWOT, KSA or ADDIE frameworks is the correct tool for prioritizing learning needs, when this source marks all three together as valid.
