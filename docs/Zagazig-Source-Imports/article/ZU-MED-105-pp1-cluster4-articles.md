## sections
### Definition
Bioethics is the study of ethical, social, and legal issues that arise in biomedicine and biomedical research, scoping the field to medicine and medical research specifically rather than to ethical issues in life or society in general. This source groups autonomy and decision-making, persons without the capacity to consent, non-discrimination and non-stigmatization, and respect for human vulnerability and human integrity together as its own umbrella list of core bioethical concepts. Medical ethics is the branch of bioethics consisting of key ethical principles that professionals can refer to when confusion or conflict arises in medical practice. The classical four principles of medical ethics are autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence and justice, listed together as the framework this source uses throughout its bioethics section. Autonomy is the individual capacity for self-determination, independent decisions, actions, and evaluations, distinguishing it from beneficence, non-maleficence and justice, which describe obligations toward others rather than a capacity of the individual. Medical decision making should be a rational process based on a consideration of the facts, but the final word rests with the patient, reflecting the principle of patient autonomy. Equality is the state of being equal, having the same rights and social status, and aiming to ensure that everyone gets the same things to enjoy full, healthy lives, distinguishing it from equity's need-based framing.
### Mechanism
Equity is trying to identify and give people what they need to enjoy full, healthy lives, a need-based approach distinguished from equality's same-things-for-everyone framing. Horizontal equity is the equal treatment of equal needs, the direct counterpart to vertical equity, which is the unequal treatment of unequal needs. Beneficence is taking actions that promote the wellbeing of others; in the medical context this means a practitioner should act in the best interest of the patient and serve their wellbeing. Non-maleficence is the principle "first, do no harm" (primum non nocere): it is more important not to harm patients than to do them good, often taught as a balanced pair alongside beneficence. This source frames autonomy and responsibility as mutually dependent: there is no autonomy without responsibility, since the freedom to make independent decisions is inseparable from being answerable for them. Any physical examination or even a blood test performed without the patient's permission or consent is considered an assault, except in some narrowly defined cases such as medical emergencies.

## sections
### Definition
Given consent is considered invalid when the operation itself has no genuine medical indication, such as amputating a finger specifically to escape military service, even if the patient otherwise agrees freely. Privacy is the right of an individual or group to be free from intrusion from others, and includes the right to determine which information about them should be disclosed to others. Confidentiality is an attribute of personal information requiring that it not be disclosed to others without sufficient reason, narrower than privacy's general right to be free from intrusion. Reasons for respecting privacy and confidentiality include that individuals own their information as part of personal integrity, that privacy is essential to dignity, and that respect for others requires protecting privacy and confidentiality, together. Vulnerable groups are defined as those at greater risk for poor health status and healthcare access, experiencing significant disparities in access to and use of healthcare services, and more likely to develop health problems as a result of exposure to risk. Vulnerability is susceptibility to actual or potential stressors that may lead to adverse effects, the underlying trait distinguished from vulnerable groups, the specific populations affected by it. This source lists the chronically ill and disabled, the elderly, racial and ethnic minorities, the unemployed and uninsured, children of low-income families, and people with HIV as vulnerable populations; people with diabetes mellitus are marked as the exception not included in this particular list.
### Mechanism
This source organises the causes of human vulnerability into biological (ageing, infectious disease), environmental (disasters, pollution) and social (war, crime, discrimination) categories, marking genetics as the exception not named as its own category in this particular taxonomy. Cultural diversity refers to the existence of more than one culture in a society or country, meaning the existence of more than one race, language, tradition, and religion, distinguished from pluralism's active affirmation and acceptance of that diversity. Pluralism refers to the treatment of people as equal citizens irrespective of any difference, whether cultural, ethnic, or religious. Non-discrimination is the concept that requires equal treatment of an individual or group irrespective of their characteristics, the direct opposite of discrimination. Stigmatization is, in its basic sense, a distinguishing mark or characteristic attached to a person or group. Responsibility is, in its basic dictionary sense, a charge, trust or duty for which one is responsible. Accountability is the obligation of being answerable for one’s own judgments and actions to an appropriate person or authority recognized as having the right to demand information and explanation.

### Definition
This source lists Laissez-Faire leadership, Servant leadership, Transactional leadership, and Transformational leadership together as its named leadership styles. Autocratic leadership is also known as the "authoritarian style of leadership," characterised by centralised, top-down decision-making by the leader. This source's first list of leadership skills includes communication, negotiation, conflict resolution, adaptability, critical thinking, and decision-making, together. Reliability and trust is the cornerstone of a successful organization, from this source's second list of leadership skills. Problem-solving is the ability to foresee issues in the workplace, define the problem, identify their causes, develop a plan to remedy the problem, and learn from the problem to avoid future issues.
### Mechanism
Team dynamics is the relationships and interactions between team members that can affect productivity and performance. A contingency team deals with emergencies and specific events, for example a cardiac-arrest team. A coordinating team is responsible for operational management, coordinating functions, and resource management of the care team. Support services provide indirect, task-specific services in a health care facility. Cleaners and domestic staff are considered the ancillary team, this source's named example of a team providing indirect support to a health care facility. Forming is the stage of team dynamics characterized by ambiguity and confusion, as team members are still unclear about their tasks. Norming is the stage of team dynamics with open communication between team members, who start to confront the task at hand as procedures and communication patterns become established.
