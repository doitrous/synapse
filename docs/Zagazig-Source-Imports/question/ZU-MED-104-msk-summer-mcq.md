## title
Nerve paralysed in inability to invert the foot

## question
A 16-year-old boy is unable to invert his foot. Which of the following nerves is most likely to be paralyzed?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Superficial peroneal

## explanation_a
Correct per this paper's marked key. The paper circles the superficial peroneal nerve for a case of inability to invert the foot. Reviewers should note this runs against the standard teaching that active inversion is produced by tibialis anterior (deep peroneal nerve) and tibialis posterior (tibial nerve), while the superficial peroneal nerve instead supplies the evertors, peroneus longus and brevis — see the concept's own uncertainty note.

## answer_b
Deep peroneal

## explanation_b
Not the paper's marked answer. Standard teaching would instead point here, since the deep peroneal nerve supplies tibialis anterior, a prime invertor of the foot — but the paper marks superficial peroneal.

## answer_c
Lateral plantar

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The lateral plantar nerve is a branch of the tibial nerve supplying intrinsic foot muscles and sensation to the lateral sole, not a nerve classically tied to ankle inversion.

## answer_d
Medial plantar

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The medial plantar nerve, also a tibial nerve branch, supplies intrinsic foot muscles and sensation to the medial sole, not a nerve classically tied to ankle inversion.

## title
The axis of the foot

## question
The axis of the foot is a line that passes through which of the following toes?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
1st

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The big (first) toe abducts away from and adducts toward the foot's reference axis; it does not define the axis itself.

## answer_b
2nd

## explanation_b
Correct. Toe abduction and adduction are described relative to a reference line through the second toe, the long axis of the foot — the lower-limb counterpart to the hand's own middle-finger axis. Toes moving away from this line are abducting, toward it are adducting, and the second toe itself is conventionally treated as having no abduction/adduction of its own. This is a frequently tested contrast with the hand, whose reference digit is the middle (third) finger, not a numerically equivalent digit.

## answer_c
3rd

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The third toe abducts away from and adducts toward the reference axis through the second toe; it does not define the axis itself.

## answer_d
4th

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The fourth toe abducts away from and adducts toward the reference axis through the second toe; it does not define the axis itself.

## title
Muscle damaged in inability to unlock the knee

## question
A 22-year-old patient is unable to unlock the knee joint to allow flexion of the leg. Which of the following muscles is most likely damaged?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Popliteus

## explanation_a
Correct. Popliteus unlocks the fully extended, screw-home-locked knee by laterally rotating the femur on the fixed tibia, or medially rotating the tibia on the fixed femur. This unlocking must happen before the hamstrings can flex the knee at all. Damage to popliteus therefore specifically blocks the ability to initiate flexion from full extension.

## answer_b
Biceps femoris

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Biceps femoris is a hamstring that flexes the already-unlocked knee; it plays no role in the initial unlocking step.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gastrocnemius, though it crosses the knee and contributes a small flexor action, is not the muscle responsible for unlocking the screw-home-locked extended knee.

## answer_d
Semitendinosus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Semitendinosus, like biceps femoris, is a hamstring that flexes the already-unlocked knee, not the unlocking muscle itself.

Movement affected by ischial tuberosity fracture

## question
A fracture to the ischial tuberosity would affect which of the following movements of the lower limb?

## subject
msk

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Thigh abduction is produced by gluteus medius/minimus (superior gluteal nerve), muscles arising from the ilium, not the ischial tuberosity.

Flexion of knee

## explanation_b
Correct. The three hamstring muscles (semitendinosus, semimembranosus, and the long head of biceps femoris) all arise from the ischial tuberosity. Incorrect. Thigh flexion is chiefly produced by iliopsoas, sartorius and rectus femoris, none of which arise from the ischial tuberosity.

## answer_d
Extension of knee

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Knee extension is produced by quadriceps femoris, arising from the ilium and femoral shaft, not the ischial tuberosity.

Brachial plexus part supplying skin of the lateral forearm

## question
Which part of the brachial plexus supplies the skin of the lateral side of the forearm?

## subject
msk

## status

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The medial cord gives rise to the medial cutaneous nerves of the arm and forearm, supplying the medial, not lateral, forearm skin.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The lower trunk is a proximal brachial plexus element that contributes to the medial cord; it does not itself carry a discrete cutaneous branch to the forearm.

Upper trunk

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The upper trunk is a proximal element contributing to the lateral and posterior cords; it does not itself carry a discrete cutaneous branch to the forearm.

Lateral cord

Correct. The musculocutaneous nerve leaves the lateral cord and pierces coracobrachialis. Beyond that muscle it continues as a purely cutaneous nerve, the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm, supplying the skin of the lateral forearm. This makes the lateral cord the correct brachial plexus element for this territory.

Extension of wrist joint

Incorrect. Elbow extension (triceps) is supplied by the radial nerve proper well above the posterior interosseous nerve's own branch point, so it is not the deficit tested here.

## answer_d
Opposition of the thumb

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Thumb opposition is produced by opponens pollicis, a median-nerve-supplied thenar muscle, unrelated to the posterior interosseous nerve.

## title
Muscle retaining function after a median nerve injury at the wrist

## question
A 24-year-old male had his median nerve injured in an industrial accident at the wrist. Which of the following muscles would still retain its function?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The lumbricals of the index and middle fingers are median-supplied and are lost with a median nerve injury at the wrist.

## answer_b
Abductor pollicis brevis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Abductor pollicis brevis is wholly supplied by the median nerve's recurrent branch and is lost outright with this injury.

## answer_c
Lumbricals of ring and little finger

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The lumbricals of the ring and little fingers are indeed ulnar-supplied and spared — but this option is not the paper's marked answer, and flexor pollicis brevis is the more specific fact tested here.

Flexor pollicis brevis

## explanation_d
Correct. Flexor pollicis brevis has a superficial head that is median-supplied and a deep head that, in most hands, is instead supplied by the ulnar nerve's deep branch. The muscle can therefore retain partial function via its ulnar-supplied deep head after an isolated median nerve injury at the wrist. This distinguishes it from abductor pollicis brevis and the index/middle lumbricals, which are wholly median-supplied and lost outright.

## title
Character of skeletal muscle chronaxie compared to its nerve

## question
Both nerve and muscle are excitable tissues. Which one of the following is a character of skeletal muscle chronaxie?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Shorter than that of its nerve

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Skeletal muscle chronaxie is not shorter than its nerve's — muscle fibres respond more slowly than the nerve supplying them.

## answer_b
Variable compared to its nerve

## explanation_b
Correct per this paper's marked key. The paper marks that skeletal muscle chronaxie is variable compared to its nerve. Reviewers should note that standard teaching instead makes the specific claim that normally-innervated skeletal muscle chronaxie is longer than that of its nerve, a fact this paper's own key states only vaguely — see the concept's own uncertainty note.

## answer_c
Equal to that of its nerve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Skeletal muscle chronaxie is not equal to its nerve's; the two excitable tissues have characteristically different chronaxie values.

## answer_d
Longer than that of its nerve

## explanation_d
Not the paper's marked answer, though this specific, standard-teaching comparison (muscle chronaxie longer than its nerve's) is the more precise textbook statement — the paper nonetheless marks the vaguer option B.
