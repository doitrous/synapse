## title
Lung carcinoma and the lingula

## question
A 75-year-old woman was admitted to hospital, and bronchograms and radiographs revealed a lung carcinoma in her left lung. Which of the following structures or characteristics does the cancerous (left) lung contain?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Transverse fissure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Both lungs have an oblique fissure, but the transverse (horizontal) fissure is unique to the right lung, where it separates the upper from the middle lobe — the left lung has no horizontal fissure at all.

## answer_b
Groove for the superior vena cava

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The superior vena caval groove is an impression on the mediastinal surface of the right lung, related to the right atrium's venous drainage, not a feature of the left lung.

## answer_c
Middle lobe

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A middle lobe exists only on the right lung, produced by its unique horizontal fissure. The left lung has only an upper and lower lobe.

## answer_d
Lingula

## explanation_d
Correct. The lingula is a tongue-shaped extension of the left lung's upper lobe, lying below the cardiac notch on the anterior border — it occupies the same anatomical territory the middle lobe occupies on the right, but without being a separate lobe. It is the single most reliable answer among these options for any question asking what the left (rather than right) lung specifically contains, since a transverse fissure, an SVC groove and a middle lobe are all right-lung-only features.

## title
Ligation for inferior thyroid artery bleeding

## question
During surgery on a 56-year-old man for a squamous cell carcinoma of the neck, a surgeon notices profuse bleeding from the inferior thyroid artery. Which of the following arteries must be ligated immediately to stop the bleeding?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Transverse cervical artery

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The transverse cervical artery is itself a branch of the thyrocervical trunk, not its parent vessel — ligating it alone would not achieve proximal control of the trunk that also supplies the bleeding inferior thyroid artery.

## answer_b
Thyrocervical trunk

## explanation_b
Correct. The inferior thyroid artery is short and retracts into the deep neck once torn, so proximal control of profuse bleeding from it is achieved by ligating its parent vessel, the thyrocervical trunk, a short branch of the first part of the subclavian artery. This trunk also gives the transverse cervical and suprascapular arteries, but it is its relationship to the inferior thyroid artery that makes it the vessel to ligate here.

## answer_c
Costocervical trunk

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The costocervical trunk is a separate branch of the subclavian artery, supplying the superior intercostal and deep cervical territory — it has no relationship to the inferior thyroid artery or thyroid bleeding.

## answer_d
Ascending cervical artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The ascending cervical artery is a small branch of the inferior thyroid artery itself, too distal and too small to provide proximal control of the bleeding.

## title
Recurrent laryngeal nerve lesion and the thyroarytenoid

## question
A 26-year-old singer visits her ENT physician complaining of changes in her voice. A laryngoscopic examination demonstrates a lesion of the recurrent laryngeal nerve, causing weakness of which of the following muscles?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cricothyroid

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cricothyroid is the one intrinsic laryngeal muscle supplied by the external laryngeal nerve, not the recurrent laryngeal nerve — it would be spared, not weakened, by a recurrent laryngeal nerve lesion.

## answer_b
Superior constrictor

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The superior constrictor is a pharyngeal muscle supplied by the pharyngeal plexus (vagus via the pharyngeal branch), not the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

## answer_c
Thyroarytenoid

## explanation_c
Correct. The recurrent laryngeal nerve supplies every intrinsic laryngeal muscle except the cricothyroid, including the thyroarytenoid — a lesion of the nerve therefore weakens the thyroarytenoid, producing the hoarseness and voice change this patient presents with. This is why laryngeal-nerve palsies are grouped by which muscles they spare rather than which they weaken — cricothyroid sparing is the diagnostic clue that localises the lesion to the recurrent, not the external, laryngeal nerve.

## answer_d
Middle constrictor

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The middle constrictor, like the superior constrictor, is a pharyngeal muscle supplied by the pharyngeal plexus, not an intrinsic laryngeal muscle supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

Sphenoethmoidal recess swelling

## question
A 53-year-old man has difficulty breathing through his nose. On examination, his physician finds swelling of the mucous membranes of the sphenoethmoidal recess. Which opening of the paranasal sinuses is most likely plugged?

resp

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The middle ethmoidal cells drain into the middle meatus, not the sphenoethmoidal recess.

## answer_b
Sphenoid sinus

## explanation_b
Correct. The sphenoethmoidal recess is a narrow space in the roof of the nasal cavity into which the sphenoid sinus alone drains, so swelling confined to this recess plugs the sphenoid sinus specifically. Anterior ethmoidal sinus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The anterior ethmoidal cells drain into the middle meatus, along with the frontal and maxillary sinuses — none of these use the sphenoethmoidal recess.

## title
Foreign body aspiration in a child

## question
A 3-year-old boy develops sudden cyanosis and dyspnea. During bronchoscopy, where is an inhaled foreign body most likely to be lodged, causing this obstruction?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Right main bronchus

## explanation_a
Correct. The right main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertically oriented — a straighter continuation of the trachea's axis — than the left, which is longer and more oblique because it passes beneath the aortic arch. An inhaled foreign body follows the path of least resistance and lodges in the right main bronchus far more often than the left, in both children and adults.

## answer_b
Left main bronchus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The left main bronchus is the less likely site precisely because of its greater length and obliquity, crossing beneath the aortic arch.

## answer_c
Left superior lobar bronchus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A lobar bronchus is a smaller-calibre branch beyond the main bronchus; a foreign body typically lodges more proximally, in the wider right main bronchus itself, before reaching a lobar branch.

## answer_d
Right superior lobar bronchus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although the right side is favoured, the obstruction is described as occurring at the main bronchus, the widest and most direct pathway, not at the narrower superior lobar branch.

## title
Thoracic duct injury and chylothorax

## question
A 28-year-old man presented with severe trauma, and the thoracic duct was accidentally damaged during assessment. The resulting accumulation of lymph in the pleural cavity is referred to as which of the following?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Pleurisy

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pleurisy names inflammation of the pleura; it does not specify a particular fluid type collecting in the pleural cavity.

## answer_b
Chylothorax

## explanation_b
Correct. Chylothorax is lymph (chyle, the fat-rich lymph the thoracic duct carries) accumulating in the pleural cavity, which is exactly what results when the thoracic duct — running through the thorax alongside the oesophagus and aorta before draining into the left venous angle — is torn. Recognising chylothorax specifically (rather than a generic traumatic effusion) matters clinically, since chyle drainage carries different nutritional and management implications from blood, pus or simple inflammatory fluid.

## answer_c
Lymphedema

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Lymphedema is lymph accumulating in the tissues (typically a limb), not in a body cavity such as the pleural space.

## answer_d
Pyothorax

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pyothorax (empyema) is pus in the pleural cavity, arising from infection, not from lymphatic duct injury.

## title
Inability to abduct the vocal cords

## question
A young singer visits her physician complaining of vocal difficulties. On examination, she is unable to abduct the vocal cords during quiet breathing. Which of the following muscles is most likely paralyzed?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Vocalis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The vocalis (part of the thyroarytenoid) shortens and relaxes the vocal fold rather than abducting it, so its paralysis would not remove abduction specifically.

## answer_b
Cricothyroid

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cricothyroid tenses (lengthens) the vocal folds to raise pitch — it plays no role in abduction.

## answer_c
Oblique arytenoid

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The oblique arytenoid, along with the transverse arytenoid, adducts the vocal folds (closing the airway), the opposite action to abduction.

## answer_d
Posterior cricoarytenoid

## explanation_d
Correct. The posterior cricoarytenoid is the only abductor of the vocal folds, rotating the arytenoid cartilage to swing the vocal process laterally and open the airway — its paralysis removes the only muscle capable of abduction, explaining this patient's inability to abduct her vocal cords. The lateral cricoarytenoid and interarytenoid muscles adduct the folds instead, and the cricothyroid only tenses them, so none of those muscles could produce a pure loss of abduction on its own.

## title
De novo fatty acid synthesis

## question
De novo synthesis of fatty acids is one of the sources of fatty acid. Which of the following is true about the de novo synthesis of fatty acids?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Occurs in mitochondria

## explanation_a
Incorrect. De novo fatty acid synthesis is a cytoplasmic pathway; mitochondria is where the reverse process, beta-oxidation, takes place.

## answer_b
Malonyl CoA is the direct source of all carbon atoms needed

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Malonyl CoA donates two-carbon units for chain elongation, but the acetyl CoA primer also contributes carbons — malonyl CoA is not literally the source of every carbon atom in the final product.

## answer_c
Fatty acid synthase is the key enzyme

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the single best answer here. Fatty acid synthase is genuinely the multi-enzyme complex that catalyses the pathway, but the printed key on this exam paper names the NADPH+H+ requirement as the intended single best answer; the cofactor requirement is an absolute, unconditional stoichiometric fact about every elongation cycle, while naming 'the key enzyme' is a broader, less specific description of the same pathway.

## answer_d
NADPH+H+ is required

## explanation_d
Correct. Every two-carbon addition cycle in fatty acid synthesis consumes NADPH+H+ as the reducing power for its two reduction steps (beta-ketoacyl reduction and enoyl reduction) — an absolute requirement of the pathway, and the answer the paper's own printed key selects. The pathway itself is cytoplasmic, not mitochondrial, and uses malonyl CoA as the two-carbon donor for chain elongation, with fatty acid synthase as the enzyme complex carrying out the reaction.

The splenic sinusoid is characterized by which of the following?

## subject

## answer_a
It is located in the white pulp

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Splenic sinusoids are located in the red pulp, not the white pulp.

## answer_b
Its endothelial cells are elongated

## explanation_b
Correct. Splenic sinusoids are lined by elongated (rod-shaped) endothelial cells arranged with slit-like gaps between them, an arrangement central to the open/closed-theory debate over how blood crosses from the terminal capillaries into the sinusoids. Their discontinuous basement membrane and the absence of tight junctions between them together permit blood cells to filter through the sinusoidal wall, the spleen's characteristic filtering function.

## answer_c
It has a continuous basement membrane

Incorrect. The splenic sinusoid basement membrane is discontinuous (annular/ring-like), not continuous — this discontinuity is part of what allows blood cells to pass through the sinusoidal wall.

## answer_d
Tight junctions are present between its endothelial cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The gaps between splenic sinusoidal endothelial cells are not sealed by tight junctions; this open arrangement is what permits the sinusoid's filtering function.

## subject

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The basilar artery's adventitia is thin, not thick, consistent with its overall thin-walled histological character.

Has a wide lumen and thin wall

Correct. The basilar artery, formed by the union of the two vertebral arteries and supplying the brainstem and posterior brain, is histologically characterised — like other large cerebral arteries — by a wide lumen and a comparatively thin wall. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. Arteries in general, including the basilar artery, do not contain valves — valves are a feature of veins, not arteries.

## title
Blood capillaries within nervous tissue

## question
Which of the following characterizes blood capillaries within the nervous tissue?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Contain pinocytic vesicles that permit transport

## explanation_a
Correct. Capillaries within nervous tissue are continuous capillaries with a tight, unfenestrated endothelium — the structural basis of the blood-brain barrier — so transport across them relies on pinocytic (transcytotic) vesicles rather than pores. This continuous, tightly-sealed endothelium is why many drugs effective against peripheral disease cannot reach therapeutic concentrations in the central nervous system.

## answer_b
Have an incomplete basal lamina

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Nervous-tissue capillaries have a complete, not incomplete, basal lamina, part of the tight seal that forms the blood-brain barrier.

## answer_c
Have large pores within the endothelial cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Nervous-tissue capillaries have no large pores — that describes fenestrated capillaries found elsewhere in the body, not the continuous capillaries of the blood-brain barrier.

## answer_d
Are lined by simple cuboidal epithelium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Capillary endothelium, in nervous tissue as everywhere else in the body, is simple squamous, never simple cuboidal.

## title
Character of the terminal bronchiole

## question
Which of the following is a character of the terminal bronchiole?

## subject
resp

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Lined by ciliated cells and goblet cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The terminal bronchiole is lined by ciliated cells and Clara (club) cells, but goblet cells are absent from bronchioles generally, having disappeared more proximally in the airway.

## answer_b
Its lamina propria has no glands

## explanation_b
Correct. The terminal bronchiole's lamina propria contains no glands, distinguishing its wall from the larger bronchi, whose submucosa carries seromucous glands. Has cartilage in its wall

Incorrect. Cartilage is absent from bronchiole walls entirely, including the terminal bronchiole — cartilage is a feature of the bronchi, not the bronchioles.

## answer_d
Interrupted by alveoli

## explanation_d
Incorrect. It is the respiratory bronchiole, not the terminal bronchiole, whose wall is interrupted by scattered alveoli — the terminal bronchiole itself carries no alveoli.
