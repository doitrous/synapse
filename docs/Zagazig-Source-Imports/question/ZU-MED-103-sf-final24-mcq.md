## title
First level of DNA packing

## question
What is the first level of DNA packing in metaphase chromosome?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Euchromatin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Euchromatin describes transcriptionally active, loosely packed chromatin at a much higher order of organisation, not the first level of DNA packing.

## answer_b
Gene

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A gene is a functional unit of DNA sequence, not a level of physical packing at all.

## answer_c
Histone

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Histones are the proteins that do the packing, but the named first-level packing unit they form is the nucleosome, not the histone protein by itself.

## answer_d
Nucleosome

## explanation_d
Correct. The nucleosome — a histone octamer (two copies each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4) wrapped by about 140 bp of DNA, connected to the next nucleosome by H1-bound linker DNA — is the first level of DNA packing, giving chromatin its beads-on-a-string appearance before further, higher-order coiling condenses it into the metaphase chromosome. Because histones are lysine- and arginine-rich basic proteins, they are positively charged and bind the acidic, phosphate-rich DNA electrostatically. This first packing level is what all higher orders of chromatin condensation (30 nm fibre, loops, and finally the metaphase chromosome itself) are built on top of.

## title
Crossing over and meiotic prophase

## question
Crossing over (pachytene) takes place in which phase?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Meiosis I metaphase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. By metaphase, homologous chromosomes are already aligned at the metaphase plate; the exchange of material has already occurred earlier, at prophase.

## answer_b
Meiosis I anaphase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Anaphase is when homologous chromosomes are actively separating and moving to opposite poles, well after any crossing over has taken place.

## answer_c
Meiosis I prophase

## explanation_c
Correct. Crossing over — reciprocal exchange of genetic material between paired homologous chromosomes — happens at pachytene, one of five substages of meiosis I prophase (leptotene, zygotene, pachytene, diplotene, diakinesis), after the chromosomes have fully synapsed at zygotene. This reshuffles maternal and paternal alleles between homologous chromosomes before they separate, producing genetic recombination. It is this prophase-stage exchange, not any later stage, that the pachytene substage is specifically named for.

## answer_d
Meiosis I telophase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Telophase I is the final stage of the first meiotic division, when the chromosomes have already reached the poles and nuclear envelopes are reforming — long after any crossing over.

Glands that secrete through a duct

## question
What type of gland secretes its product through a duct or tube?

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Endocrine glands are ductless — secretory cells release their hormone directly into surrounding blood capillaries, not through a duct.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Multicellular is a description of how many cells make up the gland (versus unicellular, like a goblet cell), not a classification based on the presence of a duct.

Exocrine gland

## explanation_c
Correct. Glands are classified by the presence or absence of a duct. An exocrine gland has a secretory portion and an excretory portion — its duct — that carries the secretion outside the gland (to a body surface or lumen). Merocrine gland

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Merocrine describes a mode of secretion (exocytosis, with no loss of cell material), not whether the gland has a duct — merocrine glands are a subtype of exocrine gland, not a synonym for having a duct.

## title
Striated duct surface specialisation

## question
What is the type of epithelial cell specialization found in striated ducts of salivary glands for increasing surface area for transport?

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Basal infoldings

## explanation_a
Correct. Basal infoldings of the plasma membrane, with mitochondria stacked vertically between them, increase the basal surface area of striated duct cells to power the active ion transport (Na+ reabsorption) that gives the duct its striated appearance and its name. This basal membrane specialisation is distinct in location and function from apical specialisations.

Microvilli

Basement membrane

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The basement membrane is a structural anchoring layer beneath the epithelium, not a surface-area-increasing specialisation.

## answer_d
Gap junction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gap junctions are lateral intercellular channels for direct cell-to-cell communication, unrelated to increasing transport surface area.

## title
Classification of pseudostratified epithelium

## question
Pseudostratified columnar epithelium belongs to which type of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Simple epithelium

## explanation_a
Correct. Pseudostratified columnar epithelium is classified as simple epithelium, because every one of its cells rests on the basement membrane — it only appears layered because the cells' nuclei sit at different heights, not because there are genuinely multiple cell layers stacked on top of each other. It comes in three forms distinguished by what sits on the apical surface (ciliated, non-ciliated, or with stereocilia).

## answer_b
Stratified epithelium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. True stratified epithelium has multiple genuine cell layers, with only the basal layer touching the basement membrane — unlike pseudostratified epithelium, where every cell reaches the basement membrane despite the layered appearance.

## answer_c
Neuro-epithelium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Neuro-epithelium is a specialised sensory epithelial type (e.g. olfactory epithelium), a different classification axis entirely from the simple/stratified distinction.

## answer_d
Myoepithelium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Myoepithelium refers to contractile epithelial cells (found around secretory acini and ducts), not a layering classification.

## question
Which of the following characterizes neutrophil?

## subject

Incorrect. The mature neutrophil's nucleus is multi-lobed (2 to 5 lobes connected by thin strands), not S-shaped.

## answer_b
Its immature form called band cell.

## explanation_b
Correct. The neutrophil's immature form, released early from the bone marrow under demand such as infection, is called the band cell (band neutrophil) — its nucleus is an unsegmented, curved band rather than the mature cell's multi-lobed nucleus. It lacks azurophilic granules

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The neutrophil does contain azurophilic (primary) granules, alongside its specific (secondary) granules — it does not lack them.

Platelets are formed from what type of cell?

## subject

## title
Symport across the cell membrane

## question
Regarding transport across cell membrane, which of the following is an example of co-transport (Symport)?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ca2+/Na+

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ca2+/Na+ exchange is antiport (countertransport) — the two ions move in opposite directions, not the same direction that defines symport.

## answer_b
Na+/amino acid

## explanation_b
Correct. Symport (co-transport) moves two solutes in the same direction, using the downhill electrochemical gradient of one (typically Na+) to drive the uphill movement of the other. The Na+/amino acid carrier is a textbook example, moving both Na+ and the amino acid into the cell together.

## answer_c
Na+/H+

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Na+/H+ exchange is antiport, moving Na+ in and H+ out — opposite directions, not symport.

## answer_d
Na+/K+ pump

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The Na+/K+ pump is primary active transport, using ATP hydrolysis directly to move Na+ and K+ in opposite directions across the membrane, not a symporter using another ion's gradient.
