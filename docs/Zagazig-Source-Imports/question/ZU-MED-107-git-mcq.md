## title
Antimycin and the electron transport chain

## question
Antimycin inhibits the electron transport chain at which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Complex I

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Complex I is blocked by rotenone, not antimycin — a different inhibitor entirely, at the entry point of the chain.

## answer_b
Complex II

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase) is inhibited by malonate, and is bypassed by the FADH2 route rather than being antimycin's target.

## answer_c
Complex III

## explanation_c
Correct. Antimycin A binds cytochrome b within Complex III (ubiquinol–cytochrome c oxidoreductase) and blocks electron transfer from ubiquinol to cytochrome c, halting oxidative phosphorylation at that specific point in the chain. This makes antimycin useful experimentally to isolate ETC segments proximal to Complex III from those distal to it when studying electron flow.

## answer_d
Complex IV

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase) is the site blocked by cyanide and azide, the classic terminal-step poisons, not antimycin.

## title
Substrate-level phosphorylation in the TCA cycle

## question
Substrate level phosphorylation is the process of phosphorylation at the level of the reaction itself. In the TCA cycle it occurs in which step?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Isocitrate dehydrogenase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Isocitrate dehydrogenase generates NADH for later oxidative phosphorylation; it does not phosphorylate a nucleotide directly.

## answer_b
Malate dehydrogenase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Malate dehydrogenase likewise yields NADH, not a directly phosphorylated nucleotide — it feeds the electron transport chain rather than performing substrate-level phosphorylation.

## answer_c
Aconitase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Aconitase catalyses an isomerisation (citrate to isocitrate) with no redox or phosphorylation event at all.

## answer_d
Succinyl-CoA synthetase

## explanation_d
Correct. The succinyl-CoA synthetase (succinate thiokinase) step converts succinyl-CoA to succinate while generating GTP directly from the reaction's own energy — the one substrate-level phosphorylation event in the TCA cycle, readily converted to ATP. Because GTP is a high-energy phosphate compound interconvertible with ATP via nucleoside diphosphate kinase, this single step still contributes directly to the cell's usable energy pool.

## title
Peroxidase enzyme mechanism

## question
Which of the following statements is true concerning the peroxidase enzyme?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It acts on two molecules of H2O2

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Acting on two molecules of H2O2 at once (2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2) describes catalase's dismutation reaction, not the peroxidase route, which reduces one H2O2 molecule using a hydrogen donor.

## answer_b
It does not need the presence of a hydrogen donor

## explanation_b
Incorrect. It is catalase that needs no hydrogen donor; peroxidase enzymes (such as glutathione peroxidase) specifically require a hydrogen donor — reduced glutathione — to reduce H2O2 to water.

## answer_c
It interferes with chain propagation of free radicals

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Chain propagation is the stage at which an existing free radical reacts further; enzymatic H2O2 removal acts earlier, before a radical chain has even started.

## answer_d
It prevents the process of initiation of free radicals

## explanation_d
Correct. By removing hydrogen peroxide before it can generate hydroxyl radicals (via the iron-catalysed Fenton reaction), peroxidase activity prevents the initiation step of free-radical chain reactions rather than acting once radicals are already propagating. This is why maintaining catalase and glutathione peroxidase activity is protective against oxidative stress, rather than merely mopping up radicals after damage has already begun.

## title
Thiamine-dependent enzyme for brain glucose oxidation

## question
A 55-year-old alcoholic was brought to the Emergency Department by his friends. The doctor ordered an injection of thiamine followed by overnight parenteral glucose. The next morning the patient was alert, serum thiamine was normal, and blood glucose was 73 mg/dL. Which of the following enzymes is thiamine-dependent and essential for glucose oxidation in the brain?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Pyruvate dehydrogenase

## explanation_a
Correct. Pyruvate dehydrogenase requires thiamine pyrophosphate as one of its five coenzymes and is the irreversible, committed step linking glycolysis to the TCA cycle — the brain depends on it almost exclusively for oxidative glucose metabolism. This is exactly why thiamine is given before glucose in a suspected alcoholic: giving glucose first can consume the little thiamine reserve that remains and precipitate acute Wernicke's encephalopathy.

## answer_b
Isocitrate dehydrogenase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Isocitrate dehydrogenase uses NAD+ (or NADP+ for the cytosolic isoform), not thiamine pyrophosphate, as its coenzyme.

## answer_c
Succinyl-CoA thiokinase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Succinyl-CoA thiokinase (synthetase) uses GDP/GTP and CoA, not thiamine, in its substrate-level phosphorylation reaction.

## answer_d
Succinate dehydrogenase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Succinate dehydrogenase uses FAD, not thiamine pyrophosphate, and is also the enzyme complex that doubles as Complex II of the electron transport chain.

## title
Site of activity of the hexose monophosphate pathway

## question
The hexose monophosphate pathway (HMP) is an important pathway in glucose metabolism. Which of the following statements is true?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
HMP is active in muscle

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Skeletal muscle has comparatively little HMP-pathway activity because it has low demand for NADPH-driven biosynthesis (fatty acid and steroid synthesis) compared with the tissues that do run this pathway heavily.

## answer_b
HMP is active in liver

## explanation_b
Correct. The hexose monophosphate pathway runs actively in the liver (along with adrenal cortex, adipose tissue, lactating mammary gland, testis and red cells) — tissues with a high demand for NADPH to drive fatty acid synthesis, steroidogenesis, and glutathione regeneration. This tissue distribution mirrors exactly where NADPH-dependent biosynthesis is most active, which is the physiological reason the pathway concentrates there rather than in tissues such as skeletal muscle.

## answer_c
HMP generates ATP

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The HMP pathway does not generate ATP; its purpose is to generate NADPH (for reductive biosynthesis) and ribose 5-phosphate (for nucleotide synthesis), not energy in the form of ATP.

## answer_d
HMP takes place in mitochondria

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The HMP pathway is entirely cytosolic, not mitochondrial — this is part of what lets it run independently of the cell's mitochondrial oxidative state.

## question
What is the key regulatory enzyme in haemoglobin synthesis?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Haem synthase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. "Haem synthase" is not the pathway's rate-limiting step and is more properly the name occasionally used for ferrochelatase itself, the pathway's final enzyme rather than its regulatory one.

## answer_b
Delta-amino levulinic acid hydratase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. ALA dehydratase performs the second step of the pathway (converting ALA to porphobilinogen); it is not the pathway's rate-limiting, regulated enzyme.

Delta-amino levulinic acid synthase

Correct. ALA synthase, in the mitochondrion, condenses glycine with succinyl-CoA in the first and rate-limiting step of haem synthesis, and is the enzyme haem feeds back on (via negative feedback and repression of gene transcription) to switch production down when haem is abundant. Because it sits at this very first committed step, throttling ALA synthase is the most efficient point in the whole pathway for haem to regulate its own future production.

Cytochrome P450 mechanism in phase 1 metabolism

## question
Which one of the following is correct about cytochrome P450 in phase 1 reactions of xenobiotic metabolism?

## subject
fnd

## status

## vignette

C

It is formed of 450 amino acids

Incorrect. "P450" names the 450 nm absorbance peak of its reduced, carbon-monoxide-bound form on a spectrophotometer, not an amino-acid count.

## title
Chymotrypsin's optimum pH, class and secreted form

## question
Which one of the following is correct about chymotrypsin?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Its optimum pH is acidic

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Chymotrypsin, like the other pancreatic proteases, has an alkaline (not acidic) pH optimum, matching the alkaline environment created by pancreatic bicarbonate secretion in the duodenum.

## answer_b
It is an exopeptidase enzyme

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Chymotrypsin is an endopeptidase, cleaving bonds in the middle of the polypeptide chain — the exopeptidases (carboxypeptidase, aminopeptidase) trim single residues from the chain ends instead.

## answer_c
It acts on peptide linkages between aromatic amino acids

## explanation_c
Correct. Chymotrypsin cleaves peptide bonds specifically on the carboxyl side of the bulky aromatic amino acids phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan.
