QST-ZUMED105-PP1-CLUSTER1-PP1-ZAG24-Q01-CHECKLIST-TOOL-STUDENT-SATISFACTION

## title
What a checklist tool assesses

## question
The checklist tool can be used to assess:

## subject
pop

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Annual student survey

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An annual student survey is a separate, standalone data-collection instrument in its own right, not something a checklist (a real-time, item-by-item observation tool) is used to conduct.

## answer_b
Student attendance rate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Attendance rate is a longitudinal administrative metric tracked by a separate record system over time, not something captured by a single-encounter checklist.

## answer_c
Student graduation rate

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Graduation rate is an institution-level outcome measured across a student's entire programme, far removed from the scope of a point-of-encounter checklist.

## answer_d
Student satisfaction

## explanation_d
Correct. A checklist records whether specific, predefined criteria were met during an observed encounter, which suits capturing a satisfaction rating at that point in time. This is the answer both this module's Zagazig-campus and Fakous-campus Final papers mark for the identical question, confirming it is not a source-specific quirk. Among the four options, satisfaction is the only one that is itself an item-level, in-the-moment judgment rather than a longitudinal administrative record.

Incorrect. Authority (the sanctioned power to act) is a separate precondition from the capacity to actually carry a task out, and is not the model's base layer as tested here.

## answer_b
Professionalism

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Professionalism is a broader orientation that shapes how accountability is exercised, not the specific foundational precondition this model names as its base.

Ability

## explanation_c
Correct. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. Responsibility (being assigned or accepting a task) itself presupposes the ability to carry it out, making ability the more fundamental, earlier precondition, not the model's base.

## title
Self-analysis as a source of self-awareness

## question
We can gain self-awareness from different sources, which of the following statements describes sources correctly?

## subject
pop

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Negative situations don't help us to gain self-awareness

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Negative situations are, in fact, a real source people draw self-awareness from, contradicting this option's absolute claim.

## answer_b
Self-analysis is an important source of self- awareness

## explanation_b
Correct. Self-analysis is an important source of self-awareness — deliberately examining one's own thoughts, actions and reactions is a genuine, active way to build self-understanding. This is the correctly-hedged, affirmative statement among four options, three of which are false absolutes. Note this is a distinct tested fact from this same bank's own Q3, which tests that self-awareness is affected by behaviour and perception — both are true, but they are two separate items, not one repeated.

## answer_c
Self-awareness is not affected by behavior or perception

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Self-awareness is, in fact, affected by behaviour and perception, directly contradicting this option's claim.

## answer_d
The level of self-awareness cannot be changed

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Self-awareness is described as buildable and improvable, directly contradicting this option's claim that it cannot be changed.
