## title
Basic SI unit

## question
Which one of the following is a basic SI unit?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Kilogram per cubic meter

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Kilogram per cubic meter is a derived unit of density, built by combining two base units (mass and volume), not a basic SI unit on its own.

## answer_b
Kilogram per liter

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Kilogram per liter is a derived unit of mass concentration, likewise built from two base quantities, not a basic SI unit itself.

## answer_c
Mole per liter

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mole per liter is a derived unit of amount concentration, combining the base unit for amount of substance with volume, not a basic SI unit alone.

## answer_d
Meter

## explanation_d
Correct. Meter is one of the seven SI base units, defining the fundamental quantity of length directly, unlike kilogram per cubic meter, kilogram per liter, or mole per liter, each of which is a derived unit built by combining two base quantities into a ratio. Base units are the small set from which every other SI unit is constructed by multiplication or division, which is exactly why meter alone, among these four choices, counts as "basic."

SI unit of temperature

## question
Which of the following is the SI unit of "temperature"?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Kelvin, K

## explanation_a
Correct. Kelvin (K) is the SI base unit of temperature, defined on an absolute scale starting at absolute zero, and is the unit used throughout scientific and thermodynamic calculations even though clinical practice often reports Celsius day to day. Kelvin, not Centigrade/Celsius or Fahrenheit, is the internationally standardised SI unit for this quantity. It is the temperature analogue of meter for length or kilogram for mass — a true base unit, not a locally popular scale.

## answer_b
Centigrade, C

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Centigrade (Celsius) is a widely used clinical temperature scale, but it is not the SI base unit — it is defined relative to the kelvin scale, offset by 273.15.

## answer_c
Farenheit, F

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fahrenheit is a temperature scale used mainly in a small number of countries for everyday reporting, not an SI unit at all.

## answer_d
Joule, J

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Joule is the SI unit of energy (including heat energy), not of temperature itself.

## title
SI unit of mass concentration

## question
Which of the following is the SI unit of "Mass concentration"?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Kilogram

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Kilogram alone is the SI base unit of mass, but mass concentration is a ratio of mass to volume, not mass by itself.

## answer_b
Liter

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Liter alone is a unit of volume, not a concentration, since concentration requires both an amount and a volume to be expressed together.

## answer_c
Kilogram per liter

## explanation_c
Correct. Mass concentration is defined as the mass of a dissolved solute per unit volume of solution, so its SI unit is kilogram per liter (or an equivalent scaled unit such as gram per liter, commonly used for clinical lab values like plasma protein concentration). It is built by dividing the base unit of mass by the base unit of volume, giving a single combined unit for this specific physical quantity.

## answer_d
Mole per liter

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mole per liter is the unit of amount concentration (moles of solute per volume), a related but distinct quantity from mass concentration — amount concentration counts particles (moles), while mass concentration weighs them (kilograms).

Suffix for an immature, active cell

## question
Immature active cells are named with which of the following terminal (suffix) elements?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
-cytes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. "-cyte" names a mature cell (for example, an erythrocyte or an osteocyte), the opposite maturity stage from what the question describes.

## answer_b
-blasts

## explanation_b
Correct. The suffix "-blast" names an immature, actively developing or actively dividing cell — an osteoblast is an immature bone-forming cell, and a fibroblast is an immature connective-tissue-forming cell. "-blast" cells are typically the precursor stage that later matures into the corresponding "-cyte" form once they finish differentiating. This maturity-stage contrast (immature "-blast" versus mature "-cyte") is one of the most commonly tested suffix pairs in introductory medical terminology.

## answer_c
-genic

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "-genic" means "producing" or "originating from" (as in pathogenic, producing disease), describing a cause-and-effect relationship, not a cell's maturity stage.

## answer_d
-clast

## explanation_d
Incorrect. "-clast" names a cell that breaks down tissue (as in osteoclast, which breaks down bone), functionally the opposite role from a "-blast" cell that builds tissue up.

## title
Meaning of "Haemolysis"

## question
What is the meaning of Haemolysis?

## subject
haem

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Break down of red blood cells

## explanation_a
Correct. "Hemolysis" combines "hem/o" (blood) with the suffix "-lysis" (breakdown or destruction) to mean breakdown of red blood cells, with release of their intracellular contents (such as hemoglobin) into the surrounding plasma or fluid. This is a structural-destruction process, distinct from the body simply losing cells, forming new ones, or increasing the cell count. Hemolysis is the mechanism behind hemolytic anemia and several transfusion-reaction and inherited red-cell disorders.

## answer_b
Loss of red blood cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Loss of red blood cells (as from bleeding) describes hemorrhage, not hemolysis — the cells leave the body or vessel intact rather than breaking down.

## answer_c
Formation of red blood cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Formation of red blood cells is erythropoiesis, the opposite process from hemolysis (breakdown rather than formation).

## answer_d
Increase number of red blood cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. An increase in red blood cell number is polycythemia, again the opposite direction of change from hemolysis, which reduces circulating intact red cells.

The adrenal medulla secretes both epinephrine and norepinephrine as part of the sympathetic "fight-flight-fright" stress response, raising heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose to prepare the body for exertion.
