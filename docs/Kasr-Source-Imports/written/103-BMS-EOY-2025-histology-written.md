The papillary layer of the dermis against the reticular layer

## question
Compare the reticular layer and the papillary layer of the dermis, by thickness and position, by the type of connective tissue, by cellularity, by fibres, by vascularity, and by the sensory receptors each contains.

## format
comparison_table

## written_parts
### (a) 3 marks
Compare the reticular layer and the papillary layer of the dermis, by thickness and position, by the type of connective tissue, by cellularity, by fibres, by vascularity, and by the sensory receptors each contains.
Expects: The papillary layer is the thinner, superficial layer, and it forms the dermal papillae; the reticular layer is the thicker, deep layer
Expects: The papillary layer is loose connective tissue; the reticular layer is dense connective tissue
Expects: The papillary layer is more cellular and the reticular layer less cellular, both holding fibrocytes, macrophages, lymphocytes, mast cells and adipocytes
Expects: The papillary layer has fine fibres — type III collagen with elastic fibres; the reticular layer has type I collagen in bundles with elastic fibres
Expects: The papillary layer is more vascular, to nourish the epidermis; the reticular layer is less vascular
Expects: The papillary layer carries Meissner's corpuscles; the reticular layer carries Pacinian corpuscles, Ruffini's end organs and Krause's end bulbs

## format

## written_parts
### (a) 6 marks
Compare the osteoblast and the osteoclast with respect to origin, site, light microscopic appearance and electron microscopic appearance.
Expects: Origin — the osteoblast arises from osteogenic cells
Expects: Origin — the osteoclast is formed by the fusion of mononuclear haemopoietic progenitor cells
Expects: Site — osteoblasts lie immediately under the periosteum as a continuous single layer covering the bone surface, and also under the endosteum
Expects: Site — osteoclasts lie on the bone surface near the bone marrow, within a cavity called Howship's lacuna
Expects: LM osteoblast — oval cells with a few minute processes and eccentric nuclei, the size of the cell depending on its activity
Expects: LM osteoblast — darkly basophilic cytoplasm with a negative Golgi image near the nucleus, and rich in alkaline phosphatase
Expects: LM osteoclast — large irregular cells with a brush border facing the bony surface
Expects: LM osteoclast — multinucleated, six to twelve nuclei, with foamy acidophilic cytoplasm
Expects: EM osteoblast — the characters of a protein-forming cell: rich in rough endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria, with a well-developed Golgi apparatus
Expects: EM osteoclast — a ruffled surface showing microvilli, and cytoplasm rich in lysosomes and mitochondria, with a Golgi apparatus and multiple vesicles

## title
The electron microscopic picture of smooth muscle

## subject

## status

## vignette

## question
Mention the electron microscopic picture of smooth muscle.

## format
short_answer

## written_parts
### (a) 3 marks
Mention the electron microscopic picture of smooth muscle.
Expects: The sarcolemma is thin and surrounded by a basal lamina; it shows no T-tubules, but invaginations of the cell surface called caveolae, and there is no tubular system
Expects: The caveolae control calcium release and so control contraction
Expects: The nucleus is single, oval and central, and takes a corkscrew shape during contraction
Expects: The sarcoplasm is acidophilic and holds numerous mitochondria, sarcoplasmic reticulum, free ribosomes, a small Golgi apparatus and glycogen granules, mainly in the perinuclear region
Expects: The myofilaments — thick myosin and thin actin — are irregularly arranged, which is why no striations appear
Expects: Actin filaments insert into sarcoplasmic and sarcolemma-associated dense bodies, which correspond to the Z line of striated muscle, and extend into the sarcoplasm to interact with the myosin
Expects: Abundant intermediate desmin filaments also insert into the dense bodies, and the attachment of actin and intermediate filaments to them transmits the contractile force to adjacent cells
Expects: There is no troponin; calmodulin takes its place
Concept: CON-MSK-888DFA3AA4E974
