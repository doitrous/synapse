## title
Death from disease progression after a properly performed operation

## question
A 12-year-old child with a history of rheumatic heart was admitted to hospital for tonsillectomy; he was operated on after full preoperative counseling and was discharged from hospital 2 days later. One month later, he developed heart failure and was readmitted to the hospital, but unfortunately, he passed away despite of clinical care. Can the parent sue the ENT doctor?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Yes, he can sue him for incompetence.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Incompetence means a lack of reasonable skill or knowledge, and nothing in the scenario shows the ENT surgeon lacked either — the operation and counselling were properly done.

## answer_b
Yes, he can sue him for negligence.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Negligence requires a breach of the standard of care that caused the harm, but here the care met the standard and the death came from progression of the pre-existing rheumatic heart disease.

## answer_c
Yes, he can sue him for medical error.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. There is no medical error to point to: the surgery and perioperative care were appropriate, and a later cardiac death is an outcome of the underlying disease, not a mistake.

## answer_d
No, he can't sue him

## explanation_d
Correct. The parent cannot sue, because a bad outcome that follows proper care is not actionable — liability needs fault (negligence or incompetence) that caused the harm. Here the tonsillectomy was correctly performed with full counselling, and the fatal heart failure a month later reflects the child's underlying rheumatic heart disease rather than any breach of duty. An adverse result on its own, without a proven breach of the standard of care, is not malpractice.

Basis of liability for an incompatible blood transfusion

## question
In case of incompatible blood transfusion, the doctor is liable to be sued because of:

mul

B

Negligence

Correct. Giving an incompatible transfusion is negligence, because the doctor failed to carry out the standard, expected safeguard of confirming blood-group compatibility before transfusing. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. A dispensing error refers to the pharmacy step of supplying a medication; a mismatched transfusion is a failure of the transfusion-safety duty, categorised as negligence.

## title
Breach of duty when a head-trauma patient is sent home

## question
A 50-year-old female patient suffering from a severe head trauma was admitted to the emergency room. After examination, the doctor assured her that she is in a good condition and needs no admission. Few hours later, she suffered from headache and vomiting. She was then admitted to the hospital but unfortunately, she lost consciousness and died. What is the breach of duty that occurred?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Abandonment

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Abandonment is ending the doctor-patient relationship without notice or arrangement for continued care; here the doctor did not abandon her but failed to admit and observe her.

## answer_b
Act of commission

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An act of commission is a wrong act actually performed; the fault here was not doing something indicated (admission/observation), not a wrong act carried out.

## answer_c
Act of omission

## explanation_c
Correct. Failing to admit and observe a patient with severe head trauma who needed monitoring is a failure to do something that should have been done — an act (error) of omission. The indicated step of admission for observation was omitted, and the deterioration and death followed from that omission. Omission errors are failures to act, in contrast to commission errors, which are wrong acts performed.

## answer_d
Unnecessary treatment

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Unnecessary treatment means giving care that was not indicated; the problem here was the opposite — necessary admission and observation were withheld.

## title
Cause of death in a haemolytic transfusion reaction

## question
Death in hemolytic blood transfusion may occur due to:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Adrenal failure.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adrenal failure is not the mechanism of death in a haemolytic transfusion reaction.

## answer_b
Liver failure.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Although haemolysis releases bilirubin and can cause jaundice, death is not from hepatic failure.

## answer_c
Renal failure.

## explanation_c
