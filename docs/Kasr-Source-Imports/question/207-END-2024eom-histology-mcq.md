## title
Herring bodies of the pars nervosa

## question
One of the following statements is correct concerning the pituitary gland:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Pars intermedia connects the hypothalamus with the pars nervosa

## explanation_a
Incorrect. It is the infundibulum (pituitary stalk), not the pars intermedia, that connects the hypothalamus to the pars nervosa. The pars intermedia is the thin zone of the adenohypophysis between the pars distalis and pars nervosa.

## answer_b
Herring bodies are accumulated neurosecretion in axon terminals

## explanation_b
Correct. Herring bodies are dilated axonal swellings in the pars nervosa where neurosecretory material (oxytocin and ADH bound to neurophysin) accumulates before release. The hormones are synthesised in the supraoptic and paraventricular hypothalamic nuclei and transported down the axons for storage in these terminals. They appear as pale eosinophilic bodies along the unmyelinated hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract.

## answer_c
Pituicytes are found in the pars distalis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pituicytes are the glial-like supporting cells of the pars nervosa (neurohypophysis), not the pars distalis; the pars distalis contains chromophils (acidophils and basophils) and chromophobes.

## answer_d
The pars nervosa contains many nerve cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pars nervosa contains almost no nerve cell bodies; it is made of unmyelinated axons, their terminals (Herring bodies) and pituicytes. The neuron cell bodies lie in the hypothalamus.

## title
Thyroid follicular cells and synthetic organelles

## question
In the thyroid gland, the follicular cells:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Are arranged in fascicles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Follicular cells form a simple epithelium arranged as a single layer around a colloid-filled follicle (spherical arrangement), not in fascicles (cords/bundles).

## answer_b
Do not reach the lumen of the follicle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Follicular cells line the follicle and their apical surface directly borders the colloid in the lumen; it is the parafollicular (C) cells that do not reach the lumen.

## answer_c
Have rough endoplasmic reticulum and a Golgi apparatus

## explanation_c
Correct. Follicular cells are active protein-secreting and endocytosing cells, so they are rich in rough endoplasmic reticulum, a well-developed Golgi apparatus, apical secretory vesicles and lysosomes. The rER and Golgi support synthesis and glycosylation of thyroglobulin exported into the colloid, while lysosomes digest re-endocytosed colloid to release T3 and T4. Their cytoplasm therefore shows both basophilic (rER) and secretory features.

## answer_d
Become columnar in hypothyroidism

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Follicular cells become tall/columnar when the gland is hyperactive (hyperthyroidism); in hypothyroidism/inactivity they are low cuboidal to squamous with abundant stored colloid.

## title
Basophils of the pars distalis — thyrotrophs

## question
Choose the true statement about the basophils of the pituitary gland:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
They have acidophilic cytoplasmic granules

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Basophils have basophilic granules that stain with PAS because their hormones are glycoproteins; acidophilic granules characterise acidophils (somatotrophs and mammotrophs).

## answer_b
They are the smallest cells of the pars distalis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Basophils are generally the largest cells of the pars distalis; chromophobes are the smallest and pale-staining.

## answer_c
They are non-granular and considered stem cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Basophils are granular, secretory chromophils. The non-granular, poorly staining cells are the chromophobes, which are thought to be degranulated or stem/reserve cells.

## answer_d
Thyrotrophs are one of their types

## explanation_d
Correct. Basophils are the glycoprotein-hormone secreting chromophils and comprise thyrotrophs (TSH), gonadotrophs (FSH and LH) and corticotrophs (ACTH). Thyrotrophs are therefore one recognised type of basophil. They are PAS-positive because of their glycoprotein secretory granules.

## title
Zona fasciculata spongiocytes

## question
Regarding the zona fasciculata of the adrenal (suprarenal) gland:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It is the narrowest zone of the suprarenal cortex

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The zona fasciculata is the widest (broadest) of the three cortical zones, occupying roughly the middle 70–80% of the cortex; the zona glomerulosa is the narrow outer zone.

## answer_b
Its cells are arranged in glomeruli

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fasciculata cells are arranged in straight cords (fascicles) running perpendicular to the capsule between radial sinusoids; the glomerular (rounded cluster) arrangement is that of the zona glomerulosa.

## answer_c
Its cells are rich in smooth endoplasmic reticulum and fat droplets

## explanation_c
Correct. Fasciculata cells (spongiocytes) synthesise steroids (mainly glucocorticoids), so they are packed with the machinery of steroidogenesis: abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum, numerous lipid (cholesterol) droplets and mitochondria with tubular cristae. The dissolved-out lipid gives the cytoplasm its characteristic vacuolated, spongy appearance. This is why they are named spongiocytes.

## answer_d
Its cells are called chromaffin cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chromaffin cells are the catecholamine-secreting cells of the adrenal medulla, not the cortex.

## title
Innervation of pinealocytes

## question
Pinealocytes present in the epiphysis cerebri (pineal body) are characterised by:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Having small, darkly stained nuclei

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pinealocytes have large, pale (euchromatic), often lobulated nuclei with prominent nucleoli; small dark nuclei belong to the interstitial (glial) cells.

## answer_b
Being innervated by unmyelinated (sympathetic) nerve fibres

## explanation_b
Correct. Pinealocytes receive a rich postganglionic sympathetic innervation carried by unmyelinated nerve fibres from the superior cervical ganglion. Noradrenaline released from these terminals at night drives melatonin synthesis, linking the pineal to the circadian light–dark cycle. The unmyelinated sympathetic supply is a defining feature of pinealocytes.

## answer_c
Being supporting neuroglial cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pinealocytes are the secretory parenchymal cells (modified neurons) that produce melatonin; the supporting neuroglial (interstitial astrocyte-like) cells are a separate population.

## answer_d
Being rich in smooth endoplasmic reticulum and lysosomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pinealocytes are characterised by long processes with club-shaped endings, abundant rough ER and mitochondria and secretory features; they are not specifically defined by sER and lysosome richness (that better fits steroid-secreting cells).

## title
Thyroid parafollicular (C) cells as clear cells

## question
One of the following is true regarding the thyroid parafollicular (C) cells:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
They have apical secretory granules

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Their secretory (calcitonin) granules are basal, lying between the nucleus and the basement membrane, not apical; the cell does not reach the follicular lumen so it has no true apical secretory pole.

## answer_b
They are rich in lysosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. C cells are characterised by numerous small secretory granules and organelles for peptide synthesis, not by lysosome richness; abundant lysosomes are a feature of the colloid-resorbing follicular cell.

## answer_c
They secrete thyroid hormones (T3 and T4)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Parafollicular cells secrete calcitonin, which lowers blood calcium; the iodinated thyroid hormones T3 and T4 are made by the follicular cells.

## answer_d
They are called clear cells

## explanation_d
Correct. Parafollicular cells are called clear (C) cells because their cytoplasm stains pale/clear in routine preparations. They are larger than follicular cells, lie within the follicular epithelium or in the interfollicular connective tissue, and do not touch the colloid. They secrete calcitonin from basally located granules.

## subject

Incorrect. Spongiocytes are the lipid-rich steroid-secreting cells of the zona fasciculata of the cortex, not neurons of the medulla.

## title
Hypercalcaemia from parathyroid hyperfunction

## question
Investigations of a male patient with a fractured bone revealed a very high blood calcium level. This condition may result from the hyperfunction of:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The parathyroid gland

## explanation_a
Correct. The parathyroid chief cells secrete parathyroid hormone (PTH), which raises blood calcium by stimulating osteoclastic bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption and activation of vitamin D. Hyperfunction (primary hyperparathyroidism) therefore causes hypercalcaemia and, through excessive bone resorption, weakened bone that fractures easily (osteitis fibrosa cystica). The combination of high calcium and pathological fracture points to the parathyroid.

## answer_b
The parafollicular cells of the thyroid gland

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Parafollicular (C) cells secrete calcitonin, which lowers blood calcium; their hyperfunction would tend to reduce, not raise, calcium.

## answer_c
The basophils of the pituitary gland

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pituitary basophils secrete TSH, ACTH and gonadotrophins; none of these directly raises serum calcium to cause hypercalcaemia with fractures.

## answer_d
The chromaffin cells of the suprarenal medulla

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chromaffin cells secrete catecholamines (adrenalin/noradrenalin), which affect cardiovascular and metabolic function, not calcium homeostasis.

## subject

## title
Testis as a compound tubular gland

## question
Which statement is true concerning the histological structure of the testis?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Leydig cells are the exocrine part of the parenchyma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The exocrine part of the testis is the seminiferous tubules, which produce spermatozoa; the interstitial Leydig cells are the endocrine part (testosterone).

## answer_b
It is a compound tubular gland

## explanation_b
Correct. The testis is a compound tubular gland: its secretory units are the highly convoluted seminiferous tubules (tubular units) and it is 'compound' because these drain through a branching duct system (tubuli recti, rete testis, efferent ductules). The exocrine product is spermatozoa. This architecture distinguishes it from acinar or tubulo-acinar glands.

## answer_c
The tunica vasculosa forms the mediastinum testis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The mediastinum testis is a thickening of the tunica albuginea (the dense fibrous coat) projecting into the posterior testis; the tunica vasculosa is the inner vascular layer lining the albuginea.

## answer_d
Its inner coat is the tunica vaginalis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The innermost coat of the wall proper is the tunica vasculosa; the tunica vaginalis is the outer serous covering derived from peritoneum, external to the tunica albuginea.
