## title
Defining feature of a facultative parasite

## question
A facultative parasite:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Lives only in the environment

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An organism that lives ONLY in the environment and never parasitises a host is a free-living organism, not a parasite at all; a facultative parasite is defined by its capacity to switch between the two modes.

## answer_b
Exists in different hosts

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 'Existing in different hosts' describes host range, not the facultative habit; the defining feature is the ability to live independently of any host.

## answer_c
Able of existing independently of a host

## explanation_c
Correct. A facultative parasite is normally free-living and is ABLE to exist independently of a host, but it can also invade and live as a parasite when the opportunity arises (for example when it gains entry to compromised tissue). This capacity to survive and complete part of its existence without a host is exactly what distinguishes a facultative parasite from an obligate parasite, which cannot live outside a host. The bank tests recognition of this independence-from-a-host criterion.

## answer_d
Spends part of its life cycle in a host or lives independently

## explanation_d
Incorrect. While a facultative parasite can indeed live independently, the single defining criterion the bank asks for is its ability to exist independently of a host (option C); D blends the facultative habit with the obligate life-cycle wording and is not the intended defining statement.

## explanation_b

Incorrect. Trichuris (whipworm) is a soil-transmitted nematode acquired by ingesting embryonated eggs from soil, not a metacercaria on vegetation.

## title
Route of Fasciola infection

## question
Infection with Fasciola is through:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ingestion of raw liver of sheep

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Eating raw sheep liver containing adult flukes gives a spurious infection (eggs merely pass through the gut) or halzoun, not a true established infection.

## answer_b
Ingestion of encysted metacercaria on plants

## explanation_b
Correct. True Fasciola infection follows ingestion of the encysted METACERCARIA attached to aquatic plants such as watercress. After ingestion the metacercaria excysts in the duodenum, and the juvenile fluke penetrates the gut wall, crosses the peritoneal cavity and Glisson's capsule, and migrates through liver parenchyma to reach the bile ducts. Ingesting the plant-borne metacercaria is therefore the mode of infection.

## answer_c
Ingestion of eggs of Fasciola

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fasciola eggs passed in faeces are unembryonated and non-infective; they must develop in water and pass through a snail, so swallowing eggs does not cause infection.

## answer_d
Penetration of skin by cercariae

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Skin penetration by cercariae is the route for schistosomes, not Fasciola, whose cercariae encyst on vegetation instead of penetrating skin.

## title
Mode of human Heterophyes infection

## question
Man is infected by Heterophyes through:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Eating insufficiently cooked crabs

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Undercooked crabs transmit the lung fluke Paragonimus, not Heterophyes.

## answer_b
Handling the snail Pirenella conica

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Merely handling the Pirenella snail does not transmit infection; the infective metacercaria is in the fish, not on the snail's surface.

## answer_c
Drinking contaminated water with cercaria

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Heterophyes cercariae do not infect by being drunk in water; they leave the snail and encyst in fish, and infection requires eating that fish.

Eating improperly cooked fish (Tilapia or Mugil)

## explanation_d
Correct. Humans acquire Heterophyes heterophyes by eating raw or improperly cooked brackish-water FISH such as Tilapia or Mugil that contain encysted metacercariae. On digestion the metacercariae excyst and the young flukes attach to the small-intestinal mucosa to mature. Eating undercooked fish is therefore the mode of infection, mirroring the snail (Pirenella)–fish two-host cycle.

## title
Consequence of egg emboli

## question
Egg emboli may cause:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ocular toxocariasis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ocular toxocariasis is caused by migrating Toxocara LARVAE (larva migrans), not by egg emboli.

## answer_b
Convulsions in diphyllobothriasis latum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Diphyllobothrium causes B12-deficiency megaloblastic anaemia; it does not embolise eggs to the brain.

## answer_c
Granulomatous brain lesions in heterophyiasis

## explanation_c
Correct. In heterophyiasis the small adult flukes can penetrate the intestinal wall so that their EGGS enter mesenteric venules and are carried as emboli to distant organs, including the brain and spinal cord (and heart), where they lodge and incite GRANULOMATOUS inflammation. This ectopic egg embolisation is the recognised cause of neurological and cardiac heterophyiasis. The question tests that trapped, embolised eggs — not larvae — produce these granulomas.

## answer_d
Loeffler syndrome in strongyloidiasis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Loeffler syndrome is a transient eosinophilic pneumonitis from larval lung migration (e.g. Ascaris, Strongyloides), not from egg emboli.

## title
True statement about Fasciola infection

## question
The following about Fasciola spp. infection is TRUE:

## subject

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Proline secretion by the worm causes minimal abscesses

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Proline secreted by the fluke causes bile-duct hyperplasia and can produce sizeable, not minimal, changes; the statement understates its effect.

## answer_b
Immature worms produce destruction in liver parenchyma

## explanation_b
Correct. During the acute (invasive/hepatic) phase the IMMATURE juvenile flukes burrow through the liver, tunnelling through and DESTROYING the hepatic parenchyma before they settle in the bile ducts. This migratory tissue destruction produces the fever, tender hepatomegaly and eosinophilia of acute fascioliasis. Recognising that the immature worms damage liver parenchyma is the true statement the question rewards.

## answer_c
No ectopic migration occurs

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ectopic migration DOES occur in fascioliasis (aberrant flukes reach skin, lungs and other sites), so this statement is false.

## answer_d
Praziquantel is the drug of choice

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The drug of choice for Fasciola is TRICLABENDAZOLE; praziquantel (effective for most other flukes) is notably ineffective against Fasciola.

## title
Definition of the definitive host

## question
The definitive host is:

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Host in which the larval asexual phase of parasite development occurs

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The host harbouring the larval/asexual stages is the INTERMEDIATE host, not the definitive host.

## answer_b
Host in which the adult or sexual phase of parasite development occurs

## explanation_b
Correct. The DEFINITIVE host is the one in which the parasite reaches its ADULT stage and/or undergoes SEXUAL reproduction. For example, humans are the definitive host of Schistosoma (adult worms mating in the veins) and of Taenia (adult tapeworm in the gut). Defining the definitive host by the adult/sexual phase is the atomic fact tested, and it is what separates it from the intermediate host that carries only larval/asexual stages.

## answer_c
An insect which transmits a parasite to its host

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A blood-sucking insect that transmits a parasite is a VECTOR (a form of intermediate host in some cycles), not by definition the definitive host.

## answer_d
A host harbouring the parasite in an arrested stage of development

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A host that merely carries the parasite in an arrested (hypobiotic) or transport stage is a paratenic/reservoir host, not the definitive host.

## title
Main cause of pathology in schistosomiasis

## question
The main pathology in Schistosoma infection is due to:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Presence of adults in the venules

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The paired adult worms live in the venous plexuses and are largely tolerated (they coat themselves in host antigens); they are not the main source of disease.

## answer_b
Eggs trapped in tissues

## explanation_b
Correct. Most schistosomal disease is caused by the EGGS that become TRAPPED in tissues (bladder wall in S. haematobium; intestinal wall and liver in S. mansoni/japonicum). Antigens secreted by the miracidium inside the egg drive a T-cell-mediated GRANULOMATOUS and fibrotic reaction around each trapped egg, producing the haematuria, bladder fibrosis, periportal (pipe-stem) fibrosis and portal hypertension of chronic disease. The egg-induced granuloma is therefore the central mechanism of pathology.

## answer_c
Toxic byproducts of migrating juveniles

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Migrating schistosomula provoke only transient reactions; they are not the main cause of chronic pathology.

## answer_d
Traumatic effect of adult worms

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adult worms cause little direct trauma; the granulomatous response to trapped eggs, not mechanical injury by adults, drives the disease.
