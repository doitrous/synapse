## title
Histological features of the hepatocyte

## question
Hepatocytes are characterized by:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The cell membrane facing the bile canaliculus shows long microvilli

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The hepatocyte surface facing the bile canaliculus bears only short, sparse microvilli; the abundant, longer microvilli project from the sinusoidal (basolateral) surface into the space of Disse to maximise exchange with blood.

## answer_b
Cells are acidophilic and vacuolated

## explanation_b
Correct. Hepatocytes are large polygonal cells with an acidophilic cytoplasm (abundant mitochondria and smooth ER) that also stains basophilic in patches (rough ER, ribosomes). The cytoplasm is characteristically vacuolated because of dissolved-out glycogen and lipid stores. Their nuclei are large, round, pale (euchromatic) with one or more prominent nucleoli, and many cells are binucleate.

## answer_c
Cells are columnar with basal oval nuclei

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hepatocytes are polygonal, not columnar, and are arranged in plates one-to-two cells thick; a columnar shape with basal oval nuclei describes an absorptive epithelium such as the gall-bladder lining.

## answer_d
Their nuclei are dark with non-clear nucleoli

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hepatocyte nuclei are pale and euchromatic with clear, prominent nucleoli, reflecting high synthetic activity, rather than dark condensed nuclei.

## title
Urothelium and umbrella cells of the ureter

## question
Which of the following is well matched?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Ureters / urothelium (transitional epithelium) with umbrella cells

## explanation_a
Correct. The ureters, like the renal pelvis, calyces and bladder, are lined by urothelium (transitional epithelium). Its most superficial layer is formed of large, often binucleate umbrella (dome) cells whose luminal membrane and tight junctions create the impermeable blood-urine barrier. This epithelium flattens as the wall distends and piles up when relaxed.

## answer_b
Intraglomerular mesangial cells / juxtaglomerular apparatus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The juxtaglomerular apparatus is formed of the macula densa, extraglomerular (Lacis) mesangial cells and the JG cells of the afferent arteriole; the intraglomerular mesangial cells lie within the glomerular tuft and are supportive/phagocytic, not part of the JGA proper.

## answer_c
Distal convoluted tubule cells / ill-defined cell boundaries

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ill-defined (indistinct) lateral cell boundaries are the hallmark of the proximal convoluted tubule, whose extensive basolateral interdigitations blur the borders; distal tubule cells have relatively clearer outlines.

## answer_d
Thin segment of Henle's loop / simple cuboidal epithelium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The thin segment of the loop of Henle is lined by simple squamous epithelium, not cuboidal; cuboidal epithelium lines the thick segments.

## title
Ultrastructure of the macula densa

## question
Which of the following is true regarding the macula densa?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Its cells have few, basally placed pale nuclei

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The macula densa is a plaque of tall, narrow, closely packed cells with apically crowded nuclei, giving a 'dense' appearance; the nuclei are numerous and apical, not few and basal.

## answer_b
Its cells are called pole cushion (polkissen) cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pole cushion (polster/polkissen) cells is another name for the juxtaglomerular (JG) cells of the afferent arteriole, not the macula densa.

## answer_c
It contains PAS-positive secretory granules

## explanation_c
Incorrect. PAS-positive membrane-bound secretory (renin) granules characterise the JG cells; the macula densa is a sensor of tubular NaCl and is not a granulated secretory cell.

## answer_d
Its cells show an infranuclear (basal) Golgi complex

## explanation_d
Correct. The macula densa is the specialised region of the distal tubule wall abutting the vascular pole. Its cells are polarised so that the Golgi apparatus lies infranuclear (basal) instead of the usual supranuclear position, reflecting their sensing/signalling role toward the arterioles. This inverted polarity is the classic ultrastructural clue to the macula densa.

## title
The glomerular (blood-renal) filtration barrier

## question
Regarding the blood-renal (glomerular filtration) barrier:

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Molecular size is the only parameter governing passage across it

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Passage depends on both molecular size and electrical charge; the barrier's fixed negative charges repel anionic macromolecules, so charge is a second, independent parameter.

## answer_b
The filtration slit diaphragm is the least effective layer

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The podocyte slit diaphragm is generally regarded as the most selective (most effective) size-limiting layer of the barrier, not the least effective.

## answer_c
The endothelium of the glomerular capillaries is a continuous layer

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The glomerular capillary endothelium is fenestrated, not continuous; its pores allow plasma to reach the basement membrane while retaining blood cells.

## answer_d
The laminae rarae of the glomerular basement membrane are negatively charged

## explanation_d
Correct. The glomerular basement membrane has a central lamina densa flanked by a lamina rara interna and externa. These laminae rarae are rich in polyanions such as heparan sulfate proteoglycans, so they carry a strong fixed negative charge. This charge repels anionic plasma proteins (e.g. albumin) and is central to the barrier's charge selectivity.

## title
Histology of the proximal convoluted tubule

## question
Regarding proximal convoluted tubules, which of the following is true?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
They have a small diameter and a wide lumen

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The proximal convoluted tubule has a large diameter and a narrow, often star-shaped lumen partly filled by the tall brush border; a small tubule with a wide lumen is more typical of the distal tubule.

## answer_b
Their lining cells have a deeply basophilic cytoplasm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. PCT cells have an acidophilic (eosinophilic) cytoplasm because of their abundant mitochondria, not a deeply basophilic one.

## answer_c
They have basal infoldings with parallel (vertically aligned) mitochondria

## explanation_c
Correct. Proximal convoluted tubule cells have deep basal plasma-membrane infoldings that enclose long mitochondria aligned vertically (parallel) to the basement membrane. This basal striation houses the Na+/K+-ATPase that drives the tubule's heavy reabsorptive work. Together with the apical brush border it marks the PCT as the nephron's main resorptive segment.

## answer_d
Their lining cells have clear, distinct cell boundaries

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Extensive lateral interdigitations between neighbouring PCT cells make the cell boundaries indistinct under the light microscope; clear boundaries are seen in the distal tubule.

## title
Structure of the podocyte

## question
One of the following is true regarding the podocytes:

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
They form the outer parietal layer of Bowman's capsule

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Podocytes form the visceral layer of Bowman's capsule, wrapping the glomerular capillaries; the outer parietal layer is a separate simple squamous epithelium.

## answer_b
They are involved in formation of the blood-testis barrier

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The blood-testis barrier is formed by tight junctions between Sertoli cells; podocytes are components of the glomerular filtration barrier, not the testis.

## answer_c
Their cytoplasm contains bundles of actin microfilaments

## explanation_c
Correct. Podocytes send out primary processes that branch into numerous foot processes (pedicels) interdigitating over the glomerular basement membrane. The pedicels are packed with contractile bundles of actin microfilaments, which let the podocyte adjust the width of the filtration slits and resist the high glomerular pressure. Between adjacent pedicels lie the filtration slits bridged by slit diaphragms.

## answer_d
Each has one major and one minor process

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Each podocyte gives rise to several primary processes, each dividing into many secondary foot processes; it is not limited to one major and one minor process.

Epithelium lining the urinary bladder

## question
A 60-year-old man complains of haematuria and a urinary bladder biopsy is reported as normal. What type of epithelium normally lines this patient's urinary bladder?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

D

## explanation_a

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Stratified columnar epithelium is found in parts of the male urethra and large excretory ducts, not the bladder body.

## question
The histological structure of the urinary bladder shows:

## subject
renal

## status

## owner

## vignette

## answer_a
In the empty bladder the urothelium is only 2-3 cell layers thick

## explanation_a
Incorrect. In the empty (contracted) bladder the urothelium is thick, appearing as 5-6 or more cell layers with rounded surface cells; it thins to 2-3 layers only when the bladder is fully distended.

Umbrella cells have an extensive intercellular junctional complex

Correct. Incorrect. The detrusor muscle is arranged as inner longitudinal, middle circular and outer longitudinal layers, not inner/outer circular with a middle longitudinal layer.

The submucosa is formed of dense connective tissue

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The bladder wall has no distinct submucosa; a lamina propria of loose-to-moderately dense connective tissue lies beneath the epithelium, and it is not described as a dense-CT submucosa.

## title
The upper two-thirds of the ureter

## question
The upper two-thirds of the ureter is characterized by:

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Being covered by a serosa

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The ureter is retroperitoneal and covered by an adventitia (fibrous C.T.), not a serosa, along its abdominal course.

## answer_b
A muscularis of two smooth-muscle layers only

## explanation_b
Correct. In its upper two-thirds the ureteric muscularis has just two smooth-muscle layers, an inner longitudinal and an outer circular. In the lower third a third, outer longitudinal layer is added, giving three layers near the bladder. This two-then-three layer pattern is the classic discriminator for the level of a ureteric section.

## answer_c
A lining of stratified columnar epithelium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The ureter is lined by transitional epithelium (urothelium) throughout, not stratified columnar epithelium.

## answer_d
A thick submucosa
