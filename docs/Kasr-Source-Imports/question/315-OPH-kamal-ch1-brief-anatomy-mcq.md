## title
Site of aqueous humour secretion

## question
Aqueous humour is secreted by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Angle of the anterior chamber

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The angle of the anterior chamber is the drainage route for aqueous, not a secretory structure.

## answer_b
The ciliary body

## explanation_b
Correct. Aqueous humour is actively secreted by the epithelium of the ciliary body into the posterior chamber. From there it passes through the pupil into the anterior chamber and drains at the angle. The ciliary body is therefore the source of aqueous, and the balance between its secretion and drainage sets the intraocular pressure.

## answer_c
Posterior surface of the iris

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The posterior surface of the iris borders the posterior chamber but does not secrete aqueous.

## answer_d
The lens

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The lens is an avascular refractive structure and plays no part in aqueous secretion.

## title
Route of aqueous humour drainage

## question
Aqueous humour is drained through:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Angle of the anterior chamber

## explanation_a
Correct. Aqueous humour drains out of the eye at the angle of the anterior chamber, passing through the trabecular meshwork into the canal of Schlemm and then to the episcleral veins. Obstruction of this outflow pathway raises the intraocular pressure and causes glaucoma. The angle is thus the exit route, in contrast to the ciliary body which produces the fluid.

## answer_b
The ciliary body

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The ciliary body secretes aqueous humour; it is the site of production, not drainage.

## answer_c
Posterior surface of the iris

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The posterior surface of the iris is not a drainage structure.

## answer_d
The lens

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The lens has no role in aqueous drainage.

## title
Chamber where aqueous humour is produced

## question
Aqueous humour is produced in which chamber of the eye:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Anterior chamber

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The anterior chamber receives aqueous through the pupil; it is not the site of production.

## answer_b
Posterior chamber

## explanation_b
Correct. The ciliary body secretes aqueous humour into the posterior chamber, the space behind the iris and in front of the lens and zonules. Incorrect. The vitreous cavity contains vitreous gel, not freshly secreted aqueous.

## answer_d
Trabecular meshwork

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The trabecular meshwork is a drainage structure at the angle, not a chamber.

## title
The outer coat of the eye

## question
The outer coat of the eye:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Is formed of cornea, sclera and iris

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The outer fibrous coat is formed of cornea and sclera only; the iris belongs to the middle (uveal) coat.

## answer_b
Is only protective in function

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The outer coat is protective but the cornea is also the eye's main refractive element, so it is not only protective.

## answer_c
Cornea does not have a refractive function

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The cornea provides about 43 diopters, the largest single refractive contribution of the eye.

## answer_d
Protects against the invasion of micro-organisms

## explanation_d
Correct. The outer (fibrous) coat is the cornea and sclera and acts as a tough protective barrier against mechanical injury and microbial invasion. An intact corneal epithelium and dense scleral collagen resist penetration by organisms. The cornea additionally serves a major refractive function, so the coat is both protective and optical.

Opening of the nasolacrimal duct

## question
The nasolacrimal duct opens in:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

Superior nasal meatus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The superior meatus receives the posterior ethmoidal sinus, not the nasolacrimal duct.

Inferior nasal meatus

## explanation_b
Correct. The nasolacrimal duct carries tears from the lacrimal sac and opens into the inferior nasal meatus beneath the inferior turbinate. This is why tears ultimately reach the nose, and why nasolacrimal obstruction causes watering (epiphora). Its opening is guarded by the valve of Hasner.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The middle meatus drains most of the paranasal sinuses (frontal, maxillary, anterior ethmoid), not the tear duct.

## answer_d
Nasopharynx

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The duct opens into the inferior meatus of the nasal cavity, not directly into the nasopharynx.

## title
Factors of corneal transparency (except)

## question
Corneal transparency is due to all of the following factors EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Compact arrangement of corneal stromal fibres

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS a factor). The regular, compact lamellar arrangement of stromal collagen minimises light scatter and is essential for transparency.

## answer_b
Lack of blood vessels

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this IS a factor). Avascularity keeps the cornea clear; corneal neovascularisation reduces transparency.

## answer_c
Myelinated nerve fibres

## explanation_c
Correct. Myelinated nerve fibres are NOT a factor in corneal transparency. Corneal nerves in fact lose their myelin sheaths shortly after entering the cornea, precisely because myelin would scatter light. Transparency instead depends on avascularity, the regular stromal lamellae, the non-keratinized epithelium and the endothelial dehydration pump.

## answer_d
Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this IS a factor). The smooth non-keratinized epithelium provides a clear refracting surface.

## title
A property of the cornea

## question
The cornea has:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
3 layers

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cornea has five layers (epithelium, Bowman, stroma, Descemet, endothelium), not three.

## answer_b
Rich vasculature

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cornea is avascular; its clarity depends on the absence of blood vessels.

## answer_c
Active endothelial pumps

## explanation_c
Correct. The corneal endothelium contains active metabolic pumps that continuously move fluid out of the stroma (deturgescence), keeping the cornea relatively dehydrated and transparent. Endothelial failure lets the stroma swell and cloud. This active pumping is a defining physiological property of the cornea.

## answer_d
Irregularly arranged fibres

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The stromal collagen fibres are regularly and compactly arranged, which is why the cornea is clear.

## answer_e
Rich water content

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The cornea is kept relatively dehydrated by the endothelial pump; excess water content causes oedema and opacity.

## question
Regarding the paranasal sinuses, the following is true EXCEPT:

## subject

C

## answer_a
The ethmoid sinus is located medially

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). The ethmoid sinus lies along the medial wall of the orbit.

## answer_b
The roof of the orbit separates the orbit from the frontal sinus

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). The orbital roof separates the orbit from the frontal sinus (and anterior cranial fossa).

## answer_c
The lateral wall separates the orbit from the sphenoid sinus

## explanation_c
Correct (this is the FALSE statement). The lateral wall is the thickest orbital wall and is NOT related to the sphenoid sinus; the sphenoid sinus is related posteriorly to the medial wall/orbital apex. Sinus relations of the orbit are with the roof (frontal), floor (maxillary) and medial wall (ethmoid and, posteriorly, sphenoid). The lateral wall separates the orbit from the temporal and middle cranial fossae, not a sinus.

## answer_d
The floor separates the orbit from the maxillary sinus

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). The orbital floor separates the orbit from the maxillary sinus, which is why blow-out fractures involve the maxillary antrum.

## title
Suspensory apparatus of the lens

## question
The crystalline lens is suspended from the ciliary body processes by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Sutures

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Lens sutures are the Y-shaped junctions of lens fibres inside the lens, not its suspensory apparatus.

## answer_b
Zonules

## explanation_b
Correct. The crystalline lens is suspended from the ciliary body processes by the zonular fibres (zonules of Zinn), a ring of fine fibres running from the ciliary body to the lens equator. These zonules hold the lens in position and transmit the pull of the ciliary muscle during accommodation. Weakness or rupture of the zonules causes lens subluxation.

## answer_c
Lens capsule

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The lens capsule is the elastic envelope of the lens itself, not the suspensory ligament.

## answer_d
Lens fibres

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lens fibres are the internal cellular substance of the lens.

Contents of the superior orbital fissure

The superior orbital fissure transmits:

## subject
mul

B

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The ophthalmic artery passes through the optic canal, not the superior orbital fissure.

Ophthalmic branch of the trigeminal nerve

Optic nerve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The optic nerve passes through the optic canal, not the superior orbital fissure.

Central retinal artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The central retinal artery travels within the optic nerve through the optic canal.
