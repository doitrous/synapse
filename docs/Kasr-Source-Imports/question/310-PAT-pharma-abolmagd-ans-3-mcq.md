## title
Reversal of non-depolarising blockade by encapsulation

## question
Which of the following reverses a non-depolarising neuromuscular blocker by forming a complex with it that diffuses away from the neuromuscular junction into the circulation and is excreted in the urine?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Edrophonium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Edrophonium is a short-acting anticholinesterase; it reverses blockade indirectly by raising acetylcholine, not by binding the blocker.

## answer_b
Neostigmine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Neostigmine also acts as an anticholinesterase, increasing junctional acetylcholine rather than encapsulating the drug.

## answer_c
Pyridostigmine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pyridostigmine is likewise an anticholinesterase and does not form a complex with the blocker.

## answer_d
Sugammadex

## explanation_d
Correct. Sugammadex is a modified gamma-cyclodextrin that directly ENCAPSULATES steroidal non-depolarising blockers (rocuronium, vecuronium) in a tight 1:1 complex. The complex diffuses away from the neuromuscular junction into the plasma and is excreted unchanged in the urine, rapidly ending the block without relying on acetylcholinesterase inhibition. This encapsulation mechanism is unique among the options.

## title
Autonomic receptor mismatched with its response

## question
Each of the following types of receptor is correctly matched with the RESPONSE resulting from its stimulation EXCEPT:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Muscarinic receptors — decrease heart rate

## explanation_a
Correct match (so not the answer). Muscarinic (M2) stimulation at the SA node slows the heart rate.

## answer_b
Alpha-2 adrenergic receptors — increase release of noradrenaline

## explanation_b
Correct answer. This is the WRONG match: presynaptic alpha-2 adrenoceptors mediate NEGATIVE feedback, so their stimulation DECREASES, not increases, noradrenaline release from the nerve terminal. This autoinhibition is exploited by alpha-2 agonists such as clonidine to lower sympathetic outflow. Because the stated response is the opposite of the true one, this pairing is the exception.

## answer_c
Alpha-1 adrenergic receptors — increase blood pressure

## explanation_c
Correct match (so not the answer). Alpha-1 stimulation causes vasoconstriction and raises blood pressure.

## answer_d
Beta-2 adrenergic receptors — bronchodilatation

## explanation_d
Correct match (so not the answer). Beta-2 stimulation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle, causing bronchodilatation.

## title
Why neostigmine is preferred over physostigmine in myasthenia

## question
Neostigmine is preferred over physostigmine for treating myasthenia gravis because:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It is better absorbed orally

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Both are absorbed; oral absorption is not the distinguishing advantage in myasthenia gravis.

## answer_b
It has a longer duration of action

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Duration is not the key reason; several anticholinesterases are used, and pyridostigmine actually offers longer action.

## answer_c
It has an additional direct agonistic action on nicotinic receptors at the muscle end-plate

## explanation_c
Correct. Besides inhibiting acetylcholinesterase, neostigmine has an ADDITIONAL direct agonist action on nicotinic receptors at the skeletal-muscle end-plate. This directly augments neuromuscular transmission where it is needed in myasthenia gravis, giving it an advantage over physostigmine, which lacks this direct end-plate action. That dual mechanism is why it is preferred.

## answer_d
It penetrates the blood-brain barrier

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Physostigmine, not neostigmine, crosses the blood-brain barrier; CNS penetration would be a disadvantage in treating a peripheral neuromuscular disease.

## title
Atropine substitute used in Parkinsonism

## question
The following atropine substitute is useful in the treatment of Parkinsonism:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pirenzepine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pirenzepine is a selective M1 antagonist used to reduce gastric acid secretion, not a central antiparkinsonian agent.

## answer_b
Hyoscine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hyoscine is used for motion sickness and as a sedative antimuscarinic, not for Parkinsonism.

## answer_c
Homatropine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Homatropine is a topical mydriatic/cycloplegic for the eye.

## answer_d
Benztropine

## explanation_d
Correct. Benztropine is a centrally acting antimuscarinic that reduces the relative cholinergic overactivity in the basal ganglia caused by dopamine deficiency, so it relieves the tremor and rigidity of Parkinsonism (especially drug-induced parkinsonism). Its central antimuscarinic action is what makes it the useful atropine substitute here.

## title
Component NOT part of organophosphate poisoning treatment

## question
Treatment of acute organophosphorus poisoning includes all the following EXCEPT:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

B

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it IS part of treatment). Pralidoxime reactivates cholinesterase and is a mainstay of therapy.

Methacholine

## explanation_b
Correct answer. Methacholine is a direct cholinergic (muscarinic) AGONIST, so giving it would ADD to the acetylcholine excess and worsen the cholinergic crisis; it is therefore NOT part of treatment and is the exception. Diazepam

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it IS part of treatment). Diazepam controls organophosphate-induced seizures and agitation.

## answer_d
Gastric lavage

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it IS part of treatment). Gastric lavage/decontamination limits further absorption after ingestion.

## title
Endpoint NOT targeted by atropine in organophosphate poisoning

## question
The following are target endpoints for atropine therapy in organophosphate poisoning EXCEPT:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Bradycardia < 60 beats/minute

## explanation_a
Correct answer. Atropinisation aims to REVERSE the cholinergic excess, so a persisting bradycardia below 60/min is a sign of UNDER-treatment, not a therapeutic endpoint. Wanting bradycardia as a goal is the opposite of the aim, making this the exception.

## answer_b
Dry axillae

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it IS an endpoint). Drying of secretions, shown by dry axillae, is a classic marker of adequate atropinisation.

## answer_c
Mydriasis

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it IS an endpoint). Mydriasis reflects successful antimuscarinic effect.

## answer_d
Systolic blood pressure > 80 mmHg

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it IS an endpoint). Restoring an adequate systolic blood pressure (>80 mmHg) confirms reversal of the cardiovascular depression.
