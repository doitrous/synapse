## title
Motor supply of the intrinsic laryngeal muscles

## question
Innervation of the intrinsic muscles of the larynx comes mainly from:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The superior laryngeal nerve

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The superior laryngeal nerve's external branch supplies only the cricothyroid muscle, and its internal branch is sensory to the supraglottis; it does not supply the other intrinsic muscles.

## answer_b
The recurrent laryngeal nerve

## explanation_b
Correct. The recurrent laryngeal nerve is the motor nerve of all the intrinsic muscles of the larynx except the cricothyroid. It also carries sensation from below the vocal cords. Injury to it therefore paralyses cord movement and changes the voice.

## answer_c
The glossopharyngeal nerve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The glossopharyngeal nerve supplies the pharynx and posterior third of the tongue, not the intrinsic laryngeal muscles.

## answer_d
The hypoglossal nerve

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The hypoglossal nerve is the motor nerve of the tongue muscles and has no role in laryngeal muscle innervation.

## title
Muscle not supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve

## question
The recurrent laryngeal nerve supplies all the muscles of the larynx EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Interarytenoid

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The interarytenoid is an intrinsic laryngeal muscle supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve, so it is not the exception.

## answer_b
Cricothyroid

## explanation_b
Correct. The cricothyroid is the one intrinsic laryngeal muscle NOT supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve; it is supplied by the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. It tenses the vocal cords, so a superior laryngeal palsy weakens and lowers the voice. Every other intrinsic laryngeal muscle receives the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

## answer_c
Thyrohyoid

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The thyrohyoid is not an intrinsic laryngeal muscle at all — it is an infrahyoid strap muscle supplied by the C1 fibres running with the hypoglossal nerve, so it lies outside the intrinsic-muscle group the stem is asking about.

## answer_d
Posterior cricoarytenoid

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The posterior cricoarytenoid, the sole abductor of the vocal cords, is supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

## answer_e
Lateral cricoarytenoid

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The lateral cricoarytenoid is an adductor supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

## title
Correct statement about laryngomalacia

## question
Choose the correct statement regarding laryngomalacia:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It starts in infancy and persists till adulthood.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Laryngomalacia appears in the first weeks of life and typically resolves by 18-24 months as the laryngeal cartilage stiffens; it does not persist into adulthood.

## answer_b
There is hoarseness of voice in all cases.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The voice and cry are characteristically normal because the vocal cords are not involved, so hoarseness in every case is untrue.

## answer_c
Stridor decreases on lying on the back and increases on lying on the face.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The stridor of laryngomalacia is worse when the infant lies supine (on the back) and improves in the prone position, the opposite of what this option states.

## answer_d
Most cases need only observation as the condition resolves spontaneously.

## explanation_d
Correct. Laryngomalacia is a self-limiting congenital softening of the supraglottic cartilages, and the great majority of infants need only reassurance and observation because it resolves spontaneously as the larynx matures. Surgery (supraglottoplasty) is reserved for the few with failure to thrive or severe obstruction. It is the commonest congenital cause of stridor.

## answer_e
It frequently needs an artificial airway with tracheostomy or endotracheal intubation.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Only a small minority with severe obstruction or feeding failure need airway intervention; most never require tracheostomy or intubation.

## title
Infant stridor with a hoarse cry — the unlikely diagnosis

## question
An infant is noticed to have stridor and a hoarse cry. All of the following diagnoses are possible EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Laryngeal web

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A laryngeal web lies at the level of the vocal cords and produces both stridor and an abnormal (hoarse or weak) cry, so it fits the picture.

## answer_b
Laryngeal paralysis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Congenital laryngeal (vocal cord) paralysis affects cord movement and causes both stridor and a hoarse or weak cry.

## answer_c
Congenital laryngeal cyst

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A congenital laryngeal cyst can obstruct the airway and distort the cords, giving stridor together with a hoarse cry.

## answer_d
Laryngomalacia

## explanation_d
Correct. Laryngomalacia is the exception because it is a supraglottic disorder that spares the vocal cords, so the cry and voice are normal even though the infant has inspiratory stridor. A hoarse cry points instead to a lesion at cord level (web, paralysis or cyst). A normal voice with stridor is what suggests laryngomalacia.

## subject

Incorrect. Inspiratory stridor beginning within the first weeks of life is characteristic, so this statement is true.

## title
Clues to an inhaled foreign body in a child

## question
Which of the following raises the suspicion of an inhaled foreign body in a young child?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
A history of a sudden attack of cough, choking and cyanosis.

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the single answer. A witnessed choking crisis strongly suggests an inhaled foreign body, but it is only one of several clues listed.

## answer_b
A chest X-ray showing unilateral lung emphysema.

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the single answer. A ball-valve foreign body traps air distally and produces unilateral obstructive emphysema — a real sign, but not the only one.

## answer_c
A chest X-ray showing unilateral lung atelectasis.

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the single answer. Complete bronchial obstruction instead collapses that lung (unilateral atelectasis); this too is only one of the listed features.

## answer_d
Persistent wheezes over one lung.

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the single answer. Persistent wheeze localised to one lung is a classic sign of a bronchial foreign body, but again only one of several clues.

## answer_e
All of the above.

## explanation_e
Correct. All the listed features — a sudden choking, coughing and cyanotic attack, unilateral emphysema or atelectasis on the chest film, and persistent unilateral wheeze — raise the suspicion of an inhaled foreign body. Any of them in a young child mandates bronchoscopy. That is why 'all of the above' is the answer.

Commonest site an inhaled foreign body lodges

## question
An inhaled foreign body is more commonly lodged in the:

## subject
mul

## status

Right main bronchus

## explanation_a

Left main bronchus

Incorrect. The left main bronchus is narrower and more horizontal, so it receives aspirated objects less often than the right.

Right or left main bronchi equally

Incorrect. Although a foreign body can occasionally enter either side, the question asks for the commonest site, which is specifically the right.

## answer_d
Larynx

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Large objects may impact at the larynx, but small inhaled foreign bodies usually pass through to a main bronchus, the right one most often.

## title
Premalignant benign laryngeal lesion

## question
Which of the following benign lesions of the larynx can be pre-cancerous?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Leukoplakia

## explanation_a
Correct. Laryngeal leukoplakia is a white keratotic patch of the vocal cord, driven by smoking, and it carries epithelial dysplasia that can progress to squamous cell carcinoma. It is the classic premalignant lesion of the larynx and must be biopsied and followed up. Stopping smoking is essential.

## answer_b
Reinke's oedema

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Reinke's oedema (polypoid cord swelling from smoking and voice abuse) causes a deep, hoarse voice but is not itself premalignant.

## answer_c
Laryngeal nodule

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A laryngeal (singer's) nodule is a benign response to voice abuse with no malignant potential.

## answer_d
Laryngosclerosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Laryngosclerosis (laryngoscleroma) is a chronic granulomatous infection; it causes stenosis, not malignant transformation.

## answer_e
Juvenile-onset recurrent respiratory papillomatosis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Juvenile-onset recurrent respiratory papillomatosis is generally benign and only rarely undergoes malignant change, so among these options leukoplakia is the established premalignant lesion.

## title
Laser used in microlaryngosurgery

## question
The laser commonly used in microlaryngosurgery is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
CO2 laser.

## explanation_a
Correct. The carbon dioxide (CO2) laser is the one commonly used in microlaryngeal surgery, because its beam is strongly absorbed by water in soft tissue, giving very precise, shallow cutting with minimal thermal spread to the surrounding cords. This precision suits delicate work such as excising papillomas or small cord lesions. It is coupled to the operating microscope for microlaryngoscopy.

## answer_b
YAG laser.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The Nd:YAG laser penetrates deeply and is used for bulkier or vascular lesions (e.g. tracheobronchial tumours), not for precise cord microsurgery.

## answer_c
Argon laser.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The argon laser is absorbed by pigment and is used mainly in ophthalmology and dermatology, not routinely for microlaryngosurgery.

## answer_d
KTP laser.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The KTP laser is used for some vascular and airway lesions, but the standard laser for microlaryngeal surgery is the CO2 laser.
