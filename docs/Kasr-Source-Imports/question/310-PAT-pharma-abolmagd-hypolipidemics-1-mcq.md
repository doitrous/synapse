## title
Agent that blocks absorption of exogenous cholesterol

## question
A patient on pravastatin needs an added agent to block the absorption of exogenous (dietary) cholesterol. Which of the following serves this function?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Niacin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Niacin lowers lipids by reducing hepatic VLDL production and lipolysis, not by blocking intestinal cholesterol absorption.

## answer_b
Probucol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Probucol is an antioxidant lipid-lowering agent; it does not act by blocking cholesterol absorption.

## answer_c
Gemfibrozil

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gemfibrozil is a fibrate (PPAR-alpha agonist) that mainly lowers triglycerides; it does not block cholesterol absorption.

## answer_d
Ezetimibe

## explanation_d
Correct. Ezetimibe inhibits the NPC1L1 sterol transporter in the intestinal brush border, selectively BLOCKING absorption of dietary and biliary cholesterol. This complements a statin (which reduces synthesis), so adding ezetimibe to reduce absorbed cholesterol is exactly the required action.

## title
Antihyperlipidemic that predisposes to gallstones

## question
A patient on treatment for hyperlipidemia presents with gallstones. Which of the following drugs could have caused them?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Statins do not increase biliary cholesterol saturation; their main adverse effects are myopathy and transaminase elevation.

## answer_b
Fenofibrate

## explanation_b
Correct. FIBRATES such as fenofibrate increase biliary cholesterol secretion and raise the cholesterol saturation of bile, promoting formation of cholesterol GALLSTONES. This lithogenic effect is a recognised adverse effect of fibrates, so fenofibrate is the offending drug.

Ezetimibe

Incorrect. Ezetimibe reduces cholesterol absorption and is not associated with gallstones.

## answer_d
Colestipol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Bile-acid sequestrants like colestipol bind bile acids in the gut; their adverse effects are gastrointestinal (constipation), not gallstones.

## title
Risk of combining statins with fibrates

## question
Combined use of statins with fibrates increases the risk of:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This combination does not promote coagulation.

## answer_b
Cardiac disease

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Both drugs are used to reduce cardiovascular risk, not increase it.

## answer_c
Gall bladder stones

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gallstones are a fibrate effect; they are not the specific hazard of the statin-fibrate combination.

## answer_d
Myopathy

## explanation_d
Correct. Both statins and fibrates can cause muscle toxicity, and together they markedly increase the risk of MYOPATHY and rhabdomyolysis (worst with gemfibrozil, which raises statin levels). This additive/pharmacokinetic muscle toxicity is the key danger of co-prescribing them, so fenofibrate is preferred if a fibrate must be combined.

## title
Antihyperlipidemic drug incorrectly paired with its mechanism

## question
Which of the following antihyperlipidemic drugs is INCORRECTLY paired with its mechanism of action?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cholestyramine — Prevents reabsorption of bile acids

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this pairing is correct). Cholestyramine binds bile acids in the gut and prevents their reabsorption, increasing hepatic conversion of cholesterol to bile acids.

## answer_b
Atorvastatin — Inhibits HMG-CoA reductase

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this pairing is correct). Atorvastatin competitively inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis.

## answer_c
Fenofibrate — Inhibits lipoprotein lipase activity

## explanation_c
Correct. This is the MISMATCH. Fibrates are PPAR-alpha agonists that ACTIVATE (not inhibit) lipoprotein lipase, enhancing clearance of triglyceride-rich lipoproteins. Stating that fenofibrate inhibits lipoprotein lipase reverses its true action, making this the incorrect pairing.

## answer_d
Ezetimibe — Decreases intestinal absorption of dietary cholesterol

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this pairing is correct). Ezetimibe blocks the intestinal NPC1L1 transporter, reducing cholesterol absorption.

## title
False statement about cholesterol-synthesis inhibitors

## question
All the following statements concerning drugs that inhibit cholesterol synthesis (statins) are TRUE, EXCEPT:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
These drugs often cause myopathy

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). Myopathy (and rarely rhabdomyolysis) is a recognised statin adverse effect.

## answer_b
Do not initiate statin therapy after acute coronary syndromes

## explanation_b
Correct. This is the FALSE statement. High-intensity statin therapy should be STARTED EARLY after an acute coronary syndrome, where it reduces recurrent events and mortality (partly through pleiotropic, plaque-stabilising effects). Advising against initiation after ACS is therefore wrong.

## answer_c
They work in part by increasing the rate of LDL clearance from the plasma

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). By lowering intracellular cholesterol, statins upregulate hepatic LDL receptors, increasing LDL clearance from plasma.

## answer_d
They have pleiotropic effects

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). Statins have pleiotropic effects (improved endothelial function, anti-inflammatory and plaque-stabilising actions) beyond LDL lowering.

## title
Mechanism of the hypolipidemic action of simvastatin

## question
The hypolipidemic action of simvastatin is due to which of the following?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inhibition of HMG-CoA reductase

## explanation_a
Correct. Simvastatin competitively inhibits HMG-CoA REDUCTASE, the rate-limiting enzyme of hepatic cholesterol synthesis. Falling intracellular cholesterol upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, increasing clearance of LDL from plasma and lowering LDL cholesterol. Enzyme inhibition with compensatory LDL-receptor upregulation is the statin mechanism.

## answer_b
Reducing lipolysis in adipose tissues

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Inhibiting adipose lipolysis (reducing free fatty acid flux) is a mechanism of niacin, not statins.

## answer_c
Sequestering bile acids in the small intestine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bile-acid sequestration is the mechanism of resins (cholestyramine), not statins.

## answer_d
Binding PPAR-alpha receptors in the liver and adipose tissue

## explanation_d
Incorrect. PPAR-alpha agonism is the mechanism of fibrates, not statins.

## title
Pathway for which cholesterol is NOT a precursor

## question
Cholesterol serves as a precursor to all the following pathways EXCEPT:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Bile acid synthesis

## explanation_a
Incorrect (cholesterol IS a precursor). Bile acids are synthesised from cholesterol in the liver.

## answer_b
Thyroid hormone synthesis

## explanation_b
Correct. This is the EXCEPTION. Thyroid hormones are made from the amino acid TYROSINE and iodine, not from cholesterol. Cholesterol is the precursor of bile acids, steroid hormones and vitamin D, but has no role in thyroid hormone synthesis, so this is the pathway it does not feed.

## answer_c
Steroid hormone synthesis

## explanation_c
Incorrect (cholesterol IS a precursor). Adrenal and gonadal steroid hormones are derived from cholesterol.

## answer_d
Vitamin D3 synthesis

## explanation_d
Incorrect (cholesterol IS a precursor). 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin is converted to vitamin D3 on UV exposure.
