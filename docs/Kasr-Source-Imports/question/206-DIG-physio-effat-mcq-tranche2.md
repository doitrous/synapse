## title
Why an oral glucose load clears faster than an intravenous one

## question
One group of subjects is given 50 g of glucose intravenously and another 50 g orally. Which factor best explains why the oral glucose load is cleared from the blood faster than the intravenous load?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
CCK-induced insulin release

## explanation_a
Incorrect. CCK is released by fat and protein, not by an oral glucose load, and it is not the incretin responsible for enhanced insulin secretion after oral glucose.

## answer_b
CCK-induced vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) release

## explanation_b
Incorrect. VIP is an enteric neurotransmitter causing smooth-muscle relaxation and secretion; it does not mediate the insulin response to oral glucose.

## answer_c
GIP-induced glucagon release

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Glucagon raises blood glucose and would slow clearance, so a glucagon-releasing action cannot explain faster clearance of oral glucose.

## answer_d
GIP-induced insulin release

## explanation_d
Correct. Oral glucose passes the gut and releases glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP) from duodenal K cells. GIP is an incretin that strongly potentiates glucose-stimulated insulin release from pancreatic beta cells. Because intravenous glucose bypasses the gut and releases no incretin, the oral load produces more insulin and is cleared faster — the incretin effect.

## title
How the cephalic phase of gastric secretion is abolished

## question
The cephalic phase of gastric secretion accounts for about 30 percent of the acid response to a meal. Which of the following can completely eliminate the cephalic phase?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Atropine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Atropine blocks muscarinic receptors and reduces vagally driven acid secretion, but it does not completely abolish the cephalic phase and has widespread anticholinergic effects.

## answer_b
Histamine H2 blocker

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An H2 blocker reduces histamine-mediated acid secretion but only blunts, rather than eliminates, the vagally mediated cephalic phase.

## answer_c
Vagotomy

## explanation_c
Correct. The cephalic phase — triggered by the sight, smell, taste and thought of food — is mediated entirely through the vagus nerve. Cutting the vagus (vagotomy) removes this efferent pathway. Therefore vagotomy can completely abolish the cephalic phase of gastric secretion.

## answer_d
Sympathectomy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The sympathetic system is generally inhibitory to gastric secretion, so sympathectomy would not eliminate the vagally driven cephalic phase.

## title
Second messenger raised by cholera toxin in enterocytes

## question
After a community outbreak of Vibrio cholerae, the toxin acts on the intestinal crypt epithelial cells. It is most likely to cause an increase in which of the following?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)

## explanation_a
Correct. Cholera toxin irreversibly activates the stimulatory G protein (Gs) of adenylate cyclase in the enterocyte. This causes a sustained, irreversible rise in intracellular cAMP. The elevated cAMP then drives the secretory response responsible for the diarrhoea.

## answer_b
Cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. cGMP is the second messenger raised by the heat-stable enterotoxin of E. coli, not by cholera toxin.

## answer_c
Chloride absorption

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cholera toxin causes chloride secretion into the lumen, not chloride absorption.

## answer_d
Sodium absorption

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The toxin impairs, rather than increases, sodium absorption; net secretion of ions and water is the result.

## title
Ion channel irreversibly opened in cholera

## question
In the same patients with cholera, which type of ion channel in the intestinal epithelial cells is most likely to be irreversibly opened?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Calcium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Calcium channels are not the effector opened by the cholera-toxin/cAMP pathway.

## answer_b
Chloride

## explanation_b
Correct. The raised cAMP produced by cholera toxin phosphorylates and irreversibly opens the apical chloride channel (CFTR). Chloride pours into the lumen, sodium follows to preserve electroneutrality, and water follows the osmotic gradient. The result is the massive secretory diarrhoea of cholera.

## answer_c
Magnesium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Magnesium channels play no role in the cholera secretory mechanism.

## answer_d
Potassium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although potassium is lost in the stool, the channel irreversibly opened by the toxin is the chloride (CFTR) channel, which is the primary driver of secretion.

## title
Fall in which mediator allows the post-prandial rise in acid secretion

## question
After a meal the gastric pH first rises, and a few minutes later the rate of acid secretion increases again. A decrease in which substance most likely facilitates this secondary increase in acid secretion?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Gastrin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Gastrin stimulates acid secretion, so a decrease in gastrin would reduce, not increase, acid output.

## answer_b
Cholecystokinin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. CCK has essentially no physiological role in regulating gastric acid secretion in this setting.

## answer_c
Somatostatin

## explanation_c
Correct. When the stomach is empty and acidic, the low pH stimulates antral D cells to release somatostatin, which directly and indirectly (via suppression of gastrin) inhibits acid secretion. When a meal buffers the acid and raises the pH, somatostatin release falls. The removal of this paracrine brake allows the secondary rise in acid secretion.

## answer_d
Vasoactive intestinal peptide

## explanation_d
Incorrect. VIP is not a physiological regulator of the post-prandial rise in gastric acid secretion.

## title
Forerunner mechanism of gastric ulcer formation

## question
A man is found to have a chronic gastric ulcer that has perforated toward the liver. Which mechanism is a forerunner to gastric ulcer formation?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Back-leak of hydrogen ions into the mucosa

## explanation_a
Correct. When the gastric mucosal barrier is damaged, hydrogen ions leak back from the lumen into the mucosa. The intracellular buffers become saturated, cell pH falls, and injury with histamine release, capillary damage and focal ischaemia follows. This mucosal lesion is the forerunner of a gastric ulcer.

## answer_b
Mucus secretion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Mucus secretion strengthens and protects the mucosal barrier; it opposes ulcer formation rather than causing it.

## answer_c
Proton pump inhibition

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Proton pump inhibition reduces acid and is a treatment for ulcers, not a cause.

## answer_d
Tight junctions between cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tight junctions between surface cells are part of the intact barrier that prevents acid back-leak; they protect against, rather than cause, ulceration.

## title
Mediator of receptive relaxation of the LES and fundus

## question
As a boy swallows, his lower oesophageal sphincter and gastric fundus relax while the food is still in the oesophagus. Which substance most likely mediates this relaxation?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Gastrin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Gastrin has no significant effect on smooth-muscle relaxation at physiological levels and does not mediate receptive relaxation.

## answer_b
Motilin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Motilin drives the migrating motor complex between meals; it is not the mediator of receptive relaxation during swallowing.

## answer_c
Nitric oxide

## explanation_c
Correct. Receptive relaxation (and the accompanying relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter and fundus) is a vagovagal reflex. The inhibitory enteric neurotransmitter released onto the smooth muscle is nitric oxide (with VIP). Nitric oxide therefore mediates the relaxation that accommodates the swallowed bolus.

## answer_d
Norepinephrine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Norepinephrine is a sympathetic transmitter and is not involved in the vagally mediated receptive relaxation.

## title
Hormone whose loss causes GI mucosal atrophy on intravenous feeding

## question
A man is fed intravenously for several weeks after a severe accident, and his gastrointestinal mucosa atrophies. This is most likely because the blood level of which hormone is reduced?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cholecystokinin only

## explanation_a
Incorrect. CCK stimulates growth of the exocrine pancreas rather than being the main trophic hormone for the gastrointestinal mucosa.

## answer_b
Gastrin only

## explanation_b
Correct. Gastrin has a critical trophic role, stimulating growth of the mucosa throughout the stomach and intestines. Intravenous feeding removes the luminal nutrients that normally release gastrin, so blood gastrin falls. The loss of this trophic drive leads to atrophy of the gastrointestinal mucosa.

## answer_c
Secretin only

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Secretin stimulates pancreatic and biliary bicarbonate secretion and pancreatic growth, not general mucosal growth.

## answer_d
Gastrin and cholecystokinin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although CCK is trophic to the pancreas, the mucosal atrophy of the gut is attributed to reduced gastrin, so the single best answer is gastrin only.
