## title
Correct statement about gastric emptying in gastroparesis

## question
A patient with diabetes mellitus presents with gastroesophageal reflux disease accompanying gastroparesis. Which of the following statements about gastric emptying is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Solids empty more rapidly than liquids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Solids must be liquefied by trituration before they leave the stomach, so the emptying of liquids begins before that of solids, not after.

## answer_b
Meals containing fat empty faster than carbohydrate-rich food

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Emptying is fastest for a carbohydrate meal and slowest after a fatty meal, because fat in the duodenum evokes the strongest inhibitory feedback.

## answer_c
Hyperosmolality of duodenal contents initiates a decrease in gastric emptying

## explanation_c
Correct. Gastric emptying is governed by inhibitory feedback from the proximal small bowel. Duodenal osmoreceptors sense hyperosmolar chyme and, largely through a neural (enterogastric) reflex, slow gastric emptying. This feedback is exaggerated relative to gastric drive in diabetic gastroparesis.

## answer_d
Acidification of the antrum increases gastric emptying

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Acid in the antrum inhibits gastrin secretion and tends to reduce gastric motility, so it slows rather than increases emptying.

## answer_e
Vagal stimulation decreases receptive relaxation in the upper portion of the stomach

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The vagus mediates receptive relaxation: vagal activity relaxes the fundus and upper body to accommodate a meal, so vagal stimulation increases, not decreases, receptive relaxation.

## title
Correct statement about small intestinal motility

## question
A 27-year-old medical student with irritable bowel syndrome has altered intestinal motility with fluctuating constipation and diarrhoea. Which of the following statements about small intestinal motility is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Contractile frequency is constant from duodenum to terminal ileum

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The basal electrical rhythm falls along the small intestine, from roughly 12 cycles/min in the duodenum to about 8/min in the distal ileum, so contractile frequency is not constant.

## answer_b
Peristalsis is the only contractile activity that occurs during feeding

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Feeding drives peristalsis together with segmental and tonic contractions, so peristalsis is not the only activity.

## answer_c
Migrating motor complexes occur during the digestive period

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Migrating motor complexes are a fasting (interdigestive) pattern that sweeps residue toward the ileum; they stop immediately when food is eaten, so they do not occur during the digestive period.

## answer_d
Vagotomy abolishes contractile activity during the digestive period

## explanation_d
Incorrect. After vagotomy small-intestinal contractile activity becomes irregular and chaotic but is not abolished, because it is driven by the enteric nervous system and the interstitial cells of Cajal.

## answer_e
Contractile activity is initiated in response to bowel wall distention

## explanation_e
Correct. Small-intestinal contractions are initiated by distension of the bowel wall. The interstitial cells of Cajal set the basal electrical rhythm, and contractions occur on its depolarising phase; wall stretch triggers the peristaltic and segmenting activity that mixes and propels chyme.

## title
Malabsorption after terminal ileal resection

## question
A 57-year-old man undergoes resection of the distal 100 cm of the terminal ileum as part of treatment for Crohn's disease. The patient will most likely develop malabsorption of which of the following?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Iron

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Iron is absorbed in the duodenum and proximal jejunum, so terminal ileal resection does not selectively impair it.

## answer_b
Folate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Folate is absorbed in the proximal small intestine (duodenum and jejunum) and is spared by terminal ileal resection.

## answer_c
Lactose

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Lactose is digested by brush-border lactase and absorbed as glucose and galactose in the proximal small intestine, not the terminal ileum.

## answer_d
Bile salts

## explanation_d
Correct. The terminal ileum carries the specialised active transporters that reabsorb conjugated bile salts in the enterohepatic circulation. Resecting it prevents bile-salt reabsorption, causing bile-salt (and fat) malabsorption with steatorrhoea; unabsorbed bile acids reaching the colon also stimulate secretion and cause diarrhoea.

## answer_e
Protein

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Protein is absorbed as amino acids and small peptides in the proximal small intestine, so it is not the nutrient selectively lost after distal ileal resection.
