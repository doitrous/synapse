## title
Glaucoma drug class that reduces aqueous production

## question
Regarding the treatment of glaucoma, which one of the following groups of drugs reduces aqueous humour production?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Beta-blockers

## explanation_a
Correct. Topical beta-blockers such as timolol block beta receptors on the ciliary epithelium, reducing the formation (production) of aqueous humour and thereby lowering intraocular pressure. Decreasing inflow, rather than increasing outflow, is their mechanism in glaucoma. This makes beta-blockers the aqueous-suppressing group here.

## answer_b
Miotics

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Miotics (pilocarpine) lower pressure by INCREASING trabecular outflow through ciliary-muscle contraction, not by reducing production.

## answer_c
Prostaglandin analogues

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost) lower pressure by increasing UVEOSCLERAL outflow, again an outflow effect, not reduced production.

## answer_d
Hyperosmotic agents

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hyperosmotic agents (mannitol) draw fluid out of the eye osmotically for acute pressure reduction; they do not reduce aqueous production.

## title
Mechanism of apraclonidine in glaucoma

## question
Apraclonidine is used for the treatment of glaucoma. Which of the following describes its mechanism of action?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Selective stimulation of alpha-2 receptors

## explanation_a
Correct. Apraclonidine is a selective alpha-2 adrenergic AGONIST. Stimulating alpha-2 receptors on the ciliary body reduces aqueous humour formation (and enhances outflow), lowering intraocular pressure. This alpha-2 agonism, shared with brimonidine, is its mechanism in glaucoma.

## answer_b
Non-selective block of beta receptors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Beta-blockade is the mechanism of timolol, not apraclonidine.

## answer_c
Selective stimulation of muscarinic receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Muscarinic stimulation is the action of miotics such as pilocarpine, not apraclonidine.

## answer_d
Agonistic action on prostaglandin receptors

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prostaglandin-receptor agonism describes latanoprost, a different class.

## title
Adrenaline prodrug used topically in glaucoma

## question
Which of the following is a prodrug of adrenaline used topically in glaucoma?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Brimonidine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Brimonidine is a selective alpha-2 agonist, not a prodrug of adrenaline.

## answer_b
Dipivefrine

## explanation_b
Correct. Dipivefrine (dipivefrin) is the dipivalyl ester PRODRUG of adrenaline (epinephrine). Its lipophilic ester groups improve corneal penetration; once inside the eye, esterases hydrolyse it to active adrenaline, which lowers intraocular pressure. Being an inactive ester that is converted to adrenaline in situ is precisely what makes it a prodrug.

## answer_c
Phenylpropanolamine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Phenylpropanolamine is a systemic sympathomimetic decongestant, not an ocular adrenaline prodrug.

## answer_d
Dorzolamide

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dorzolamide is a topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, not a prodrug of adrenaline.

## title
Mechanism of botulinum toxin at the neuromuscular junction

## question
Botulinum toxin blocks neuromuscular transmission by which of the following mechanisms?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Interfering with the release of acetylcholine from vesicles at the synapse

## explanation_a
Correct. Botulinum toxin cleaves SNARE proteins (such as SNAP-25) that are required for synaptic-vesicle fusion, so it PREVENTS the release of acetylcholine from the presynaptic motor nerve terminal. Without acetylcholine release there is no end-plate depolarisation, producing flaccid paralysis. Blocking transmitter release is its defining mechanism.

## answer_b
Closure of Na+ channels at the postsynaptic membrane

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Botulinum toxin does not act on postsynaptic sodium channels; it works presynaptically on transmitter release.

## answer_c
Opening of K+ channels at the presynaptic membrane

## explanation_c
Incorrect. It does not open presynaptic potassium channels; it disables the vesicle-fusion machinery.

## answer_d
Opening of Cl- channels at the postsynaptic membrane

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Postsynaptic chloride-channel opening is not the mechanism; the block is presynaptic and cholinergic.

## title
Classification of baclofen

## question
Baclofen is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
A centrally acting muscle relaxant

## explanation_a
Correct. Baclofen is a CENTRALLY acting muscle relaxant: it is a GABA-B receptor agonist that reduces excitatory transmission in the spinal cord, decreasing spasticity in conditions such as multiple sclerosis and spinal injury. Its action on central (spinal) reflexes, not on the muscle itself, defines it as centrally acting.

## answer_b
A directly acting muscle relaxant

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A directly (peripherally) acting relaxant works on the muscle fibre itself, e.g. dantrolene, which blocks calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum; baclofen does not.

## answer_c
A peripherally acting muscle relaxant

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Peripherally acting relaxants act at the neuromuscular junction or muscle; baclofen acts centrally.

## answer_d
Both a centrally and a peripherally acting muscle relaxant

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Baclofen is purely central, not both central and peripheral.
