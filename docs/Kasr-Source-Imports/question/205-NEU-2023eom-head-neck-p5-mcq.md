## title
Changes during near vision (accommodation)

## question
Which of the following occurs during near vision?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
More light enters the eye

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Near vision is accompanied by pupillary CONSTRICTION (part of the near reflex), so less, not more, light enters; and light entry is not the mechanism of focusing.

## answer_b
The curvature of the cornea increases

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cornea has a fixed curvature; it is the lens, not the cornea, whose curvature changes during accommodation.

## answer_c
The ciliary muscles relax and decrease the tension from the suspensory ligaments

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The ciliary muscle CONTRACTS (it does not relax) during near vision; this is what slackens the zonules. The statement inverts the muscle action.

## answer_d
Increase in the curvature of the anterior surface of the lens

## explanation_d
Correct. In accommodation the ciliary muscle contracts, relaxing the suspensory ligaments so the elastic lens bulges and the curvature of its anterior surface increases. This raises the lens's refractive power to focus near objects on the retina. The lens change is the essence of accommodation.

## title
Sound transmission through the oval and round windows

## question
During transmission of sound waves in the cochlea:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The foot of the stapes moves inward against the oval window, and the round window bulges outward

## explanation_a
Correct. The stapes footplate sits in the oval window; when it moves inward it displaces the incompressible cochlear fluid, and because the fluid cannot compress, the round window membrane bulges outward to accommodate the volume change. This oval-in/round-out reciprocity drives the travelling wave on the basilar membrane.

## answer_b
The foot of the stapes moves inward against the round window, and the oval window bulges outward

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The stapes seats on the OVAL window, not the round window; the round window is the pressure-relief membrane that bulges outward.

## answer_c
The head of the malleus moves inward against the oval window, and the round window bulges outward

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The malleus transmits vibration from the tympanic membrane to the incus, but it is the stapes footplate, not the malleus head, that contacts the oval window.

## answer_d
The incus moves inward against the oval window, and the round window bulges outward

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The incus links malleus to stapes; it does not itself seat on the oval window.

## title
Ion that depolarizes the hair cell

## question
Which molecules move from the endolymph into the stereocilia and depolarize the hair cell?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Calcium ions

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Calcium entry occurs basally at the synapse to trigger transmitter release, but the apical transduction current that depolarises the hair cell is carried by potassium.

## answer_b
Chloride ions

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Chloride is not the transduction ion; the apical mechanotransduction channels admit cations, chiefly K+.

## answer_c
Potassium ions

## explanation_c
Correct. The endolymph bathing the stereocilia is uniquely rich in potassium; when the stereocilia bend toward the tallest row the mechanogated channels open and K+ flows from the endolymph into the hair cell, depolarising it. This unusual use of K+ influx (rather than Na+) as the depolarising current is a hallmark of hair-cell transduction.

## answer_d
Sodium ions

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sodium is the usual depolarising ion in most excitable cells, but in hair cells the endolymph is K+-rich and it is K+, not Na+, that carries the transduction current.

## title
Histology of the retinal pigment epithelium

## question
In the retinal pigmented epithelium, the correct answer is:

## subject
neuro

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inner parts of the cells are rich in melanin pigments

## explanation_a
Correct. The inner (apical) cytoplasm and apical microvilli of the retinal pigment epithelial cells are rich in melanin granules, which absorb stray light and improve image quality. The apical processes also ensheath the photoreceptor outer segments. This apical concentration of melanin is the correct statement.

## answer_b
It is resting on the outer limiting membrane

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The RPE rests externally on Bruch's membrane, not on the outer limiting membrane (which is formed by Muller-cell junctions deeper in the neural retina).

## answer_c
Their lateral cell membranes lack cellular junctions

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Adjacent RPE cells are joined laterally by junctional complexes (tight junctions) that form the outer blood-retinal barrier; their lateral membranes do NOT lack junctions.

## answer_d
Their microvilli interdigitate with ganglion cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. RPE microvilli interdigitate with the outer segments of the rods and cones (photoreceptors), not with ganglion cells, which lie on the opposite (inner) side of the retina.
