## title
Branches of the abdominal aorta

## question
Regarding the abdominal aorta, select the CORRECT statement:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It gives four pairs of lumbar arteries

## explanation_a
Correct. The abdominal aorta gives four pairs of lumbar arteries from its posterolateral wall, which run around the bodies of the upper four lumbar vertebrae to supply the posterior abdominal wall and spinal structures. These paired parietal branches are analogous to the posterior intercostal arteries of the thorax.

## answer_b
Its first branch is the subcostal artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The subcostal arteries arise from the lower part of the thoracic (descending) aorta, below the twelfth rib, not from the abdominal aorta. The first branch of the abdominal aorta is usually the (unpaired) coeliac trunk, preceded by the small paired inferior phrenic arteries.

## answer_c
It ends at the level of the L5/S1 intervertebral disc

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The abdominal aorta ends at the lower border of the fourth lumbar vertebra (L4) by dividing into the two common iliac arteries; the L5/S1 disc is well below this bifurcation.

## answer_d
It gives three direct branches to each suprarenal gland

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The aorta gives only one direct branch to each suprarenal gland, the middle suprarenal artery; the superior suprarenal arteries come from the inferior phrenic and the inferior suprarenal from the renal artery.

## title
Peritoneal covering of the caecum

## question
Regarding the caecum, which of the following statements is NOT true?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It is the first part of the large intestine and is continuous with the ascending colon

## explanation_a
True statement, so not the answer. The caecum is the blind, first part of the large intestine and continues above with the ascending colon at the level of the ileocaecal junction.

## answer_b
It has no mesentery

## explanation_b
True statement, so not the answer. The caecum has no mesentery of its own; it is nonetheless usually clothed with peritoneum and is fairly mobile in the right iliac fossa.

## answer_c
It is covered by peritoneum on its anterior surface only

## explanation_c
This is the untrue statement and therefore the answer. Although the caecum has no mesentery, it is normally invested with peritoneum on all its surfaces (anterior, posterior and lateral), which is why it is mobile. Describing it as covered anteriorly only confuses it with a truly retroperitoneal organ such as the ascending colon, whose bare posterior surface is bound to the abdominal wall.

## answer_d
It lies in the right iliac fossa

## explanation_d
True statement, so not the answer. The caecum occupies the right iliac fossa, resting on the iliacus and psoas major and often overlapping the pelvic brim.

Regarding the lesser sac (omental bursa), select the INCORRECT statement:

True statement, so not the answer. The posterior surface of the stomach and the lesser omentum form the anterior wall of the lesser sac.

## answer_d
The pancreas is one of its posterior relations

## explanation_d
True statement, so not the answer. The pancreas, together with the left kidney and suprarenal gland, forms part of the posterior wall (stomach bed) behind the lesser sac.

Position of the vermiform appendix

## question
Regarding the vermiform appendix, select the INCORRECT statement:

## subject

## answer_b
The mesoappendix carries the appendicular vessels in its free edge

## explanation_b
True statement, so not the answer. The appendicular artery (a branch of the ileocolic) and vein run in the free border of the mesoappendix.

True statement, so not the answer. The appendiceal orifice lies on the posteromedial wall of the caecum, about 2 cm below the ileocaecal opening.

The position of the appendix is variable: it is most often retrocaecal (behind the caecum), but it may be pelvic, subcaecal, pre-ileal or post-ileal. Because its tip can lie in several positions, the signs of appendicitis vary, and stating that it "always" lies behind the ileum is false.

## question
Regarding the external oblique muscle of the abdomen, select the TRUE statement:

## subject
gi

## vignette

## answer_a
It arises from the outer surfaces of the lower eight ribs (5th to 12th)

## explanation_a
Correct. The external oblique arises by fleshy digitations from the outer surfaces of the lower eight ribs (5th to 12th), interdigitating above with serratus anterior and below with latissimus dorsi. Its fibres run downwards and forwards to insert into the xiphoid process, linea alba, pubic crest, pubic tubercle and anterior half of the iliac crest, its lower free border forming the inguinal ligament.

Incorrect. The external oblique arises from the ribs; it does not insert into them. Its aponeurosis inserts into the linea alba and pubis.

## answer_d
It is inserted into the xiphoid process and the 5th to 7th costal cartilages

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Insertion into the xiphoid process and 5th–7th costal cartilages describes part of the rectus abdominis, not the external oblique.

The involuntary sphincter made of smooth muscle at the neck of the urinary bladder is the:

Drainage of the testicular veins

## question
The right and left testicular veins drain, respectively, into the:

## subject
gi

## status

Incorrect. The right testicular vein drains into the inferior vena cava, not the right renal vein; only the left drains into a renal vein.

Timing of testicular descent

The testis normally completes its descent into the scrotum during which month of intra-uterine life?

## subject

Incorrect. During the eighth month the testis is passing through the inguinal canal; it usually reaches the bottom of the scrotum only in the ninth month.

Regarding the kidney, select the CORRECT statement:

## subject

At the hilum, the renal artery lies anterior to the renal vein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. At the renal hilum the structures are arranged, from front to back, as renal vein, renal artery, then renal pelvis; so the vein — not the artery — is the most anterior structure.
