## title
Term for organisms that require oxygen for growth
## question
Which term best describes organisms that require oxygen for growth?
## subject
inf
## status
Draft
## source
past-paper
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
D
## answer_a
Aerotolerant anaerobes
## explanation_a
Incorrect. Aerotolerant anaerobes tolerate the presence of oxygen but do not use it for growth; they do not require it.
## answer_b
Facultative anaerobes
## explanation_b
Incorrect. Facultative anaerobes grow either with or without oxygen, so oxygen is not a requirement for them.
## answer_c
Microaerophilic organisms
## explanation_c
Incorrect. Microaerophilic organisms need only low oxygen tensions and are harmed by atmospheric oxygen; the term does not mean a plain requirement for oxygen.
## answer_d
Obligate aerobes
## explanation_d
Correct. Obligate (strict) aerobes can grow only in the presence of molecular oxygen, which they use as the terminal electron acceptor of aerobic respiration; without oxygen they cannot generate energy and do not grow.

## question
Which statement regarding generalized transduction is CORRECT?
## subject
inf
## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
C
## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Uptake of naked DNA by a competent recipient describes transformation, not transduction.
## answer_b
It is a result of the transfer of a DNA strand through a sex pilus.
## explanation_b
Incorrect. Transfer of DNA through a sex pilus describes conjugation, not transduction.
## answer_c
It occurs during the lytic cycle of a bacteriophage.
## explanation_c
Correct. In generalized transduction a bacteriophage, during its lytic cycle, fragments the host chromosome and by a packaging error encloses a random piece of host DNA in a phage head, which then transfers it to a new recipient cell.
## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Transduction transfers genes and produces a genotypic (heritable) change, not a transient phenotypic variation.

Lecithinase

## title
True statement concerning plasmids
## question
Which statement is TRUE concerning plasmids?
## subject
inf
## status
Draft
## source
past-paper
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
D
## answer_a
They are essential for bacterial survival
## explanation_a
Incorrect. Plasmids are accessory genetic elements; a bacterium can survive and divide without them, so they are not essential.
## answer_b
They are located on the bacterial chromosome
## explanation_b
Incorrect. Plasmids are extrachromosomal - they replicate independently of, and are physically separate from, the bacterial chromosome.
## answer_c
Non-conjugative plasmids are usually stringent
## explanation_c
Incorrect. This is not the defining truth about plasmids and conflates copy-number control with conjugation; conjugative plasmids are the transmissible ones, and stringency refers to copy-number regulation, not to non-conjugative status.
## answer_d
They may carry genes for antimicrobial resistance
## explanation_d
Correct. Plasmids are self-replicating extrachromosomal DNA that can carry accessory genes, including resistance (R) determinants that encode antimicrobial resistance and can be transferred between bacteria - a major route for the spread of resistance.

## subject

Transformation
## explanation_a
Incorrect. Transformation is uptake of naked foreign DNA that changes the genotype; it is a heritable genetic variation.
## answer_b
Use of flagella on exposure to phenol
## explanation_b
Correct. Loss (or altered use) of flagella when a bacterium is exposed to phenol is a temporary, reversible response to the environment with no change in the genome - the definition of a phenotypic variation.

Single-base mutation
## explanation_c
Incorrect. A single-base mutation alters the genome and is heritable; it is a genotypic, not phenotypic, variation.

Prokaryotic functional equivalent of mitochondria

In prokaryotes, the functional equivalent of mitochondria is:
## subject
inf
## status

## explanation_a

Ribosomes
## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis, not of respiration/energy generation.
