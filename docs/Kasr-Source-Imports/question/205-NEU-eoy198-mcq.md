## title
Structural feature of blood-brain barrier endothelial cells

## question
Endothelial cells of the blood-brain barrier are characterized by:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## source
past-paper

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Have numerous pinocytotic vesicles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. BBB capillary endothelium has very FEW pinocytotic vesicles, which is exactly what limits non-specific transcellular transport across the barrier — numerous vesicles are a feature of fenestrated, non-barrier capillaries elsewhere.

## answer_b
Connected by apical adherent junctions

## explanation_b
Incorrect. BBB endothelial cells are joined by tight junctions (zonulae occludentes), not apical adherent junctions; tight junctions are what seal the paracellular route.

## answer_c
Lie on discontinuous basal lamina

## explanation_c
Incorrect. BBB capillaries sit on a continuous, not discontinuous, basal lamina — a discontinuous lamina is a feature of sinusoidal (e.g. hepatic, splenic) capillaries.

## answer_d
Completely surrounded by end feet of astrocytes

## explanation_d
Correct. Protoplasmic astrocyte foot processes (end-feet) completely ensheathe the outer surface of BBB capillaries, inducing and maintaining the endothelial tight-junction phenotype — though the tight-junctioned endothelium itself, not the end-feet, is the actual diffusion barrier.

## title
Localising a lesion of the right gracile nucleus

## question
Lesion in the right gracile nucleus is expected to affect:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## source
past-paper

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Tactile localization at the right arm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The gracile nucleus/fasciculus serves the lower limb and lower trunk, not the arm — arm (upper-limb) fine touch and proprioception travel via the more lateral cuneate fasciculus and nucleus cuneatus instead.

## answer_b
Fine touch at the left arm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This combines the wrong side (the gracile pathway is still pre-decussation at the nucleus, so effects are ipsilateral, not left) with the wrong territory (arm is cuneate, not gracile).

## answer_c
Proprioception at the right leg

## explanation_c
Correct. Fasciculus gracilis carries fine touch, vibration and proprioception from the lower limb, ascending ipsilaterally without crossing until its fibres synapse in the nucleus gracilis in the closed medulla; only the second-order internal arcuate fibres decussate afterward to form the medial lemniscus. A lesion at the nucleus itself is therefore pre-decussation, producing an ipsilateral (same-side, right) leg deficit.

## answer_d
Fine touch at the left leg

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The side is wrong — a nucleus gracilis lesion is pre-decussation and therefore produces an ipsilateral (right, not left) deficit, since crossing occurs only after the nucleus via the internal arcuate fibres.

Dendritic span of cerebellar cortex Golgi cells

## question
Regarding the cerebellar cortex, which of the following statements is true?

## subject

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Granular cells have many long dendrites

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Granule cells have short, claw-like dendrites confined to the granular layer, not many long dendrites.

## answer_b
Each Purkinje cell receives an inhibitory signal from a single climbing fiber

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Climbing fibres (from the inferior olive) are EXCITATORY to Purkinje cells, not inhibitory — Purkinje cell inhibition instead comes from basket, stellate and Golgi cells.

## answer_c
The dendrites of Golgi cells extend in all layers of the grey matter

## explanation_c
Correct. Golgi cell dendrites ramify widely, spanning all three layers of the cerebellar cortex (molecular, Purkinje, granular) — unlike granule cells' short, layer-confined dendrites — letting Golgi cells be excited by parallel fibres in the molecular layer and inhibit granule cells in feedback/feedforward loops.

## answer_d
Purkinje cells are unipolar cells arranged in one row

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Purkinje cells are large multipolar (flask-shaped) cells, not unipolar, though they are correctly described as arranged in a single row.
