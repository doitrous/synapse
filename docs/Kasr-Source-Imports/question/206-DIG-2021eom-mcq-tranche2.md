## title
Territory of the inferior mesenteric artery

## question
The inferior mesenteric artery supplies blood to:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The stomach and spleen

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The stomach and spleen are foregut-related and are supplied by branches of the coeliac trunk (left gastric, splenic and short gastric arteries), not the inferior mesenteric artery.

## answer_b
The small intestine and ascending colon

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The small intestine and ascending colon are midgut derivatives supplied by the superior mesenteric artery, not the inferior mesenteric artery.

## answer_c
The transverse and descending colon, sigmoid colon, and rectum

## explanation_c
Correct. The inferior mesenteric artery is the artery of the hindgut, supplying the distal (left) third of the transverse colon, the descending and sigmoid colon and the upper rectum through its left colic, sigmoid and superior rectal branches. Its territory begins near the splenic flexure, the watershed zone of Griffiths, where it meets the superior mesenteric supply. The proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon remain superior mesenteric (midgut) territory.

## answer_d
The liver, gallbladder, and pancreas

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The liver, gallbladder and pancreas are foregut derivatives supplied from the coeliac trunk (via the hepatic and splenic arteries), with the pancreatic head also receiving superior mesenteric supply.

## title
Branches of the common hepatic artery

## question
The common hepatic artery, a branch of the coeliac trunk, gives rise to which of the following arteries?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Left gastric and right gastric arteries

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The left gastric artery is a separate direct branch of the coeliac trunk, not a branch of the common hepatic artery.

## answer_b
Proper hepatic and left gastric arteries

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The common hepatic artery continues as the hepatic artery proper after giving off the gastroduodenal artery; the proper hepatic artery is its continuation, not a co-branch alongside the left gastric.

## answer_c
Right gastric and gastroduodenal arteries

## explanation_c
Correct. The common hepatic artery runs to the right along the upper border of the pancreas and divides into the gastroduodenal artery, which descends behind the first part of the duodenum, and the hepatic artery proper, which continues to the liver in the free edge of the lesser omentum. The right gastric artery arises from the hepatic artery proper (or occasionally the common hepatic artery) and runs along the lesser curvature to anastomose with the left gastric artery. Thus the right gastric and gastroduodenal arteries are the vessels derived from this stem.

## answer_d
Splenic and left gastric arteries

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The splenic and left gastric arteries are the other two direct branches of the coeliac trunk itself, not branches of the common hepatic artery.

## title
Artery whose occlusion causes appendicular gangrene

## question
Gangrene of the appendix is due to occlusion of which artery?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Ileocolic artery

## explanation_a
Correct. The appendix is supplied by the appendicular artery, a branch of the ileocolic artery (itself a branch of the superior mesenteric artery), which runs to the appendix in the free edge of the mesoappendix. The appendicular artery is an end artery with no effective anastomosis, so thrombosis of it during appendicitis rapidly produces ischaemia and gangrene of the appendix. Occlusion of the parent ileocolic artery similarly cuts off this sole supply.

## answer_b
Middle colic artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The middle colic artery supplies the transverse colon, well away from the appendix.

## answer_c
Right colic artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The right colic artery supplies the ascending colon, not the appendix.

## answer_d
Left colic artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The left colic artery is a branch of the inferior mesenteric artery supplying the descending colon and has no relation to the appendix.

## title
Shape of the right suprarenal gland

## question
Regarding the right suprarenal gland, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is triangular (pyramidal) in shape

## explanation_a
Correct. The right suprarenal (adrenal) gland is pyramidal or triangular in shape and caps the upper pole of the right kidney, whereas the left gland is more elongated and semilunar (crescentic). It lies behind the inferior vena cava anteriorly and against the diaphragm and bare area of the liver. Its hilum, where the suprarenal vein leaves to drain directly into the inferior vena cava, faces anteromedially.

## answer_b
It reaches the hilum of the kidney

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The suprarenal gland sits on the upper pole of the kidney and does not reach the renal hilum, which lies at the medial border of the kidney lower down.

## answer_c
It is related anteriorly to the stomach

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The right suprarenal gland is related anteriorly to the inferior vena cava and the bare area of the liver, not to the stomach; the stomach relates to the left gland's region.

## answer_d
Its hilum is directed downwards

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The hilum of the suprarenal gland faces anteromedially (transmitting the suprarenal vein), not downwards.

## title
Development of the greater curvature of the stomach

## question
Regarding the development of the stomach, mark the one correct statement:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It is derived partly from foregut and partly from midgut

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The stomach develops entirely from the foregut; the foregut-midgut junction lies distally, at the level of the major duodenal papilla in the second part of the duodenum.

## answer_b
After rotation of 90 degrees around its anteroposterior axis, its right surface becomes posterior and its left surface anterior

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The stomach rotates 90 degrees clockwise around its longitudinal (craniocaudal) axis, so its left surface becomes anterior and its right surface posterior; the anteroposterior-axis rotation is a separate, lesser movement.

## answer_c
Its rotation around its longitudinal axis forms the greater sac

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Rotation of the stomach and the growth of its dorsal mesogastrium create the lesser sac (omental bursa) behind the stomach, not the greater sac.

## answer_d
The original posterior border of the stomach becomes the greater curvature after its rapid growth

## explanation_d
Correct. The dorsal (originally posterior) border of the developing stomach grows much faster than the ventral border, and this differential growth balloons it out to form the greater curvature. The slower-growing ventral border becomes the lesser curvature. The accompanying 90-degree clockwise rotation about the longitudinal axis then carries the greater curvature to the left and the lesser curvature to the right, and pulls the dorsal mesogastrium out to form the greater omentum.

## title
Formation of the main pancreatic duct

## question
The main pancreatic duct (of Wirsung) is formed by:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The dorsal pancreatic duct only

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The proximal part of the dorsal duct usually persists only as the small accessory duct (of Santorini); the dorsal duct alone does not form the main duct.

## answer_b
The ventral pancreatic duct only

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The ventral duct forms only the distal, downstream part of the main duct near the ampulla; it cannot account for the whole main pancreatic duct.

## answer_c
The whole dorsal pancreatic duct and the whole ventral pancreatic duct

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The proximal part of the dorsal duct does not contribute to the main duct; it either disappears or remains as the accessory duct, so not the whole dorsal duct is included.

## answer_d
The distal part of the dorsal pancreatic duct and the duct of the ventral pancreas

## explanation_d
Correct. As the ventral and dorsal pancreatic buds fuse, their duct systems anastomose. The main pancreatic duct (of Wirsung) is formed by the entire ventral pancreatic duct together with the distal part of the dorsal pancreatic duct, and it opens with the bile duct at the major duodenal papilla. The proximal part of the dorsal duct persists as the accessory duct of Santorini, draining into the minor duodenal papilla.

Male derivative of the mesonephric duct

## question
Which of the following structures develops from the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct in the male?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The efferent ductules (vasa efferentia) develop from persisting mesonephric tubules that link the rete testis to the duct, not from the mesonephric duct.

Prostatic utricle

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The prostatic utricle is the male remnant of the fused paramesonephric (Mullerian) ducts, not a mesonephric-duct derivative.
