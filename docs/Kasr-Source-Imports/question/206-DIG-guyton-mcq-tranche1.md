## title
Ion with the highest concentration in basal saliva

## question
Which ion has the highest concentration in saliva under basal conditions?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Bicarbonate

## explanation_a
Correct. Although potassium in saliva is about seven times its plasma level and bicarbonate about three times, the actual concentrations differ: basal salivary bicarbonate is roughly 50 to 70 mEq/L while potassium is about 30 mEq/L. Bicarbonate therefore has the highest absolute concentration in basal saliva. This is distinct from which ion is most concentrated relative to plasma.

## answer_b
Chloride

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Chloride is low in saliva, only about 10-15 percent of its plasma concentration, because it follows sodium reabsorption in the ducts.

## answer_c
Potassium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Potassium is high relative to plasma but its absolute concentration (about 30 mEq/L) is below that of bicarbonate.

## answer_d
Sodium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sodium is actively reabsorbed along the ducts, so its absolute concentration in saliva is low.

## title
Ion most concentrated in saliva relative to plasma

## question
Compared with plasma, saliva has the highest relative concentration of which ion under basal conditions?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Bicarbonate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bicarbonate is enriched in saliva about threefold over plasma, less than potassium's roughly sevenfold enrichment.

## answer_b
Chloride

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Chloride is low relative to plasma (about 10-15 percent), following sodium reabsorption.

## answer_c
Potassium

## explanation_c
Correct. Salivary acini secrete a plasma-like fluid, but as it flows along the ducts sodium is actively reabsorbed and potassium is actively secreted in exchange. Potassium ends up about seven times its plasma concentration, the highest relative enrichment of any salivary ion. Bicarbonate is enriched about threefold, while sodium and chloride fall well below plasma levels.

## answer_d
Sodium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sodium is reabsorbed in the ducts, leaving it well below its plasma concentration.

## title
Digestion most impaired in achlorhydria

## question
Digestion of which of the following is impaired to the greatest extent in patients with achlorhydria?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Carbohydrate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Carbohydrate digestion by salivary and pancreatic amylase and brush-border enzymes does not depend on gastric acid.

## answer_b
Fat

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fat digestion relies on bile salts and pancreatic lipase in the small intestine, not on gastric acid.

## answer_c
Protein

## explanation_c
Correct. Achlorhydria means the stomach fails to secrete hydrochloric acid. Without acid, pepsinogen is not converted to pepsin, and any pepsin that forms cannot work because it needs an acid medium. Protein digestion, which begins in the stomach with pepsin, is therefore the most impaired.

## answer_d
Nucleic acid

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nucleic acid digestion depends on pancreatic nucleases, not on gastric acid.

## title
Source of pepsinogen secretion

## question
The proenzyme pepsinogen is secreted mainly from which of the following structures?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Acinar cells of the pancreas

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pancreatic acinar cells secrete digestive enzymes such as trypsinogen and lipase, not pepsinogen.

## answer_b
Ductal cells of the pancreas

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pancreatic ductal cells secrete a bicarbonate-rich fluid, not pepsinogen.

## answer_c
Epithelial cells of the duodenum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Duodenal epithelium secretes mucus and enteropeptidase, not pepsinogen.

## answer_d
Gastric glands of the stomach

## explanation_d
Correct. Pepsinogen is secreted by the peptic (chief) cells of the gastric (oxyntic) glands. Contact with hydrochloric acid, or with pepsin itself, converts pepsinogen to active pepsin, which digests protein. It is therefore a product of the stomach's gastric glands.

## title
Hormone that decreases gastric emptying

## question
Which hormone is released by the presence of fat and protein in the small intestine and has a major effect in decreasing gastric emptying?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Cholecystokinin

## explanation_a
Correct. Cholecystokinin, released by fat and protein in the small intestine, is the only gastrointestinal hormone that inhibits gastric emptying under physiological conditions. It relaxes the orad stomach, raising its compliance so the stomach holds food longer, which is why fatty, protein-rich meals feel more filling. CCK also acts on brain feeding centres to reduce further eating.

## answer_b
Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide is an incretin stimulating insulin; it is not the physiological brake on gastric emptying named here.

## answer_c
Gastrin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gastrin stimulates acid secretion and mucosal growth and does not physiologically slow emptying.

## answer_d
Motilin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Motilin drives the interdigestive migrating motor complex rather than slowing emptying of a meal.

## answer_e
Secretin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Secretin can slow emptying only at pharmacological doses; it is not the major physiological inhibitor.

## title
Structure that screens bolus size in swallowing

## question
Swallowing involves signalling between the pharynx and the brain-stem swallowing centre. Which structure is critical for determining whether a bolus of food is small enough to be swallowed?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Epiglottis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The epiglottis swings back to cover the laryngeal opening and protect the airway; it does not screen bolus size.

## answer_b
Larynx

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The larynx is pulled upward and its cords approximate to guard the airway, not to gauge bolus size.

## answer_c
Palatopharyngeal folds

## explanation_c
Correct. During the pharyngeal phase the palatopharyngeal folds on each side are pulled medially, forming a sagittal slit. This slit acts as a selective gate, letting adequately chewed food pass while impeding larger objects. It therefore determines whether a bolus is small enough to be swallowed.

## answer_d
Soft palate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The soft palate is pulled upward to close the posterior nares and prevent nasal regurgitation, not to select bolus size.

## answer_e
Upper oesophageal sphincter

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The upper oesophageal sphincter relaxes to admit the bolus into the oesophagus but does not screen its size.

## title
Ion transport defect in cystic fibrosis

## question
In cystic fibrosis, mucus in exocrine glands becomes thick and blocks the ducts. A primary disruption in the transfer of which ion across cell membranes leads to the decreased secretion of fluid?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Calcium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Calcium transport is not the primary defect in cystic fibrosis.

## answer_b
Chloride

## explanation_b
Correct. Cystic fibrosis is caused by defective chloride transport across the apical membrane of exocrine epithelial cells. The CFTR protein is a cAMP-regulated chloride channel; moving chloride out of the cell drives fluid secretion. When CFTR is absent or nonfunctional, chloride and therefore water secretion fall, so exocrine mucus becomes thick and blocks the ducts.

## answer_c
Phosphate

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Phosphate handling is not the defective transport in cystic fibrosis.

## answer_d
Potassium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Potassium transport is not the primary abnormality.

## answer_e
Sodium

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Although sodium and water movement are affected secondarily, the primary defect is in chloride (CFTR) transport.
