## title
The aim of defensive medicine

## question
The aim of defensive medicine is to secure the:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Patient

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Defensive medicine is driven by the physician's wish to avoid litigation rather than the patient's interest; it often adds unnecessary tests or denies care, which does not serve the patient.

## answer_b
Physician

## explanation_b
Correct. The aim of defensive medicine is to secure the physician: it is the practice of ordering tests, procedures or referrals, or avoiding high-risk patients, mainly to reduce the risk of malpractice liability rather than to benefit the patient. The driving motive is the doctor's legal self-protection. It is a response to the fear of being sued.

## answer_c
Resources

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Defensive medicine typically wastes rather than secures resources, because it drives overutilisation of investigations and services.

## answer_d
Society

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Society bears the added cost and harm of defensive medicine; the practice is aimed at shielding the individual physician, not benefiting society.

## answer_d
Increasing physician's creativity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Defensive practice is risk-averse and formulaic, the opposite of clinical creativity.

## title
Informed consent to high operative risk protects the physician

## question
A cardiac patient presented to the hospital for open heart surgery. The physician took informed consent from the patient and informed him and his relatives about the high risk of the surgery. After 2 days the patient died and his relatives sued the physician. Regarding this suit, what do you think?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The medical syndicate council would punish the doctor with just “Alarm”.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. There is no wrongdoing to punish: proper informed consent about the high operative risk means the physician committed no breach, so no disciplinary sanction applies.

## answer_b
The physician is guilty.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Guilt requires a fault (negligence or incompetence); here the doctor obtained valid informed consent disclosing the high risk, and the death followed a recognised, consented risk rather than a breach.

## answer_c
The physician is protected.

## explanation_c
Correct. The physician is protected: by taking proper informed consent and disclosing the high risk of the surgery to the patient and relatives, he fulfilled his legal duty of disclosure, so a death from a recognised, consented risk is not actionable. Valid informed consent is a defence because the patient accepted the known risk. Without a breach of duty, the suit cannot succeed.

## answer_d
The relatives will be compensated.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Compensation requires proven fault causing harm; a consented, disclosed operative risk that materialises is not negligence, so no compensation is due.
