## title
Acute unilateral vision loss with a swollen disc and RAPD
## question
A 60-year-old male presented with acute onset of drop of vision in his left eye. On examination, visual acuity was 6/6 in the right eye and 6/60 in the left eye. Fundus examination showed blurred edges of the left optic nerve head, and RAPD. What is the most probable diagnosis?
## subject
mul
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
D
## answer_a
Raised intra cranial pressure
## explanation_a
Incorrect. Raised intracranial pressure gives bilateral papilledema with early preserved vision, not a unilateral acute drop with a RAPD.
## answer_b
Raised ocular tension
## explanation_b
Incorrect. Raised intraocular pressure does not produce a swollen disc with a RAPD in this way.
## answer_c
Central retinal artery occlusion
## explanation_c
Incorrect. CRAO gives a pale oedematous retina with a cherry-red spot, not blurred disc edges.
## answer_d
Optic neuritis
## explanation_d
Correct. Acute unilateral visual loss with a swollen (papillitis) disc and a relative afferent pupillary defect is optic neuritis.
