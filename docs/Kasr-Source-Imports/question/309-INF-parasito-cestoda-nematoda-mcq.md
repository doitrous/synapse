## title
Adult stage of Cysticercus bovis

## question
Cysticercus bovis is infective stage of:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Broad tapeworm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The broad tapeworm is Diphyllobothrium latum, whose larval stage is the plerocercoid in fish, not the Cysticercus bovis of cattle muscle.

## answer_b
Beef tapeworm

## explanation_b
Correct. Cysticercus bovis is the bladder-worm (cysticercus) larval stage that develops in the muscles of cattle; when a human eats raw or undercooked beef the cysticercus everts its scolex, attaches to the small intestine and grows into the adult BEEF tapeworm, Taenia saginata. Cysticercus bovis is therefore the infective stage of Taenia saginata. The name 'bovis' (of cattle) marks the beef-tapeworm cycle and distinguishes it from Cysticercus cellulosae of Taenia solium.

## answer_c
Pinworm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The pinworm is Enterobius vermicularis, a nematode with no cysticercus stage.

## answer_d
Giant intestinal roundworm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The giant intestinal roundworm is Ascaris lumbricoides, a nematode; its infective stage is the embryonated egg, not a cysticercus.

## title
Cestode with an operculated egg

## question
This parasite has an operculated egg:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Hymenolepis nana

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hymenolepis nana has a spherical, non-operculated egg with polar filaments between its two membranes.

## answer_b
Taenia solium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Taenia solium (like all taeniid tapeworms) has a rounded, radially striated, non-operculated egg.

## answer_c
Diphyllobothrium latum

## explanation_c
Correct. Diphyllobothrium latum is the only common cestode whose egg is OPERCULATED: it is an oval, yellow-brown egg bearing a lid (operculum) at one pole and a small knob at the other, closely resembling a trematode egg. This operculate morphology reflects the fish-tapeworm's aquatic life cycle, in which the egg releases a ciliated coracidium into water. Recognising the operculated egg as Diphyllobothrium is the identifying fact tested.

## answer_d
Taenia saginata

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Taenia saginata shares the non-operculated, radially striated taeniid egg and cannot be told apart from T. solium on egg morphology.

## title
Helminth egg directly infective to man

## question
Which of the following helminth eggs are directly infective to man?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ascaris lumbricoides

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ascaris eggs are passed unembryonated and must mature in soil for weeks before they become infective, so they are not directly infective when shed.

## answer_b
Diphyllobothrium latum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Diphyllobothrium eggs must reach water and pass through a copepod (Cyclops) and then a fish; the egg is not infective to man.

## answer_c
Hymenolepis nana

## explanation_c
Correct. Hymenolepis nana is exceptional in that its egg is DIRECTLY infective to man: an egg swallowed from a contaminated hand, food or water hatches in the small intestine and the oncosphere completes the whole cycle in one host, needing no intermediate host. This direct egg infectivity also underlies internal autoinfection and person-to-person spread. The atomic fact tested is that H. nana eggs infect man directly.

## answer_d
Fasciola hepatica

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fasciola eggs are unembryonated when passed and require water and the snail Lymnaea; the egg is not infective to man.

## title
Infection complicated by cysticercosis

## question
Cysticercosis may complicate:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Strongyloidiasis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Strongyloidiasis is a nematode infection; its complication is hyperinfection/autoinfection with filariform larvae, not cysticercosis.

## answer_b
Sparganosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sparganosis is itself a larval (sparganum) tissue infection by Spirometra, not a complication caused by cysticerci.

## answer_c
Taeniasis saginata

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Taeniasis saginata (Taenia saginata) does not cause human cysticercosis because its eggs are not infective to man; only cattle harbour its cysticercus.

## answer_d
Taeniasis solium

## explanation_d
Correct. Cysticercosis complicates taeniasis SOLIUM: a person carrying the adult Taenia solium can ingest its eggs (by faecal–oral contamination or internal autoinfection), and the hatched oncospheres disseminate to tissues — muscle, subcutaneous tissue, eye and especially brain — to form cysticerci. Because only Taenia solium eggs are infective to man, only T. solium causes human cysticercosis, making it far more dangerous than T. saginata.

## title
Cestode for which man is only an intermediate host

## question
Man acts as intermediate host only for:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Hymenolepis nana

## explanation_a
Incorrect. For Hymenolepis nana man is chiefly the definitive host harbouring the adult worm (and, through direct egg infection, also carries the larval stage); he is not exclusively an intermediate host.

## answer_b
Taenia solium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. For Taenia solium man is the definitive host of the adult tapeworm and only occasionally, in cysticercosis, an accidental intermediate host — not intermediate 'only'.

## answer_c
Diphyllobothrium latum

Incorrect. For Diphyllobothrium latum man is the definitive host of the adult worm; the intermediate hosts are the copepod and fish.

Echinococcus granulosus

## explanation_d
Correct. For Echinococcus granulosus man acts ONLY as an (accidental) intermediate host: swallowed eggs hatch and the oncospheres form hydatid cysts in liver, lung and other organs, but man never harbours the adult worm. The adult tapeworm lives only in the definitive host, the dog. Being solely an intermediate host — a dead end that does not continue the cycle — is the atomic fact tested.

## question
PAIR technique is used for treatment of:

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## answer_b
Hydatidosis

## explanation_b
Correct. PAIR (Puncture, Aspiration, Injection, Re-aspiration) is a percutaneous, image-guided treatment for HYDATID disease: the hydatid cyst is punctured, its fluid aspirated, a protoscolicidal agent (e.g. hypertonic saline) injected and then re-aspirated, usually under albendazole cover. It is used for suitable liver hydatid cysts as an alternative to surgery. Linking PAIR to hydatidosis (Echinococcus) is the fact tested.

Taeniasis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Taeniasis (intestinal adult tapeworm) is cured with a single dose of praziquantel or niclosamide, not a drainage procedure.

## answer_d
Coenurosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Coenurosis (a Taenia multiceps larval cyst) is treated by surgical excision, not by PAIR.

## title
Ultrasound cartwheel sign in hydatid disease

## question
Cartwheel appearance in sonar is a pathognomonic feature of:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Pulmonary hydatid cyst

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A pulmonary hydatid cyst is imaged mainly by chest X-ray/CT (a rounded opacity or the 'water-lily' sign after rupture), not by the sonographic cartwheel of daughter cysts.

## answer_b
Calcified hepatic hydatid cyst

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A calcified hepatic hydatid cyst shows a dense calcified rim (often signifying an inactive cyst), not the cartwheel pattern of viable daughter cysts.

## answer_c
Endogenous daughter hydatid cyst

## explanation_c
Correct. The cartwheel (rosette / 'wheel-spoke') appearance on ultrasound is produced by multiple ENDOGENOUS DAUGHTER cysts arranged within the mother hydatid cyst, each separated by septa radiating like the spokes of a wheel. This multivesicular pattern is characteristic of an active Echinococcus granulosus cyst. Recognising the cartwheel as daughter-cyst formation is the atomic imaging fact tested.

## answer_d
Osseous hydatid cyst

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Osseous (bone) hydatid disease is infiltrative and imaged by X-ray/CT/MRI as lytic destruction, and it typically lacks the well-formed daughter-cyst cartwheel.

## title
Stool finding after eating undercooked salmon

## question
Parasitic infection caused by eating under cooked salmon fish could be diagnosed by stool analysis showing:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Operculated eggs & mature segments

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diphyllobothrium does not shed distinct mature proglottids in the stool the way Taenia does, so paired 'operculated eggs & mature segments' is not the finding.

## answer_b
Translucent eggs with gravid segments

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Translucent eggs with gravid segments describe the Taenia pattern (taeniid eggs plus motile gravid proglottids), not the fish tapeworm.

## answer_c
Mature eggs & mature segments

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 'Mature eggs & mature segments' again implies passing intact segments, which is not typical of Diphyllobothrium.

## answer_d
Oval operculated yellowish brown mature eggs with no segments

## explanation_d
Correct. Eating undercooked salmon transmits Diphyllobothrium latum, and its diagnosis on stool microscopy rests on the characteristic egg: an OVAL, OPERCULATED, yellowish-brown egg with a small terminal knob. The worm releases eggs continuously and its proglottids disintegrate in the gut, so intact segments are usually NOT seen in stool. Operculated yellow-brown eggs without segments is therefore the expected finding.
