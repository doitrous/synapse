## title
Junction between corona radiata and oocyte

## question
The junction between the cytoplasmic processes of the corona radiata cells and the microvilli of the oocyte is a:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Tight (occluding) junction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A tight junction seals the space between epithelial cells and does not permit the metabolic coupling seen between the corona cells and the oocyte.

## answer_b
Desmosome

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A desmosome is a mechanical anchoring junction and does not transfer ions or signalling molecules.

## answer_c
Adherens junction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An adherens junction anchors actin filaments and does not provide the communication between the corona cells and the oocyte.

## answer_d
Gap junction

## explanation_d
Correct. The corona radiata cells send processes across the zona pellucida to meet the oocyte microvilli, forming gap (communicating) junctions. These channels let ions, metabolites and signalling molecules pass directly between the follicular cells and the oocyte, nourishing it and helping to keep it arrested in meiosis until the LH surge.

## title
Timing of the first meiotic division

## question
In the female, the first meiotic division is completed:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
After fertilisation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. It is the second meiotic division, not the first, that is completed only after fertilisation.

## answer_b
Before fertilisation, well before ovulation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The first division is not completed early; the primary oocyte stays arrested in prophase I until just before ovulation.

## answer_c
Just before ovulation

## explanation_c
Correct. The primary oocyte is arrested in prophase of the first meiotic division from fetal life until the LH surge, just before ovulation, lets it complete meiosis I to form a secondary oocyte and the first polar body. The secondary oocyte then arrests in metaphase II and completes the second division only if fertilised. Thus meiosis I finishes just before ovulation.

## answer_d
At puberty

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Puberty reactivates cyclical follicle growth but does not itself complete meiosis I in a given oocyte; completion occurs just before that oocyte's ovulation.

## title
Lining of the oviduct wall

## question
The wall of the oviduct (fallopian tube):

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Is lined by cells with cilia together with secretory cells

## explanation_a
Correct. The oviduct mucosa is a simple columnar epithelium of two cell types: ciliated cells, whose cilia beat toward the uterus to move the ovum, and non-ciliated peg (secretory) cells, which produce fluid that nourishes the ovum and activates sperm. Both cell types are present, so the epithelium is ciliated and secretory. The mucosa is most folded in the ampulla, where fertilisation usually occurs.

## answer_b
Is lined by non-ciliated columnar cells only

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The lining is not purely non-ciliated; ciliated cells are a key component.

## answer_c
Is highly folded throughout the intramural portion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The mucosal folds are most elaborate in the ampulla and least in the intramural (uterine) part, not highly folded throughout the intramural portion.

## answer_d
Is covered by adventitia (no peritoneal covering)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The fallopian tube is largely intraperitoneal and covered by serosa (peritoneum), not simply adventitia.

## title
Territory of the spiral arteries

## question
Concerning the spiral arteries of the endometrium, which statement is correct?

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
They supply the maturing ovarian follicles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ovarian follicles are supplied by ovarian and uterine artery branches, not by the endometrial spiral arteries.

## answer_b
They supply the corpus luteum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The corpus luteum is supplied by ovarian vessels, not by endometrial spiral arteries.

## answer_c
They supply the endometrial basal zone

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The basal zone is supplied by the short straight (basal) arteries, which are not shed; this preserves the regenerative basal layer.

## answer_d
They supply the endometrial functional zone

## explanation_d
Correct. The coiled spiral arteries extend into the functional zone of the endometrium, which thickens through the cycle and is shed at menstruation. Constriction of these hormone-sensitive spiral arteries causes the ischaemia that sheds the functional zone, while the straight-artery-supplied basal zone is spared.

## title
Glands of the vaginal wall

## question
Concerning the vagina, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It contains mucous glands

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The vaginal wall has no mucous glands; its surface mucus comes from the cervix above.

## answer_b
It contains serous glands

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The vagina has no serous glands either; it is a glandless mucosa.

## answer_c
It is vascular and has no glands

## explanation_c
Correct. The vagina is lined by non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium and contains no glands. It is kept moist by cervical mucus and by transudate from its highly vascular, elastic-rich lamina propria, so 'vascular and without glands' is the correct description. Its acidic pH is produced by lactobacilli acting on epithelial glycogen, not by glands.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Because the vagina genuinely is a glandless, vascular mucosa, option C is correct and 'none of the above' cannot be selected.
