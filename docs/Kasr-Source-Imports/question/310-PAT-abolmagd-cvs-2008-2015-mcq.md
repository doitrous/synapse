## title
True statement about rheumatic fever

## question
Which of the following statements is true about rheumatic fever?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It most commonly affects adults above the age of 25 years.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Rheumatic fever is a disease of CHILDREN and young people (typically 5-15 years), following streptococcal pharyngitis; it is not primarily a disease of adults over 25.

## answer_b
It follows staphylococcal infection.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Rheumatic fever follows a group A beta-haemolytic STREPTOCOCCAL (Streptococcus pyogenes) pharyngitis, not a staphylococcal infection; the injury is immune-mediated (molecular mimicry), not a direct bacterial invasion.

## answer_c
It may be complicated by acute infective endocarditis.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. ACUTE infective endocarditis is caused by virulent organisms (e.g. Staphylococcus aureus) attacking previously NORMAL valves; a rheumatic-damaged valve is the substrate for the subacute, not the acute, form.

## answer_d
It may be complicated by subacute infective endocarditis.

## explanation_d
Correct. Rheumatic fever scars and deforms the heart valves (chronic rheumatic valve disease), and these previously DAMAGED valves are the classic substrate seeded by low-virulence organisms (Streptococcus viridans) to produce SUBACUTE infective endocarditis. So subacute infective endocarditis is a recognised late complication of rheumatic valve disease. This contrasts with acute infective endocarditis, which strikes previously normal valves, and it is why a patient with old rheumatic valve disease is at risk of an insidious subacute endocarditis.

## title
Site of focal embolic glomerulonephritis (flea-bitten kidney)

## question
Focal embolic glomerulonephritis (flea-bitten kidney) occurs in:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Subacute infective endocarditis.

## explanation_a
Correct. Focal embolic glomerulonephritis (Loehlein's focal embolic nephritis), giving the petechial 'flea-bitten' kidney, is the classic renal lesion of SUBACUTE infective endocarditis. Small septic/immune-complex emboli from the friable valve vegetations of Streptococcus viridans lodge in glomerular capillaries, producing scattered FOCAL segmental necrosis and haemorrhage over the cortical surface. The insidious low-grade bacteraemia of the subacute form, seeding previously damaged valves, is what generates the showers of small emboli that pepper the kidney.

## answer_b
Acute infective endocarditis.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Acute infective endocarditis (virulent organisms on normal valves) tends to throw larger SEPTIC emboli that cause renal infarcts and abscesses rather than the small-vessel focal embolic glomerulonephritis pattern typical of the subacute form.

## answer_c
Immune complex disease.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Generic immune-complex glomerulonephritis is characteristically DIFFUSE (all glomeruli, global involvement), not the focal embolic 'flea-bitten' pattern; the flea-bitten kidney here is specifically the embolic lesion of subacute endocarditis.

## answer_d
Systemic lupus erythematosus.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Systemic lupus erythematosus produces immune-complex lupus nephritis (commonly diffuse proliferative), and its cardiac lesion is sterile Libman-Sacks endocarditis; it is not the cause of focal embolic (flea-bitten) glomerulonephritis.
