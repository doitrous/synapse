## title
Antihypertensive most likely to cause rebound hypertension on sudden withdrawal

## question
Rebound hypertension on sudden stoppage of medication is most likely to occur with which of the following drugs?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Prazosin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Prazosin is an alpha-1 blocker; its main withdrawal issue is first-dose hypotension, not rebound hypertension.

## answer_b
Lisinopril

## explanation_b
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors such as lisinopril are not associated with a rebound hypertensive surge when stopped.

## answer_c
Clonidine

## explanation_c
Correct. Clonidine is a central alpha-2 agonist that suppresses sympathetic outflow; abrupt withdrawal removes this suppression and unleashes a surge of catecholamines, producing REBOUND HYPERTENSION (tachycardia, sweating, severe pressure rise). It must therefore be tapered, which is exactly why it is the answer here.

## answer_d
Hydrochlorothiazide

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Thiazide diuretics have a long, gentle offset and do not cause rebound hypertension.

Drug of choice for hypertension in toxaemia of pregnancy

## question
Which of the following is the drug of choice in the treatment of hypertension in toxaemia of pregnancy?

## subject
pharm

## status

## owner

Labetalol

Correct. Labetalol, a combined alpha/beta blocker, is a first-line agent for hypertension in pre-eclampsia/toxaemia: it lowers blood pressure effectively and safely, can be given intravenously in severe cases, and does not reduce uteroplacental perfusion. ## question
Which one of the following is a very powerful mixed balanced vasodilator?

ACE inhibitor safe in renal impairment because it is excreted in bile

## question
Which of the following angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs) could be used in a patient with renal impairment as it is excreted in bile only?

pharm

## status

Antihypertensive causing fetal renal failure and oligohydramnios

## question
A hypertensive lady got pregnant and continued her antihypertensive treatment. In the third trimester, sonography showed oligohydramnios and the baby was found to have renal failure attributed to the medication. Which of the following did she most likely use?

## status

Incorrect. Methyldopa is the classic SAFE antihypertensive in pregnancy and does not cause fetal renal failure.

## explanation_b
Correct. ACE inhibitors such as captopril block fetal angiotensin II, which is needed for renal development and perfusion. Used in the second/third trimester they cause fetal RENAL FAILURE, reduced urine output and therefore OLIGOHYDRAMNIOS, plus skull hypoplasia. This fetopathy is exactly the described picture, so captopril is the offending drug and ACEi are contraindicated in pregnancy.

Hydralazine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hydralazine is used in pregnancy-related hypertension and is not linked to this fetal renal syndrome.

## answer_d
Verapamil

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Verapamil is not associated with fetal renal failure or oligohydramnios.

## title
Reason for combining a beta blocker with hydralazine

## question
A hypertensive patient was put on hydralazine combined with a beta blocker. Which of the following is the most likely reason for adding the beta blocker?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
To decrease the incidence of lupus-like manifestations of hydralazine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Beta blockers do not prevent hydralazine-induced lupus, which relates to acetylator status and dose.

## answer_b
To decrease the reflex tachycardia caused by hydralazine's vasodilatation

## explanation_b
Correct. Hydralazine's arteriolar vasodilatation drops blood pressure and triggers a baroreceptor-mediated REFLEX TACHYCARDIA (and increased contractility) that both limits its effect and raises myocardial oxygen demand. Adding a beta blocker blocks this reflex sympathetic drive, controlling heart rate and improving tolerability — the standard rationale for the combination.

## answer_c
To enhance the vasodilatation produced by hydralazine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Beta blockers do not augment vasodilatation; non-selective ones can even oppose beta-2 vasodilation.

## answer_d
To reduce hydralazine's tendency to cause salt and water retention

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Salt and water retention is countered by a diuretic, not by a beta blocker.

IV antihypertensive causing arrhythmia and acidosis after continuous infusion

A patient with hypertensive emergency was receiving an antihypertensive drug by IV infusion. After two days of continuous therapy the patient developed arrhythmia and acidosis. Which of the following is the most likely drug causing this toxicity?

## title
Contraindication to ACE inhibitors

## question
Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs) are contraindicated in which of the following?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Diabetes mellitus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors are actually preferred in diabetic patients for their renoprotective effect.

## answer_b
Ischaemic heart disease

## explanation_b
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors are beneficial after myocardial infarction and in ischaemic heart disease, not contraindicated.

## answer_c
Bilateral renal artery stenosis

## explanation_c
Correct. In BILATERAL RENAL ARTERY STENOSIS, glomerular filtration depends on angiotensin-II-mediated constriction of the efferent arteriole. ACE inhibitors abolish this constriction, causing GFR to collapse and precipitating acute renal failure. That mechanism makes bilateral renal artery stenosis the classic contraindication to ACE inhibitors.

## answer_d
Severe heart failure

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors are a cornerstone of heart failure therapy, including severe cases (with careful monitoring), not a contraindication.
