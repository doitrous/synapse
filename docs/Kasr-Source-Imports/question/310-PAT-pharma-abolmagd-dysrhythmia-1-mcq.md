## title
Drug for severe refractory supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias

## question
Which of the following drugs can treat severe and refractory supraventricular AND ventricular arrhythmias?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Amiodarone

## explanation_a
Correct. Amiodarone is a broad-spectrum (predominantly Class III) antiarrhythmic that blocks potassium, sodium and calcium channels and has beta-blocking activity. This multichannel action makes it effective against both SUPRAVENTRICULAR and VENTRICULAR arrhythmias, including severe and refractory cases, which is why it is the answer. Its broad efficacy is balanced against significant long-term organ toxicity.

## answer_b
Captopril

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Captopril is an ACE inhibitor with no antiarrhythmic action.

## answer_c
Lidocaine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Lidocaine (Class IB) treats VENTRICULAR arrhythmias only; it is ineffective for supraventricular arrhythmias.

## answer_d
Minoxidil

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Minoxidil is an antihypertensive vasodilator, not an antiarrhythmic.

## title
Drug for ventricular arrhythmia without heart block

## question
Which one of the following drugs is used in the treatment of ventricular arrhythmia WITHOUT causing heart block?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Lidocaine

## explanation_a
Correct. Lidocaine is a Class IB antiarrhythmic that preferentially blocks sodium channels in depolarised/ischaemic VENTRICULAR tissue. It shortens the action potential, has little effect on normal atrial or nodal conduction and does NOT depress the AV node, so it treats ventricular arrhythmias without causing heart block. That ventricular selectivity is exactly why it is chosen here.

## answer_b
Phenytoin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Phenytoin (also IB-like) is used mainly for digoxin-induced arrhythmias; lidocaine is the standard answer for ventricular arrhythmia without block, and phenytoin is not a first-line antiarrhythmic here.

## answer_c
Propranolol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Propranolol (Class II) slows AV conduction and can cause heart block.

## answer_d
Bisoprolol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Bisoprolol is a beta blocker that likewise slows AV conduction and may precipitate block.

## title
Antiarrhythmic drug causing thyroid dysfunction

## question
Thyroid dysfunction is an adverse effect of which of the following antiarrhythmic drugs?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Amiodarone

## explanation_a
Correct. Amiodarone is an iodine-rich molecule that structurally resembles thyroxine. It interferes with thyroid hormone synthesis and peripheral deconversion and can cause either HYPOthyroidism or (iodine-induced) HYPERthyroidism, so thyroid function must be monitored. This iodine-related thyroid dysfunction is characteristic of amiodarone among antiarrhythmics.

Lidocaine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lidocaine does not affect the thyroid; its toxicity is neurological and cardiac.

## answer_c
Dronedarone

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Dronedarone was designed WITHOUT the iodine moiety specifically to avoid the thyroid toxicity of amiodarone.

## answer_d
Propafenone

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Propafenone (Class IC) does not cause thyroid dysfunction.

## title
Pharmacological characteristic of ivabradine

## question
Which one of the following is a pharmacological characteristic of ivabradine?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It is a positive inotropic agent

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ivabradine has no inotropic effect; it does not change contractility.

## answer_b
It inhibits all cardiac properties

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ivabradine acts selectively on the sinoatrial node, not on all cardiac properties.

## answer_c
It inhibits the If current in the SA node

## explanation_c
Correct. Ivabradine selectively blocks the funny (If) pacemaker current in the sinoatrial node, slowing the rate of diastolic depolarisation. This lowers heart rate (pure bradycardic effect) WITHOUT affecting contractility, conduction elsewhere or blood pressure, which is why it is used for rate control in angina and heart failure with a high heart rate. If-current inhibition is its defining characteristic.

## answer_d
Tachycardia is one of its side effects

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ivabradine causes bradycardia, not tachycardia (its main visual side effect is luminous phosphenes).

## title
Mechanism of action of amrinone in heart failure

## question
Which of the following describes the mechanism of action of amrinone in heart failure?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Decreases intracellular calcium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Amrinone increases intracellular calcium indirectly (through raised cAMP); it does not decrease it.

## answer_b
Releases nitric oxide

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Amrinone does not act by releasing nitric oxide.

## answer_c
Decreases cAMP

## explanation_c
Incorrect. It RAISES cAMP by preventing its breakdown; decreasing cAMP would be negatively inotropic.

## answer_d
Inhibits phosphodiesterase enzyme

## explanation_d
Correct. Amrinone (inamrinone) inhibits phosphodiesterase III, so cAMP is not broken down. Higher cAMP increases intracellular calcium in cardiac muscle (positive inotropy) and relaxes vascular smooth muscle (vasodilation), making it an "inodilator". Phosphodiesterase inhibition is its mechanism; a key adverse effect is thrombocytopenia.

## title
Mechanism by which minoxidil dilates arterioles

## question
Minoxidil is a potent arteriolar dilator acting by:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Blocking of calcium channels

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Minoxidil is not a calcium channel blocker.

## answer_b
Release of nitric oxide

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Nitric-oxide donation describes nitroprusside and nitrates, not minoxidil.

## answer_c
Blocking of alpha adrenergic receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Alpha blockade is the mechanism of prazosin, not minoxidil.

## answer_d
Opening of potassium channels

## explanation_d
Correct. Minoxidil (via its active sulphate metabolite) OPENS ATP-sensitive potassium channels in arteriolar smooth muscle. The resulting potassium efflux hyperpolarises the membrane, closing voltage-gated calcium channels and relaxing the arteriole. This potassium-channel opening is its mechanism as a potent arteriolar (afterload-reducing) vasodilator; it causes reflex tachycardia, fluid retention and hypertrichosis.

## title
Mechanism of action of ranolazine

## question
Which of the following is the mechanism of action of the antianginal drug ranolazine?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Prevents abnormal opening of the late Na+ channels

## explanation_a
Correct. Ranolazine inhibits the LATE (persistent) inward sodium current in ischaemic myocardium. Less sodium entry reduces sodium-driven calcium overload via the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger, lowering diastolic wall tension and myocardial oxygen demand. It relieves angina WITHOUT significantly changing heart rate or blood pressure, and inhibiting the late sodium current is its defining mechanism.

## answer_b
Improves myocardial glucose utilisation in cardiac cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Metabolic modulation of glucose utilisation describes trimetazidine, not ranolazine.

## answer_c
Releases nitric oxide and increases cGMP

## explanation_c
Incorrect. NO donation and raised cGMP are the mechanism of nitrates.

## answer_d
Blocks voltage-dependent L-type calcium channels

## explanation_d
Incorrect. L-type calcium channel blockade is the mechanism of calcium channel blockers, not ranolazine.
