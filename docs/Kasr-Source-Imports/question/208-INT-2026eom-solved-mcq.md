## title
Chronic inflammation identified from a gastric-ulcer biopsy

## question
A 60 year old female with gastric ulcer. The biopsy revealed infiltration by lymphocytes and plasma cells with evidence of tissue destruction. This indicates:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Acute inflammation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Acute inflammation is dominated by neutrophils and abundant edema fluid, not by lymphocytes and plasma cells, and it is a short-lived response rather than the prolonged, destruction-with-healing picture described.

## answer_b
Chronic inflammation

## explanation_b
Correct. Infiltration by mononuclear cells -- lymphocytes, plasma cells and macrophages -- is the pathologic hallmark of chronic inflammation, distinct from the neutrophil-dominated picture of acute inflammation. A chronic gastric ulcer is a classic example in which continuing tissue injury and attempted healing by fibrosis proceed together, so the coexistence of a mononuclear infiltrate with evidence of tissue destruction points specifically to chronicity. Plasma cells are B lymphocytes that have transformed into antibody-secreting cells, and their presence alongside lymphocytes is the expected chronic-inflammatory response at a persistent ulcer.

## answer_c
Hyperplastic change

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hyperplastic change is an increase in the number of cells of a tissue, not an inflammatory infiltrate; the biopsy describes an infiltrate of inflammatory cells with tissue destruction, which is not what hyperplasia means.

## answer_d
Malignant transformation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Malignant transformation would be recognised by cellular and nuclear pleomorphism, atypical mitoses and invasion, none of which are described here -- a lymphocyte-and-plasma-cell infiltrate is a reactive inflammatory finding, not evidence of malignancy.

## title
Healing by second intention

## question
Which of the following best describes healing by second intention:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Healing of large infected wound with extensive tissue loss

## explanation_a
Correct. Second intention healing is the mode used for open, gaping, infected or tissue-loss wounds, where the edges cannot simply be apposed. It closes through abundant granulation tissue growing from the base of the wound, takes longer than first intention, and is completed partly by myofibroblast-driven wound contraction. The large amount of granulation tissue and contraction leave a larger, less regular scar than the fine linear scar of first intention.

## answer_b
Restoration of tissue to normal without scar formation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Restoration of tissue to normal without a scar is regeneration by labile or stable cells, not healing by second intention, which necessarily ends in a scar because it repairs by fibrosis.

## answer_c
The direct conversion of fibrin to collagen without inflammation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Collagen is laid down by fibroblasts within granulation tissue during an inflammatory repair response; there is no direct fibrin-to-collagen conversion and healing is not an inflammation-free process.

## answer_d
Healing of clean, uninfected surgical wound with sutured edges

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Healing of a clean, uninfected, sutured surgical wound with apposed edges is first intention healing, which forms only a small amount of granulation tissue and a fine pale linear scar -- the opposite of the tissue-loss situation of second intention.

## title
Histologic hallmark of an abscess

## question
Histologically, an abscess is characterized by which feature:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Diffuse involvement of tissue without a local collection

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diffuse involvement of tissue without a walled-off local collection describes cellulitis (diffuse suppurative inflammation), which spreads because streptococcal fibrinolysin and hyaluronidase break down fibrin -- the opposite of an abscess.

## answer_b
A thick capsule of mature scar tissue

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An abscess is walled off by an acute pyogenic membrane and, only later, granulation tissue; a thick capsule of mature scar tissue is a feature of chronic walled-off lesions, not the defining histology of a suppurative abscess.

## answer_c
A central zone of caseation necrosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A central zone of caseation necrosis is the hallmark of a tuberculous (caseating granulomatous) lesion, not a pyogenic abscess, whose centre is liquefied pus rather than cheesy caseous material.

## answer_d
A localized collection of suppuration inflammatory tissue

## explanation_d
Correct. An abscess is a form of localized suppurative inflammation: a discrete, walled-off cavity containing pus -- neutrophils, pus cells, necrotic tissue and edema fluid. It is commonly caused by Staphylococcus aureus, whose coagulase converts fibrinogen to fibrin and walls the pus off into a local collection. This walling-off into a localized collection of suppuration is exactly what distinguishes an abscess from diffuse cellulitis.

## title
Feature marking an inflammation as chronic

## question
The most characteristic feature that an inflammatory process has become chronic rather than acute is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Dilatation of local capillaries

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Dilatation of local capillaries (vascular congestion producing the redness and heat of inflammation) is a feature of the early acute vascular response, not a marker of chronicity.

## answer_b
Predominance of neutrophils

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A predominance of neutrophils is the hallmark of acute inflammation; chronic inflammation is defined instead by a mononuclear (lymphocyte, plasma cell, macrophage) infiltrate.

## answer_c
Presence of angiogenesis and fibrosis

## explanation_c
Correct. In chronic inflammation, continuing tissue injury and attempted healing proceed together, so the tissue simultaneously shows new blood vessel formation (angiogenesis) and fibrosis alongside the mononuclear infiltrate. This coexistence of ongoing repair -- angiogenesis and fibrosis -- with persisting inflammation is what most characteristically distinguishes a chronic process from an acute one. Acute inflammation, by contrast, shows vascular dilatation, edema and neutrophils without this concurrent fibrotic healing.

## answer_d
Presence of predominant edema

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Predominant edema (protein-rich exudate) reflects the increased vascular permeability of acute inflammation; chronic inflammation shows relatively little edema fluid compared with the acute phase.

## title
Definition of margination

## question
During the process of acute inflammation, “margination” refers to which cellular behavior:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Rapid proliferation of endothelial cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Rapid proliferation of endothelial cells is a feature of angiogenesis during repair, not the definition of margination, which is a movement of leukocytes rather than a proliferative event.

## answer_b
Leukocytes move from the central blood flow to the vessel wall

## explanation_b
Correct. Margination is the first step of leukocyte recruitment: as blood flow slows (stasis) at the inflamed site, neutrophils move out of the normal central (axial) stream into the peripheral plasmatic zone next to the endothelium. Positioned at the vessel wall, they can then roll (selectin-mediated), adhere firmly (integrin-mediated pavementing) and finally emigrate. So margination specifically names the redistribution of leukocytes from the centre of the flow to the vessel wall.

## answer_c
Leukocytes squeezing through endothelial junction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Leukocytes squeezing between endothelial cells through the vessel wall is emigration (transmigration/diapedesis), a later step that follows margination, rolling and firm adhesion.

## answer_d
The clustering of RBCs in the center of blood vessel

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Clustering of red cells in the centre of the vessel (rouleaux/axial packing from stasis) is a rheologic change that helps push leukocytes peripherally, but margination refers to the leukocytes moving to the wall, not to the red cells clustering centrally.

## title
Malignancy complicating Schistosoma haematobium infection

## question
Chronic inflammation with Schistosoma haematobium may be complicated by the following type of malignancy:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Adenocarcinoma of the colon

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adenocarcinoma of the colon is associated with Schistosoma mansoni colonic disease and adenomatous change, not with Schistosoma haematobium, which is the urinary (vesical) species.

## answer_b
Renal cell carcinoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Renal cell carcinoma arises from renal tubular epithelium and has no established causal link to bilharzial infection; haematobium disease targets the bladder wall, not the renal parenchyma.

## answer_c
Gastric carcinoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gastric carcinoma is linked to Helicobacter pylori, chronic atrophic gastritis and intestinal metaplasia of the stomach, not to Schistosoma haematobium.

## answer_d
Squamous cell carcinoma of the urinary bladder

## explanation_d
Correct. Schistosoma haematobium lodges in the vesical (bladder) plexus, and its chronic inflammation drives squamous metaplasia of the transitional (urothelial) lining. Squamous cell carcinoma of the urinary bladder is stated directly among the predisposing factors for squamous cell carcinoma as arising on top of this bilharzial squamous metaplasia. This is why bilharzial (schistosomal) cystitis is the classic cause of squamous -- rather than transitional-cell -- carcinoma of the bladder.

## title
Most common bilharzial lesion of the colon

## question
The most common bilharzial lesion in the colon is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Sandy patches

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sandy patches (mucosal deposits of calcified dead ova) do occur in colonic bilharziasis, but they are not the most common lesion of the colon and are more characteristic of the urinary bladder.

## answer_b
Ulcers

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Bilharzial ulcers are part of the colonic lesion spectrum but are less common than polyps at this site; the colon is wide and lesions are usually superficial.

## answer_c
Leukoplakia

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Leukoplakia is a keratinizing white plaque of squamous or metaplastic squamous epithelium (mouth, bladder), not a lesion of the columnar-lined colon in bilharziasis.

## answer_d
Polyps

## explanation_d
Correct. Bilharziasis of the large intestine affects mainly the rectosigmoid segment, and bilharzial polyps are much more common in the colon than at any other site. They form as inflammatory/granulomatous outgrowths around deposited ova and typically present with bleeding. Their frequency at the rectosigmoid is why polyps are the most common colonic bilharzial lesion.

## title
Opportunistic infections in AIDS (EXCEPT)

## question
Opportunistic infections in AIDS include all of the following EXCEPT:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Candidiasis

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it IS an opportunistic infection). Candidiasis is a fungal opportunistic infection of the AIDS crisis phase, and oral/oesophageal candidiasis is one of the common defining infections.

## answer_b
Bilharziasis

## explanation_b
Correct (this is the EXCEPTION). Bilharziasis (schistosomiasis) is a primary parasitic infection acquired from contaminated fresh water, not an opportunistic infection that exploits the CD4 depletion of AIDS. The opportunistic infections that define the AIDS crisis phase are protozoal/helminthic (strongyloidiasis, pneumocystis), fungal (candidiasis, histoplasmosis, cryptococcosis), bacterial (tuberculosis, atypical mycobacteria, salmonella) and viral (CMV, HSV, EBV) -- bilharziasis is not on that list.

## answer_c
Pneumocystis carinii

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it IS an opportunistic infection). Pneumocystis (carinii/jirovecii) pneumonia is one of the classic AIDS-defining opportunistic infections.

## answer_d
Atypical mycobacteria

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it IS an opportunistic infection). Atypical (non-tuberculous) mycobacteria, such as Mycobacterium avium complex, are named bacterial opportunistic infections of advanced AIDS.

False statement about AIDS

## question
Which of the following is FALSE about AIDS:

## subject
fnd

Homosexuals, Intravenous drug abusers and blood recipients are at high risk for developing AIDS

Human Papilloma virus causes genital warts and cervical carcinoma, an entirely different agent and disease. Naming HPV as the cause of AIDS is therefore the false statement.
