## title
Ionic content of the synaptic cleft

## question
Which of the following is correct as regards the synapse?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Neurons communicate with each other mostly through electrical synapses

## explanation_a
Incorrect. In the mammalian CNS the overwhelming majority of interneuronal communication is through chemical synapses; electrical (gap-junction) synapses are relatively uncommon and confined to a few specialised sites.

## answer_b
V-SNARE protein is present on the presynaptic membrane

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The vesicle-associated SNARE (v-SNARE, e.g. synaptobrevin/VAMP) lies on the synaptic vesicle membrane; the target-SNAREs (syntaxin, SNAP-25) are the ones on the presynaptic plasma membrane, and the two pair to fuse the vesicle.

## answer_c
They are static structures

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Synapses are dynamic, plastic structures: their strength varies with use through facilitation, potentiation and depression, which is the cellular basis of learning and memory.

## answer_d
The synaptic cleft contains fluid that is rich in Na+

## explanation_d
Correct. The synaptic cleft is a narrow extracellular gap continuous with the interstitial fluid, so it is filled with extracellular fluid rich in Na+ and the other ECF ions. Released transmitter diffuses across this Na+-rich fluid to reach postsynaptic receptors. Its ionic composition matches extracellular, not intracellular, fluid.

## title
Net postsynaptic potential and membrane excitability

## question
The grand (net) postsynaptic potential may cause the postsynaptic membrane to:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
be more excitable if EPSPs are more than IPSPs

## explanation_a
Correct. The grand postsynaptic potential is the algebraic sum of all converging EPSPs and IPSPs. When EPSPs exceed IPSPs the net effect is depolarisation toward threshold, so the membrane becomes more excitable and, if threshold is reached, fires. This is the basis of spatial and temporal summation.

## answer_b
be less excitable if IPSPs are lesser than EPSPs

## explanation_b
Incorrect. If IPSPs are LESS than EPSPs the net effect is excitatory, so the membrane becomes MORE excitable, not less; the statement inverts the relationship.

## answer_c
reach firing if there is summation of IPSPs

## explanation_c
Incorrect. IPSPs are hyperpolarising; summing more IPSPs moves the membrane further from threshold and makes firing less likely, not more.

## answer_d
propagate easily when it is hyperpolarized

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A hyperpolarised membrane is further from threshold and less likely to generate or propagate an action potential; hyperpolarisation opposes firing.

## title
Cause of synaptic fatigue

## question
As regards fatigue of synaptic transmission, which of the following is correct?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is due to activation of postsynaptic receptors

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fatigue is a presynaptic phenomenon; it is not produced by activation of postsynaptic receptors, which mediate the postsynaptic response rather than limiting transmitter supply.

## answer_b
It is due to exhaustion of neurotransmitters in the presynaptic terminal

## explanation_b
Correct. When a synapse is stimulated rapidly and repeatedly, the presynaptic transmitter stores are used up faster than they can be resynthesised and repackaged, so the postsynaptic response progressively declines. This depletion of releasable transmitter is the principal mechanism of synaptic fatigue. Recovery follows once transmitter is replenished.

## answer_c
It helps over-excitation of neurons

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fatigue does the opposite: by damping transmission it PROTECTS against over-excitation of neurons, helping to terminate excessive or runaway (e.g. seizure-like) discharge.

## answer_d
It occurs due to increased impulse discharge from the postsynaptic neuron

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fatigue is a decline in transmission, reflecting reduced presynaptic release, not increased impulse discharge from the postsynaptic neuron.

## title
Mechanism of ionotropic receptors

## question
Ionotropic receptors:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
affect neuronal activity through activating ion channels

## explanation_a
Correct. An ionotropic receptor is itself a ligand-gated ion channel: binding of the transmitter opens an intrinsic pore, so ions flow and the membrane potential changes directly and within milliseconds. This gives the fast synaptic responses of the nicotinic ACh, AMPA/NMDA glutamate and GABA-A receptors. No second messenger is required.

## answer_b
affect neuronal activity directly by changing levels of intracellular cAMP

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Acting through changes in intracellular cAMP is the hallmark of metabotropic (G-protein-coupled) receptors, which work indirectly and more slowly, not of ionotropic receptors.

## answer_c
act by increasing intracellular IP3

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Coupling to increased IP3 is again a metabotropic, second-messenger mechanism, not the direct channel-opening action of an ionotropic receptor.

## answer_d
act by voltage-gated ion channels

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ionotropic receptor channels are opened by their LIGAND, not by membrane voltage; voltage-gated channels are a separate class that generate and conduct the action potential.

## title
Mechanism of tetanus toxin

## question
Tetanus toxin leads to:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
spastic paralysis due to inhibition of GABA or glycine in the CNS

## explanation_a
Correct. Tetanus toxin is transported to the spinal cord and blocks the release of the inhibitory transmitters glycine and GABA from inhibitory interneurons. With inhibition removed, motor neurons fire unopposed and muscles contract excessively, producing spastic paralysis with rigidity and spasms (lockjaw, opisthotonus). The lesion is loss of central inhibition.

## answer_b
convulsions due to competitive inhibition with inhibitory transmitters

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The toxin does not act by competitive antagonism at inhibitory receptors; it blocks the RELEASE of the inhibitory transmitters. Although clinical spasms occur, the mechanism stated here is wrong.

## answer_c
flaccid paralysis due to inhibition of acetylcholine release

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Blocking acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction, causing flaccid paralysis, is the mechanism of BOTULINUM toxin, not tetanus toxin.

## answer_d
flaccid paralysis due to inhibition of GABA release

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tetanus does block GABA (and glycine) release, but the result is SPASTIC, not flaccid, paralysis; removing inhibition increases muscle tone.

## title
Mechanism of synaptic sensitization

## question
As regards sensitization of synapses, which statement is correct?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It is due to decreased release of neurotransmitter from the presynaptic neuron

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Decreased presynaptic transmitter release underlies habituation and presynaptic inhibition, which DEPRESS the response; sensitization enhances it.

## answer_b
It causes hyperpolarization of the postsynaptic neuron

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sensitization increases the excitatory postsynaptic response; it does not hyperpolarise the postsynaptic neuron.

## answer_c
It is explained by presynaptic facilitation and excess release of neurotransmitters

## explanation_c
Correct. Sensitization is produced by presynaptic facilitation: a modulatory (facilitating) interneuron acts axo-axonically on the sensory terminal, prolonging its action potential and raising Ca2+ entry, so MORE transmitter is released and the postsynaptic response is enhanced. It is the mirror image of presynaptic inhibition. The Aplysia serotonergic facilitating synapse is the classic model.

## answer_d
It is due to activation of a 3rd inhibitory neuron that inhibits a sensory neuron

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Activation of a third INHIBITORY neuron acting on a sensory neuron describes presynaptic INHIBITION, which reduces transmission; sensitization uses a facilitating, not inhibitory, third neuron.

## title
Classifying a membrane-potential change as an EPSP

## question
An input neuron changes the membrane potential of a target neuron from -65 mV to -55 mV. Which of the following best describes this change (in millivolts)?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Excitatory postsynaptic potential = +10

## explanation_a
Correct. Moving from -65 mV to -55 mV is a DEPOLARISATION (the membrane becomes less negative, toward threshold), which is by definition an excitatory postsynaptic potential. The size of the change is -55 minus -65 = +10 mV. Hence it is an EPSP of +10 mV.

## answer_b
Excitatory postsynaptic potential = -10

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The classification (excitatory) is right, but the sign is wrong: a depolarisation from -65 to -55 mV is +10 mV, not -10 mV.

## answer_c
Inhibitory postsynaptic potential = +10

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A depolarisation toward threshold is excitatory, not inhibitory; an IPSP would move the potential to a more negative value.

## answer_d
Inhibitory postsynaptic potential = -10

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This is doubly wrong: the change is excitatory (depolarising), not inhibitory, and its value is +10 mV, not -10 mV.

## title
Definition of reciprocal innervation

## question
Reciprocal innervation is defined as:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The number of neurons with which one afferent neuron synapses

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The number of neurons contacted by one afferent describes DIVERGENCE (the field of an input), not reciprocal innervation.

## answer_b
The stimulation of one muscle and inhibition of its antagonist by excitation of one nerve

## explanation_b
Correct. Reciprocal innervation couples excitation of a muscle with simultaneous inhibition of its antagonist: the afferent excites the agonist's motor neurons and, via a Ia inhibitory interneuron, inhibits the antagonist's, so the antagonist relaxes as the agonist contracts. This allows smooth movement about a joint. It is also called reciprocal inhibition.

## answer_c
Many neuronal inputs stimulating one neuron in a neuronal pool

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Many inputs converging on one neuron is CONVERGENCE, a property of neuronal pools, not the agonist-antagonist coupling of reciprocal innervation.

## answer_d
One neuron stimulating many neurons in a neuronal pool

## explanation_d
Incorrect. One neuron exciting many others is DIVERGENCE; it does not describe the reciprocal excitation-inhibition of a muscle and its antagonist.

## title
Sensory modalities carried by A-beta fibres

## question
All of the following sensations are carried by A-beta fibres EXCEPT:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Fine touch

## explanation_a
Incorrect (A-beta DOES carry it). Fine, discriminative touch is a classic A-beta (group II) modality carried by large myelinated cutaneous mechanoreceptor afferents.

## answer_b
Vibration

## explanation_b
Incorrect (A-beta DOES carry it). Vibration sense is transduced by Pacinian corpuscles and conveyed by A-beta fibres.

## answer_c
Proprioception

## explanation_c
Correct (this is the EXCEPTION). Muscle and tendon proprioception arises from primary muscle-spindle endings (Ia) and Golgi tendon organs (Ib), which are carried by the largest A-ALPHA (group I) fibres, not A-beta. Hence proprioception is the odd one out on this list.

## answer_d
Stereognosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect (A-beta DOES carry it). Stereognosis is the recognition of objects by touch and depends on fine discriminative touch, so it too travels in A-beta fibres.
