## title
Composition of Nissl bodies

## question
Nissl bodies consist of:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Clusters of synaptic vesicles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Synaptic vesicles are small membrane-bound sacs of neurotransmitter clustered at the axon terminal (presynaptic bouton), not the basophilic cytoplasmic masses of the perikaryon that are stained as Nissl bodies.

## answer_b
Ribosomes and rough endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_b
Correct. Nissl bodies (Nissl substance) are the basophilic granular masses of the neuronal cell body and dendrites, composed of rough endoplasmic reticulum studded with ribosomes together with free polyribosomes. Their abundant ribosomal RNA is what gives them their strong basophilia and reflects the neuron's high rate of protein synthesis. They extend into the dendrites but are absent from the axon hillock and axon, a classic identifying feature, and their dispersal (chromatolysis) after axonal injury mirrors this protein-synthesising role.

## answer_c
Golgi bodies

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The Golgi apparatus is a separate organelle that packages and modifies proteins; it is argyrophilic (silver-staining) rather than basophilic and is not what the term Nissl body denotes.

## answer_d
Lysosomes and lipofuscin granules

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lysosomes and lipofuscin (an age-related residual-body pigment) are distinct cytoplasmic components; lipofuscin does accumulate with age in neurons but is not the basophilic Nissl substance.

## title
Location of bipolar neurons

## question
Bipolar neurons are present in:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Vestibular ganglia

## explanation_a
Correct. Bipolar neurons carry two processes — a single dendrite and a single axon — extending from opposite poles of the cell body, and in the adult they occur in the special sensory pathways: the retina, the olfactory epithelium, and the vestibular (Scarpa's) and cochlear (spiral) ganglia of the vestibulocochlear nerve. The vestibular ganglion is therefore the correct location among the options offered. This bipolar morphology suits their role as a first-order relay in a sensory pathway.

## answer_b
Spinal ganglia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The neurons of the spinal (dorsal root) ganglia are pseudounipolar — a single process leaves the cell body and divides into central and peripheral branches — not bipolar.

## answer_c
Sympathetic ganglia

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sympathetic (autonomic) ganglion cells are multipolar neurons with many dendrites, not bipolar.

## answer_d
Mesencephalic nucleus of the trigeminal nerve

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The mesencephalic nucleus of the trigeminal nerve contains pseudounipolar primary sensory neurons (a unique CNS location for such cells), not bipolar neurons.

## title
Unencapsulated sensory nerve ending

## question
One of the following is classified as unencapsulated:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Golgi tendon organ

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The Golgi tendon organ is an encapsulated proprioceptor enclosed in a connective-tissue capsule at the myotendinous junction.

## answer_b
Meissner's corpuscle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Meissner's (tactile) corpuscles are encapsulated mechanoreceptors of the dermal papillae, wrapped in a thin connective-tissue capsule.

## answer_c
Krause end bulb

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Krause end bulbs are encapsulated receptors, a coiled nerve terminal surrounded by a thin connective-tissue capsule.

## answer_d
Free nerve endings

## explanation_d
Correct. Sensory nerve endings are classified as encapsulated (enclosed in a connective-tissue capsule — Meissner, Pacinian, Ruffini and Krause corpuscles and the Golgi tendon organ) or unencapsulated (naked terminals lacking such a capsule). Free nerve endings are the prototypical unencapsulated ending: bare terminal branches, mostly of pain and temperature fibres, ramifying among epithelial and connective-tissue cells without any capsule. Merkel discs and peritrichial (hair-follicle) endings are the other unencapsulated types.
