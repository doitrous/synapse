## title
Composition of Nissl bodies

## question
Nissl bodies consist of:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Clusters of synaptic vesicles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Synaptic vesicles are small membrane-bound sacs of neurotransmitter clustered at the axon terminal (presynaptic bouton), not the basophilic cytoplasmic masses of the perikaryon that are stained as Nissl bodies.

## answer_b
Ribosomes and rough endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_b
Correct. Nissl bodies (Nissl substance) are the basophilic granular masses of the neuronal cell body and dendrites, composed of rough endoplasmic reticulum studded with ribosomes together with free polyribosomes. Their abundant ribosomal RNA is what gives them their strong basophilia and reflects the neuron's high rate of protein synthesis. They extend into the dendrites but are absent from the axon hillock and axon, a classic identifying feature, and their dispersal (chromatolysis) after axonal injury mirrors this protein-synthesising role.

## answer_c
Golgi bodies

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The Golgi apparatus is a separate organelle that packages and modifies proteins; it is argyrophilic (silver-staining) rather than basophilic and is not what the term Nissl body denotes.

## answer_d
Lysosomes and lipofuscin granules

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lysosomes and lipofuscin (an age-related residual-body pigment) are distinct cytoplasmic components; lipofuscin does accumulate with age in neurons but is not the basophilic Nissl substance.

## title
Location of bipolar neurons

## question
Bipolar neurons are present in:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Vestibular ganglia

## explanation_a
Correct. Bipolar neurons carry two processes — a single dendrite and a single axon — extending from opposite poles of the cell body, and in the adult they occur in the special sensory pathways: the retina, the olfactory epithelium, and the vestibular (Scarpa's) and cochlear (spiral) ganglia of the vestibulocochlear nerve. The vestibular ganglion is therefore the correct location among the options offered. This bipolar morphology suits their role as a first-order relay in a sensory pathway.

## answer_b
Spinal ganglia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The neurons of the spinal (dorsal root) ganglia are pseudounipolar — a single process leaves the cell body and divides into central and peripheral branches — not bipolar.

## answer_c
Sympathetic ganglia

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sympathetic (autonomic) ganglion cells are multipolar neurons with many dendrites, not bipolar.

## answer_d
Mesencephalic nucleus of the trigeminal nerve

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The mesencephalic nucleus of the trigeminal nerve contains pseudounipolar primary sensory neurons (a unique CNS location for such cells), not bipolar neurons.

## title
Unencapsulated sensory nerve ending

## question
One of the following is classified as unencapsulated:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Golgi tendon organ

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The Golgi tendon organ is an encapsulated proprioceptor enclosed in a connective-tissue capsule at the myotendinous junction.

## answer_b
Meissner's corpuscle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Meissner's (tactile) corpuscles are encapsulated mechanoreceptors of the dermal papillae, wrapped in a thin connective-tissue capsule.

## answer_c
Krause end bulb

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Krause end bulbs are encapsulated receptors, a coiled nerve terminal surrounded by a thin connective-tissue capsule.

## answer_d
Free nerve endings

## explanation_d
Correct. Sensory nerve endings are classified as encapsulated (enclosed in a connective-tissue capsule — Meissner, Pacinian, Ruffini and Krause corpuscles and the Golgi tendon organ) or unencapsulated (naked terminals lacking such a capsule). Free nerve endings are the prototypical unencapsulated ending: bare terminal branches, mostly of pain and temperature fibres, ramifying among epithelial and connective-tissue cells without any capsule. Merkel discs and peritrichial (hair-follicle) endings are the other unencapsulated types.

## title
Cell forming CNS myelin

## question
Cell type that forms the myelin sheath around myelinated axons in the CNS:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ependymal cell

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ependymal cells line the ventricles and central canal; they do not form myelin.

## answer_b
Schwann cell

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Schwann cells myelinate axons in the PERIPHERAL nervous system, each internode formed by one Schwann cell; they are not the myelinating cell of the CNS.

## answer_c
Oligodendrocyte

## explanation_c
Correct. Oligodendrocytes are the myelinating neuroglia of the central nervous system: a single oligodendrocyte extends several processes, each wrapping an internode of myelin around a different CNS axon (unlike a Schwann cell, which myelinates only one internode of one peripheral axon). This one-cell-to-many-axons arrangement is a defining CNS feature, and oligodendrocyte loss underlies central demyelinating disease.

## answer_d
Astrocyte

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Astrocytes provide structural and metabolic support and contribute to the blood-brain barrier; they do not form myelin.

## title
Why white matter appears white

## question
White matter appears white because of the:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Absence of blood vessels

## explanation_a
Incorrect. White matter is well vascularised; blood vessels do not account for its colour, and their presence would if anything add colour rather than whiteness.

## answer_b
Presence of many collagen fibres

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The central nervous system contains essentially no collagen/connective tissue within its substance; there are no abundant collagen fibres to colour white matter.

## answer_c
Absence of glial cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. White matter is rich in glial cells (oligodendrocytes and astrocytes); their presence, not absence, is normal there.

## answer_d
Presence of many myelinated axons

## explanation_d
Correct. White matter owes its pale, glistening appearance to its dense population of MYELINATED axons: the lipid-rich myelin sheaths scatter light and give the tissue its white colour, which is why fibre tracts look white while neuron-cell-body-rich grey matter looks grey. This colour difference is the gross correlate of the histological distinction between myelinated tracts and cell-body-dense cortex/nuclei.

## subject
neuro

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Microglia are the small phagocytic (immune) cells of the CNS; they have no role in propelling cerebrospinal fluid.

## explanation_b
Correct. Ependymal cells form the ciliated simple epithelium lining the ventricles, cerebral aqueduct and central canal; the coordinated beating of their apical cilia helps propel and circulate the cerebrospinal fluid through the ventricular system. Incorrect. Astrocytes provide structural/metabolic support and contribute to the blood-brain barrier; they are not ciliated and do not move CSF.

Incorrect. Oligodendrocytes form CNS myelin; they play no part in CSF circulation.

## subject

Incorrect. The muscle spindle is a receptor located within skeletal muscle (a proprioceptor among the intrafusal muscle fibres), classified as a muscular receptor rather than a connective-tissue receptor.

Krause's end bulb, an encapsulated ending in the connective tissue of the dermis and mucous membranes, is therefore the connective-tissue receptor among the options.

## title
Why the CNS is soft in consistency

## question
The central nervous system is soft because:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It consists of neurons joined by connective tissue

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Neurons in the CNS are not held together by connective tissue; support is provided by neuroglia, so this description is wrong.

## answer_b
It is rich in blood vessels

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Although the CNS is vascular, its blood supply is not what determines its soft consistency.

## answer_c
It does not contain connective tissue

## explanation_c
Correct. The substance of the central nervous system contains essentially no connective tissue internally (collagen/fibrous stroma is confined to the meninges and the perivascular spaces); mechanical support instead comes from the neuroglial framework. Because it lacks the tough fibrous connective-tissue stroma that firms up most organs, the CNS is soft and gel-like in consistency. This absence of internal connective tissue is a defining histological feature of nervous tissue.

## answer_d
It contains fat cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The CNS does not contain fat (adipose) cells in its substance; myelin lipid is intracellular in glial membranes, not stored in fat cells.

## title
Epithelium lining the central canal

## question
The central canal of the spinal cord is lined by which epithelium?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Simple squamous

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The central canal is not lined by simple squamous epithelium; that lining (mesothelium/endothelium) belongs to serous cavities and vessels.

## answer_b
Stratified squamous

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Stratified squamous epithelium is a protective surface epithelium (e.g. epidermis, oesophagus), not the lining of the central canal.

## answer_c
Simple cuboidal, partially ciliated

## explanation_c
Correct. The central canal, like the ventricular system, is lined by ependyma — a simple cuboidal to low-columnar epithelium whose cells bear apical cilia (and microvilli). The cilia help move cerebrospinal fluid, and this ciliated simple cuboidal/columnar ependymal lining is the correct description among the options. Its single layer at the CSF interface is what makes it simple (not stratified) and ciliated.

## answer_d
Simple columnar, non-ciliated

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The ependyma is ciliated, so a 'non-ciliated' columnar description is wrong; the presence of cilia is a defining feature of the central-canal lining.
