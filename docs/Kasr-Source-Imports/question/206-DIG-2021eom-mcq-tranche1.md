## title
Referred pain of appendicitis

## question
Regarding the large intestine, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Pain of appendicitis is usually referred to the region of the umbilicus

## explanation_a
Correct. Early appendicular pain is visceral, carried by sympathetic afferents that accompany the midgut to the T10 spinal segment, so it is referred to the T10 dermatome around the umbilicus. Only later, when the inflamed appendix irritates the parietal peritoneum, does the pain shift and localise to McBurney's point in the right iliac fossa. This visceral-to-somatic migration of pain is a classic diagnostic feature of appendicitis.

## answer_b
The left colic flexure lies in front of the right kidney

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The left colic (splenic) flexure lies in front of the left kidney; it is the right colic (hepatic) flexure that relates to the right kidney.

## answer_c
Appendices epiploicae are present in the appendix and caecum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Appendices epiploicae are fat-filled peritoneal tags characteristic of the colon; they are absent on the appendix and the caecum.

## answer_d
The appendix mostly lies in the subcaecal position (75%)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The appendix most often lies in the retrocaecal/retrocolic position (about 65-75%); the subcaecal position is uncommon.

## title
Ureter crossing the common iliac bifurcation

## question
Regarding the ureter, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pain sensation is carried from the ureter to the upper two thoracic and upper two lumbar segments of the spinal cord

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ureteric pain is referred to the T11-L2 segments (loin to groin), not the upper two thoracic segments.

## answer_b
The inferior vena cava lies lateral to the right ureter

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The inferior vena cava lies medial to the right ureter, not lateral to it.

## answer_c
While lying on the psoas major it is crossed anteriorly by the genitofemoral nerve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. On the psoas major the ureter is crossed anteriorly by the gonadal (testicular/ovarian) vessels; the genitofemoral nerve lies behind it, on the surface of the muscle.

It crosses the bifurcation of the common iliac artery

## explanation_d
Correct. The abdominal ureter descends on the psoas major and enters the pelvis by crossing in front of the bifurcation of the common iliac artery at the pelvic brim, over the sacroiliac joint. This crossing is a key surgical landmark used to identify the ureter. It is also one of the three normal sites of ureteric narrowing where calculi tend to impact.

## title
Inferior mesenteric artery territory

## question
Regarding the blood supply of the gut, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
The splenic artery lies deep to the body of the pancreas

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The splenic artery runs a tortuous course along the superior border of the body of the pancreas, not deep to it.

## answer_b
The superior mesenteric vein lies on the left side of the superior mesenteric artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The superior mesenteric vein lies on the right side of the superior mesenteric artery.

## answer_c
The superior pancreaticoduodenal artery is a branch of the superior mesenteric artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior pancreaticoduodenal artery is a branch of the gastroduodenal artery (from the coeliac trunk); only the inferior pancreaticoduodenal artery comes from the SMA.

## explanation_d
Correct. The inferior mesenteric artery supplies the hindgut: the distal (left) third of the transverse colon, the descending and sigmoid colon and the upper rectum, through its left colic, sigmoid and superior rectal branches. The boundary with the SMA territory near the splenic flexure is the watershed zone of Griffiths, vulnerable to ischaemia. The proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon remain SMA (midgut) territory.

Superior mesenteric vessels and the uncinate process

## question
Regarding the pancreas, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status

The tail passes through the gastrosplenic ligament

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The tail of the pancreas reaches the hilum of the spleen within the splenorenal (lienorenal) ligament, not the gastrosplenic ligament.

## answer_b
The superior mesenteric vessels lie anterior to the uncinate process of the head

## explanation_b
Correct. The uncinate process is the hook-like inferior projection of the head of the pancreas that passes behind the superior mesenteric vessels, so the SMA and SMV lie anterior to it. The left renal vein lies anterior to the body

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The left renal vein passes posterior to the body of the pancreas (and anterior to the aorta), not anterior to the body.

## answer_d
The pyloroduodenal junction lies behind the neck

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pyloroduodenal junction lies to the right of the neck, not behind it.

## subject

The second part of the duodenum is related to the anterior surface of the right kidney close to the hilum

Correct. The descending (second) part of the duodenum passes vertically over the anterior surface of the medial part of the right kidney, close to the hilum and the right renal vessels. The renal fascia is derived from the fascia iliaca

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The renal fascia (of Gerota) is condensed extraperitoneal/transversalis fascia, not a derivative of the fascia iliaca.

## title
Nerve through the superficial inguinal ring

## question
Which of the following nerves passes through the superficial inguinal ring?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Iliohypogastric nerve

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The iliohypogastric nerve pierces the external oblique aponeurosis about 2 cm above the superficial inguinal ring; it does not pass through the ring.

## answer_b
Obturator nerve

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The obturator nerve leaves the pelvis through the obturator foramen to reach the medial thigh and has no relation to the inguinal canal.

## answer_c
Ilioinguinal nerve

## explanation_c
Correct. The ilioinguinal nerve (L1) enters the inguinal canal by piercing the internal oblique rather than passing through the deep ring, runs anterior to the spermatic cord, and emerges through the superficial inguinal ring. It supplies the skin of the upper medial thigh and the anterior scrotum or labium majus. It is therefore the nerve at risk during herniorrhaphy at the superficial ring.

## answer_d
Pudendal nerve

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pudendal nerve exits the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen and re-enters via the lesser sciatic foramen to supply the perineum; it does not traverse the inguinal canal.

## title
Formation of the hepatopancreatic ampulla

## question
The common bile duct and the main pancreatic duct join to form the:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The (right and left) hepatic ducts drain bile out of the liver lobes and unite to form the common hepatic duct; they lie upstream of the common bile duct.

## answer_b
Common hepatic duct

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The common hepatic duct is formed by the union of the right and left hepatic ducts, before the cystic duct joins it.

## answer_c
Cystic duct

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The cystic duct connects the gall bladder to the common hepatic duct to form the common bile duct; it is not formed by the union in the question.

Correct. The common bile duct and the main pancreatic duct (of Wirsung) unite to form the hepatopancreatic ampulla (ampulla of Vater), which opens at the major duodenal papilla on the posteromedial wall of the second part of the duodenum. The ampulla is encircled by the sphincter of Oddi. A gallstone impacted here obstructs both biliary and pancreatic outflow and can cause gallstone pancreatitis.

Which one of the following forms a connection between the liver and the stomach?

## subject
gi

## status

Incorrect. The left triangular ligament connects the left lobe of the liver to the diaphragm, not to the stomach.

Gastrosplenic ligament

Incorrect. The gastrosplenic ligament connects the greater curvature of the stomach to the hilum of the spleen.

## title
Inferior epigastric artery at the deep ring

## question
Regarding the anterior abdominal wall, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The inferior epigastric artery ascends on the medial side of the deep inguinal ring

## explanation_a
Correct. The inferior epigastric artery arises from the external iliac artery just above the inguinal ligament and ascends on the medial side of the deep inguinal ring, raising the lateral umbilical fold before entering the rectus sheath. It therefore lies medial to the neck of an indirect inguinal hernia and lateral to a direct hernia, distinguishing the two. Surgeons ligate this vessel to reduce a hernia at the deep ring.

## answer_b
The ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerves pierce the rectus abdominis muscle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerves run in the neurovascular plane between internal oblique and transversus abdominis; they do not pierce the rectus abdominis.

## answer_c
The upper part of the rectus abdominis muscle rests directly on the fascia transversalis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Above the costal margin the rectus abdominis rests on the 5th-7th costal cartilages, and lower down on the posterior rectus sheath and fascia transversalis; it does not rest directly on fascia transversalis in its upper part.

## answer_d
The tendinous intersections of the rectus abdominis are connected to the posterior wall of the rectus sheath

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The tendinous intersections adhere firmly to the anterior wall of the rectus sheath, not the posterior wall, which is why they produce the surface furrows of the 'six-pack'.

## title
Origin of the short gastric arteries

## question
Considering the short gastric arteries, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The short gastric arteries supply the fundus of the stomach, not the lesser curvature, which is supplied by the left and right gastric arteries.

## answer_b
They reach the stomach through the lienorenal ligament

## explanation_b
Incorrect. They reach the stomach through the gastrosplenic ligament, not the lienorenal (splenorenal) ligament, which transmits the splenic vessels and tail of pancreas.

## answer_c
They are branches of the splenic artery

## explanation_c
Correct. The short gastric arteries (vasa brevia) are four to six small vessels arising from the splenic artery or its terminal branches near the hilum of the spleen. They run in the gastrosplenic ligament to supply the fundus of the stomach and anastomose with the left gastric and left gastro-omental arteries. Because they are short and fragile they are readily torn during splenectomy.

## answer_d
They pass between the two layers of the greater omentum

## explanation_d
Incorrect. They run within the gastrosplenic ligament, not between the two layers of the greater omentum.

## subject

Incorrect. The conjoint tendon lies anterior to the lower part of the rectus sheath, blending with it, not posterior to it.

It is formed by the fused arched fibres of the external oblique and transversus abdominis muscles

## explanation_d
Incorrect. It is formed by the internal oblique and transversus abdominis, not the external oblique.

## title
Vein outside the portal system

## question
One of the following veins does NOT belong to the portal system:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Superior rectal vein

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the answer. The superior rectal vein drains via the inferior mesenteric vein into the portal system and is a site of portosystemic anastomosis.

## answer_b
Superior mesenteric vein

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the answer. The superior mesenteric vein is one of the two main tributaries that unite behind the neck of the pancreas to form the hepatic portal vein.

## answer_c
Left suprarenal vein

## explanation_c
Correct (the odd one out). The left suprarenal (adrenal) vein drains into the left renal vein and thence to the inferior vena cava, so it belongs to the systemic (caval) venous system, not the portal system. By contrast the superior rectal, superior mesenteric and splenic veins are all tributaries of the hepatic portal vein. The left suprarenal vein is therefore the systemic exception.

## answer_d
Splenic vein

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the answer. The splenic vein unites with the superior mesenteric vein to form the hepatic portal vein and receives the inferior mesenteric vein.

It receives its arterial supply from the abdominal aorta

Incorrect. Most of the duodenum is secondarily retroperitoneal and covered by peritoneum on its anterior surface only; just the proximal cap is completely peritonealised.
