## title
Cyclopropagative transmission = Anopheles and malaria

## question
Cyclopropagative transmission in arthropods is seen in:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Fleas & plague

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Plague in the flea is propagative (the bacillus multiplies but does not undergo a developmental cycle).

## answer_b
Anopheles & malaria

## explanation_b
Correct. In cyclopropagative transmission the pathogen both multiplies and undergoes a developmental change in the vector, exactly as the malaria parasite does in the Anopheles mosquito (sporogony). Anopheles and malaria are the classic example. This pairing is therefore cyclopropagative.

## answer_c
Mosquitoes & filaria

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Filaria in mosquitoes is cyclodevelopmental: the larva develops but does not multiply.

## answer_d
Aedes aegypti & yellow fever

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Yellow fever virus in Aedes is propagative (multiplication without a developmental cycle).

## title
Prevent house dust mite asthma with allergen avoidance

## question
A young boy has a runny nose and cough and was prescribed several antibiotics with no response; his mother noticed his asthmatic attacks recur all year round. How can we prevent repetition of this asthma?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The use of D'allergen

## explanation_a
Correct. Year-round asthma unresponsive to antibiotics in a child points to house dust mite allergy, and recurrence is prevented by reducing exposure to the mite allergen with acaricidal/allergen-reducing measures such as D'allergen applied to bedding and the home. Removing the causative allergen source is the preventive step. D'allergen is therefore the answer.

## answer_b
Treatment of asthma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Symptomatic treatment of the asthma relieves attacks but does not prevent their recurrence, which requires removing the mite allergen.

## answer_c
Sanitary disposal of human faeces

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sanitary disposal of faeces controls faecal-oral parasites, not house dust mite asthma.

## answer_d
Proper washing of raw vegetables and fruits before eating

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Washing vegetables prevents food-borne parasites, not mite-driven asthma.

## title
Humidity control helps with house dust mites

## question
Optimizing humidity at home may help with infestations of:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
House dust mites

## explanation_a
Correct. House dust mites depend on high ambient humidity to absorb water and survive, so reducing indoor humidity is a recognised way to control them. Optimizing humidity therefore helps most with house dust mite infestation. House dust mites are the answer.

## answer_b
Domestic mites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. "Domestic mites" is a broad category and not the specific humidity-dependent target of this control measure.

## answer_c
Storage mites

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Storage mites are associated with stored food; the classic humidity-controlled domestic pest is the house dust mite.

## answer_d
Bedbug

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The bedbug is a blood-sucking insect controlled by insecticides and physical measures, not by humidity optimization.

## title
Mosquito shows cyclopropagative transmission

## question
The following arthropod shows cyclopropagative transmission of diseases:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Housefly

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The housefly is only a mechanical carrier of pathogens; it supports no cyclopropagative cycle.

## answer_b
Sand flea

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The sand flea (Tunga) causes tungiasis and is not a cyclopropagative vector.

## answer_c
Bedbug

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The bedbug is not an established cyclopropagative vector of disease.

## answer_d
Mosquito

## explanation_d
Correct. Cyclopropagative transmission requires the pathogen to both develop and multiply in the vector, as the malaria parasite does in the mosquito (Anopheles). The mosquito is therefore the arthropod that shows cyclopropagative transmission. It is the answer.

## title
The larva is the disease stage of chigger mites

## question
The stage of chigger mites that is responsible for causing disease:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Adult

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The adult trombiculid mite is a free-living soil predator, not parasitic.

## answer_b
Egg

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The egg is a non-parasitic environmental stage.

## answer_c
Nymph

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The nymph is free-living, like the adult.

## answer_d
Larva

## explanation_d
Correct. In trombiculid (chigger/harvest) mites only the six-legged larva is parasitic: it attaches to feed on skin, causing trombiculid dermatitis and, in Asia, transmitting scrub typhus, while nymphs and adults are free-living. The larva is therefore the disease-causing stage. It is the answer.
