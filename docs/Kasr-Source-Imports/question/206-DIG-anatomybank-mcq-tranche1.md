## title
Vertebral level at which the rectum begins

## question
Regarding the rectum, which of the following statements is WRONG?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It is continuous above with the sigmoid colon

## explanation_a
Correct statement, so not the answer. The rectum is directly continuous above with the sigmoid colon at the rectosigmoid junction.

## answer_b
It is partly covered by peritoneum

## explanation_b
Correct statement, so not the answer. Peritoneum covers the front and sides of the upper third of the rectum and only the front of the middle third, while the lower third is entirely below the peritoneal reflection.

## answer_c
It begins at the level of the first sacral vertebra (S1)

## explanation_c
This is the wrong statement and therefore the answer. The rectum begins at the rectosigmoid junction in front of the third sacral vertebra (S3), not S1. It then descends along the curve of the sacrum and coccyx to end by piercing the pelvic diaphragm and becoming the anal canal. The S1 level marks the sacral promontory, well above the true origin of the rectum.

## answer_d
It is supplied by the superior, middle and inferior rectal arteries

## explanation_d
Correct statement, so not the answer. The rectum receives the superior rectal artery (from the inferior mesenteric), the middle rectal artery (from the internal iliac) and the inferior rectal artery (from the internal pudendal).

## title
Structure medial to the second part of the duodenum

## question
Which of the following structures lies medial to the second (descending) part of the duodenum?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Head of the pancreas

## explanation_a
Correct. The second part of the duodenum descends to the right of the midline and curves around the head of the pancreas, which lies immediately medial to it in the concavity of the duodenal C-loop. The bile and main pancreatic ducts run in this groove and open into its posteromedial wall at the major duodenal papilla. This intimate relation is why carcinoma of the pancreatic head obstructs the bile duct and, when advanced, the duodenum itself.

## answer_b
Cystic duct

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cystic duct lies above and to the right, running from the neck of the gall bladder to join the common hepatic duct, not medial to the descending duodenum.

## answer_c
Gall bladder

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The gall bladder is an anterior and superior relation of the first and second parts of the duodenum, not a medial one.

## answer_d
Hepatic flexure of the colon

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The hepatic (right colic) flexure of the colon is an anterior relation of the second part of the duodenum, lying in front of it rather than medial to it.

## title
Muscular sling around the anorectal junction

## question
Which of the following structures forms a U-shaped sling around the anorectal junction?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Sacrospinous ligament

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The sacrospinous ligament runs from the sacrum to the ischial spine and takes no part in the anorectal ring.

## answer_b
Anococcygeal ligament

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The anococcygeal ligament (body) is a midline raphe behind the anal canal between it and the coccyx; it anchors the canal but does not form a sling around it.

## answer_c
Superficial transverse perineal muscle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superficial transverse perineal muscle lies in the superficial perineal pouch and stabilises the perineal body, not the anorectal junction.

## answer_d
Puborectalis muscle

## explanation_d
Correct. The puborectalis is the most medial part of levator ani; its fibres pass from the pubis backwards around the anorectal junction as a U-shaped sling and rejoin the opposite side behind the gut. Its tone pulls the junction forward, creating the anorectal angle that is essential for faecal continence. Voluntary relaxation of this sling straightens the angle and permits defaecation.

Junction marked by the ligament of Treitz

## question
The ligament (suspensory muscle) of Treitz is the landmark of the:

## subject
gi

## status

## owner
Claude

Duodenojejunal junction

Incorrect. The pyloroduodenal junction is marked externally by the prepyloric vein of Mayo, not the ligament of Treitz.

## answer_d
Gastro-oesophageal junction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The gastro-oesophageal junction is marked by the cardiac orifice and the angle of His, not by the ligament of Treitz.

Structure not on the anterior surface of the stomach

## question
Which of the following is NOT related to the anterior surface of the stomach?

## subject
gi

Incorrect. Below the liver and costal margin the anterior surface of the stomach is in direct contact with the anterior abdominal wall.

Diaphragm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The diaphragm is related to the upper part of the anterior surface of the stomach and separates it from the pleura, lung and pericardium.

Structure lying on the base of the urinary bladder

One of the following structures lies on the base (fundus) of the urinary bladder in the male:

## title
Origin and course of the vas deferens

## question
Regarding the ductus (vas) deferens, select the INCORRECT statement:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It begins as a continuation of the tail of the epididymis at the upper pole of the testis

## explanation_a
Correct, because this statement is false. The vas deferens does begin as the continuation of the tail (cauda) of the epididymis, but the tail lies at the LOWER pole of the testis, not the upper pole. From there the vas ascends on the medial side of the epididymis to enter the spermatic cord. Placing its origin at the upper pole reverses the true topography of the epididymis, whose head caps the upper pole and whose tail hangs from the lower pole.

## answer_b
It is palpable in the spermatic cord

## explanation_b
Incorrect statement to choose, because it is true. The thick, cord-like vas is readily palpable between finger and thumb within the spermatic cord.

## answer_c
It leaves the spermatic cord at the deep inguinal ring

## explanation_c
Incorrect statement to choose, because it is true. The vas leaves the other cord contents at the deep inguinal ring, hooks around the inferior epigastric artery and passes into the pelvis.

## answer_d
The ampulla of the vas lies behind the base of the bladder

## explanation_d
Incorrect statement to choose, because it is true. The dilated terminal ampulla of the vas lies on the base of the bladder, medial to the seminal vesicle.

## title
Developmental origin of the renal glomeruli

## question
The glomeruli of the kidney are derived from the:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ureteric bud

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The ureteric bud forms the collecting system — the ureter, renal pelvis, calyces and collecting ducts — but not the glomeruli.

## answer_b
Paramesonephric ducts

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The paramesonephric (Mullerian) ducts form the female genital tract and regress in the male; they contribute nothing to the kidney.

## answer_c
Metanephric cap (metanephric mesenchyme)

## explanation_c
Correct. The definitive kidney (metanephros) forms from two interacting tissues: the ureteric bud and the metanephric cap of mesenchyme (metanephric blastema) that condenses over its tip. The metanephric cap is induced to form the nephrons — the renal (Bowman's) capsule and glomerulus, proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle and distal convoluted tubule. The glomerular capillary tuft itself is invaginated into Bowman's capsule, so the glomerulus is a derivative of this metanephric mesenchyme.

## answer_d
Mesonephric ducts

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The mesonephric (Wolffian) ducts give rise to the ureteric bud and, in the male, to the genital ducts, but not directly to the glomeruli.

## title
Attachment of the apex of the urinary bladder

## question
Regarding the urinary bladder, select the TRUE statement:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Its epithelial lining is derived from ectoderm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The epithelium (transitional urothelium) of the bladder is of endodermal origin, derived from the vesical part of the urogenital sinus, not from ectoderm.

## answer_b
Exstrophy of the bladder is due to persistence of the urachus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Exstrophy of the bladder results from failure of the infra-umbilical mesoderm to reinforce the anterior abdominal wall, so the wall and anterior bladder rupture; persistence of the urachus instead produces a urachal fistula, cyst or sinus.

## answer_c
It has no peritoneal covering

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Peritoneum covers the superior surface of the bladder and, when the bladder fills, is stripped upward off the anterior abdominal wall, allowing suprapubic puncture without entering the peritoneal cavity.

## answer_d
Its apex is connected to the umbilicus by the median umbilical ligament

## explanation_d
Correct. The apex of the bladder points forward toward the upper margin of the pubic symphysis and is continuous with the median umbilical ligament. This ligament is the fibrous remnant of the obliterated urachus (allantois) and runs upward in the extraperitoneal tissue to the umbilicus, raising the median umbilical fold of peritoneum. It anchors the apex of the bladder to the anterior abdominal wall.
