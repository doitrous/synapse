## title
Drug that increases cardiac output in heart failure without an inotropic action

## question
Which of the following medications is most likely to increase the cardiac output in a heart failure patient WITHOUT having an inotropic action?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Captopril

## explanation_a
Correct. Captopril is an ACE inhibitor: by reducing angiotensin II it dilates arterioles and lowers AFTERLOAD, so the failing ventricle ejects more effectively and cardiac output rises without any direct increase in contractility. Raising output through afterload (and preload) reduction rather than inotropy is exactly the non-inotropic mechanism described.

## answer_b
Digoxin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Digoxin increases cardiac output by a positive INOTROPIC action (Na+/K+-ATPase inhibition), which the stem explicitly excludes.

## answer_c
Isoproterenol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Isoproterenol is a beta agonist with strong positive inotropic and chronotropic effects.

## answer_d
Theophylline

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Theophylline has positive inotropic and chronotropic activity (phosphodiesterase inhibition), so it acts partly through inotropy.

## title
Cellular action underlying the inotropic effect of digoxin

## question
What cellular action of digoxin accounts for its positive inotropic effect in the treatment of heart failure?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Inhibition of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) synthesis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Digoxin does not work by reducing cAMP; that would be negatively inotropic.

## answer_b
Inhibition of mitochondrial calcium release

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Digoxin does not act on mitochondrial calcium handling.

## answer_c
Inhibition of the Na+/K+ ATPase enzyme

## explanation_c
Correct. Digoxin inhibits the sarcolemmal Na+/K+ ATPase, raising intracellular sodium. This slows the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger, so intracellular CALCIUM accumulates and is stored in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, increasing calcium available for contraction. The result is enhanced myocardial contractility (positive inotropy), which is the basis of digoxin's action in heart failure.

## answer_d
Inhibition of adrenergic stimulation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Digoxin does not act by blocking adrenergic stimulation; that describes beta blockers.

## title
Electrolyte disturbance that increases digitalis toxicity

## question
Which of the following could increase digitalis toxicity?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Use of spironolactone

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Spironolactone is potassium-SPARING; by preventing hypokalaemia it tends to protect against, not precipitate, digitalis toxicity.

## answer_b
Hypocalcaemia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. It is HYPERcalcaemia that potentiates digitalis toxicity; low calcium does not.

## answer_c
Hypokalaemia

## explanation_c
Correct. Digoxin and potassium compete for the same site on the Na+/K+ ATPase. HYPOKALAEMIA (commonly caused by loop or thiazide diuretics) reduces this competition, so more digoxin binds the pump and toxicity — arrhythmias in particular — is greatly increased. Correcting potassium is therefore central to preventing and managing digoxin toxicity.

## answer_d
Antacids

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Antacids tend to reduce digoxin absorption, lowering rather than raising its levels.

## title
Why digitalis is used in atrial fibrillation

## question
Digitalis is used in cases of atrial fibrillation because:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It restores atrial rhythm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Digoxin does not convert atrial fibrillation to sinus rhythm; it is a rate-control, not a rhythm-control, agent.

## answer_b
It decreases A-V conduction

## explanation_b
Correct. Digoxin increases vagal (parasympathetic) tone at the atrioventricular node, slowing AV CONDUCTION and prolonging its refractory period. This reduces the number of atrial impulses reaching the ventricles, controlling the ventricular rate in atrial fibrillation. Rate control through delayed AV conduction is exactly why digitalis is used here.

## answer_c
It decreases atrial excitability

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Digoxin does not primarily reduce atrial excitability; its benefit in AF is at the AV node.

## answer_d
It decreases automaticity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Digoxin can actually increase ectopic automaticity in toxicity; its therapeutic AF effect is AV nodal slowing.

Effect of ACE inhibitors in HFrEF

## question
Which of the following is an effect of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors in a patient with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)?

## subject
pharm

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

Cardiac preload, afterload and cardiac output are all decreased

## explanation_a
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors reduce preload and afterload but INCREASE cardiac output; not all three fall.

Benefit is expected only in symptomatic patients

## explanation_b
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors benefit HFrEF across the spectrum, including asymptomatic left ventricular dysfunction.

Correct. ACE inhibitors block conversion of angiotensin I to II, so both ANGIOTENSIN II and (downstream) ALDOSTERONE fall. This reduces vasoconstriction, sodium/water retention and adverse cardiac remodeling, improving symptoms and survival in HFrEF. Reduced angiotensin II and aldosterone is the defining neurohormonal effect.

## title
Drug protecting the heart from sympathetic cardiotoxicity in heart failure

## question
Which of the following drugs is used to protect the heart from the cardiotoxic effect induced by increased sympathetic activity in heart failure?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Digoxin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Digoxin improves contractility and rate control but does not blunt the sympathetic cardiotoxicity that drives progression and mortality.

## answer_b
Hydralazine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hydralazine is a vasodilator (afterload reduction) and does not protect against sympathetic overactivity.

## answer_c
Frusemide

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Frusemide relieves congestion but has no direct cardioprotective anti-adrenergic action.

## answer_d
Bisoprolol

## explanation_d
Correct. Chronic heart failure activates the sympathetic nervous system, and sustained catecholamine exposure is cardiotoxic (remodeling, arrhythmia, apoptosis). A beta blocker such as BISOPROLOL blocks this overactivation, and evidence-based beta blockers (bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate) reduce mortality in HFrEF. Blocking the cardiotoxic sympathetic drive is exactly this protective effect.
