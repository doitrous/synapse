## title
Exchange of nuclear material between two protozoa

## question
Exchange of nuclear materials between two protozoa is called:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Syngamy

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Syngamy is the fusion of two gametes to form a zygote, not the exchange of nuclear material between two organisms.

## answer_b
Conjugation

## explanation_b
Correct. Conjugation is a form of sexual reproduction in ciliate protozoa (such as Balantidium coli) in which two organisms temporarily join and exchange micronuclear (nuclear) material before separating. It is the reciprocal transfer of nuclear material that defines the process. This distinguishes conjugation from the other listed processes.

## answer_c
Gamogony

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gamogony is the formation of gametes, not the exchange of nuclear material.

## answer_d
Budding

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Budding is an asexual mode of reproduction and involves no exchange of nuclear material.

## title
Haemolytic anaemia in pathogenesis

## question
Haemolytic anaemia is involved in the pathogenesis of:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Malaria

## explanation_a
Correct. Malaria produces anaemia chiefly through haemolysis: intra-erythrocytic Plasmodium schizonts rupture infected red cells at the end of each erythrocytic cycle, and immune and splenic clearance also destroys uninfected cells. The result is a haemolytic anaemia. This is the mechanism of malarial anaemia.

Hookworm infection

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hookworm causes a chronic iron-deficiency anaemia from intestinal blood loss, not a haemolytic anaemia.

## answer_c
Schistosomiasis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Schistosomiasis causes anaemia mainly through chronic blood loss and inflammation, not haemolysis.

## answer_d
Diphyllobothriasis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Diphyllobothrium latum causes a megaloblastic (vitamin B12-deficiency) anaemia, not a haemolytic one.

True statement on complications of malaria

## question
As regards complications of malaria, the following is true:

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Blackwater fever is the result of cytoadherence of RBCs

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Blackwater fever results from massive intravascular haemolysis of red cells, not from cytoadherence.

## answer_b
Infection with Plasmodium falciparum may end up with multiorgan failure

## explanation_b
Correct. Plasmodium falciparum causes severe, complicated malaria and can progress to multiorgan failure because its infected red cells cytoadhere and sequester in the microvasculature of the brain, kidney and lung. ## explanation_c
Incorrect. Nephrotic syndrome is a complication of chronic Plasmodium malariae infection, not of P. vivax.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hyper-reactive malarial splenomegaly is associated with elevated serum IgM, not IgG.

True statement on in vitro culture methods

## question
As regards in vitro culture methods, the following is true:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

It reveals amastigotes of Leishmania

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Culture of Leishmania (for example on NNN medium) yields promastigotes, the flagellated insect-stage form, not amastigotes.

## answer_b
Stool culture for 48 hours yields filariform larvae of Strongyloides

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Filariform larvae of Strongyloides develop in faecal culture (Harada-Mori) but over about a week, not reliably in 48 hours, and this is not the true statement.

Culture on NNN medium reveals trypomastigotes of Trypanosoma cruzi

Culture on modified Diamond's medium yields trophozoites of Trichomonas vaginalis

## explanation_d
Correct. In-vitro culture on modified Diamond's medium is used to grow Trichomonas vaginalis, which appears as motile trophozoites. Diamond's medium is the standard culture system for this flagellate. Culture yielding Trichomonas trophozoites is therefore the true statement.

Cause of high IgM in Trypanosoma brucei infection

High level of IgM in Trypanosoma brucei infection is due to:

inf

Antigenic variation

African trypanosomes repeatedly change their variant surface glycoprotein coat by antigenic variation, so the host mounts wave after wave of new antibody responses, producing the very high polyclonal IgM levels characteristic of sleeping sickness. ## title
When malaria shows no relapse

## question
Malaria infection with no relapse occurs when:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Transmission is transplacental

## explanation_a
Correct. Relapse is caused by dormant liver hypnozoites of Plasmodium vivax and ovale, which form only when sporozoites are inoculated by a mosquito bite. In transplacental (congenital) malaria the fetus receives blood-stage parasites directly across the placenta, so no liver stage and no hypnozoites are established, and the infection cannot relapse. Transplacental transmission therefore gives malaria with no relapse.

## answer_b
Patients are immunosuppressed

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Immunosuppression does not remove hypnozoites and is not the reason for absence of relapse.

## answer_c
Treatment is incomplete

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Incomplete treatment tends to permit recrudescence or relapse, not to abolish relapse.

## answer_d
There's mixed infection with different species

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mixed-species infection does not explain the absence of relapse.

## title
Finding that confirms babesiosis

## question
To confirm diagnosis of babesiosis, we should detect:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ring stages in RBCs

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ring stages in red cells occur in Babesia but closely resemble Plasmodium falciparum rings, so they are not confirmatory.

## answer_b
Maltese cross form in RBCs

## explanation_b
Correct. Babesia multiplies inside red cells and can form a tetrad of four merozoites arranged as a Maltese cross, which is pathognomonic and confirms babesiosis on a stained blood film. This tetrad distinguishes Babesia from the ring-only forms of Plasmodium. Detecting the Maltese cross therefore confirms the diagnosis.

## answer_c
Sporozoites in RBCs

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Babesia does not present diagnostic sporozoites within red cells.

## answer_d
Merozoites in liver cell

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Babesia has no exo-erythrocytic liver stage, so liver-cell merozoites are not seen.

Which of the following statements is true regarding Acanthamoeba keratitis?

It therefore does not depend on a human host to complete its life cycle. This is the true statement.
