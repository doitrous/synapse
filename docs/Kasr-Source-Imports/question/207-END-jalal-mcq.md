## title
Opening of the prostatic utricle

## question
Regarding the prostatic urethra, all of the following statements are correct EXCEPT:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It continues directly into the membranous urethra.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this statement is true). The prostatic urethra runs down through the prostate and continues directly into the membranous urethra as it pierces the perineal membrane.

## answer_b
It is the widest part of the male urethra.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this statement is true). The prostatic urethra is the widest and most dilatable part of the male urethra.

## answer_c
Its anterior wall carries the opening of the prostatic utricle.

## explanation_c
Correct. This is the FALSE statement, so it is the answer to the 'except' stem. The prostatic utricle and the ejaculatory ducts open on the POSTERIOR wall of the prostatic urethra, on the summit of the urethral crest (the colliculus seminalis / verumontanum), not on the anterior wall. The anterior wall is featureless.

## answer_d
Its posterior wall carries the urethral crest.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this statement is true). The urethral crest is a median ridge on the posterior wall of the prostatic urethra; the prostatic utricle and ejaculatory ducts open on it at the colliculus.

## subject

## title
Nerve supplying the external urethral sphincter

## question
A 68-year-old man developed urinary incontinence after radical prostatectomy because of paralysis of the external urethral sphincter. Which nerve was injured during the operation?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Pelvic splanchnic nerve.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The pelvic splanchnic nerves are parasympathetic to the detrusor and do not supply the voluntary external sphincter.

## answer_b
Sacral splanchnic nerve.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The sacral splanchnic nerves are sympathetic and supply the internal (involuntary) sphincter, not the external sphincter.

## answer_c
Pudendal nerve.

## explanation_c
Correct. The external urethral sphincter (sphincter urethrae) is voluntary skeletal muscle supplied by the pudendal nerve (S2-S4), largely through its perineal branch. Injury to it during radical prostatectomy causes stress urinary incontinence.

## answer_d
Inferior gluteal nerve.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The inferior gluteal nerve supplies gluteus maximus and has no role in urethral sphincter control.
