## title
Secretions of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei

## question
The supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei secrete all the following EXCEPT:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Oxytocin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Oxytocin is one of the two hormones synthesised by the magnocellular neurons of these nuclei, so it is not the exception.

## answer_b
Vasopressin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone) is the second hormone made by these nuclei and transported to the pars nervosa, so it is not the exception.

## answer_c
Endorphin

## explanation_c
Correct. The magnocellular neurons of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei synthesise only oxytocin and vasopressin, which pass down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract to be stored in Herring bodies of the pars nervosa. Beta-endorphin is an opioid peptide cleaved from pro-opiomelanocortin in anterior-pituitary corticotrophs and arcuate neurons, not a product of these magnocellular nuclei, so it is the exception.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Because endorphin is genuinely not a product of these nuclei, there is a valid exception, so 'none of the above' cannot be selected.

Correct. Pituicytes are branched glial cells, related to astrocytes, and are the only resident cell of the pars nervosa. Incorrect. Vasopressin is synthesised by hypothalamic magnocellular neurons and only stored in the pars nervosa; pituicytes do not produce it.

## answer_d
Produce prolactin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prolactin is a product of the acidophil mammotrophs of the pars distalis, not of pituicytes.

## title
Contents of the pars nervosa

## question
The pars nervosa contains all the following EXCEPT:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Nerve cells

## explanation_a
Correct. The pars nervosa is made of the unmyelinated axons of hypothalamic magnocellular neurons, their Herring-body storage terminals, glial pituicytes and fenestrated capillaries. The neuron cell bodies lie in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus, so the pars nervosa itself contains no nerve cell bodies, making this the exception.

## answer_b
Pituicytes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pituicytes are the resident glial cells of the pars nervosa, so they are present.

## answer_c
Herring bodies

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Herring bodies are the dilated axon terminals that store oxytocin and vasopressin in the pars nervosa, so they are present.

## answer_d
Fenestrated blood capillaries

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pars nervosa is supplied by fenestrated capillaries that receive the released hormones, so they are present.

## question

## subject
endo

## status

Acidophils of the pars distalis

## explanation_a
Correct. Orange G is an acid dye taken up by the acidophils of the pars distalis (somatotrophs and mammotrophs), which appear orange. Orange-G positivity is therefore a selective marker of the acidophil population.

## answer_b
Basophils of the pars distalis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Basophils are instead PAS-positive because of the carbohydrate of their glycoprotein hormones; they are not stained by orange G.

## answer_c
Chromophobes of the pars distalis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Chromophobes are poorly granulated cells with little staining affinity and do not take up orange G.

## answer_d
Pituicytes of the pars nervosa

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pituicytes are glial cells of the pars nervosa and are not stained by orange G.
