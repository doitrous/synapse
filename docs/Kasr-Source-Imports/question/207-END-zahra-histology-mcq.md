Secretions of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei

## question
The supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei secrete all the following EXCEPT:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Oxytocin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Oxytocin is one of the two hormones synthesised by the magnocellular neurons of these nuclei, so it is not the exception.

## answer_b
Vasopressin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone) is the second hormone made by these nuclei and transported to the pars nervosa, so it is not the exception.

## answer_c
Endorphin

## explanation_c
Correct. The magnocellular neurons of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei synthesise only oxytocin and vasopressin, which pass down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract to be stored in Herring bodies of the pars nervosa. Beta-endorphin is an opioid peptide cleaved from pro-opiomelanocortin in anterior-pituitary corticotrophs and arcuate neurons, not a product of these magnocellular nuclei, so it is the exception.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Because endorphin is genuinely not a product of these nuclei, there is a valid exception, so 'none of the above' cannot be selected.

## title
Role of lysosomes in thyroid follicular cells

## question
In follicular cells of the thyroid gland, lysosomes are essential because their enzymes carry out:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Synthesis of thyroglobulin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Thyroglobulin is synthesised on the rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, not by lysosomes.

## answer_b
Iodination of thyroglobulin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Iodination of thyroglobulin occurs extracellularly at the apical membrane in the follicular lumen (colloid), catalysed by thyroid peroxidase, not by lysosomal enzymes.

## answer_c
Hydrolysis of thyroglobulin

## explanation_c
Correct. To release hormone, follicular cells endocytose iodinated thyroglobulin from the colloid, and the colloid droplets fuse with lysosomes whose proteolytic enzymes hydrolyse thyroglobulin. This proteolysis liberates free T3 and T4, which then diffuse into the blood, so lysosomes are essential for hormone release.

## answer_d
Iodide trapping

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Iodide trapping is performed by the basal sodium-iodide symporter of the follicular cell membrane, not by lysosomes.

Regulation and nature of mineralocorticoids

## question
Mineralocorticoids are:

## subject

## answer_a
Regulated by angiotensin II

## explanation_a
Correct. Mineralocorticoids, chiefly aldosterone, are steroid hormones made in the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal cortex. Their secretion is controlled mainly by the renin-angiotensin system, in which angiotensin II stimulates the glomerulosa to release aldosterone. A rise in plasma potassium acts as a second stimulus, whereas ACTH plays only a minor permissive role here.

## answer_b
Secreted from the zona fasciculata

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Mineralocorticoids are secreted from the zona glomerulosa; the zona fasciculata secretes glucocorticoids.

## answer_c
Regulating carbohydrate metabolism

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Regulation of carbohydrate metabolism is a glucocorticoid (cortisol) action; mineralocorticoids regulate sodium, potassium and water balance.

## answer_d
Protein in nature

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mineralocorticoids are steroid hormones derived from cholesterol, not proteins.

## answer_a
Chromaffin cells

## explanation_a
Correct. Paraganglia are small collections of chromaffin cells located outside the adrenal medulla, alongside the sympathetic chain and autonomic plexuses. A well-known example is the para-aortic bodies of Zuckerkandl. Zona reticularis cells

## answer_c
Zona fasciculata cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Zona fasciculata cells are cortical glucocorticoid-secreting cells, not chromaffin cells.

## answer_d
Zona glomerulosa cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Zona glomerulosa cells are cortical mineralocorticoid-secreting cells, not chromaffin cells.

Brain sand (corpora arenacea) of the pineal gland:

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Corpora arenacea are a normal, age-related accumulation of calcified concretions, not a marker of pineal disease.

## answer_b
Serves as a landmark for radiologists

## explanation_b
Correct. Corpora arenacea are calcified, radio-opaque concretions that accumulate in the midline pineal gland. Because they show up on skull radiographs and CT, they serve as a useful landmark. A shift of this calcified pineal from the midline signals a space-occupying lesion, which is the classic clinical use.

## answer_c
Is intensely acidophilic

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Corpora arenacea are calcified (mineralised) concretions that stain with haematoxylin (basophilic), not intensely acidophilic.

## answer_d
Decreases in size throughout life

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Brain sand accumulates and increases with age rather than decreasing throughout life.
