## title
Nature of the zona pellucida

## question
Regarding the zona pellucida, which of the following is correct?

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It is formed mainly by the oocyte (with the granulosa cells)

## explanation_a
Incorrect as a sole answer. The oocyte (together with the granulosa cells) does form the zona pellucida, but this is only one of several true statements.

## answer_b
It is formed of glycoprotein

## explanation_b
Incorrect as a sole answer. The zona pellucida is indeed a glycoprotein coat (ZP1-3), but this alone is not the complete answer.

## answer_c
It is traversed by microvilli projecting from the oocyte

## explanation_c
Incorrect as a sole answer. Oocyte microvilli do traverse the zona pellucida, but this is only one true statement.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. The zona pellucida is a thick glycoprotein coat between the oocyte and the follicular cells, synthesised by both the oocyte and the granulosa cells and traversed by oocyte microvilli and corona radiata processes. All three statements are therefore true, which makes 'all of the above' the single best answer.

## answer_e
None of the above

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Because statements A, B and C are all true, 'none of the above' cannot be selected.

## title
Follicular cells of the unilaminar primary follicle

## question
Unilaminar primary follicles are:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Present at birth as the only pre-pubertal follicle

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The follicle present at birth and before puberty is the primordial follicle, which has flattened follicular cells; the unilaminar primary follicle forms when those cells become cuboidal.

## answer_b
Developing at puberty under the effect of LH

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Early follicle growth to the primary stage is largely gonadotropin-independent; it is not driven by LH.

## answer_c
Containing a secondary oocyte with a haploid chromosome number

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The primary follicle contains a primary oocyte arrested in meiosis I (diploid), not a haploid secondary oocyte.

## answer_d
Surrounded by a single layer of cuboidal follicular cells

## explanation_d
Correct. The unilaminar primary follicle is defined histologically by a single layer of cuboidal follicular (granulosa) cells around the primary oocyte, in contrast to the flat cells of the primordial follicle. The zona pellucida also begins to appear at this stage. The change from flat to cuboidal cells marks the primordial-to-primary transition.

Feature absent from the primary follicle

## question
The following structures are characteristic of a primary follicle EXCEPT:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

B

## answer_a
Layers of granulosa cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Multilaminar primary follicles have several layers of granulosa cells, so this is a genuine feature.

## answer_b
An antrum

## explanation_b
Correct. The antrum is a fluid-filled cavity formed by coalescing follicular fluid (liquor folliculi) and it first appears in the secondary (antral) follicle. The primary follicle has granulosa layers, a zona pellucida and a developing theca but no antrum, so the antrum is the required exception.

Theca interna

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A theca interna differentiates around the multilaminar primary follicle, so it is a true feature.

## answer_d
Zona pellucida

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The zona pellucida appears in the primary follicle, so it is a true feature.

## title
Layer separating the oocyte from the cumulus cells

## question
In a secondary follicle, the oocyte is separated from the follicular cells of the cumulus oophorus by the:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Theca interna

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The theca interna is a vascular, steroid-producing layer outside the granulosa basement membrane, not the layer next to the oocyte.

## answer_b
Zona glomerulosa

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The zona glomerulosa is a zone of the adrenal cortex and has nothing to do with the ovarian follicle.

## answer_c
Zona pellucida

## explanation_c
Correct. The oocyte is immediately surrounded by the zona pellucida, which separates it from the innermost follicular cells of the corona radiata and cumulus oophorus. The corona cells send processes through the zona to reach the oocyte, but the intervening layer is the zona pellucida itself. This is why the zona is the structure between the oocyte and the follicular cells.

## answer_d
Theca externa

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The theca externa is the fibrous outer coat of the follicle, far from the oocyte.

## title
Origin of the corpus albicans

## question
Concerning the corpus albicans:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It appears in the fetal ovary

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The corpus albicans is not a fetal structure; it forms after ovulation and luteal regression in the reproductive-age ovary.

## answer_b
It develops from the corpus luteum

## explanation_b
Correct. When pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates and is replaced by fibroblasts and dense collagen, forming the white corpus albicans. It is therefore derived from the corpus luteum. Being an inactive scar, it secretes no hormones and is slowly resorbed.

## answer_c
It develops from growing follicles

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The corpus albicans arises from the regressed corpus luteum, not directly from growing follicles.

## answer_d
It secretes estrogen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The corpus albicans is fibrous scar tissue and has no secretory (estrogen-producing) function.
