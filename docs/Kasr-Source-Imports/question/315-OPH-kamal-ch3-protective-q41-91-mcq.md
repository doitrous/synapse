## title
Source of the tear-film mucin layer

## question
The mucin layer of the pre-corneal tear film is secreted by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The goblet cells

## explanation_a
Correct. The innermost mucin layer of the pre-corneal tear film is produced by the conjunctival goblet cells. Mucin lets the aqueous tears wet the hydrophobic corneal epithelium and keeps the film stable. Loss of goblet cells therefore causes mucin-deficient dry eye.

## answer_b
The lacrimal gland

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The lacrimal gland secretes the aqueous (middle) layer, not mucin.

## answer_c
The meibomian gland

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The meibomian glands secrete the outer lipid layer that retards evaporation.

## answer_d
The limbal stem cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Limbal stem cells renew the corneal epithelium; they do not secrete the tear film.

## title
False statement about the pre-corneal tear film

## question
All the following statements about the pre-corneal tear film are correct EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It provides nutrition to the cornea.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it is a true statement). The film supplies oxygen and nutrients to the avascular cornea.

## answer_b
It protects the cornea through its anti-bacterial effect.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it is a true statement). Tear lysozyme, lactoferrin and immunoglobulins give an anti-bacterial action.

## answer_c
It lubricates the corneal surface.

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it is a true statement). The film lubricates the ocular surface for smooth lid movement.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Correct. This is the false statement: the mucin layer comes from the goblet cells, so goblet-cell deficiency destabilises the tear film and produces mucin-deficient dry eye. The film is therefore very much affected by goblet-cell loss. Saying it is unaffected is wrong.

## title
Indication for levator resection in ptosis

## question
Levator resection operation should be used in:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Mild ptosis is usually corrected by a smaller procedure such as a Muller-muscle (Fasanella-Servat) resection.

## answer_b
Moderate ptosis with good levator function

## explanation_b
Correct. Levator resection shortens the levator, so it needs useful residual levator action and is the operation of choice for moderate ptosis with good levator function. The amount of resection is matched to the degree of ptosis and the levator power. This is the classic indication.

Moderate ptosis with poor levator function

## explanation_c
Incorrect. With poor levator function a resection gives an unpredictable result.

## answer_d
Severe ptosis with poor levator function

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Severe ptosis with poor levator function is treated by a frontalis (brow) sling, not levator resection.

Incorrect. Muller's muscle only fine-tunes lid elevation; its loss causes slight ptosis, not lagophthalmos.

Frontalis suspension is done in mild cases.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Frontalis suspension is reserved for severe ptosis with poor levator function, not mild cases.
