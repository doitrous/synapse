## title
Pro-opiomelanocortin is the precursor of ACTH

## question
Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) is the precursor of:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)

## explanation_a
Correct. Pro-opiomelanocortin is a large precursor peptide made by the corticotrophs of the anterior pituitary. Post-translational cleavage of POMC yields ACTH together with melanocyte-stimulating hormone, beta-lipotropin and beta-endorphin, so ACTH is the pituitary hormone derived from POMC.

## answer_b
Cortisol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cortisol is a steroid synthesised from cholesterol by the adrenal zona fasciculata under the drive of ACTH; it is not a peptide and is not cleaved from POMC.

## answer_c
Growth hormone

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Growth hormone is a single-chain polypeptide transcribed from its own gene in the somatotrophs; it is not part of the POMC family.

## answer_d
Prolactin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prolactin is a distinct single-chain polypeptide made by the mammotrophs and is unrelated to POMC.

## answer_e
Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)

## explanation_e
Incorrect. TSH is a glycoprotein of an alpha and a beta subunit encoded by separate genes; it is not derived from POMC.

## title
Growth hormone signals through the JAK-STAT pathway

## question
JAK-STAT pathways mediate the effects of:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Thyroid hormones are lipophilic and act on nuclear thyroid-hormone receptors that bind DNA response elements; they do not use a membrane JAK-STAT cascade.

## answer_b
Vasopressin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Vasopressin acts through G-protein-coupled receptors: V2 receptors raise cyclic AMP and V1 receptors raise intracellular calcium via phospholipase C, not JAK-STAT.

Incorrect. Glucagon works through a Gs-coupled receptor that raises cyclic AMP; it does not signal through JAK-STAT.

Growth hormone promotes protein synthesis via amino-acid transport

## question
Growth hormone effects include:

## subject
endo

## status

Growth by a direct action on the epiphyseal cartilage

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Growth hormone acts on the epiphyseal cartilage mainly indirectly, by inducing hepatic somatomedins (IGF-1) which drive chondrocyte proliferation; the epiphyseal effect is not a direct GH action.

## answer_b
Protein synthesis by increasing amino acid transport through cell membranes

## explanation_b
Correct. Growth hormone is anabolic for protein. It increases the active transport of amino acids into cells and stimulates their incorporation into protein while reducing protein catabolism. The net result is a positive nitrogen balance, which supports tissue growth.

Incorrect. Growth hormone is diabetogenic - it decreases cellular glucose utilisation and antagonises insulin, so it does not increase glucose uptake by cells.

Lipogenesis in adipose tissue

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Growth hormone is lipolytic, mobilising fatty acids from adipose tissue for energy; it promotes lipolysis, not lipogenesis.

## subject
endo

Incorrect. Somatomedins do feed back on growth-hormone secretion, but they inhibit it (by stimulating hypothalamic somatostatin and acting on the pituitary), rather than stimulate it.

Are insulin-like growth factors secreted by the hypothalamus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Somatomedins stimulate the synthesis of collagen and chondroitin sulphate by chondrocytes, promoting cartilage and bone growth; they do not decrease it.

## title
ADH inserts aquaporin-2 water channels in the renal tubule

## question
The functions of ADH include:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
V1 receptor activation to induce the production of prostaglandin E2

## explanation_a
Incorrect. V1 receptors act through phospholipase C to cause vascular smooth-muscle contraction; renal prostaglandin E2 actually opposes ADH, so PGE2 production is not an ADH effect.

## answer_b
Decreased potassium excretion in urine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. ADH regulates water rather than potassium handling; it does not reduce urinary potassium excretion (that is a feature of aldosterone-driven sodium-potassium exchange).

## answer_c
Insertion of aquaporin-2 channels into the distal renal tubules

## explanation_c
Correct. ADH binds V2 receptors on the basolateral membrane of collecting-duct principal cells, raising intracellular cyclic AMP. The cyclic AMP signal drives insertion of preformed aquaporin-2 water channels into the apical membrane. Water is then reabsorbed down the medullary osmotic gradient, so a concentrated urine is formed.

## answer_d
V2 receptor stimulation in pituitary corticotropes during stress

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The renal antidiuretic action is mediated by V2 receptors on the collecting duct, not on pituitary corticotropes; ADH does potentiate ACTH release, but through V1b (V3) receptors, not V2.

Cervical dilation stimulates oxytocin release (Ferguson reflex)

## subject

Incorrect. Milk ejection is the result of oxytocin action on breast myoepithelial cells, not a stimulus for its secretion; the stimulus is suckling.

Dilation of cervix

Correct. Stretch of the cervix and vagina during labour sends afferent signals to the hypothalamus that trigger oxytocin release. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. A rise in extracellular fluid volume suppresses ADH, and volume is not a physiological stimulus for oxytocin secretion.

Iodide trapping is secondary active transport

## question
Iodine is concentrated in thyroid follicular epithelial cells by:

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The iodide pump does not hydrolyse ATP directly, so it is not primary active transport; its energy comes indirectly from the sodium gradient.

Secondary active transport

## explanation_b
Correct. Iodide enters the follicular cell on the basolateral sodium-iodide symporter (NIS). Simple diffusion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Simple diffusion moves a solute down its gradient, so it cannot concentrate iodide against its electrochemical gradient.

## answer_d
Facilitated diffusion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Facilitated diffusion is also passive and cannot accumulate iodide against its gradient.

## title
Peripheral T4 to T3 conversion by type 1 deiodinase

## question
Most of the T3 in the liver is formed from circulating T4 by the action of:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Type 1 deiodinase (D1)

## explanation_a
Correct. In the liver, T3 is produced mainly by outer-ring (5'-) deiodination of T4 by type 1 deiodinase (D1). D1 is abundant in liver, kidney and thyroid and supplies most of the circulating T3. Because roughly four-fifths of plasma T3 comes from this peripheral conversion, T4 behaves largely as a prohormone.

## answer_b
Type 2 deiodinase (D2)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Type 2 deiodinase (D2) also generates T3 but acts intracellularly in tissues such as brain, pituitary and brown fat for local supply, not the bulk of plasma/hepatic T3.

Type 3 deiodinase (D3)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Type 3 deiodinase (D3) is an inner-ring deiodinase that inactivates T4 and T3 to reverse T3 and T2; it does not form active T3.

## answer_d
Type 4 deiodinase (D4)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. There is no type 4 deiodinase; only three deiodinase isoenzymes (D1, D2 and D3) exist.

## title
Only free (unbound) thyroxine is physiologically active

## question
Physiologically active thyroxine exists in which form?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Bound to albumin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Thyroxine bound to albumin is part of the transport reservoir and is biologically inert until released.

## answer_b
Bound to prealbumin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Thyroxine bound to prealbumin (transthyretin) is likewise a carried, inactive fraction.

## answer_c
Bound to globulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Thyroxine bound to thyroxine-binding globulin is the largest reservoir but is inactive while it remains bound.

Unbound

## explanation_d
Correct. Only the small unbound (free) fraction of thyroxine can cross cell membranes and reach the nuclear receptor, so the free hormone is the biologically active form. Protein-bound hormone is inert until it dissociates. This free-hormone principle is why free T4, not total T4, best reflects thyroid status.
