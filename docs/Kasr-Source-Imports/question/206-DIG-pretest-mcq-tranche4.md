## title
Correct statement about bile acids

## question
A 56-year-old man has postprandial diarrhoea since an ileal resection and the surgeon suspects bile-acid malabsorption. Which statement about bile acids is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
They are essentially water-insoluble

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bile salts are amphipathic and, especially when conjugated, quite water-soluble; that solubility is central to fat digestion.

## answer_b
The majority of bile acids is absorbed by passive diffusion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Most bile acids are reclaimed by active, sodium-dependent transport in the terminal ileum, not by passive diffusion.

## answer_c
Glycine conjugates are more soluble than taurine conjugates

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Taurine conjugates are more water-soluble than glycine conjugates, not the reverse.

## answer_d
The amount lost in the stool each day represents the daily loss of cholesterol

## explanation_d
Correct. Only small amounts of bile acids escape the enterohepatic circulation into the stool each day, but this faecal loss is the body's only route for eliminating cholesterol, from which bile acids are synthesised. Conserving bile acids is therefore tightly coupled to cholesterol balance. Interrupting their reabsorption (ileal resection) increases this loss and lowers plasma cholesterol.

## answer_e
The bile acid-dependent fraction of bile is stimulated by secretin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The bile acid-dependent fraction of bile is driven by the returning bile-acid pool, not by secretin, which stimulates the bicarbonate-rich (bile acid-independent) fraction.

## title
Feature distinguishing osmotic from secretory diarrhoea

## question
A 14-year-old ballerina has chronic diarrhoea that improves when she fasts; she drinks skim milk and denies laxatives. In contrast to secretory diarrhoea, which statement is correct about osmotic diarrhoea?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is characterised by an increase in the stool osmotic gap

## explanation_a
Correct. Osmotic diarrhoea occurs when poorly absorbed, osmotically active solutes draw water into the lumen, so the stool osmotic gap is increased. It characteristically ceases with fasting or on stopping the offending solute, as here. Common causes are lactase deficiency, magnesium salts and non-absorbable sugars.

## answer_b
It is the result of increased crypt cell secretion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Increased crypt-cell secretion of an isosmotic chloride solution is the mechanism of secretory, not osmotic, diarrhoea.

## answer_c
It is the result of decreased electroneutral sodium absorption

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Inhibited electroneutral NaCl absorption underlies secretory diarrhoea; the stool gap there is normal.

## answer_d
It is caused by bacterial toxins

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Bacterial toxins typically cause secretory diarrhoea, which does not stop with fasting.

## answer_e
It occurs only in the colon

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Osmotic diarrhoea arises from unabsorbed solute in the small intestine or colon, not the colon alone.

## title
Segment where short-chain fatty acids are absorbed

## question
An 83-year-old woman with constipation is given a high-fibre diet, raising production of short-chain fatty acids. Short-chain fatty acid absorption occurs almost exclusively from which segment of the gut?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Stomach

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The stomach neither generates nor absorbs short-chain fatty acids.

## answer_b
Duodenum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The duodenum is a site of digestion of dietary fat, not of colonic short-chain fatty acid absorption.

## answer_c
Jejunum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The jejunum absorbs digested nutrients but is not the site of short-chain fatty acid uptake.

## answer_d
Ileum

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The ileum reabsorbs bile salts and vitamin B12, not the bacterially generated short-chain fatty acids.

## answer_e
Colon

## explanation_e
Correct. Short-chain fatty acids are products of colonic bacterial fermentation of undigested carbohydrate, and the colon is the major site for both their generation and their absorption. They also exert trophic effects on the colonic mucosa and promote colonic sodium absorption.

## title
Correct statement about GI water and electrolyte absorption

## question
A patient has vomiting and severe watery diarrhoea after spoiled shellfish and stool is positive for Vibrio cholerae. Which statement best describes water and electrolyte absorption in the gut?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Most water and electrolytes come from ingested fluids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Most of the 9-12 litres handled daily comes from gastrointestinal secretions, not ingested fluid.

## answer_b
The small intestine and colon have similar absorptive capacities

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The small intestine absorbs far more fluid than the colon, whose capacity is limited to a few litres per day.

## answer_c
Osmotic equilibration of chyme occurs in the stomach

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Osmotic equilibration of chyme occurs in the duodenum, not the stomach.

## answer_d
The majority of absorption occurs in the jejunum

## explanation_d
Correct. Most water and electrolyte absorption occurs in the jejunum, with the duodenum equilibrating chyme osmotically. Water follows passively the active absorption of sodium. The colon reclaims only a small remaining fraction.

## answer_e
Cholera toxin inhibits sodium-coupled nutrient transport

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cholera toxin drives secretion by raising cyclic AMP; crucially it does not impair sodium-coupled glucose transport, which is why oral rehydration therapy works.

## title
Mechanism of symptoms in dumping syndrome

## question
After a jejunum-to-stomach anastomosis a patient has post-meal weakness, dizziness and sweating. The symptoms of dumping syndrome are caused in part by which of the following?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Increased blood pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Blood pressure tends to fall, not rise, as fluid shifts into the gut.

## answer_b
Increased secretion of glucagon

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glucagon excess is not the mechanism of dumping symptoms.

## answer_c
Increased secretion of cholecystokinin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cholecystokinin release is not the cause of the weakness, dizziness and sweating.

## answer_d
Hypoglycaemia

## explanation_d
Correct. When the stomach's reservoir and control of emptying are lost, a hypertonic meal enters the intestine rapidly. This drives a large insulin surge that produces reactive hypoglycaemia, causing weakness, dizziness and sweating. Rapid osmotic movement of water into the gut also causes hypovolaemia and hypotension.

## answer_e
Hyperglycaemia

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Although an early glucose spike can occur, the symptomatic component highlighted here is the subsequent reactive hypoglycaemia, not sustained hyperglycaemia.

## title
Major mechanism of sodium absorption in the small intestine

## question
Which of the following is the major mechanism for absorption of sodium from the small intestine?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Na+-H+ exchange alone

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Na+-H+ exchange is one component but, on its own, is not the major mechanism named here.

## answer_b
Cotransport with potassium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sodium is not absorbed by cotransport with potassium in the small intestine.

## answer_c
Electrogenic transport

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Electrogenic sodium transport predominates in the colon, not as the major small-intestinal route.

## answer_d
Neutral NaCl absorption

## explanation_d
Correct. Sodium absorption is the primary absorptive event of the small intestine and drives the uptake of water and other electrolytes. Its major mechanism is neutral NaCl absorption, coupling Na+-H+ exchange with Cl--HCO3- exchange so that no net charge moves. Nutrient-coupled and other pathways contribute additionally.

## answer_e
Solvent drag

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Solvent drag carries some sodium with absorbed water but is not the major mechanism.

## title
Major protection of the duodenal mucosa from gastric acid

## question
A 33-year-old woman on large doses of NSAIDs has burning epigastric pain that improves after meals, suggesting a duodenal ulcer. What is the major factor protecting the duodenal mucosa from damage by gastric acid?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Pancreatic bicarbonate secretion

## explanation_a
Correct. Unlike the stomach, the duodenal mucosa has little endogenous barrier of its own, so it depends on neutralisation of the acid delivered to it. Acid entering the duodenum releases secretin, which drives pancreatic bicarbonate secretion; this pancreatic output matches gastric acid output and neutralises it. It is therefore the major protective factor.

## answer_b
The endogenous mucosal barrier of the duodenum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The duodenum lacks the substantial endogenous mucosal barrier that protects the stomach, so this is not the major factor.

## answer_c
Duodenal bicarbonate secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Duodenal (Brunner gland) bicarbonate contributes but is minor compared with the large pancreatic bicarbonate output.

## answer_d
Hepatic bicarbonate secretion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The liver's bicarbonate contribution is small and not the principal neutraliser.

## answer_e
Bicarbonate contained in bile

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Bile contains some bicarbonate but not in amounts sufficient to neutralise gastric acid.
