## title
Commonest source of laboratory errors

## question
Most lab errors are due to:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Pre-analytical errors

## explanation_a
Correct. The great majority of laboratory errors arise in the pre-analytical phase — everything that happens before the sample is actually measured, such as test ordering, patient identification, sample collection, labelling and transport. Studies consistently attribute roughly two-thirds of all lab errors to this stage, because it involves many manual, human-dependent steps. Reducing pre-analytical error (correct identification, correct tube, correct handling) therefore yields the biggest improvement in laboratory safety.

## answer_b
Analytical errors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Analytical errors — those occurring during the actual measurement on the analyser — are now relatively uncommon because of automation and internal quality control.

## answer_c
Post-analytical errors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Post-analytical errors (result transcription, reporting, interpretation) are a real but smaller contributor than the pre-analytical phase.

## answer_d
Samples mixed up

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mixing up samples is one specific example of a pre-analytical error, not the overarching category the question asks for.

Incorrect. Including the medication's purpose (indication) improves safety and is encouraged, not prohibited.

Incorrect. Orders should always consider patient-specific information such as weight, renal function and allergies.
