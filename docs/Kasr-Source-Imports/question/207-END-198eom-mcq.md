## title
Duration of the indifferent gonad stage

## question
The sex gland is called "the indifferent gonad" until the:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
7th week.

## explanation_a
Correct. The early gonad forms from the genital ridge as a sexually undifferentiated (indifferent) structure with both a cortex and a medulla. It remains morphologically identical in male and female embryos until about the 7th week, when the SRY gene (in the presence of a Y chromosome) drives medullary development into a testis, and in its absence the cortex develops into an ovary a little later. Before the 7th week the gonad cannot be told apart histologically as male or female.

## answer_b
9th week.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. By the 9th week sexual differentiation of the gonad has already begun; the indifferent stage ends earlier, around the 7th week.

## answer_c
12th week.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The 12th week is far too late; testicular cords and early ovarian differentiation are established well before this.

## answer_d
15th week.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. By the 15th week the external genitalia are also differentiating; the gonad is long past its indifferent stage.

## title
Adult growth-hormone-secreting tumour (acromegaly)

## question
A 34-year-old woman who develops a growth-hormone-secreting tumour:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

C

## answer_a
May increase in length to reach 2 metres or more.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. In an adult the epiphyseal plates are already fused, so linear (long-bone) growth cannot resume; gigantism only occurs when excess growth hormone acts before epiphyseal closure.

## answer_b
Has decreased secretion of 4-hydroxyproline in the urine.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Growth hormone stimulates collagen turnover, so urinary hydroxyproline is increased, not decreased.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Correct. The feet broaden, so the patient characteristically needs progressively larger shoes and gloves. This acral enlargement, not increased height, is the hallmark of adult growth-hormone excess.

## answer_d
May become hypoglycaemic.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Growth hormone is a counter-regulatory, diabetogenic hormone; it raises blood glucose and tends to cause hyperglycaemia, not hypoglycaemia.

High TSH with low T4

## title
Hypocalcaemia and tetany after thyroid surgery

## question
A patient presents with hypocalcaemia and tetany after thyroid surgery. What is the expected cause?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hyperthyroidism.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hyperthyroidism does not cause hypocalcaemic tetany; if anything thyroid hormone excess tends to raise, not lower, plasma calcium.

## answer_b
Parathyroid removal.

## explanation_b
Correct. The parathyroid glands lie against the posterior surface of the thyroid, so thyroidectomy can inadvertently remove or devascularise them. Loss of parathyroid hormone removes the main defence against a falling plasma calcium, so ionised calcium drops. The resulting hypocalcaemia increases neuromuscular excitability and produces tetany (carpopedal spasm, Chvostek and Trousseau signs).

## answer_c
Vitamin D excess.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Vitamin D excess raises intestinal calcium absorption and causes hypercalcaemia, the opposite of this picture.

## answer_d
Calcitonin excess.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although calcitonin lowers calcium, physiological or even tumour calcitonin excess rarely produces symptomatic hypocalcaemia; the temporal link to thyroid surgery points to parathyroid loss.

## subject

## title
Why T3 is more active than T4

## question
T3 is more active than T4 because:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is more abundant.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. T4 is actually the more abundant secretory product of the thyroid; T3 is present in smaller amounts, much of it formed peripherally by deiodination of T4.

## answer_b
It has higher affinity to receptors.

## explanation_b
Correct. T3 binds the nuclear thyroid-hormone receptor with roughly ten times the affinity of T4, so it drives gene transcription far more effectively. Because the receptor is what mediates thyroid-hormone action, this higher receptor affinity makes T3 the metabolically more potent (active) hormone. T4 largely serves as a circulating prohormone that is converted to T3 in the tissues.

## answer_c
It is more bound to plasma proteins.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. T3 is in fact less tightly bound to plasma proteins than T4; greater protein binding would reduce, not increase, the active free fraction.

## answer_d
It is stored longer.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. T4 has the longer plasma half-life and larger stored/bound pool; longer storage does not explain the greater biological activity of T3.

## subject

Incorrect. The hypermetabolic state increases energy expenditure and, despite a good appetite, leads to weight loss.

## title
Insulin and IGF-1 share a receptor

## question
Regarding insulin hormone, which statement is correct?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Insulin release from beta cells is decreased by inactivating ATP-sensitive K+ channels.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Closing (inactivating) the ATP-sensitive K+ channel depolarises the beta cell and increases insulin release; it does not decrease it.

## answer_b
Leptin stimulates insulin release.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Leptin inhibits insulin secretion (the adipo-insular axis); it does not stimulate it.

## answer_c
Insulin facilitates glucose entry into the renal tubules by increasing the number of GLUT4.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Renal tubular glucose handling uses insulin-independent transporters (SGLT and GLUT2); insulin does not recruit GLUT4 into renal tubular cells.

## answer_d
Insulin and IGF-1 can bind to the same receptor.

## explanation_d
Correct. Insulin and insulin-like growth factor-1 are structurally homologous, and their receptors are closely related tyrosine-kinase receptors. Because of this homology the two ligands show cross-reactivity, and insulin can bind and weakly activate the IGF-1 receptor (and vice versa). This shared, overlapping receptor binding underlies some of the growth-promoting actions seen when insulin is present at high concentrations.

## title
Fruity breath with deep rapid breathing

## question
An unconscious patient has rapid deep breathing and a fruity breath odour. The most likely explanation is:

## subject

## status

This combination in an unconscious patient points to ketoacidosis.

Incorrect. Adrenal (Addisonian) crisis presents with hypotension, hyponatraemia and shock rather than Kussmaul breathing with a fruity smell.

Hormone deficient in the anaemia of chronic kidney disease

## question
A patient with chronic kidney disease develops anaemia. Which hormone is deficient?

## subject

## status

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Calcitonin is a thyroid C-cell hormone that lowers plasma calcium; it has no role in red-cell production.

## answer_b
Erythropoietin.

## explanation_b
Correct. Erythropoietin is a glycoprotein hormone produced mainly by peritubular interstitial (fibroblast-like) cells of the renal cortex in response to hypoxia. It stimulates erythroid progenitors in the bone marrow to proliferate and mature. In chronic kidney disease the loss of functioning renal tissue reduces erythropoietin output, so red-cell production falls and a normocytic normochromic anaemia develops.

Cortisol.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cortisol is an adrenocortical glucocorticoid; its deficiency causes Addison's disease, not the anaemia specific to renal failure.

## answer_d
Insulin.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Insulin is a pancreatic hormone regulating glucose metabolism and is not responsible for the anaemia of kidney disease.

Incorrect. LH is also an anterior pituitary gonadotropin; it acts on the theca interna but is not secreted by it.

The mammary gland is classified as being a:

## subject
gyn

## status

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Branched alveolar exocrine gland.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The mammary gland has a branching duct system draining many lobules, so it is compound (not simple branched) and its secretory units are tubuloalveolar rather than purely alveolar.

## answer_b
Compound tubuloalveolar apocrine gland.

## explanation_b
Correct. The mammary gland is a compound gland: multiple lobules of secretory tubuloalveolar units drain through a branching duct system to the nipple. Its secretory mode is classically described as apocrine for the lipid component, in which the apical cytoplasm containing the fat droplet is pinched off with the secretion. This makes it a compound tubuloalveolar apocrine gland.

## answer_c
Branched tubuloalveolar holocrine gland.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Holocrine secretion (whole-cell disintegration, as in sebaceous glands) does not describe the mammary gland.

## answer_d
Compound tubuloalveolar merocrine gland.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although the protein (milk) component is released by merocrine exocytosis, the gland's characteristic classification includes its apocrine lipid secretion, so "merocrine" alone is not the intended answer.

Incorrect. Fibrinolysin, which reliquefies coagulated semen, is a prostatic product, not a bulbourethral one.

Incorrect. Beating to move the ovum is the job of the ciliated cells; peg cells are secretory and non-ciliated.

Rete testis.
