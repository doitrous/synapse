## title
Venous drainage of the thyroid gland

## question
Regarding the thyroid gland, which of the following statements is correct?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Its isthmus lies on the 3rd to 5th tracheal rings.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The isthmus lies in front of the 2nd, 3rd and 4th tracheal rings, not the 3rd to 5th.

## answer_b
Its lobes are related medially to the carotid sheath.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The lobes are related posterolaterally, not medially, to the carotid sheath; medially they relate to the trachea, oesophagus and larynx.

## answer_c
Its upper pole is related to the superior laryngeal nerve.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The upper pole and superior thyroid artery are related to the external laryngeal nerve; the superior laryngeal nerve lies higher and more medially.

## answer_d
It is drained by three pairs of veins.

## explanation_d
Correct. The thyroid gland is drained by three pairs of veins — superior, middle and inferior thyroid veins. The superior and middle thyroid veins drain into the internal jugular vein, while the inferior thyroid veins descend in front of the trachea to the brachiocephalic veins. Three paired veins therefore carry its venous blood.

## title
Why the thyroid gland moves on swallowing

## question
Regarding the thyroid gland, which of the following statements is correct?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is enclosed by the investing layer of deep fascia.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The false capsule of the gland is derived from the pretracheal, not the investing, layer of deep cervical fascia.

## answer_b
It moves up and down with swallowing.

## explanation_b
Correct. The thyroid gland is enclosed by the pretracheal fascia, which binds it to the larynx and trachea, notably through the suspensory ligament of Berry. Because the larynx and trachea rise during deglutition, the gland moves up and down with swallowing. This mobility on swallowing is the classic sign that a midline neck swelling is thyroid in origin.

## answer_c
Its lobes extend up to the hyoid bone.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Each lobe extends upward only to the oblique line of the thyroid cartilage, not as high as the hyoid bone.

## answer_d
The external carotid artery is the only source of its arterial supply.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The gland is supplied by the superior thyroid artery (external carotid) and the inferior thyroid artery (thyrocervical trunk), sometimes with a thyroidea ima; the external carotid is not its only source.

## title
Hoarseness after partial thyroidectomy

## question
Following partial thyroidectomy, a patient complained of hoarseness of voice. This is due to injury of which of the following nerves?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Superior laryngeal nerve.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The superior laryngeal nerve divides into internal and external branches; injury to the whole trunk is uncommon and does not selectively produce cord-paralysis hoarseness.

## answer_b
Internal laryngeal nerve.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The internal laryngeal nerve is sensory to the mucosa above the cords; its injury causes loss of the cough reflex and aspiration risk, not hoarseness.

## answer_c
External laryngeal nerve.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. External laryngeal nerve injury weakens the cricothyroid and lowers voice pitch, but the cord is not paralysed, so the voice is not truly hoarse.

## answer_d
Recurrent laryngeal nerve.

## explanation_d
Correct. The recurrent laryngeal nerve runs in the tracheo-oesophageal groove behind the gland, close to the inferior thyroid artery, and supplies all intrinsic laryngeal muscles except the cricothyroid. Injury during thyroidectomy paralyses the vocal cord and produces hoarseness. It is the nerve classically injured in partial thyroidectomy.

## title
Relation of the superior mesenteric vessels to the uncinate process

## question
Regarding the pancreas, which of the following statements is correct?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The tail passes through the gastrosplenic ligament.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The tail runs in the splenorenal (lienorenal) ligament, not the gastrosplenic ligament.

## answer_b
The superior mesenteric vessels lie anterior to the uncinate process of the head.

## explanation_b
Correct. The uncinate process is a hook-like extension of the head that projects behind the superior mesenteric vessels. The superior mesenteric artery and vein therefore lie anterior to the uncinate process as they emerge from behind the neck into the mesentery. This relation is why an uncinate carcinoma can encase these vessels.

## answer_c
The left renal vein lies anterior to the body.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The body of the pancreas lies anterior to the left renal vein and left kidney; the vein is posterior, not anterior, to the body.

## answer_d
The pyloroduodenal junction lies behind the neck.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The neck lies in front of the beginning of the portal vein; the pyloroduodenal junction lies above and in front of the head, not behind the neck.

## title
Upper border of the body of the pancreas

## question
Regarding the pancreas, which one of the following statements is NOT correct?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The common bile duct lies deep to its head.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This statement is true: the common bile duct descends in a groove on, or a tunnel through, the posterior surface of the head, deep to it.

## answer_b
Its tail passes through the lienorenal ligament.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This statement is true: the tail is the mobile intraperitoneal part and lies within the lienorenal ligament with the splenic vessels.

## answer_c
The splenic vein lies along the upper border of its body.

## explanation_c
Correct. This is the incorrect statement and therefore the answer. It is the splenic ARTERY that runs a tortuous course along the upper border of the body of the pancreas; the splenic VEIN lies behind the body, below the artery, before joining the superior mesenteric vein to form the portal vein. Placing the splenic vein along the upper border is therefore wrong.

## answer_d
It is a retroperitoneal organ.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This statement is true: except for the tail, the pancreas is a retroperitoneal organ.

## title
Arterial supply of the uncinate process of the pancreas

## question
The arterial supply of the uncinate process of the pancreas is derived mainly from the:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Coeliac trunk.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The coeliac trunk supplies the upper head/neck indirectly through the gastroduodenal and superior pancreaticoduodenal arteries, not the uncinate process directly.

## answer_b
Splenic artery.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The splenic artery supplies the neck, body and tail, not the uncinate process.

## answer_c
Superior mesenteric artery.

## explanation_c
Correct. The uncinate process and lower head are supplied mainly by the inferior pancreaticoduodenal artery, a branch of the superior mesenteric artery. The upper head is supplied by the superior pancreaticoduodenal artery from the gastroduodenal branch of the coeliac trunk, and the two form an anastomotic arcade. Because the uncinate lies against the superior mesenteric vessels, its dominant supply is the SMA.

## answer_d
Inferior mesenteric artery.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The inferior mesenteric artery supplies the hindgut and has no pancreatic branches.

## title
Duodenal obstruction in annular pancreas

## question
A 4-year-old boy was admitted with severe vomiting; investigations revealed an annular pancreas. Which of the following structures is most typically obstructed in this condition?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Pylorus of the stomach.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The pylorus lies proximal to the ring and is not encircled by the annular tissue.

## answer_b
First part of the duodenum.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The first part (duodenal cap) lies above the ampullary level and the pancreatic ring.

## answer_c
Second part of the duodenum.

## explanation_c
Correct. In annular pancreas a ring of pancreatic tissue encircles the descending (second) part of the duodenum, at the site where the ventral bud normally rotates. Constriction of this segment produces duodenal obstruction with vomiting in infancy. The second part of the duodenum is therefore the classically obstructed site.

## answer_d
Third part of the duodenum.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The third part is crossed by the superior mesenteric vessels but is not the segment encircled by an annular pancreas.

## title
Embryological origin of the thyroid gland

## question
Regarding the development of the thyroid gland, choose the correct statement:

## subject
endo

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The thyroglossal duct remains patent after birth.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The thyroglossal duct normally atrophies and disappears in the fetus; persistence is abnormal and produces cysts or fistulae.

## answer_b
During its descent it passes behind the thyroid cartilage.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. During descent the gland passes in front of the hyoid and laryngeal cartilages, not behind them.

It is derived from the floor of the pharynx.

Correct. The thyroid gland develops from a median endodermal downgrowth of the floor of the primitive pharynx, at the site later marked by the foramen cecum of the tongue. This median thyroid diverticulum descends in the neck to its pretracheal position. Its follicular cells are therefore endodermal derivatives of the pharyngeal floor.

## answer_d
The thyroid follicles are mesodermal in origin.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The thyroid follicles are endodermal, not mesodermal, in origin.

## title
Anterior relation of the head of the pancreas

## question
Regarding the head of the pancreas, choose the correct statement:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

It lies in the left hypochondrium.

## explanation_a

It is related anteriorly to the transverse colon.

Correct. Incorrect. The pancreas is retroperitoneal, covered by peritoneum only on its anterior surface, not completely.

## answer_d
It is related posteriorly to loops of small intestine.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Posteriorly the head is related to the inferior vena cava, right renal vessels, bile duct and aorta, not to loops of small intestine.
