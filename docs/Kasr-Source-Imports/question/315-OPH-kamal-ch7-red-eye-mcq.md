False statement about viral conjunctivitis

## question
In viral conjunctivitis, all of the following are true, EXCEPT:

It is self-limiting, resolving in about 14 days

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true statement). Viral (adenoviral) conjunctivitis is self-limiting and resolves over about two weeks.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (a true statement). Viral conjunctivitis produces a follicular conjunctival reaction.

## title
Contraindicated drop in acute anterior uveitis

## question
A 37-year-old man presents with aching pain and blurred vision in the left eye for 5 days. Slit-lamp examination shows fine keratic precipitates, aqueous cells and flare. Which of the following medications is CONTRAINDICATED?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Topical corticosteroid eye drops

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Topical corticosteroids are a mainstay of treating anterior uveitis, to suppress the inflammation.

## answer_b
Topical pilocarpine 2% eye drops

## explanation_b
Correct. Pilocarpine, a miotic, is contraindicated in acute anterior uveitis: constricting the inflamed pupil promotes posterior synechiae and worsens pain and inflammation. The pupil should instead be dilated with a cycloplegic such as atropine to rest the ciliary body and prevent synechiae. So the miotic is the contraindicated drop.

## answer_c
Topical atropine 1% eye drops

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Atropine, a cycloplegic, is exactly what is indicated to dilate the pupil and prevent synechiae.

## answer_d
Topical timolol 0.5% eye drops

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Timolol may be added if the pressure rises, and is not contraindicated.

## title
Danger of topical steroids in a viral corneal ulcer

## question
Topical steroids are contraindicated in a case of viral corneal ulcer for fear of:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

C

Secondary uveitis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Secondary uveitis is not the feared consequence of steroids in a herpetic ulcer.

## answer_b
Cortical cataract

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cataract is a long-term steroid effect, not the acute danger in a viral ulcer.

Incorrect. Some scarring may occur, but the dangerous acute risk driving the contraindication is perforation.

## title
Best vital stain for a corneal ulcer

## question
The best vital stain to detect a corneal ulcer is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Fluorescein stain

## explanation_a
Correct. Fluorescein is the best vital stain to detect a corneal ulcer: it pools in and stains areas of denuded epithelium bright green under cobalt-blue light, outlining the ulcer and its dendritic or geographic shape. It is the standard bedside stain for an epithelial defect. So fluorescein is the answer.

## answer_b
Rose Bengal stain

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Rose Bengal stains devitalised cells and mucus (useful in dry eye), not the first choice for an ulcer.

## answer_c
Trypan blue stain

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Trypan blue is used to stain the anterior capsule during cataract surgery, not to detect an ulcer.

## answer_d
Lissamine green stain

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lissamine green stains devitalised cells like Rose Bengal, not the ulcer bed.

Spring catarrh (Vernal keratoconjunctivitis):

## subject
mul

Is usually seasonal type of allergic conjunctivitis and affects conjunctiva and cornea

Is usually symptoms

## explanation_d
Incorrect. As printed this option is incomplete, and vernal disease is not symptomless: it causes marked itching, photophobia and a stringy discharge.

## title
Acute viral conjunctivitis with subconjunctival haemorrhage

## question
Which statement is correct?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 30-year-old patient has a watery eye with scanty mucous discharge and a sudden subconjunctival haemorrhage in the right eye. He reports repeated attacks of herpes simplex.

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Herpes simplex and herpes zoster are the commonest cause of viral conjunctivitis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adenovirus, not herpes, is the commonest cause of viral conjunctivitis.

## answer_b
Acute subconjunctival haemorrhage may be a symptom of acute viral conjunctivitis

## explanation_b
Correct. Acute haemorrhagic viral conjunctivitis can present with a sudden subconjunctival haemorrhage together with a watery discharge. This is a recognised feature of the viral disease. So subconjunctival haemorrhage may be a symptom.

## answer_c
Involvement of the other eye will never occur

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Viral conjunctivitis is highly contagious and typically spreads to the second eye.

## answer_d
Topical and systemic antivirals are indicated for treatment

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adenoviral conjunctivitis is self-limiting and managed supportively; antivirals are not indicated.

## title
Acute purulent conjunctivitis in a child — complications

## question
Which statement is correct?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
An 11-year-old boy has had 4 days of irritation and redness in the left eye then the right, with a severe yellow discharge and lid swelling that sticks the eyelids together in the morning.

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Culture and sensitivity are done routinely before treatment

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Acute bacterial conjunctivitis is treated empirically with broad-spectrum antibiotics; culture is reserved for severe, hyperacute or unresponsive cases.

## answer_b
The commonest causative agent is adenovirus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The severe purulent discharge points to a bacterial cause, not adenovirus.

## answer_c
Treatment with steroids is usually effective

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Steroids are not the treatment and can worsen an infective conjunctivitis.

## answer_d
Secondary keratitis and corneal ulcers are common complications

## explanation_d
Correct. Neglected bacterial conjunctivitis can spread to the cornea, causing secondary keratitis and corneal ulceration. These corneal complications are the main danger of untreated disease. So secondary keratitis and corneal ulcers are recognised complications.
