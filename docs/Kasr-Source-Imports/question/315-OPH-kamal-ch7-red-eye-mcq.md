False statement about viral conjunctivitis

## question
In viral conjunctivitis, all of the following are true, EXCEPT:

It is self-limiting, resolving in about 14 days

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true statement). Viral (adenoviral) conjunctivitis is self-limiting and resolves over about two weeks.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (a true statement). Viral conjunctivitis produces a follicular conjunctival reaction.

## title
Contraindicated drop in acute anterior uveitis

## question
A 37-year-old man presents with aching pain and blurred vision in the left eye for 5 days. Slit-lamp examination shows fine keratic precipitates, aqueous cells and flare. Which of the following medications is CONTRAINDICATED?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Topical corticosteroid eye drops

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Topical corticosteroids are a mainstay of treating anterior uveitis, to suppress the inflammation.

## answer_b
Topical pilocarpine 2% eye drops

## explanation_b
Correct. Pilocarpine, a miotic, is contraindicated in acute anterior uveitis: constricting the inflamed pupil promotes posterior synechiae and worsens pain and inflammation. The pupil should instead be dilated with a cycloplegic such as atropine to rest the ciliary body and prevent synechiae. So the miotic is the contraindicated drop.

## answer_c
Topical atropine 1% eye drops

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Atropine, a cycloplegic, is exactly what is indicated to dilate the pupil and prevent synechiae.

## answer_d
Topical timolol 0.5% eye drops

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Timolol may be added if the pressure rises, and is not contraindicated.

## title
Danger of topical steroids in a viral corneal ulcer

## question
Topical steroids are contraindicated in a case of viral corneal ulcer for fear of:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

C

Secondary uveitis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Secondary uveitis is not the feared consequence of steroids in a herpetic ulcer.

## answer_b
Cortical cataract

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cataract is a long-term steroid effect, not the acute danger in a viral ulcer.

Incorrect. Some scarring may occur, but the dangerous acute risk driving the contraindication is perforation.

## title
Best vital stain for a corneal ulcer

## question
The best vital stain to detect a corneal ulcer is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Fluorescein stain

## explanation_a
Correct. Fluorescein is the best vital stain to detect a corneal ulcer: it pools in and stains areas of denuded epithelium bright green under cobalt-blue light, outlining the ulcer and its dendritic or geographic shape. It is the standard bedside stain for an epithelial defect. So fluorescein is the answer.

## answer_b
Rose Bengal stain

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Rose Bengal stains devitalised cells and mucus (useful in dry eye), not the first choice for an ulcer.

## answer_c
Trypan blue stain

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Trypan blue is used to stain the anterior capsule during cataract surgery, not to detect an ulcer.

## answer_d
Lissamine green stain

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lissamine green stains devitalised cells like Rose Bengal, not the ulcer bed.

Spring catarrh (Vernal keratoconjunctivitis):

## subject
mul

Is usually seasonal type of allergic conjunctivitis and affects conjunctiva and cornea

Is usually symptoms

## explanation_d
Incorrect. As printed this option is incomplete, and vernal disease is not symptomless: it causes marked itching, photophobia and a stringy discharge.
