## title
Semicircular canal involved in benign paroxysmal positional vertigo

## question
Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo is caused by displacement of calcium-carbonate crystals (otoconia) from the utricle to the:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cochlea

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cochlea is the organ of hearing; it is not where displaced otoconia settle, and BPPV causes vertigo without any hearing loss.

## answer_b
Posterior semicircular canal

## explanation_b
Correct. In benign paroxysmal positional vertigo the otoconia dislodge from the utricle and settle most often in the posterior semicircular canal, where a change of head position moves them and triggers brief attacks of rotatory vertigo with no hearing loss.

## answer_c
Superior semicircular canal

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior (anterior) canal is only rarely involved; the posterior canal is by far the commonest site.

## answer_d
The saccule of the inner ear

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The otoconia originate from the utricular (and saccular) maculae but produce positional vertigo only once they enter a semicircular canal — typically the posterior one — not while in the saccule.

## title
Perforation site that affects hearing more

## question
A perforation at which site of the tympanic membrane affects the hearing more?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Anterior perforation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An anterior (antero-inferior) perforation is remote from the round window and the ossicles, so it causes comparatively little hearing loss.

## answer_b
Posterior perforation

## explanation_b
Correct. A posterior (postero-superior) perforation causes greater hearing loss because it overlies the round window and the ossicular chain and abolishes the normal round-window baffle — the sound-pressure difference between the oval and round windows.

## answer_c
Superior perforation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. It is the posterior/postero-superior site, related to the round window and ossicles, that impairs hearing most, not a purely superior perforation.

## answer_d
Inferior perforation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. An inferior perforation lies away from the ossicles and round window and affects hearing far less than a posterior one.

Prolonged use of vasoconstrictor (decongestant) nasal drops may lead to:

mul

## answer_a
Airway obstruction

## explanation_a
Correct. In Ludwig's angina the brawny cellulitis of the submandibular space elevates the floor of the mouth and pushes the tongue upward and backward, which can rapidly occlude the airway. Airway obstruction is therefore the most dangerous complication and demands early airway control.

## answer_b
Extension of infection to the tongue

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Involvement of the tongue and floor of mouth is part of the disease, but it is the resulting airway compromise, not the spread itself, that threatens life.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bleeding is not a characteristic or life-threatening feature of Ludwig's angina.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although a lower molar infection is the usual source, loss of teeth is not the dangerous complication of the condition.
