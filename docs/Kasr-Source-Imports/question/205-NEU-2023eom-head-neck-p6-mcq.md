## title
Layers of the tear film

## question
About the layers of the tear film:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The innermost mucin layer is produced by Meibomian glands

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The innermost mucin layer is produced by conjunctival GOBLET cells, not Meibomian glands (which make the lipid layer).

## answer_b
The superficial lipid layer is produced by conjunctival goblet cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The superficial lipid layer is produced by the MEIBOMIAN glands; goblet cells make the mucin layer, not the lipid layer.

## answer_c
The aqueous (watery) layer is the middle, thickest layer

## explanation_c
Correct. The tear film is three-layered, and the middle aqueous (watery) layer, secreted by the lacrimal and accessory lacrimal glands, is the thickest, carrying most of the tear volume and its dissolved substances. The lipid layer is superficial and the mucin layer innermost. Hence the aqueous layer is the middle, thickest layer.

## answer_d
The middle layer is produced by Meibomian glands

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The middle (aqueous) layer is produced by the LACRIMAL glands; the Meibomian glands make the superficial lipid layer.

Middle ear boundaries and contents

## question
As regards the middle ear boundaries and contents, select the correct answer:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The handle of the malleus fits into the oval window

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The handle of the malleus is attached to the tympanic membrane; it is the STAPES footplate that sits in the oval window.

## answer_b
The tensor tympani and stapedius muscles regulate sound conduction

## explanation_b
Correct. The tensor tympani (attached to the malleus, supplied by V3) and the stapedius (attached to the stapes, supplied by the facial nerve) are the two intratympanic muscles. Their reflex contraction stiffens the ossicular chain and dampens its vibration, so it regulates sound conduction through the middle ear. This attenuation (acoustic) reflex protects the inner ear from damagingly loud sounds.

## answer_c
The auditory tube is lined by stratified cuboidal epithelium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The auditory (Eustachian) tube is lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium, not stratified cuboidal epithelium.

## answer_d
The Eustachian tube connects the middle ear cavity to the oropharynx

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The Eustachian tube connects the middle ear to the NASOPHARYNX, not the oropharynx.

## question

## subject

## answer_a
Phalangeal cells make direct support to hair cells

## explanation_a
Correct. The phalangeal cells (inner phalangeal and outer/Deiters' cells) are the supporting cells that directly support the hair cells, cupping their bases and extending processes to the reticular lamina. They are the immediate structural support of the hair cells.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The OUTER hair cells are arranged in three (to four) rows; it is the INNER hair cells that form a single row.

Inner phalangeal cells are arranged as 3-4 rows

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Inner phalangeal cells accompany the single row of inner hair cells; the 3-4-row arrangement belongs to the outer hair cells/Deiters' cells.

## answer_d
The organ of Corti is a neuroreceptor for equilibrium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The organ of Corti is the neuroreceptor for HEARING; equilibrium is detected by the vestibular maculae and cristae.

Hair cells of the vestibular maculae

## question
Regarding the maculae of the utricle and saccule:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
They lie parallel to each other

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The utricular and saccular maculae lie roughly PERPENDICULAR to each other (utricle horizontal, saccule vertical), not parallel, which lets them detect linear acceleration in different planes.

## answer_b
They are covered by a proteoglycan membrane called the tectorial membrane

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The maculae are covered by a gelatinous OTOLITHIC (statoconial) membrane bearing otoconia; the tectorial membrane belongs to the cochlear organ of Corti.

## answer_c
They have type 1 and type 2 mechanoreceptor hair cells

## explanation_c
Correct. The macular sensory epithelium contains type 1 (flask-shaped, with a chalice/calyx nerve ending) and type 2 (cylindrical) mechanoreceptor hair cells, the same two hair-cell types found in the cristae. This is the correct statement.

## answer_d
The supporting cell has an apical true cilium (kinocilium)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The true kinocilium is on the HAIR cell (with the stereocilia), not on the supporting cell.

Select the correct answer regarding the ascending spinal tracts:

The ventral spinocerebellar tract originates from cells in the gracile and cuneate nuclei

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The ventral (anterior) spinocerebellar tract arises from spinal border cells in the lower thoracic/lumbar cord, not from the gracile and cuneate nuclei (which are the dorsal-column relay nuclei in the medulla).

## answer_b
The external arcuate fibres arise from Clarke's nucleus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. As stated this is not the intended key; the posterior spinocerebellar tract and dorsal external arcuate fibres relate to Clarke's column, but the phrasing does not make this the correct choice.

The spinotectal tract is concerned with the spino-visual reflex

## explanation_c
Correct. The spinotectal tract ascends to the superior colliculus (tectum) of the midbrain and mediates the spino-visual reflex — reflex turning of the eyes and head toward a somatic stimulus. This is the accurate statement.

## answer_d
The spino-olivary tract originates from cells in laminae I and V in the posterior horn

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The spino-olivary tract projects to the inferior olive and does not simply originate from posterior-horn laminae I and V as described.

## title
Descending extrapyramidal spinal tracts

## question
At view of the extrapyramidal tracts of the spinal cord:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Sulcomarginal and reticulospinal tracts are present in all levels

## explanation_a
Correct. The sulcomarginal tract (the medial longitudinal fasciculus and anterior vestibulospinal fibres running beside the anterior median fissure) and the reticulospinal tracts both extend through all levels of the spinal cord. They descend from cervical to sacral segments rather than being confined to one region. Both contribute to posture, muscle tone and reflex movement. Hence they are present at all levels.

## answer_b
The tectospinal tract originates from the red nucleus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The tectospinal tract arises from the superior colliculus (tectum) of the midbrain; the tract originating from the RED nucleus is the rubrospinal tract.

## answer_c
The rubrospinal tract is found in the lumbar level only

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The rubrospinal tract descends throughout the length of the cord, not the lumbar level only.

## answer_d
The olivospinal tract is a paired tract (ventral and lateral)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The olivospinal tract is a small, inconstant bundle at cervical levels, not a paired ventral-and-lateral tract.

## title
Description of the arachnoid barrier

## question
Which of the following best describes the arachnoid barrier?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It separates the blood in choroid capillaries from CSF in the brain ventricles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Separating choroid-capillary blood from ventricular CSF describes the blood-CSF barrier at the choroid plexus, not the arachnoid barrier.

## answer_b
End feet of astrocytes share in this barrier

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Astrocyte end-feet contribute to the blood-BRAIN barrier, not the arachnoid barrier, which is built from the arachnoid barrier cells themselves.

## answer_c
It is formed by tight junctions and desmosomes between its cells

## explanation_c
Correct. The arachnoid barrier is the outer cellular layer of the arachnoid mater whose cells are linked by tight junctions and desmosomes; this continuous junctional belt makes the layer impermeable and forms the barrier at the outer margin of the subarachnoid space. That is its defining feature.

## answer_d
It separates blood in intracerebral capillaries from nerve cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Separating intracerebral capillary blood from nerve cells is the blood-brain barrier (endothelial tight junctions plus astrocyte end-feet), not the arachnoid barrier.
