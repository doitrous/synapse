## title
Cause of polyarteritis nodosa

## question
Polyarteritis nodosa is due to:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Infection

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Polyarteritis nodosa is not a direct infection of the arterial wall; although about a third of cases are associated with hepatitis B, the vessel damage is caused by the immune response, not by organisms infecting the wall.

## answer_b
Autoimmune reaction

## explanation_b
Incorrect (per this source). ‘Autoimmune’ implies autoantibodies directed against self antigens; classic polyarteritis nodosa is ANCA-negative and is better described as immune-COMPLEX mediated rather than a classic autoantibody autoimmune disease, so the source separates it from ‘autoimmune reaction’.

## answer_c
Unknown cause

## explanation_c
Incorrect (per this source). Although many cases are idiopathic, the source specifies an identifiable IMMUNE-mediated mechanism rather than ‘unknown cause’, so this is not the keyed answer.

## answer_d
Immune-mediated reaction

## explanation_d
Correct. Polyarteritis nodosa is a necrotising vasculitis of small and medium muscular arteries caused by an IMMUNE-MEDIATED (immune-complex) reaction — classically deposition of circulating immune complexes (notably HBsAg–antibody complexes in the ~30% of cases linked to hepatitis B) in the vessel wall, triggering complement activation, neutrophil recruitment and fibrinoid necrosis. This immune-complex (type III) mechanism is why it is classed as immune-mediated rather than a direct infection or a classic autoantibody autoimmune disease.

## title
Most important pathologic feature of polyarteritis nodosa

## question
The most important pathologic feature of polyarteritis nodosa is:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hyalinosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hyalinosis (hyaline arteriolosclerosis) is the small-vessel lesion of benign hypertension and diabetes, not the defining feature of polyarteritis nodosa.

## answer_b
Fibrinoid necrosis

## explanation_b
Correct. The defining pathologic feature of polyarteritis nodosa is transmural FIBRINOID NECROSIS of the wall of small and medium muscular arteries, with a mixed inflammatory infiltrate. Segments of the artery undergo acute necrotising inflammation in which fibrin and plasma proteins deposit in the necrotic wall (fibrinoid necrosis), weakening it and producing the small nodular aneurysms that name the disease. Different segments show lesions of different ages, and healing leads to fibrosis — but the hallmark acute lesion is fibrinoid necrosis.

## answer_c
Elastosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Elastosis (elastic-tissue reduplication) is a chronic adaptive change of hypertension, not the lesion of polyarteritis nodosa.

## answer_d
Fibrosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fibrosis is the healed/late change of a resolved lesion; it is not the most important (defining) acute feature, which is fibrinoid necrosis.

## question
Which of the following diseases is directly related to smoking:

## subject

Incorrect. A varicocele is dilatation of the pampiniform venous plexus of the scrotum, unrelated to smoking.
