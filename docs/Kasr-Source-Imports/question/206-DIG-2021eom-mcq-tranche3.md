## title
Inhibition of gastrin secretion by antral acid

## question
Gastrin secretion is decreased by:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The vagus nerve

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The vagus nerve stimulates gastrin release, both directly and through gastrin-releasing peptide (GRP), so it increases rather than decreases gastrin.

## answer_b
Amino acids in the stomach

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Amino acids and small peptides in the antrum are among the strongest chemical stimulants of G-cell gastrin secretion.

## answer_c
Food in the stomach

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Food in the stomach, by distension and by its protein content and buffering of acid, stimulates gastrin secretion.

## answer_d
Excess hydrochloric acid in the antrum

## explanation_d
Correct. A high concentration of hydrochloric acid (a low antral pH, below about 3) is the principal negative-feedback signal that shuts off gastrin secretion. The falling pH triggers antral D cells to release somatostatin, which acts in a paracrine manner on neighbouring G cells to inhibit gastrin release. This loop prevents excessive acid production once the meal has been buffered and the antral contents become acidic.

## title
Mechanism not contributing to gastric HCl secretion

## question
Which of the following does NOT contribute to hydrochloric acid secretion in the stomach?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
A basolateral HCO3-/Cl- exchanger, producing an 'alkaline tide' in the venous blood

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the answer. As the parietal cell pumps H+ into the lumen, HCO3- generated inside the cell leaves across the basolateral membrane in exchange for Cl- (which then follows H+ into the lumen); this HCO3- efflux is the 'alkaline tide' in gastric venous blood.

## answer_b
Hydrogen ions supplied by a basolateral hydrogen/sodium pump

## explanation_b
Correct. The parietal cell does not use a basolateral hydrogen/sodium pump to supply the secreted H+. The hydrogen ions come from the dissociation of carbonic acid within the cell and are pumped into the lumen by the apical H+/K+ ATPase; there is no basolateral H+/Na+ pump role in acid secretion, so this statement is the one that does not contribute.

## answer_c
Water passively following acid into the lumen to maintain osmotic balance

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the answer. Water follows the secreted HCl osmotically into the lumen, keeping the gastric juice roughly isotonic.

## answer_d
An H+/K+ ATPase at the luminal (apical) border as the primary contributor

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the answer. The apical H+/K+ ATPase (proton pump) is indeed the primary transporter that secretes H+ into the lumen and is the target of proton-pump inhibitors.

## title
Acetylcholine and gastric acid secretion

## question
Regarding gastric acid secretion, which one of the following statements is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Acetylcholine increases gastric acid secretion

## explanation_a
Correct. Acetylcholine, released from vagal and enteric postganglionic fibres, acts on M3 muscarinic receptors on the parietal cell to stimulate acid secretion, and also drives gastrin and histamine release. It is one of the three principal secretagogues, together with gastrin and histamine, that converge on the parietal cell. This is why vagotomy and anticholinergic drugs reduce gastric acid output.

Gastrin reduces gastric acid secretion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Gastrin is a powerful stimulant, not an inhibitor, of gastric acid secretion.

## answer_c
Histamine reduces gastric acid secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Histamine, acting on H2 receptors, strongly stimulates acid secretion; H2-blockers reduce it.

## answer_d
Prostaglandins increase gastric acid secretion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prostaglandins (E series) inhibit acid secretion and protect the mucosa, which is why NSAIDs, by blocking them, promote ulceration.

## question
Which of the following does NOT help the healing of peptic ulcers?

## subject
gi

Incorrect as the answer. Eradicating Helicobacter pylori removes the major cause of peptic ulceration and greatly improves healing and reduces recurrence.

A 50-year-old man presents with vomiting after eating spoiled food.

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Deep inspiration followed by contraction of the abdominal muscles

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the answer. Vomiting begins with a deep inspiration, then forceful contraction of the abdominal muscles and diaphragm raises intra-abdominal pressure to expel gastric contents.

## answer_b
Closure of the glottis to protect the airway

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the answer. The glottis closes and the larynx is elevated during vomiting to protect the airway from aspiration.

## answer_c
Relaxation of the pyloric sphincter

## explanation_c
Correct. The pyloric sphincter does not relax during vomiting; it (and the pyloric antrum) contract, while the body of the stomach and the lower oesophageal sphincter relax, so that gastric contents are propelled retrogradely up the oesophagus rather than onward into the duodenum. Relaxation of the pylorus is therefore the event that does not occur.

## answer_d
Elevation of the soft palate to close off the nasal cavity

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the answer. The soft palate elevates to close off the nasopharynx and prevent nasal regurgitation during vomiting.
