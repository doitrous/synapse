## title
Best description of the Golgi tendon organs

## question
Which of the following best describes the Golgi tendon organs?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
They are located in parallel to the extrafusal muscle fibers

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Golgi tendon organs lie in SERIES (at the musculotendinous junction), not in parallel; the in-parallel receptors are the muscle spindles.

## answer_b
They are inhibited when the muscle actively contracts

## explanation_b
Incorrect. They are ACTIVATED (excited) by active contraction because it raises tendon tension; they are not inhibited by it.

## answer_c
They are the receptors for the inverse stretch reflex

## explanation_c
Correct. Golgi tendon organs are tension receptors in series with the muscle fibres, and their group Ib afferents drive the inverse (autogenic) stretch reflex. Through an inhibitory interneuron they inhibit the homonymous alpha motor neurons, so a steep rise in tension makes the contracting muscle relax. This protects the muscle-tendon unit from excessive force and helps grade contraction.

## answer_d
They are connected to group Ia afferent fibers

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Golgi tendon organs are connected to group Ib afferents; the group Ia afferents belong to the muscle spindle's primary ending.

## title
Bladder during spinal shock

## question
Which of the following describes the urinary bladder of a patient during the stage of spinal shock?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Retention with overflow

## explanation_a
Correct. During spinal shock all reflexes below a sudden complete cord transection are abolished, so the detrusor is atonic (areflexic). The bladder therefore fills passively and cannot empty, producing urinary retention, and once over-full it dribbles out passively as retention with overflow. Only when reflexes later return does an automatic bladder develop.

## answer_b
Automatic bladder

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The automatic (reflex) bladder appears in the RECOVERY stage after spinal shock, not during it.

## answer_c
Hyperactive bladder with mass reflex

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A hyperactive bladder with mass reflex is also a late, post-shock recovery pattern, not a feature of spinal shock.

## answer_d
Evacuated only by voluntary control

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Voluntary control requires intact descending pathways, which are lost below a complete transection; the shock-stage bladder cannot be voluntarily emptied.

Lesion at which level of the spinal cord causes immediate death?

## subject
neuro

## status

Incorrect. An upper thoracic lesion leaves diaphragmatic (phrenic) breathing intact and is not immediately fatal.

## answer_d
Transection in the lower lumbar region

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A lower lumbar lesion is well below the respiratory outflow and does not threaten breathing.

## title
Hair cells of the crista ampullaris

## question
Which statement concerning the hair cells of the crista ampullaris is correct?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Movement that bends the stereocilia towards the kinocilium depolarizes the hair cell

## explanation_a
Correct. Each hair cell has a bundle of stereocilia and one tall kinocilium. Deflection of the stereocilia TOWARD the kinocilium opens mechanically-gated cation channels and admits K+ from the surrounding endolymph, depolarising the cell and increasing afferent firing; bending away hyperpolarises it. This directional sensitivity lets the crista signal the direction of angular acceleration.

## answer_b
Bending of the stereocilia to the kinocilium activates voltage-gated sodium channels

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Transduction opens mechanically-gated cation channels carrying a K+ current from endolymph, not voltage-gated sodium channels.

## answer_c
Depolarization of the hair cell is achieved by linear acceleration

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The crista responds to ANGULAR acceleration; linear acceleration is detected by the maculae of the utricle and saccule.

## answer_d
They are stimulated with constant speed rotation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cristae respond to CHANGES in rotation (angular acceleration/deceleration); at constant speed rotation the endolymph catches up and the cupula returns, so firing subsides.
