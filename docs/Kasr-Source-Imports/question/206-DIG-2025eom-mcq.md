## title
Portosystemic anastomosis causing haematemesis
## question
A 36 years old man comes to the emergency department with severe haematemesis. What site of porto-systemic anastomosis is likely to be the cause?
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
A
## answer_a
The lower end of oesophagus.
## explanation_a
Correct. The lower oesophagus is the classic portosystemic anastomosis site behind severe, life-threatening haematemesis: oesophageal branches of the (portal) left gastric vein anastomose with (systemic) azygos-draining oesophageal veins, and in portal hypertension this submucosal plexus dilates into thin-walled oesophageal varices prone to rupture.
## answer_b
The lower end of rectum and upper end of anal canal.
## explanation_b
Incorrect. This anorectal portosystemic anastomosis (superior rectal vein, portal, with middle/inferior rectal veins, systemic) produces haemorrhoids under portal hypertension, not haematemesis.
## answer_c
Around the umbilicus.
## explanation_c
Incorrect. The paraumbilical anastomosis (paraumbilical veins, portal, with superficial abdominal wall veins, systemic) produces the visible dilated periumbilical veins of caput medusae, not haematemesis.
## answer_d
Posterior abdominal wall.
## explanation_d
Incorrect. The retroperitoneal (bare-area) anastomoses, the veins of Retzius, are typically asymptomatic collaterals rather than a source of severe upper GI bleeding.

## title
Posterior gastric perforation site
## question
Gastroscopy and a CT scan reveal a perforating ulcer in the posterior wall of the stomach, spilling stomach contents and blood directly into which of the following areas?
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
C
## answer_a
Right subhepatic space.
## explanation_a
Incorrect (this is a greater-sac recess). The right subhepatic space is reached by an anterior gastric perforation or peritonitis tracking through the greater sac, not by a posterior perforation, which is confined to the lesser sac.
## answer_b
Hepatorenal space.
## explanation_b
Incorrect (this is a greater-sac recess). The hepatorenal space (Morison's pouch) is part of the greater peritoneal cavity, reached from an anterior perforation or fluid tracking along the right paracolic gutter, not directly from a posterior gastric perforation.
## answer_c
Omental bursa.
## explanation_c
Correct. The posterior wall of the stomach forms the anterior wall of the omental bursa (lesser sac); a posterior perforation therefore spills gastric contents and blood directly into this contained space, which communicates with the greater sac only through the narrow epiploic foramen.
## answer_d
Right subphrenic space.
## explanation_d
Incorrect (this is a greater-sac recess). The right subphrenic space lies in the greater peritoneal cavity above the liver, reached by an anterior perforation, not a posterior one.
