## title
Enzyme deficient in lactose intolerance

## question
Lactose intolerance is a condition that occurs because of a deficiency of which of the following enzymes?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Gluten

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Gluten is a wheat storage protein, not an enzyme; sensitivity to it causes coeliac disease, which is unrelated to lactose digestion.

## answer_b
Maltase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Maltase hydrolyses maltose to glucose; its deficiency would impair starch-derived maltose digestion, not lactose.

## answer_c
Lactase

## explanation_c
Correct. Lactose intolerance results from a deficiency of intestinal lactase (a brush-border beta-galactosidase), the enzyme that splits lactose into glucose and galactose. Undigested lactose remains osmotically active in the gut lumen and is fermented by colonic bacteria, causing bloating, cramps and osmotic diarrhoea after dairy. Lactase deficiency is the commonest disaccharidase deficiency worldwide.

## answer_d
Pepsin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pepsin is a gastric protease that digests protein, not the disaccharide lactose.

## title
Main lipid component of chylomicrons

## question
Chylomicrons are composed mainly of:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Triacylglycerol (TAG)

## explanation_a
Correct. Chylomicrons are the largest and least dense lipoproteins and are composed mainly of triacylglycerol (about 85-90% by mass), the dietary fat absorbed from the intestine. They carry this exogenous triacylglycerol from the gut, through the lymph, to peripheral tissues where lipoprotein lipase releases the fatty acids. Their very high triacylglycerol content is why they scatter light and make post-prandial plasma turbid.

## answer_b
Cholesterol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cholesterol is only a minor component of chylomicrons; cholesterol-rich particles are the LDL fraction.

## answer_c
Free fatty acids

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Free fatty acids are transported in blood bound to albumin, not packaged as the core of chylomicrons.

## answer_d
Phospholipids

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phospholipids form only a thin surface monolayer of the chylomicron, not its bulk; the core is triacylglycerol.

## title
End product of protein digestion

## question
The end product of protein digestion in the gastrointestinal tract is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Dipeptide

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Dipeptides are intermediate products; most are further hydrolysed by brush-border and cytoplasmic peptidases before or after absorption.

## answer_b
Tripeptide

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Tripeptides are likewise intermediates that are largely split to amino acids by intracellular peptidases.

## answer_c
Polypeptide

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Polypeptides are early, partial products of gastric and pancreatic protease action, not the final product.

## answer_d
Amino acid

## explanation_d
Correct. The final end products of complete protein digestion are free amino acids, produced by the combined action of gastric pepsin, pancreatic proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidases, elastase) and intestinal brush-border and cytoplasmic peptidases. Small di- and tri-peptides are also absorbed but are hydrolysed to amino acids inside the enterocyte, so amino acids are what enter the portal blood. Amino acids are therefore the definitive end product.

Component not found in pancreatic juice

## question
Pancreatic juice contains all of the following EXCEPT:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Trypsinogen

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the answer. Trypsinogen is a pancreatic zymogen secreted in the juice and activated to trypsin by enteropeptidase in the duodenum.

## answer_b
Lipase

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the answer. Pancreatic lipase is a major enzyme of pancreatic juice, responsible for triacylglycerol digestion.

Cholecystokinin

## explanation_c
Correct. Cholecystokinin is not a component of pancreatic juice; it is a hormone secreted by the duodenal I cells that stimulates the pancreatic acinar cells to release enzyme-rich juice. ## answer_d
Chymotrypsinogen

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the answer. Chymotrypsinogen is another pancreatic zymogen in the juice, activated to chymotrypsin by trypsin.

## title
Enzyme deficient in pancreatitis-related protein maldigestion

## question
The most likely reason for this protein-digestion disorder is hyposecretion of which enzyme?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 30-year-old man with acute pancreatitis is found to have a disorder of protein digestion.

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Trypsin

## explanation_a
Correct. Trypsin is the principal pancreatic protease and also the activator of the other pancreatic zymogens (chymotrypsinogen, proelastase, procarboxypeptidases). When a damaged pancreas hyposecretes its enzymes, the loss of trypsin most directly impairs protein digestion, both by its own proteolytic action and by failing to activate the downstream proteases. This makes trypsin hyposecretion the best explanation for the maldigestion of protein.

## answer_b
Lipase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lipase deficiency impairs fat digestion (causing steatorrhoea), not protein digestion.

## answer_c
Pepsin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pepsin is a gastric, not pancreatic, protease; its secretion is not the primary defect in pancreatitis, and gastric proteolysis alone cannot compensate for lost pancreatic proteases.

## answer_d
Amylase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amylase digests carbohydrate (starch), so its deficiency would affect carbohydrate rather than protein digestion.
