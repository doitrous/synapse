## title
Properties of GLUT4

## question
GLUT4:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Is present in adipose tissue

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the sole answer. GLUT4 is indeed expressed in adipose tissue (and skeletal and cardiac muscle), but this is only one of several true statements here.

## answer_b
Facilitates transport of glucose

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the sole answer. GLUT4 does facilitate glucose transport (facilitated diffusion down the gradient), but again this is not the only true statement.

## answer_c
Is insulin dependent

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the sole answer. GLUT4 is the insulin-responsive transporter, recruited to the membrane by insulin, but this too is only one true statement.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. All three statements are true of GLUT4: it is present in adipose tissue and muscle, it facilitates glucose transport by facilitated diffusion, and its surface expression is insulin dependent (insulin triggers translocation of GLUT4 vesicles to the plasma membrane). Because A, B and C are each correct, 'All of the above' is the best answer.

## title
Why dietary pentoses are not utilised

## question
Dietary pentoses are not utilised in our body due to:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The presence of endogenous pentose

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The body does synthesise pentoses (e.g. ribose from the pentose phosphate pathway), but the availability of endogenous pentose is not the reason dietary pentoses go unused.

## answer_b
Deficiency of a specific pentokinase

## explanation_b
Correct. Dietary pentoses (such as arabinose and xylose) are absorbed but cannot be metabolised because the body lacks a specific kinase to phosphorylate them; unphosphorylated, they cannot enter metabolic pathways and are excreted largely unchanged. Endogenous ribose needed for nucleotides is instead made from glucose via the pentose phosphate pathway.

## answer_c
Cannot be absorbed

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pentoses are in fact absorbed from the gut (partly by passive diffusion); the block is metabolic, not absorptive.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Option B gives the correct reason, so 'none of the above' is wrong.

Incorrect. Fruit sugar (fructose) is an absorbable monosaccharide used for energy, not a source of fibre.

fnd

## title
Transport form of dietary fat

## question
The dietary fats are transported as:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Micelles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Micelles carry the products of fat digestion to the enterocyte surface within the gut lumen; they are not the form in which absorbed dietary fat is transported in blood/lymph.

## answer_b
Chylomicrons

## explanation_b
Correct. After absorption, dietary triacylglycerols are re-esterified in the enterocyte and packaged into chylomicrons, which enter the lymphatics and then the blood to deliver exogenous fat to peripheral tissues. Chylomicrons are the transport form of dietary (exogenous) fat.

Fatty acid-albumin complex

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The fatty acid-albumin complex transports free fatty acids released from adipose tissue, not absorbed dietary fat.

## answer_d
Liposomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Liposomes are artificial lipid vesicles, not a physiological lipid-transport particle.

## title
Substrate added to diacylglycerol to form triacylglycerol

## question
To form triacylglycerol, which of the following is added to diacylglycerol?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Glycerol

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Glycerol is the backbone alcohol, not the group added at the final step; free glycerol is not the acyl donor.

## answer_b
Glycerol-3-phosphate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glycerol-3-phosphate is the starting backbone that is acylated early in the pathway, not the substrate added to diacylglycerol.

## answer_c
Fatty acyl-CoA

## explanation_c
Correct. The final step of triacylglycerol synthesis is catalysed by diacylglycerol acyltransferase (DGAT), which transfers a third fatty acid from fatty acyl-CoA onto diacylglycerol. The activated acyl donor is therefore fatty acyl-CoA.

## answer_d
Acetyl-CoA

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Acetyl-CoA is a two-carbon unit for fatty-acid synthesis, not the long-chain acyl donor used to esterify diacylglycerol.

## title
Function of bile salts in the intestine

## question
What is the function of bile salts in the intestine?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Activator of lipase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. It is colipase, not bile salt, that anchors and activates pancreatic lipase; bile salts alone actually tend to displace lipase from the fat surface.

## answer_b
Emulsifier

## explanation_b
Correct. Bile salts are amphipathic molecules that emulsify dietary fat, breaking large fat globules into a fine emulsion of small droplets. This hugely increases the surface area available to pancreatic lipase and helps form the mixed micelles that carry digestion products to the mucosa.

## answer_c
Cofactor for cholesteryl esterase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bile salts help solubilise substrate but are not described as the specific cofactor of cholesteryl esterase; their defining role here is emulsification.

## answer_d
Inhibitor of lipid absorption

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Bile salts promote, rather than inhibit, lipid digestion and absorption.

## title
Orientation of fatty acids in a micelle

## question
Micelles of fatty acids in water are organised such that the ___ face the solvent and the ___ are directed toward the interior:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

D

Carboxylic acid groups, hydrocarbon chains

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the sole answer. Naming the polar carboxylic-acid groups as facing the water and the hydrocarbon chains as the interior is correct, but it is the same arrangement stated in B, so A alone is not the best answer.

## answer_b
Hydrophilic heads, hydrophobic tails

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the sole answer. 'Hydrophilic heads out, hydrophobic tails in' is correct, but it merely restates A in general terms, so B alone is not the best answer.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This reverses the arrangement: the hydrophobic hydrocarbon chains cannot face the aqueous solvent.

Both A and B

## explanation_d
Correct. In water, fatty acids form micelles with their polar hydrophilic heads (the carboxylic-acid groups) facing the aqueous solvent and their hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails packed toward the interior.
