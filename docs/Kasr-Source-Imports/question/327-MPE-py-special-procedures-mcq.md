## title
Permitted source of organ transplantation in Egypt

## question
In Egypt, organ transplantation is allowed from:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cadavers

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Egyptian organ-transplantation law is built on living, related donation; routine retrieval from cadavers is not the permitted source tested here.

## answer_b
Brain dead

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Egyptian law does not treat the brain-dead individual as a legal source of organs for transplantation in the framework this bank examines; donation is from a living, related donor.

## answer_c
Living spouse

## explanation_c
Correct. In Egypt, organ transplantation is permitted from a living donor who is related to the recipient, and the spouse is accepted within that relationship. The law restricts donation to living relatives to prevent organ trafficking and to keep donation altruistic and consensual. A living spouse is therefore the only listed permitted source.

## answer_d
Consenting friend

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A friend, however willing, is not a legally accepted donor: donation is confined to living relatives (including the spouse), not unrelated persons, to guard against commercialisation.

## title
Traditional (classic) surrogacy — the surrogate's role

## question
In traditional or classic surrogacy, the surrogate mother:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Conceives entirely by other couple sperm and ovum

## explanation_a
Incorrect. That describes gestational surrogacy, where the surrogate carries an embryo formed entirely from the commissioning couple's gametes; in traditional surrogacy her own ovum is used.

## answer_b
Donates her ovum to form the embryo

## explanation_b
Correct. In traditional (classic) surrogacy the surrogate mother provides her own ovum, which is fertilised (usually by the commissioning man's sperm through insemination), so she donates the egg that forms the embryo. Because her own genetic material is used, she is genetically related to the child. This is what distinguishes traditional from gestational surrogacy.

## answer_c
Has no genetic link to the baby

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Because her own ovum forms the embryo, the traditional surrogate is genetically related to the baby; having no genetic link describes the gestational surrogate.

## answer_d
Is fertilized via IVF

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Traditional surrogacy is classically achieved by insemination of the surrogate rather than IVF of another couple's embryo; the defining feature is that her own ovum is used.

## title
Homologous artificial insemination — the exception

## question
As regards homologous artificial insemination, all of the following are true, except:

## subject
mul

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

A

## answer_a
It is obtained from a donor

## explanation_a
Correct (this is the exception — the false statement). Homologous artificial insemination uses the husband's own semen, not a donor's; "obtained from a donor" describes heterologous insemination. Because the gametes are the married couple's own, homologous insemination is the legally and ethically accepted form. This is the statement that does not apply to homologous insemination.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this statement is true). Homologous insemination is a recognised option when a couple cannot conceive naturally because of male impotence, using the husband's semen.

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this statement is true). Because it uses the spouses' own gametes, homologous artificial insemination is a legal procedure.

## answer_d
It is done in female vaginismus

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this statement is true). It is used when intercourse is not possible, such as female vaginismus, delivering the husband's semen artificially.

## title
Fundamental rule governing IVF

## question
A couple was suffering from fertility problems. They wanted to do an IVF procedure. What is the most important rule to be followed?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
A donor ovum can be used

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Using a donor ovum introduces third-party genetic material, which is not permitted; the gametes must come from the married couple.

## answer_b
A donor sperm can be used

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Donor sperm is likewise third-party genetic material and is not allowed; the sperm must be the husband's.

## answer_c
A sperm and an ovum from the meant couple

## explanation_c
Correct. The governing rule for IVF is that both the sperm and the ovum must come from the married couple themselves, with no third-party donor and no surrogate. Keeping the gametes within the marriage preserves lineage and is the ethical and legal condition for assisted reproduction. Any donor gamete or surrogate arrangement breaches this rule.

## answer_d
A surrogate mother is accepted

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Surrogacy places the couple's or a third party's embryo in another woman's uterus and is not accepted; the pregnancy must be carried by the wife herself.
