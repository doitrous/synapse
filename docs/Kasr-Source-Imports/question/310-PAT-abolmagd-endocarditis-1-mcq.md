## title
Nature of rheumatic fever

## question
Rheumatic fever is:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
A disease affecting mainly the myocardium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Rheumatic fever is a PANCARDITIS affecting all three layers (endocardium, myocardium and pericardium); it is not confined mainly to the myocardium.

## answer_b
An immune-mediated inflammatory disease

## explanation_b
Correct. Rheumatic fever is an IMMUNE-MEDIATED inflammatory disease that follows a group A streptococcal pharyngitis: antibodies and T cells raised against streptococcal antigens (M protein) cross-react with host tissues (molecular mimicry), inflaming the heart, joints, skin and brain. It is not a direct infection of the heart but an immunological sequel to the throat infection. This immune-mediated, post-streptococcal nature is the defining fact.

## answer_c
A condition that usually starts in old age

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Rheumatic fever is a disease of CHILDREN and young people (about 5–15 years), not of old age.

## answer_d
A complication of Staphylococcus infection

## explanation_d
Incorrect. It is a complication of group A STREPTOCOCCAL (not staphylococcal) pharyngitis.

## title
Disease characterised by the Aschoff body

## question
Aschoff body is the characteristic histologic lesion of which of the following:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Infective endocarditis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Infective endocarditis is characterised by bacterial vegetations, not Aschoff bodies.

## answer_b
Rheumatic endocarditis

## explanation_b
Correct. The ASCHOFF BODY is the pathognomonic histologic lesion of RHEUMATIC carditis: a focus of interstitial inflammation with central fibrinoid necrosis surrounded by lymphocytes, plasma cells and characteristic activated macrophages (Anitschkow cells with 'caterpillar' chromatin, and multinucleate Aschoff giant cells). Finding an Aschoff body is diagnostic of rheumatic heart disease. It does not occur in infective, lupus or syphilitic endocarditis.

## answer_c
Endocarditis of SLE

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Libman-Sacks (SLE) endocarditis shows sterile verrucous vegetations, not Aschoff bodies.

## answer_d
Syphilitic disease

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Syphilitic cardiovascular disease affects the aorta (aortitis), and is not characterised by Aschoff bodies.

## title
Type of pericarditis in rheumatic fever

## question
Which is the most common type of pericarditis in rheumatic fever:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Serous

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A purely serous pericarditis is typical of viral or uraemic pericarditis, not rheumatic fever.

## answer_b
Suppurative

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Suppurative (purulent) pericarditis is caused by pyogenic bacterial infection, not rheumatic fever.

## answer_c
Serofibrinous

## explanation_c
Correct. The pericarditis of rheumatic fever is characteristically SEROFIBRINOUS (fibrinous): the inflamed pericardial surfaces are covered by a fibrin-rich exudate that, when the layers are separated, gives the classic 'bread-and-butter' appearance. It usually resolves without significant constriction. This fibrinous pattern, not a serous, suppurative or haemorrhagic one, is the rheumatic pericarditis.

## answer_d
Hemorrhagic

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Haemorrhagic pericarditis suggests tuberculosis or malignancy, not rheumatic fever.

## title
Disease with Libman-Sacks endocarditis

## question
Libman-Sacks endocarditis occurs in:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Rheumatic heart disease

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Rheumatic heart disease produces small vegetations along the lines of valve closure with Aschoff bodies, not the Libman-Sacks lesion.

## answer_b
SLE

## explanation_b
Correct. LIBMAN–SACKS endocarditis is the non-bacterial verrucous (wart-like) valvular vegetation of SYSTEMIC LUPUS ERYTHEMATOSUS (SLE). Small sterile fibrinous vegetations form on either surface of the valve leaflets (classically mitral and tricuspid), reflecting immune-complex injury rather than infection. Its association with SLE is the fact tested.

## answer_c
Infective endocarditis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Infective endocarditis produces bulky infected (bacterial) vegetations, not the sterile verrucous Libman-Sacks lesion.

## answer_d
Carcinoid syndrome

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Carcinoid heart disease causes right-sided (tricuspid/pulmonary) plaque-like fibrous thickening, not Libman-Sacks vegetations.

## title
Location of MacCallum's patch

## question
MacCallum's patch is located at the posterior wall of:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Right atrium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. MacCallum's patch is not in the right atrium.

## answer_b
Left atrium

## explanation_b
Correct. MacCALLUM'S PATCH is a map-like area of subendocardial thickening on the POSTERIOR WALL of the LEFT ATRIUM in rheumatic heart disease. It results from the friction and the regurgitant jet across the inflamed mitral valve, producing subendocardial fibrosis at that site. Its left-atrial posterior-wall location is the specific fact tested.

## answer_c
Right ventricle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The right ventricle is not the site of MacCallum's patch.

## answer_d
Left ventricle

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although the left ventricle is on the mitral side, the patch classically lies on the posterior wall of the left ATRIUM, above the mitral valve.

## title
Characteristic of subacute infective endocarditis

## question
Subacute infective endocarditis is characterized by:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Purulent vegetation with valve perforation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Purulent vegetations with valve perforation are features of ACUTE infective endocarditis by virulent organisms, not the subacute form.

## answer_b
Affecting unhealthy valves

## explanation_b
Correct. SUBACUTE infective endocarditis characteristically affects previously DAMAGED or abnormal (unhealthy) valves — rheumatic, congenitally abnormal or prosthetic — seeded by low-virulence organisms such as Streptococcus viridans. It produces bulky but less destructive vegetations and an insidious course over weeks to months. This is the opposite of the acute form, which attacks previously normal valves with rapidly destructive, purulent, pyaemic and often fatal disease.

## answer_c
Pyemia

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pyaemia (widespread pyogenic seeding) characterises acute, not subacute, infective endocarditis.

## answer_d
Rapidly fatal course

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A rapidly fatal course is a feature of acute infective endocarditis; the subacute form is insidious and slowly progressive.
