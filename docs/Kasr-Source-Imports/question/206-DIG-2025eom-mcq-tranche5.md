Keratinized regions of the oral mucosa
## question
Epithelium of the oral mucosa is keratinized stratified squamous epithelium in:
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
C
## answer_a
Soft palate
## explanation_a
Incorrect. The soft palate is lining mucosa covered by non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, adapted for movement rather than abrasion.
## answer_b
Cheeks
## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cheek (buccal mucosa) is lining mucosa with a non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.

Gingiva (gums)

Correct. The gingiva, together with the hard palate, is masticatory mucosa exposed to the friction of chewing. Pharynx
## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pharynx is lining mucosa with a non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium (with respiratory epithelium over the nasopharynx).

## subject

## status

Basal cells are stem cells for taste cells only

Incorrect. Basal cells are stem cells that regenerate all cell types of the taste bud, both gustatory and sustentacular, not the gustatory cells only.

Histology of the oesophagus
## question
Regarding the histological structure of the oesophagus, the following is true:
## subject
gi
## status

## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
C
## answer_a
Mucosa is lined by stratified columnar epithelium
## explanation_a
Incorrect. The oesophageal mucosa is lined by non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, suited to the abrasion of a food bolus, not stratified columnar.
## answer_b
Autonomic Auerbach's plexus is present in the submucosa
## explanation_b
Incorrect. Auerbach's (myenteric) plexus lies between the two muscle layers of the muscularis externa; the submucosal plexus of Meissner is the one found in the submucosa.

Middle 1/3 of musculosa is a mixture of smooth and striated muscles
## explanation_c
Correct. The muscularis externa of the oesophagus changes along its length: the upper third is skeletal (striated) muscle, the middle third is a mixture of skeletal and smooth muscle, and the lower third is smooth muscle only. This gradient reflects the transition from voluntary to involuntary control of swallowing. The mixed middle third is the classic examined feature.
## answer_d
Adventitia is dense C.T., replaced by serosa above the diaphragm
## explanation_d
Incorrect. The oesophagus has an adventitia along most of its course; it acquires a serosa only for the short abdominal segment below the diaphragm, not above it.

## title
Histology of a peptic ulcer
## question
A 60-year-old female has been using non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs for long periods. Investigations revealed she had a peptic ulcer. Histologically, the affected area will reveal:
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
B
## answer_a
Increased secretion of goblet cells
## explanation_a
Incorrect. The gastric mucosa has surface mucous cells rather than goblet cells, and a peptic ulcer is defined by loss of mucosa, not by increased mucous secretion.
## answer_b
Erosion of the mucous membrane of the stomach
## explanation_b
Correct. NSAIDs inhibit cyclo-oxygenase and so reduce the protective mucosal prostaglandins, allowing acid and pepsin to breach the mucosal barrier. The histological hallmark of a peptic ulcer is a break in the mucosa (erosion) that may extend through the muscularis mucosae into deeper layers. The exposed area shows loss of the surface epithelium with underlying inflammation and necrotic debris.
## answer_c
Loss of intracellular canaliculi of parietal cells
## explanation_c
Incorrect. Loss of parietal-cell canaliculi is not the defining feature of a peptic ulcer; the ulcer is a focal destructive mucosal defect regardless of the state of individual parietal cells.
## answer_d
Proliferation of the mucus neck cells
## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mucous neck cells are the regenerative reserve of the gland, but the ulcer itself is characterised by mucosal destruction, not by a proliferation of neck cells.

Cells with a prominent infranuclear Golgi complex
## question
The prominent infra-nuclear Golgi complex is an ultrastructural feature in:
## subject

## explanation_a

## explanation_b

Incorrect. Oxyntic (parietal) cells secrete acid apically and Paneth cells discharge apical eosinophilic granules; neither has an infranuclear Golgi.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cecum is large intestine and is goblet-cell rich, but it is proximal to the sigmoid colon, which has an even higher density.

Parenchyma of the liver shows:
## subject

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Plates of hepatocytes are separated by bile canaliculi
## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hepatocyte plates are separated by the blood sinusoids; bile canaliculi are tiny channels running between adjacent hepatocytes within a plate.
## answer_b
Classic hepatic lobules are demarcated by thin C.T. septa
## explanation_b
Correct. The classic hepatic lobule is a roughly hexagonal unit with a central vein at its centre and portal tracts at its corners. It is outlined by thin connective-tissue septa carrying the portal triads (well seen in the pig, fainter in man). This connective-tissue demarcation of the hexagonal lobule is the classic descriptive feature.
## answer_c
Portal lobules drain into the central vein
## explanation_c
Incorrect. The portal lobule is centred on a portal tract and is defined by bile drainage towards the bile duct, not by drainage into the central vein.
## answer_d
Zone I of liver acinus is poor in blood supply
## explanation_d
Incorrect. Zone I (periportal) of the liver acinus lies closest to the arterioles and is the best oxygenated/best supplied zone; zone III is the poorly supplied one.

## title
Von Kupffer cells of the liver
## question
Von Kupffer cells:
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
A
## answer_a
Clear blood passing in the blood sinusoids to prevent obstruction
## explanation_a
Correct. Kupffer cells are stellate resident macrophages that lie within the hepatic sinusoids, attached to the endothelial lining. They phagocytose worn-out red cells, bacteria and debris from the portal blood as it flows through the sinusoid, keeping the blood clear. This mononuclear-phagocyte function is what the question describes as preventing obstruction of the sinusoids.
## answer_b
Cell membrane is regular
## explanation_b
Incorrect. As active phagocytes Kupffer cells have an irregular surface with pseudopodia and membrane folds, not a smooth regular membrane.
## answer_c
Cytoplasm is rich in apical secretory granules
## explanation_c
Incorrect. Kupffer cells are phagocytic, not secretory; their cytoplasm contains lysosomes and phagocytosed material rather than apical secretory granules.
## answer_d
It lines the central vein
## explanation_d
Incorrect. Kupffer cells lie in the sinusoids among the endothelial cells; the central vein is lined by ordinary endothelium.
