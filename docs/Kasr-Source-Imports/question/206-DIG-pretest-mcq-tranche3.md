## title
Transporter for glucose entry into the enterocyte

## question
A newborn with severe diarrhoea has an inherited glucose/galactose malabsorption requiring a glucose- and galactose-free diet. Which transport protein is responsible for entry of glucose into the intestinal enterocyte from the lumen?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
GLUT-2

## explanation_a
Incorrect. GLUT-2 mediates glucose exit across the basolateral membrane by facilitated diffusion, not apical uptake from the lumen.

## answer_b
GLUT-5

## explanation_b
Incorrect. GLUT-5 is the apical transporter for facilitated fructose entry, not glucose.

## answer_c
SGLT1

## explanation_c
Correct. Glucose (and galactose) enter the enterocyte across the apical brush border by the sodium-dependent cotransporter SGLT1, a secondary active transport driven by the sodium gradient. A congenital defect in SGLT1 therefore causes glucose-galactose malabsorption with severe, potentially fatal diarrhoea. Glucose then leaves the cell basolaterally through GLUT-2.

## answer_d
SGLT2

## explanation_d
Incorrect. SGLT2 handles glucose reabsorption in the renal proximal tubule, not intestinal uptake.

## answer_e
SGLT5

## explanation_e
Incorrect. SGLT5 is not the intestinal apical glucose-galactose cotransporter responsible for this uptake.

## title
Correct statement about medium-chain fatty acids

## question
Which statement about medium-chain fatty acids is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
They are more water-soluble than long-chain fatty acids

## explanation_a
Correct. Medium-chain triglycerides (6-12 carbons) are much more water-soluble than long-chain fats and are hydrolysed more rapidly. Because they are not resynthesised into triglyceride or packaged into chylomicrons, they pass directly into the portal blood. Their ready absorption makes them useful in many malabsorptive diseases.

## answer_b
Within the enterocyte they are used for triglyceride resynthesis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Medium-chain fatty acids are not used for triglyceride resynthesis within the enterocyte; long-chain fatty acids are.

## answer_c
They are packaged into chylomicrons

## explanation_c
Incorrect. They are not packaged into chylomicrons, unlike long-chain fats.

## answer_d
They are transported in the lymph

## explanation_d
Incorrect. They enter the portal blood, not the lymphatic (chylomicron) route.

## answer_e
They require emulsification before enterocyte uptake

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Their water solubility lets them be absorbed without the emulsification and micelle formation long-chain fats need.

## title
Correct statement about iron transport and absorption

## question
A 47-year-old woman with heavy menses develops iron-deficiency anaemia needing iron supplements. Which statement about iron digestion and absorption is correct?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
About 100 mg of iron is absorbed per day

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Iron absorption is very limited, only a few milligrams per day, not 100 mg.

## answer_b
Iron is absorbed rapidly from the small intestine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The rate of iron absorption is slow, tightly regulated to body need, not rapid.

## answer_c
Iron is transported in the blood bound to transferrin

## explanation_c
Correct. In plasma, iron is carried bound to the beta-globulin transferrin, and excess is stored as ferritin, chiefly in hepatocytes. Absorption is slow and closely matched to requirement. Iron is taken up in the ferrous form, so ferrous compounds treat deficiency more effectively than ferric ones.

## answer_d
Iron must be oxidised from the ferrous to the ferric state for efficient absorption

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Iron is absorbed in the ferrous (Fe2+) state; it is reduced, not oxidised to ferric, for efficient uptake.

## answer_e
Iron is transported into enterocytes by a ferroportin transporter on the apical membrane

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Ferroportin exports iron across the basolateral membrane; apical uptake uses the divalent metal transporter, not ferroportin.

## title
Inhibitory neurotransmitter relaxing GI smooth muscle

## question
A 56-year-old man with dysphagia and regurgitation has a 'bird's beak' terminal oesophagus and manometry showing raised lower oesophageal sphincter pressure with no relaxation on swallowing (achalasia). Which is a putative inhibitory neurotransmitter responsible for relaxation of gastrointestinal smooth muscle?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Dopamine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Dopamine acts as an excitatory transmitter here, not an inhibitory relaxant.

## answer_b
Vasoactive intestinal peptide

## explanation_b
Correct. Relaxation of gastrointestinal smooth muscle follows activation of nonadrenergic, noncholinergic (NANC) enteric nerves whose inhibitory transmitters are vasoactive intestinal peptide and nitric oxide. Loss of these inhibitory neurons underlies the failure of sphincter relaxation in achalasia. Acetylcholine and substance P, by contrast, are excitatory.

## answer_c
Somatostatin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Somatostatin is a paracrine secretion with many effects, not the NANC inhibitory neurotransmitter that relaxes this smooth muscle.

## answer_d
Substance P

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Substance P is an excitatory neurotransmitter in the gut.

## answer_e
Acetylcholine

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Acetylcholine is the principal excitatory transmitter causing contraction, not relaxation.

## title
Activator of trypsinogen in the duodenum

## question
After trypsinogen is secreted into the duodenum, it is converted to its active form, trypsin, by which of the following?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Enteropeptidase

## explanation_a
Correct. Enteropeptidase (enterokinase), released from duodenal mucosal cells, cleaves inactive trypsinogen to active trypsin. Trypsin then activates the other pancreatic proenzymes, including chymotrypsinogen and the procarboxypeptidases, in an amplifying cascade. Its heavy glycosylation protects enteropeptidase itself from digestion.

## answer_b
Procarboxypeptidase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Procarboxypeptidase is itself a proenzyme activated by trypsin; it does not activate trypsinogen.

## answer_c
Pancreatic lipase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pancreatic lipase digests fat and has no role in activating trypsinogen.

## answer_d
Chymotrypsin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chymotrypsin is produced downstream by trypsin and does not initiate the cascade.

## answer_e
An alkaline pH

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Alkaline duodenal pH favours enzyme activity but does not by itself convert trypsinogen to trypsin.

## title
Bilirubin pattern in infectious (hepatocellular) hepatitis

## question
An 18-year-old presents two months after a tattoo with fever, right upper quadrant pain, nausea, vomiting and jaundice. Which laboratory finding is most likely in infectious hepatitis?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
An increase in plasma alkaline phosphatase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Alkaline phosphatase rises chiefly with bile-duct obstruction, not with hepatocellular disease.

## answer_b
An increase in plasma bile acids only

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Liver disease decreases bile-acid synthesis, and an isolated bile-acid rise does not characterise hepatitis.

## answer_c
An increase in both direct and indirect plasma bilirubin

## explanation_c
Correct. Infectious hepatitis is a hepatocellular injury that impairs uptake, conjugation and excretion of bilirubin while damaged cells regurgitate pigment. Total bilirubin therefore rises with both conjugated (direct) and unconjugated (indirect) fractions raised, typically about equally. This mixed pattern distinguishes hepatocellular jaundice from pure obstruction.

## answer_d
An increase in direct bilirubin with a decrease in indirect bilirubin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Both fractions rise in hepatocellular disease; indirect bilirubin does not fall.

## answer_e
A decrease in both direct and indirect plasma bilirubin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Bilirubin rises, not falls, when jaundice appears.

## title
Primary source of colonic gas

## question
A patient complains of flatulence. Gas within the colon is primarily derived from which of the following?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
CO2 liberated by the interaction of bicarbonate and H+

## explanation_a
Incorrect. CO2 from acid-bicarbonate interaction forms in the small intestine and does not accumulate as colonic gas.

## answer_b
Diffusion from the blood

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Diffusion from blood accounts only for some nitrogen, not the bulk of colonic gas.

## answer_c
Fermentation of undigested oligosaccharides by bacteria

## explanation_c
Correct. Colonic gas comes mainly from bacterial fermentation of undigested material, producing carbon dioxide, hydrogen and methane. Foods rich in indigestible carbohydrate, such as beans, markedly increase this production. Most of the gut's gas resides in the colon and is generated by this microbial fermentation.

## answer_d
Swallowed atmospheric air

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Swallowed air collects mainly in the stomach, not the colon.

## answer_e
Air pockets within foodstuffs

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Air trapped in food is a trivial source compared with bacterial fermentation.

## title
Form in which dietary fat enters the lymph

## question
A morbidly obese man has hypertension, hyperlipidaemia and type 2 diabetes. Processed dietary fat is extruded from the mucosal cells of the gastrointestinal tract into the lymphatic ducts in the form of which of the following?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Monoglycerides

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Monoglycerides are luminal digestion products taken up into the cell, not the exported lymph form.

## answer_b
Diglycerides

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Diglycerides are digestion intermediates, not the particle secreted into lymph.

## answer_c
Triglycerides

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Triglycerides are resynthesised inside the enterocyte but are exported packaged within chylomicrons, not as free triglyceride.

## answer_d
Chylomicrons

## explanation_d
Correct. Long-chain fats absorbed as monoglycerides and fatty acids are resynthesised into triglyceride inside the enterocyte and packaged, with cholesterol, phospholipid and apoprotein, into chylomicrons. These chylomicrons are extruded into the lacteals and carried in the lymph. Short- and medium-chain fatty acids instead enter the portal blood directly.

## answer_e
Free fatty acids

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Free fatty acids (short/medium chain) enter the portal blood, not the lymph.
