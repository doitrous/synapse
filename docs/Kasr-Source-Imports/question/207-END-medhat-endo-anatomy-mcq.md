## title
Visual field defect of a pituitary tumour

## question
Regarding a tumour of the pituitary gland, one of the following is true:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It induces a decrease of the intracranial pressure.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An expanding intracranial mass tends to raise, not lower, intracranial pressure; a decrease is not a feature of a pituitary tumour.

## answer_b
It can induce homonymous hemianopia.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Homonymous hemianopia results from a lesion behind the optic chiasma (optic tract or radiation), not from a midline chiasmatic compression.

## answer_c
It can induce bitemporal hemianopia.

## explanation_c
Correct. The pituitary lies immediately below the optic chiasma, so an enlarging adenoma presses on the crossing nasal retinal fibres in the chiasma. Loss of these fibres removes the temporal halves of both visual fields, producing the classic bitemporal (heteronymous) hemianopia.

## answer_d
It can induce cerebellar ataxia.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cerebellar ataxia arises from posterior-fossa (cerebellar) disease and has no anatomical relationship to a sellar pituitary mass.

## title
Blood supply of the anterior lobe of the pituitary

## question
Regarding the blood supply of the pituitary gland, one of the following is true:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Inferior hypophyseal artery supplies the anterior lobe.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The inferior hypophyseal artery supplies the posterior lobe (neurohypophysis), not the anterior lobe.

## answer_b
The hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal circulation supplies the anterior lobe.

## explanation_b
Correct. The superior hypophyseal arteries first form a capillary plexus in the median eminence and infundibulum; blood then drains into hypophyseal portal veins that carry it down to a second sinusoidal capillary bed in the anterior lobe. So the adenohypophysis receives almost all of its blood indirectly through this portal circulation rather than from an artery directly.

## answer_c
Superior hypophyseal artery supplies the anterior lobe directly.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior hypophyseal artery does not supply the anterior lobe directly; it drains first into the portal veins, which then perfuse the anterior lobe.

## answer_d
Its venous drainage is into the superior sagittal venous sinus.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Venous blood from the pituitary drains into the neighbouring cavernous sinuses, not the superior sagittal sinus.

## title
Medial relation of the thyroid lobe

## question
Regarding the relations of the thyroid gland, one of the following is TRUE:

## subject
endo

## status

## owner

## vignette

C

## answer_a
It is related to the superior laryngeal nerve.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. It is the external laryngeal nerve (running with the superior thyroid artery) and the recurrent laryngeal nerve that are the surgically important relations, not the trunk of the superior laryngeal nerve.

## answer_b
It is related posteriorly to the external carotid artery.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The gland is not related posteriorly to the external carotid; the carotid sheath (common carotid) lies posterolateral to the lobe.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Correct. Trachea and oesophagus are therefore true medial relations.

## answer_d
It is related to the middle constrictor muscle of the pharynx.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The inferior constrictor (not the middle constrictor) is a medial relation of the thyroid lobe; the middle constrictor is higher, at the level of the hyoid.

## title
Relations of the isthmus of the thyroid gland

## question
Regarding the relations of the isthmus of the thyroid gland, one of the following is true:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is related superiorly to the anastomosis of the superior thyroid arteries.

## explanation_a
Correct. The isthmus connects the two lobes across the front of the 2nd, 3rd and 4th tracheal rings. Its upper border is crossed by the anastomosing branches of the two superior thyroid arteries, so the superior thyroid arterial anastomosis is a superior relation of the isthmus.

## answer_b
It is related posteriorly to the 6th, 7th and 8th tracheal rings.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The isthmus lies on the 2nd to 4th tracheal rings, not the 6th to 8th.

It is related below to the inferior thyroid arteries.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The inferior thyroid veins (not arteries) leave the lower border of the isthmus; the inferior thyroid arteries reach the lobes posteriorly.

## answer_d
It is related anteriorly to the sternomastoid muscle.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Anteriorly the isthmus is covered by the strap muscles (sternohyoid and sternothyroid) and the pretracheal fascia, not the sternomastoid, which lies more laterally.

## title
Superior thyroid artery and the external laryngeal nerve

## question
Regarding the blood supply of the thyroid gland, one of the following is true:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The superior thyroid artery is related to the external laryngeal nerve.

## explanation_a
Correct. As the superior thyroid artery descends to the upper pole it runs with the external laryngeal nerve, which supplies the cricothyroid. Because the two are so close, the artery must be ligated CLOSE to the gland at the apex to avoid injuring the nerve, which would weaken the voice.

## answer_b
In thyroidectomy the superior thyroid artery is ligated away from the gland.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The superior thyroid artery is ligated close to the gland (near the upper pole), precisely to keep the ligature away from the external laryngeal nerve, not away from the gland.

## answer_c
The thyroidea ima artery is found in all persons.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The thyroidea ima is an inconstant artery present in only a minority of people, not in all persons.

## answer_d
In thyroidectomy the inferior thyroid artery is ligated close to the gland.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The inferior thyroid artery is ligated AWAY from the gland (laterally) to avoid the recurrent laryngeal nerve, which lies near the gland; ligating it close to the gland risks the nerve.

Incorrect. The inferior parathyroid is the more variable of the two in position; its site is not constant, whereas the superior parathyroid is relatively constant.

## title
Termination of the main pancreatic duct

## question
Regarding the main pancreatic duct, one of the following is true:

## subject
endo

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It is formed only by the duct of the dorsal pancreatic bud.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The main pancreatic duct (of Wirsung) is formed mainly from the duct of the VENTRAL bud plus the distal part of the dorsal duct, not the dorsal duct alone.

## answer_b
It crosses the accessory pancreatic duct in the head of the pancreas.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The accessory duct (of Santorini) drains the upper head and opens separately at the minor papilla; the two ducts do not cross in this way.

## answer_c
It opens at the middle of the posterolateral aspect of the 3rd part of the duodenum.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The main duct opens at the greater duodenal papilla on the posteromedial wall of the SECOND part of the duodenum, not the middle of the third part.

## answer_d
It joins the common bile duct to form the hepatopancreatic ampulla.

## explanation_d
Correct. Near its termination the main pancreatic duct is joined by the common bile duct. Together they form the dilated hepatopancreatic ampulla (of Vater). This opens on the summit of the major duodenal papilla in the second part of the duodenum, guarded by the sphincter of Oddi.
