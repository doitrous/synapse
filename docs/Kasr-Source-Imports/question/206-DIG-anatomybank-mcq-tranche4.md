## title
Midline infra-umbilical mass in a child

## question
A 6-year-old boy has a midline intra-abdominal mass just above the pubic symphysis, attached both to the umbilicus and to the apex of the bladder. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Meckel cyst

## explanation_a
Incorrect. There is no entity called a "Meckel cyst"; Meckel's diverticulum is an ileal outpouching from a persistent vitelline duct, located on the antimesenteric border of the ileum, not in the midline suprapubic region.

## answer_b
Meckel diverticulum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A Meckel's diverticulum arises from the ileum (persistent vitelline duct) and lies in the right lower quadrant, not attached to the umbilicus and bladder apex in the midline.

## answer_c
Omphalocele

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An omphalocele is a congenital defect in which abdominal viscera herniate into the base of the umbilical cord covered by a membranous sac; it presents at birth, not as an intra-abdominal midline mass in a child.

## answer_d
Urachal cyst

## explanation_d
Correct. The urachus (median umbilical ligament) is the fibrous remnant of the allantois connecting the apex of the bladder to the umbilicus. Incomplete obliteration leaves an epithelial-lined cavity — a urachal cyst — in the midline between the umbilicus and bladder apex.

## title
Organ injured in left lower rib fracture

## question
A 25-year-old man is brought to the emergency department in shock after a road traffic accident, with tenderness along the left mid-axillary line. X-ray shows fractures of the left 9th and 10th ribs. Which organ is most likely injured?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Left kidney

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The left kidney lies more posteriorly and is largely protected; it is related to the 11th and 12th ribs behind, and its injury usually presents with haematuria and loin (not mid-axillary) tenderness.

## answer_b
Stomach

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The stomach lies anteriorly in the epigastrium and left hypochondrium and is not the organ classically ruptured by fractures of the left lower ribs.

## answer_c
Spleen

## explanation_c
Correct. The spleen lies deep to the left 9th, 10th and 11th ribs, its long axis along the 10th rib. Fractures of these ribs on the left, with hypotension and mid-axillary tenderness, point to splenic rupture — the commonest solid-organ injury in left lower thoracic trauma.

## answer_d
Descending colon

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The descending colon is retroperitoneal in the left flank and is not the organ typically lacerated by fractured left lower ribs.

Foregut derivative

## question
Which of the following organs is a derivative of the foregut?

## subject
gi

## status

Liver

Correct. The liver develops as the hepatic diverticulum, an endodermal outgrowth of the caudal foregut (into the septum transversum); the biliary tree and ventral pancreas share this foregut origin, which is why they are supplied by the coeliac trunk.

Jejunum

Incorrect. The jejunum is a midgut derivative, supplied by the superior mesenteric artery.

## answer_d
Fourth part of the duodenum

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The duodenum distal to the opening of the bile duct (including its fourth part) is a midgut derivative; only the part proximal to the ampulla is foregut.

Lymphatic spread of gastric antral carcinoma

## question
Which of the following is a content of the rectus sheath?

## subject

The fibres of the external oblique pass downward and backward

The pyramidalis is a content of the rectus sheath

Correct. The rectus sheath encloses the rectus abdominis and the small triangular pyramidalis anterior to it, together with the superior and inferior epigastric vessels and the terminal branches of the lower six thoracic nerves.

## subject

The cardiac orifice is guarded by an anatomical sphincter

Correct. The stomach is supplied entirely by branches of the coeliac trunk: the left gastric directly, the right gastric and right gastro-epiploic via the hepatic artery, and the short gastric and left gastro-epiploic via the splenic artery.

## answer_e
The right and left gastric arteries lie along its greater curvature

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The right and left gastric arteries run along the lesser curvature; the gastro-epiploic (gastro-omental) arteries run along the greater curvature.

Arrangement of structures at the renal hilum

At the hilum the renal vein lies anterior to the renal artery

The iliacus muscle is among the posterior relations

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The posterior relations of the kidney are the diaphragm, psoas major, quadratus lumborum and transversus abdominis, with the subcostal, iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves — not iliacus.
