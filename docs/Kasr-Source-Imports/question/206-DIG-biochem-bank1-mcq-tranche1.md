## title
Feature that is NOT true of SGLT1

## question
All the following about SGLT1 are correct EXCEPT:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is present in muscle and adipose tissue

## explanation_a
Correct. SGLT1 is the sodium-glucose cotransporter of the apical membrane of enterocytes (small intestine) and the renal proximal tubule; it is NOT expressed in muscle or adipose tissue, where glucose entry is instead by the facilitative GLUT4 transporter. This statement is therefore the exception.

## answer_b
It is insulin independent

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the answer. SGLT1 is genuinely insulin independent: intestinal and renal glucose uptake by SGLT1 does not require insulin, unlike GLUT4-mediated uptake in muscle and fat.

## answer_c
It causes active uptake of glucose against its concentration gradient

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the answer. SGLT1 does move glucose against its concentration gradient. This is secondary active transport: it couples uphill glucose entry to the downhill movement of sodium.

## answer_d
It transports sodium down its concentration gradient

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the answer. SGLT1 co-transports sodium down its electrochemical gradient (maintained by the basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase), and this favourable sodium gradient supplies the energy that drives glucose uptake.

## title
Transporter that absorbs fructose

## question
Fructose is absorbed in the small intestine through:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
SGLT1

## explanation_a
Incorrect. SGLT1 is the sodium-dependent cotransporter for glucose and galactose, not fructose.

## answer_b
GLUT3

## explanation_b
Incorrect. GLUT3 is a high-affinity glucose transporter of neurons and other cells; it does not mediate intestinal fructose absorption.

## answer_c
GLUT5

## explanation_c
Correct. Fructose is taken up across the apical enterocyte membrane by GLUT5, a facilitative transporter with high specificity for fructose. Uptake is sodium-independent and follows the concentration gradient (facilitated diffusion); fructose then leaves the enterocyte basolaterally via GLUT2.

## answer_d
GLUT4

## explanation_d
Incorrect. GLUT4 is the insulin-responsive glucose transporter of muscle and adipose tissue, not an intestinal fructose transporter.

## title
Nature of glucose uptake by muscle

## question
Uptake of glucose by muscles:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Occurs by an active transport mechanism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Glucose enters muscle by facilitated diffusion through GLUT4, moving down its concentration gradient; it is not active transport.

## answer_b
Is energy dependent

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Because it is facilitated diffusion, the transport itself consumes no ATP and is not energy dependent.

## answer_c
Is linked to sodium uptake

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sodium-coupled glucose uptake (via SGLT1) occurs in the intestine and kidney, not in skeletal muscle.

## answer_d
Is enhanced by insulin

## explanation_d
Correct. Insulin enhances glucose uptake by muscle: it triggers translocation of GLUT4 transporters from intracellular vesicles to the plasma membrane, increasing the number of transporters and hence the rate of facilitated glucose entry. This insulin-dependent GLUT4 recruitment is the defining feature of glucose uptake in muscle and adipose tissue.
