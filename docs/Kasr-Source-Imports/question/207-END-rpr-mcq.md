Inferior gluteal nerve as a branch of the sacral plexus

## question
Which one of the following nerves is a branch of the sacral plexus?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inferior gluteal nerve.

## explanation_a
Correct. The inferior gluteal nerve arises from the sacral plexus, carrying fibres from L5, S1 and S2. It leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis. It then supplies the gluteus maximus, the main extensor of the hip.

## answer_b
Femoral nerve.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The femoral nerve is a branch of the lumbar plexus (L2-L4).

## answer_c
Obturator nerve.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The obturator nerve is a branch of the lumbar plexus (L2-L4).

## answer_d
Lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh arises from the lumbar plexus (L2-L3).

Incorrect. The median sacral artery arises from the back of the abdominal aorta just above its bifurcation.

## title
Arterial supply of the ureter from the gonadal artery

## question
Which one of the following arteries gives a blood supply to the ureter?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

A

Testicular or ovarian artery.

## explanation_a
Correct. The ureter has a segmental blood supply along its whole length. In the abdomen it receives constant branches from the renal and the gonadal (testicular or ovarian) arteries, and in the pelvis from the vesical and uterine arteries. Of the options offered, only the gonadal artery is a recognised ureteric supplier.

## answer_b
Superior vesical artery.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The superior vesical artery supplies mainly the upper bladder; it is not the recognised principal ureteric supplier among these options (the lower ureter is supplied more by the inferior vesical/uterine arteries).

## answer_c
Superior mesenteric artery.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior mesenteric artery supplies the midgut, not the ureter.

## answer_d
External iliac artery.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The external iliac artery gives no branch to the ureter; it supplies the lower limb (its branches are the inferior epigastric and deep circumflex iliac arteries).

## question
Two months after a vasectomy in which the ductus deferens was ligated in the upper part of the scrotum, the ejaculate contains:

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Prostatic fluid is present, but so is the seminal vesicle secretion, which forms the largest volume fraction of semen, so 'prostatic only' is wrong.

Sperm only.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sperm are exactly what is lost once the ductus deferens is divided.

## answer_d
Sperm and seminal fluid.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sperm are absent after vasectomy; seminal fluid remains along with prostatic fluid.

## question
External haemorrhoids are:

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. That description (superior rectal tributaries, mucous membrane) is an internal haemorrhoid.

## answer_b
Varicosities of tributaries of the inferior rectal veins covered with skin.

## explanation_b
Correct. External haemorrhoids arise below the pectinate line, in the territory of the inferior rectal veins. A tear in the anal valves extending down to the anus.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A tear in the anal lining is an anal fissure, not a haemorrhoid.

## answer_d
An abscess in the submucosa of the anal canal.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A submucosal collection of pus is an anal abscess, not a haemorrhoid.
