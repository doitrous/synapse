## title
Inferior gluteal nerve as a branch of the sacral plexus

## question
Which one of the following nerves is a branch of the sacral plexus?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inferior gluteal nerve.

## explanation_a
Correct. The inferior gluteal nerve arises from the sacral plexus, carrying fibres from L5, S1 and S2. It leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis. It then supplies the gluteus maximus, the main extensor of the hip.

## answer_b
Femoral nerve.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The femoral nerve is a branch of the lumbar plexus (L2-L4).

## answer_c
Obturator nerve.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The obturator nerve is a branch of the lumbar plexus (L2-L4).

## answer_d
Lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh arises from the lumbar plexus (L2-L3).

## title
Deep artery of the penis as a branch of the internal pudendal artery

## question
Which one of the following arteries is a branch of the internal pudendal artery?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Deep artery of the penis.

## explanation_a
Correct. The internal pudendal artery is the chief artery of the perineum. After giving its inferior rectal and perineal branches it ends by supplying the erectile tissue. Its terminal branches are the artery of the bulb, the dorsal artery of the penis and the deep artery of the penis, the last running within the corpus cavernosum.

## answer_b
Medial sacral artery.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 'Medial sacral' is not a named branch here; the midline sacral vessel is the median sacral artery, which arises from the abdominal aorta, not the internal pudendal.

## answer_c
Median sacral artery.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The median sacral artery arises from the back of the abdominal aorta just above its bifurcation.

## answer_d
Superior rectal artery.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The superior rectal artery is the continuation of the inferior mesenteric artery.

## title
Arterial supply of the ureter from the gonadal artery

## question
Which one of the following arteries gives a blood supply to the ureter?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Testicular or ovarian artery.

## explanation_a
Correct. The ureter has a segmental blood supply along its whole length. In the abdomen it receives constant branches from the renal and the gonadal (testicular or ovarian) arteries, and in the pelvis from the vesical and uterine arteries. Of the options offered, only the gonadal artery is a recognised ureteric supplier.

## answer_b
Superior vesical artery.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The superior vesical artery supplies mainly the upper bladder; it is not the recognised principal ureteric supplier among these options (the lower ureter is supplied more by the inferior vesical/uterine arteries).

## answer_c
Superior mesenteric artery.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior mesenteric artery supplies the midgut, not the ureter.

## answer_d
External iliac artery.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The external iliac artery gives no branch to the ureter; it supplies the lower limb (its branches are the inferior epigastric and deep circumflex iliac arteries).

## title
Ischiocavernosus lies in the superficial perineal pouch

## question
The ischiocavernosus muscle is found in the:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
True pelvis.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. It is a perineal, not a true-pelvic, muscle; it lies below the pelvic diaphragm.

## answer_b
Superficial perineal space.

## explanation_b
Correct. The superficial perineal pouch lies between the perineal membrane and the deep perineal fascia. Its three paired muscles are the ischiocavernosus, the bulbospongiosus and the superficial transverse perineal muscle. The ischiocavernosus covers the crus of the penis or clitoris and helps to maintain erection.

## answer_c
Deep perineal space.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The deep perineal pouch contains the deep transverse perineal muscle and sphincter urethrae, not the ischiocavernosus.

## answer_d
Ischiorectal fossa.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The ischiorectal fossa is a fat-filled space lateral to the anal canal; the ischiocavernosus is not one of its contents.

## title
Greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands in the superficial perineal pouch

## question
Which one of the following structures is present in the superficial perineal pouch in the female?

## subject
gyn

## status

## vignette

C

## answer_a
Sphincter urethrae muscle.

## explanation_a

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The deep transverse perineal muscle lies in the deep perineal pouch.

## answer_c
Greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands.

## explanation_c
Correct. In the female the superficial perineal pouch holds the crura and bulbs of the erectile tissue with their muscles. Behind the bulbs of the vestibule lie the greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands. These glands are therefore contents of the superficial, not the deep, perineal pouch.

## answer_d
Bulbourethral glands.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The bulbourethral glands are the male homologue and lie in the deep perineal pouch in the male; they are not present in the female.
