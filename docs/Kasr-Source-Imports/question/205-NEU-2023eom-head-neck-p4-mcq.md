## title
Primary hyperalgesia

## question
In primary hyperalgesia:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It occurs in the healthy area surrounding the flare

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Spread to the healthy skin SURROUNDING the flare is the feature of SECONDARY hyperalgesia (central sensitisation); primary hyperalgesia is confined to the injured area itself.

## answer_b
It is due to release of inflammatory mediators

## explanation_b
Correct. Primary hyperalgesia results from peripheral sensitisation of nociceptors in the injured area by locally released inflammatory mediators such as bradykinin, prostaglandins, histamine, K+ and substance P. These mediators lower the firing threshold of the nociceptors, so the region becomes abnormally painful. It is the classic peripheral mechanism of increased pain at a site of injury.

## answer_c
The pain threshold is increased

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sensitisation LOWERS, not raises, the pain threshold; a raised threshold would mean reduced pain, the opposite of hyperalgesia.

## answer_d
There is decreased pain sensibility

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hyperalgesia means INCREASED pain sensibility; decreased sensibility (hypoalgesia) is the opposite state.

Nature of skeletal muscle tone

## question
Skeletal muscle tone is:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

B

a dynamic stretch reflex

## explanation_a

## explanation_b
Correct. Muscle tone is the continuous partial contraction of resting muscle produced by a tonic stretch reflex: sustained stretch keeps spindle afferents firing, maintaining a background of alpha-motor-neuron discharge to the muscle. ## answer_d
decreased during standing upright

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tone is INCREASED, not decreased, during standing upright, because antigravity muscles must contract to maintain posture.

## title
The caloric test principle

## question
Which of the following is correct as regards the caloric method of stimulation of the semicircular canals?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Checks two SCCs in two ears

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The caloric test irrigates one ear at a time, so it does not check two canals in two ears simultaneously.

## answer_b
Checks one SCC in one ear

## explanation_b
Correct. Irrigating one external auditory canal with warm or cold water sets up a convection current in the endolymph of the nearby lateral semicircular canal of THAT ear, so the test evaluates one semicircular canal in one ear at a time.
