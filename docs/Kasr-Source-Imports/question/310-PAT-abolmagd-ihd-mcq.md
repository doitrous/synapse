## title
What ischaemic heart disease is

## question
The following is true about ischemic heart disease (IHD):

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is due to embolism of the coronary arteries in more than 90% of cases.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. In more than 90% of cases IHD is due to coronary ATHEROSCLEROSIS, not embolism; emboli are a rare cause.

## answer_b
It represents a group of syndromes resulting from myocardial ischaemia.

## explanation_b
Correct. Ischaemic heart disease is not a single entity but a GROUP OF SYNDROMES (angina pectoris, acute myocardial infarction, chronic ischaemic heart disease/heart failure and sudden cardiac death) that all result from an imbalance between myocardial oxygen demand and coronary blood supply, i.e. MYOCARDIAL ISCHAEMIA. The underlying cause in the great majority is coronary atherosclerosis. Grouping these syndromes under one heading reflects their shared ischaemic mechanism.

## answer_c
Sudden coronary occlusion always causes sudden death.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sudden coronary occlusion does not ALWAYS cause sudden death; outcome ranges from angina to infarction to sudden death depending on collaterals, site and extent.

## answer_d
Posterior infarctions are the most common types of myocardial infarction.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ANTERIOR wall infarctions (left anterior descending territory) are the commonest, not posterior.

## title
The cause of IHD in >90% of cases

## question
Ischemic heart disease, in more than 90% of the cases, is due to:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Coronary emboli

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Coronary emboli (e.g. from infective endocarditis or mural thrombi) are a rare cause of IHD, not the >90% cause.

## answer_b
Coronary stenosis due to syphilitic aortitis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Syphilitic aortitis can narrow the coronary ostia but is a rare, historical cause, not the dominant one.

## answer_c
Coronary spasm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Coronary spasm (e.g. Prinzmetal angina) contributes in a minority and often on top of atherosclerotic plaque; it is not the >90% cause.

## answer_d
Coronary atherosclerosis

## explanation_d
Correct. In more than 90% of cases ischaemic heart disease is caused by CORONARY ATHEROSCLEROSIS, which narrows the coronary lumen and, on plaque rupture with superimposed thrombosis, precipitates acute ischaemia and infarction. The other listed mechanisms (emboli, spasm, syphilitic ostial stenosis) are individually uncommon. Atherosclerotic coronary artery disease is therefore essentially synonymous with IHD in practice.

This time-course error is what the question tests.

Incorrect (a true statement). Wavy myocardial fibres at the infarct margin are among the earliest microscopic changes.

## title
Not a feature of transmural MI

## question
The following is not a feature of transmural myocardial infarction:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Ischaemic necrosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it IS a feature). A transmural infarct is ischaemic (coagulative) necrosis of myocardium.

## answer_b
Always accompanied by thrombosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it IS a feature). Transmural infarction is typically caused by acute occlusive thrombosis on a ruptured coronary plaque, so it is characteristically accompanied by thrombosis.

## answer_c
Pale infarction

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it IS a feature). Being in the solid left-ventricular wall, it is a pale (anaemic) infarct.

## answer_d
Does not involve the whole thickness of the myocardium

## explanation_d
Correct (the exception). By definition a TRANSMURAL infarct extends through the FULL (whole) thickness of the ventricular wall, from endocardium to epicardium. Saying it 'does not involve the whole thickness' describes a SUBENDOCARDIAL (non-transmural) infarct instead, so this statement is false for transmural MI. Full-wall involvement is precisely what the word transmural means.

## title
When the fibrous scar of MI appears

## question
In myocardial infarction, the fibrous scar appears after:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
1-3 days

## explanation_a
Incorrect. At 1-3 days there is coagulative necrosis with neutrophil infiltration, not scarring.

## answer_b
4-7 days

## explanation_b
Incorrect. At 4-7 days macrophages are removing dead myocytes; granulation tissue is only beginning, not a fibrous scar.

## answer_c
1-2 weeks

## explanation_c
Incorrect. At 1-2 weeks well-developed granulation tissue with early collagen is present, but a dense fibrous scar has not yet formed.

## answer_d
2-6 weeks

## explanation_d
Correct. The dense collagenous FIBROUS SCAR of a healed myocardial infarct is established over roughly 2-6 WEEKS (with maturation continuing towards about 2 months): granulation tissue laid down in the second week is progressively replaced by collagen. This late fibrous replacement marks the healed stage of infarction. Hence the scar appears after about 2-6 weeks, not in the first days.

## title
Not a feature of healed MI

## question
The following may be features of healed myocardial infarction except:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cardiac aneurysm

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it IS a feature). A thin fibrous scar can bulge to form a ventricular (cardiac) aneurysm, a recognised feature of healed MI.

## answer_b
Increased serum CPK

## explanation_b
Correct (the exception). An INCREASED serum CPK (and troponin) is a marker of ACUTE myocyte necrosis; these enzymes rise and then normalise within days of the acute event. In a HEALED infarct (scar tissue), there is no ongoing necrosis, so cardiac enzymes are NORMAL, not raised. A persistently elevated CPK is therefore not a feature of a healed MI.

## answer_c
Arrhythmias

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it IS a feature). The fibrous scar disrupts conduction and is a substrate for chronic arrhythmias.

## answer_d
Chronic heart failure

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it IS a feature). Loss of functioning myocardium leads to chronic (congestive) heart failure.
