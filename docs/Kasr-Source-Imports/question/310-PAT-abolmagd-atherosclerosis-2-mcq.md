## title
Not a major atherosclerosis risk factor (elevated HDL)

## question
All of the followings are considered as major risk factors in atherosclerosis except:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cigarette smoking

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS a major risk factor). Cigarette smoking is a major modifiable risk factor for atherosclerosis, promoting endothelial injury and accelerating atherogenesis.

## answer_b
Elevated high density lipoprotein "HDL"

## explanation_b
Correct. An ELEVATED level of HDL (‘good’ cholesterol) is PROTECTIVE against atherosclerosis, not a risk factor — HDL performs reverse cholesterol transport, carrying cholesterol from vessel walls back to the liver for excretion. It is a LOW HDL that raises risk; a high HDL lowers it. Listing high HDL among the major risk factors reverses its true protective role, which is exactly the trap this question sets. The genuine major risk factors are hypertension, diabetes mellitus, smoking and hyperlipidaemia (high LDL).

## answer_c
Diabetes mellitus

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this IS a major risk factor). Diabetes mellitus is a major risk factor, roughly doubling atherosclerotic risk through dyslipidaemia and endothelial damage.

## answer_d
Hypertension

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this IS a major risk factor). Hypertension is a major modifiable risk factor, injuring endothelium and accelerating plaque formation.

## title
False characterisation of atherosclerosis (arteries and veins)

## question
Atherosclerosis is characterized by all except:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
A very common disease

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS true of atherosclerosis). Atherosclerosis is a very common disease and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide through ischaemic heart disease and stroke.

## answer_b
Affects arteries and veins

## explanation_b
Correct. This statement is FALSE, so it is the exception: atherosclerosis affects ARTERIES only — the intima of large elastic arteries (aorta, carotid, iliac) and medium muscular arteries (coronary, popliteal) — not veins. Veins undergo other changes (phlebosclerosis, varicosity) but do not form atheromatous plaques, and the myocardium is injured only indirectly through coronary narrowing. Calling atherosclerosis a disease of arteries AND veins misstates its target vessel, which is the point being tested.

## answer_c
Hypertension is a major risk factor

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this IS true of atherosclerosis). Hypertension is one of the major modifiable risk factors for atherosclerosis.

## answer_d
Lipid accumulated in the vessel wall

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this IS true of atherosclerosis). Accumulation of lipid (cholesterol) in the arterial intima is the defining event of the atheromatous plaque.

## title
Not true for atherosclerosis (fibrinoid necrosis of media)

## question
Which of the following is not true for atherosclerosis:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Common in the aorta

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS true). Atherosclerosis is common in the aorta (especially the abdominal aorta), one of its classic target vessels along with the coronary and cerebral arteries.

## answer_b
Complicated by thrombosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this IS true). Thrombosis over a ruptured or ulcerated plaque is a major complication of atherosclerosis, causing acute arterial occlusion (e.g. myocardial infarction).

## answer_c
Predispose to aneurysm

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this IS true). Atherosclerosis weakens the arterial wall (media) beneath a plaque and predisposes to aneurysm formation, classically the abdominal aortic aneurysm.

## answer_d
Fibrinoid necrosis of media

## explanation_d
Correct. This statement is FALSE, so it is the exception: FIBRINOID NECROSIS of the media (wall) is the lesion of MALIGNANT HYPERTENSION and of necrotising vasculitides, not of atherosclerosis. Atherosclerosis is an intimal plaque disease — lipid deposition, fibrous cap, calcification, ulceration, thrombosis and aneurysmal weakening — and does not produce fibrinoid necrosis of the media. Confusing the arteriolar fibrinoid necrosis of malignant hypertension with an atherosclerotic feature is exactly the error tested here.

RHEUMATIC HEART DISEASE does not predispose to atherosclerosis: it is a post-streptococcal immune-mediated valvular disease (chronic valve scarring, classically mitral stenosis), an entirely different process from the lipid-driven intimal plaque disease of atherosclerosis. Incorrect (this DOES predispose). A high-fat diet raises LDL cholesterol and promotes intimal lipid deposition, predisposing to atherosclerosis.

## title
Not a pathological feature of atherosclerosis (hyaline thickening)

## question
Pathological features of atherosclerosis include all except:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Fatty streaks

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS a feature). Fatty streaks — intimal collections of lipid-laden macrophages (foam cells) — are the earliest lesion of atherosclerosis.

## answer_b
Atheromatous plaques

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this IS a feature). The atheromatous (fibrofatty) plaque, a raised intimal lesion with a lipid core and fibrous cap, is the characteristic established lesion of atherosclerosis.

## answer_c
Hyaline thickening of arterial wall

## explanation_c
Correct. HYALINE THICKENING of the arterial wall (hyaline arteriolosclerosis) is the small-vessel lesion of BENIGN HYPERTENSION and of diabetes, not a feature of atherosclerosis. The pathological features of atherosclerosis are fatty streaks, atheromatous plaques and their complications (ulceration, calcification, thrombosis, haemorrhage into the plaque, aneurysm). Homogeneous hyaline thickening of arteriolar walls belongs to a different (hypertensive) process, which is the discrimination this question requires.

## answer_d
Thrombus formation

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this IS a feature). Thrombus formation over a complicated (ulcerated/ruptured) plaque is a classic feature and complication of atherosclerosis.

## title
Cause of intimal thickening in atherosclerosis (all of the above)

## question
Intimal thickening in atherosclerosis is due to:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Accumulation of lipids

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the single answer. Accumulation of lipid (extracellular cholesterol and intracellular foam cells) does contribute to intimal thickening, but it is not the only mechanism, so this alone is incomplete.

## answer_b
Smooth muscle proliferation

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the single answer. Migration and proliferation of smooth muscle cells from the media into the intima is a key contributor, but it is not the only one.

## answer_c
Fibrous tissue formation

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the single answer. Fibrous tissue (collagen and matrix) laid down by the smooth muscle cells forms the fibrous cap and adds to the thickening, but it is not the only mechanism.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. The intimal thickening of the atheromatous plaque results from ALL of these together: accumulation of lipid, proliferation of smooth muscle cells (which migrate from the media), and their production of fibrous tissue (collagen/extracellular matrix). This is the essence of the response-to-injury sequence — endothelial injury, lipid entry, smooth-muscle migration/proliferation and matrix synthesis build the fibrofatty plaque. Because each listed component genuinely contributes, ‘all of the above’ is the correct answer.
