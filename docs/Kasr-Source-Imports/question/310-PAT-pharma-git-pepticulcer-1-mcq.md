## title
Antisecretory drug that irreversibly inhibits the H+/K+ ATPase

## question
Which of the following is an antisecretory drug that inhibits gastric acid secretion by IRREVERSIBLE inhibition of the H+/K+ ATPase?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Ranitidine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ranitidine is a reversible, competitive H2-receptor antagonist; it does not act on the H+/K+ ATPase.

## answer_b
Pyridoxine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pyridoxine is vitamin B6, not an antisecretory drug.

## answer_c
Pirenzepine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pirenzepine selectively blocks muscarinic M1 receptors; it is not a proton-pump inhibitor.

## answer_d
Omeprazole

## explanation_d
Correct. Omeprazole is a proton-pump inhibitor: after activation in the acidic canaliculus it binds the gastric H+/K+ ATPase (proton pump) COVALENTLY and IRREVERSIBLY, blocking the final common step of acid secretion. New pump synthesis is needed to restore acid output, which is why its effect outlasts the plasma half-life. Irreversible H+/K+ ATPase inhibition identifies omeprazole.

## title
PGE1 analogue for NSAID-induced ulcer prophylaxis

## question
Which mucosal protective agent is a PGE1 analogue prescribed for long-term prophylaxis of peptic ulcer associated with the use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Misoprostol

## explanation_a
Correct. Misoprostol is a synthetic PROSTAGLANDIN E1 (PGE1) analogue. NSAIDs cause ulcers by inhibiting protective mucosal prostaglandins; misoprostol replaces them, stimulating mucus and bicarbonate secretion and reducing acid, so it prevents NSAID-induced ulcers. It is contraindicated in pregnancy (it causes uterine contraction/abortion). Being a PGE1 analogue used for NSAID-ulcer prophylaxis identifies it.

## answer_b
Carbenoxolone

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Carbenoxolone is a mucosal protective but not a prostaglandin analogue, and it causes mineralocorticoid-like adverse effects.

## answer_c
Sucralfate

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sucralfate is a locally acting coating agent, not a prostaglandin analogue.

## answer_d
Colloidal bismuth

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Colloidal bismuth is a locally acting protective with anti-H. pylori activity, not a PGE1 analogue.

## title
Standard triple therapy regimen for H. pylori

## question
If investigations for peptic ulcer show H. pylori infection, the proper triple-therapy regimen for eradication is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Tetracycline is used in bismuth-based QUADRUPLE therapy, not in standard triple therapy.

Omeprazole + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin

## explanation_c
Correct. Standard first-line triple therapy is a PROTON-PUMP INHIBITOR plus TWO antibiotics — omeprazole + amoxicillin + clarithromycin — for 10-14 days. Omeprazole + Amoxicillin + Metronidazole

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This is an acceptable alternative (e.g. in penicillin allergy metronidazole replaces amoxicillin), but the standard triple therapy asked for is PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin.

## title
Antacid causing constipation and hypophosphataemia

## question
Which of the following is an antacid that can cause constipation and hypophosphataemia?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

A

## answer_a
Aluminium hydroxide gel

## explanation_a
Correct. ALUMINIUM HYDROXIDE slows gastrointestinal motility, causing CONSTIPATION, and binds dietary phosphate in the gut to form insoluble aluminium phosphate, producing HYPOPHOSPHATAEMIA. Incorrect. Sodium bicarbonate causes systemic alkalosis and sodium load, not constipation and hypophosphataemia.

## answer_d
Calcium carbonate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Calcium carbonate can cause constipation and milk-alkali syndrome but not hypophosphataemia (it does not bind phosphate the way aluminium does).

## title
Common adverse effect of proton-pump inhibitors

## question
Which of the following is a recognised adverse effect of long-term proton-pump inhibitor therapy?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Constipation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Constipation is characteristic of aluminium/calcium antacids, not a defining PPI effect (PPIs more often cause diarrhoea, including C. difficile).

## answer_b
CNS excitability

## explanation_b
Incorrect. PPIs do not cause CNS excitability.

## answer_c
Killing of GIT bacterial flora

## explanation_c
Incorrect. PPIs do not directly kill gut flora; by raising gastric pH they alter the flora and raise enteric-infection risk, but this is not the classic listed adverse effect.

## answer_d
Decreasing the bioavailability of vitamin B12

## explanation_d
Correct. Gastric acid is required to release vitamin B12 from dietary protein for absorption. By profoundly suppressing acid, long-term PPIs reduce B12 (and also iron, calcium and magnesium) absorption, so DECREASED VITAMIN B12 bioavailability is a recognised adverse effect. Long-term users may need monitoring.

## title
Drug interacting with the antiplatelet effect of clopidogrel

## question
Which of the following might interact with the antiplatelet effect of clopidogrel?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Omeprazole

## explanation_a
Correct. Clopidogrel is a prodrug that requires activation by the hepatic enzyme CYP2C19. OMEPRAZOLE (and esomeprazole) inhibits CYP2C19, reducing conversion of clopidogrel to its active metabolite and potentially blunting its antiplatelet effect. This drug interaction is why a PPI with less CYP2C19 inhibition (e.g. pantoprazole) is preferred with clopidogrel.

## answer_b
Famotidine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Famotidine is an H2 blocker that does not inhibit CYP2C19, so it does not meaningfully affect clopidogrel activation.

## answer_c
Rabeprazole

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Rabeprazole is largely metabolised non-enzymatically and has the least CYP2C19 inhibition among PPIs, so it interacts least with clopidogrel.

## answer_d
Pirenzepine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pirenzepine is an M1 antimuscarinic and does not inhibit CYP2C19 or clopidogrel activation.
