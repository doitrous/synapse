## title
Structure separating the lentiform nucleus from the claustrum

## question
Which structure separates the lentiform nucleus from the claustrum?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Thalamus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The thalamus lies medial to the lentiform nucleus, deep to the internal capsule, and is not interposed between the lentiform nucleus and the claustrum.

## answer_b
External capsule

## explanation_b
Correct. The external capsule is the thin sheet of white matter lying immediately lateral to the lentiform nucleus (putamen), separating it from the claustrum. Lateral to the claustrum a second thin lamina, the extreme capsule, separates it from the insular cortex. The internal capsule, by contrast, lies medial to the lentiform nucleus.

## answer_c
Internal capsule

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The internal capsule lies medial to the lentiform nucleus, between it and the caudate/thalamus, not between the lentiform nucleus and the claustrum.

## answer_d
Head of caudate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The head of the caudate nucleus lies medial and anterior, forming the lateral wall of the anterior horn of the lateral ventricle; it does not lie between lentiform nucleus and claustrum.

6th (abducent)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The abducent nerve (CN VI) emerges at the pontomedullary junction, between the pons and the pyramid of the medulla.

Incorrect. The thalamus is a diencephalic mass forming part of the wall of the third ventricle, unrelated to the fourth ventricle roof.

Region to which the mammillary bodies belong

## question
The mammillary bodies are part of which region of the brain?

## subject

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Although the mammillary bodies lie just in front of the posterior perforated substance near the midbrain, they are hypothalamic, not midbrain, structures.

Diencephalon

Correct. The mammillary bodies are two small swellings of the posterior hypothalamus, and the hypothalamus is a part of the diencephalon. Being hypothalamic, they are diencephalic.

Medulla

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The medulla oblongata is the lowest part of the brainstem and contains no mammillary bodies.

## question
The lateral ventricle communicates with the third ventricle through which opening?

## subject

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The lateral aperture (foramen of Luschka) connects the fourth ventricle with the subarachnoid space at the cerebellopontine angle.

Correct. Each lateral ventricle opens into the midline third ventricle through the interventricular foramen (of Monro), bounded by the anterior column of the fornix in front and the anterior tubercle of the thalamus behind.
