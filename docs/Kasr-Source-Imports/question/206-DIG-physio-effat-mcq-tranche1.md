QST-KASR206DIG-PHYSIOEFFAT-1

## title
Reflex causing the urge to defecate on duodenal distension

## question
About thirty minutes after a mixed meal a man feels the urge to defecate. Which reflex produces this urge when the duodenum is distended?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Duodenocolic reflex

## explanation_a
Correct. Distension of the duodenum (and stomach) after a meal triggers the duodenocolic (and gastrocolic) reflex. This reflex drives mass movements in the descending and sigmoid colon. The result is the familiar post-prandial urge to defecate.

## answer_b
Enterogastric reflex

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The enterogastric reflex runs in the opposite direction: signals from the duodenum slow gastric emptying and reduce gastric motility and secretion; it does not cause the urge to defecate.

## answer_c
Intestino-intestinal reflex

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The intestino-intestinal reflex causes reflex relaxation (inhibition) of the rest of the bowel when one segment is over-distended or injured; it is protective, not a defecation trigger.

## answer_d
Rectosphincteric reflex

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The rectosphincteric (defecation) reflex is initiated by distension of the rectum, not the duodenum, and mediates internal-anal-sphincter relaxation at the final step of defecation.

## title
Deficiency after resection of the ileum

## question
The entire ileum and part of the jejunum are resected after an accident. Which deficiency is this man most likely to develop?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Atrophic gastritis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Atrophic gastritis is an autoimmune or Helicobacter-related disease of the gastric mucosa; it is not caused by resecting small intestine.

## answer_b
Constipation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Loss of ileum shortens the absorptive surface and, with bile-salt malabsorption, tends to cause diarrhoea, not constipation.

## answer_c
Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Reflux disease reflects an incompetent lower oesophageal sphincter; it is unrelated to ileal resection.

## answer_d
Vitamin B12 deficiency

## explanation_d
Correct. Vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin) is absorbed almost exclusively in the terminal ileum, bound to intrinsic factor from gastric parietal cells. Removing the ileum abolishes this site, so B12 deficiency (and, with time, megaloblastic anaemia) is the expected result.

## title
GI hormone released by all three macronutrients

## question
The presence of fat, carbohydrate, or protein in the duodenum stimulates the release of which hormone from the duodenal mucosa?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cholecystokinin (CCK)

## explanation_a
Incorrect. CCK is released from duodenal I cells by the products of fat and protein digestion, but carbohydrate does not stimulate its release, so it is not released by all three foodstuffs.

## answer_b
Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP)

## explanation_b
Correct. Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP, also called gastric inhibitory peptide) is released from duodenal K cells by all three major foodstuffs — fat, carbohydrate and protein. This makes it the incretin that couples essentially any meal to insulin release. CCK, gastrin and motilin each respond to only some nutrients, so GIP is the single hormone released by all three.

## answer_c
Gastrin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gastrin is released from antral G cells mainly by protein (peptides and amino acids); fat and carbohydrate do not stimulate it.

## answer_d
Motilin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Motilin is released mainly between meals to drive the migrating motor complex; fat has only a minor effect and protein and carbohydrate do not release it.
