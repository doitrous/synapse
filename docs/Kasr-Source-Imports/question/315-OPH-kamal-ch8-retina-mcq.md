## title
Feature not associated with Marfan syndrome

## question
Marfan syndrome is associated with all of the following, EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Retinal detachment

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true association). Marfan syndrome carries a high risk of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, largely because of the associated axial myopia and lattice degeneration.

## answer_b
Lens subluxation

## explanation_b
Incorrect (a true association). Ectopia lentis (typically superotemporal lens subluxation) is a hallmark ocular feature of Marfan syndrome, from weak or broken zonules.

## answer_c
Tall stature

## explanation_c
Incorrect (a true association). Marfan patients are characteristically tall with a long arm span.

## answer_d
Short fingers

## explanation_d
Correct. This is the false association: Marfan syndrome causes arachnodactyly — long, slender ("spider") fingers — not short fingers. The other three (retinal detachment, lens subluxation, tall stature) are all genuine features.

## title
Most common cause of vision loss in diabetic retinopathy

## question
What is the most common cause of drop of vision in diabetic retinopathy?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Macular edema

## explanation_a
Correct. Diabetic macular edema — leakage and thickening at the macula — is the commonest cause of visual loss in diabetic retinopathy, and can occur at any stage of the disease.

## answer_b
Retinal tear

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A retinal tear is the mechanism of rhegmatogenous detachment, not a characteristic cause of vision loss in diabetic retinopathy.

## answer_c
Retinal hemorrhage

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Scattered retinal haemorrhages are a sign of retinopathy but are not themselves the usual cause of reduced vision unless they involve the macula.

## answer_d
Disc edema

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Disc oedema is not the typical cause of visual loss in diabetic retinopathy; the macula is the site that determines central vision.

## title
Retinal cause of night blindness

## question
Which of the following diseases causes night blindness?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Central retinal vein occlusion

## explanation_a
Incorrect. CRVO causes sudden painless loss of vision from venous congestion and haemorrhage, not a selective night-blindness (nyctalopia) picture.

## answer_b
Degeneration of retinal photoreceptors

## explanation_b
Correct. Night blindness results from degeneration of the rod photoreceptors, as in retinitis pigmentosa; rods mediate vision in dim light, so their loss impairs night vision first.

## answer_c
Macular edema

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Macular oedema blurs central (photopic) vision; it does not produce the nyctalopia caused by rod dysfunction.

## answer_d
Retinal detachment

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Retinal detachment causes a field defect and curtain-like visual loss corresponding to the detached area, not isolated night blindness.

## title
Condition not causing iris neovascularization

## question
Iris neovascularization may be seen in the following conditions, EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Carotid occlusive diseases

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true cause). Ocular ischaemic syndrome from carotid occlusive disease chronically starves the retina of oxygen and drives iris neovascularisation (rubeosis iridis).

## answer_b
Central retinal vein occlusion

## explanation_b
Incorrect (a true cause). Ischaemic CRVO is a classic cause of rubeosis iridis and neovascular glaucoma.

## answer_c
Proliferative diabetic retinopathy

## explanation_c
Incorrect (a true cause). The retinal ischaemia of proliferative diabetic retinopathy releases VEGF, which can produce iris neovascularisation.

## answer_d
Central retinal artery occlusion

## explanation_d
Correct. This is the exception: central retinal artery occlusion only rarely causes rubeosis, because the ischaemic inner retina infarcts and does not sustain the chronic VEGF drive that ischaemic venous occlusion, diabetes and carotid disease produce.

True statement about retinoblastoma

## question
Regarding retinoblastoma, which of the following is true?

## subject
mul

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

Correct. Retinoblastoma classically presents in a young child with leukocoria — a white pupillary reflex — as the tumour fills the vitreous cavity, and may also present with strabismus.

Has no genetic predisposition.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Retinoblastoma has a well-defined genetic basis (mutation of the RB1 tumour-suppressor gene), and hereditary cases are bilateral and familial.

Incorrect. Retinoblastoma is the commonest intraocular malignancy of childhood, almost always presenting under the age of 5, not in adults.

## answer_d
There is no treatment available.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Retinoblastoma is treatable — by chemotherapy, focal therapy (laser, cryotherapy), plaque radiotherapy or enucleation — and early treatment is often life-saving and eye-preserving.

## title
Nature and treatment of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment

## question
Rhegmatogenous retinal detachment:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Is caused by a retinal tear and treated by surgery.

## explanation_a
Correct. A rhegmatogenous detachment is defined by a retinal break (tear/hole) through which fluid passes under the retina; it is a surgical emergency treated by procedures such as vitrectomy, scleral buckling or pneumatic retinopexy.

## answer_b
Is caused by a retinal tear and treatment is conservative.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Although the cause (a retinal tear) is right, the treatment is surgical, not conservative — the detachment does not resolve on its own.

## answer_c
Is a complication of diabetic retinopathy.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The detachment complicating proliferative diabetic retinopathy is tractional, not rhegmatogenous; rhegmatogenous detachment is due to a break.

## answer_d
Is a disease of the choroid.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Retinal detachment is separation of the neurosensory retina from the underlying retinal pigment epithelium, not a primary choroidal disease.

## title
Treatment of persistent vitreous hemorrhage

## question
An unresolving vitreous hemorrhage for more than 6 months is treated by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Argon laser photocoagulation.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Laser photocoagulation treats the underlying retinal ischaemia but cannot be delivered while dense vitreous blood obscures the retina, and it does not clear the haemorrhage itself.

## answer_b
Pars plana vitrectomy.

## explanation_b
Correct. A vitreous haemorrhage that fails to clear (here, persisting beyond 6 months) is removed surgically by pars plana vitrectomy, which also allows endolaser to the retina.

Oral anti-inflammatory drugs.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Oral anti-inflammatory drugs have no role in clearing an organised vitreous haemorrhage.

## answer_d
Phacoemulsification.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phacoemulsification is cataract surgery on the lens and does not address blood in the vitreous cavity.
