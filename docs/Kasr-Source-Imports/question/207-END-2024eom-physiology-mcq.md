## title
Hormone affecting nervous-system excitability

## question
Which of the following hormones can affect the excitability of the nervous system?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Aldosterone

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Aldosterone regulates sodium and potassium handling in the kidney; it does not set neuromuscular excitability through plasma calcium.

## answer_b
Prolactin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Prolactin drives lactation and suppresses gonadotropins; it has no direct effect on nerve excitability.

## answer_c
Parathyroid

## explanation_c
Correct. Parathyroid hormone is the main regulator of plasma ionized calcium, and ionized calcium sets the threshold for excitation in nerve and muscle. Hypocalcemia increases excitability (tetany) and hypercalcemia decreases it, so by controlling calcium the parathyroid hormone changes the excitability of the nervous system. The other hormones listed lack this calcium-mediated action.

## answer_d
Activin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Activin modulates FSH secretion and gonadal function; it does not influence neural excitability.

Positive feedback control of hormone secretion

## question
Which of the following shows positive feedback control of hormone secretion?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

A

## answer_a
LH surge as a result of stimulatory effect by high estrogen levels

## explanation_a
Correct. Positive feedback is rare in endocrinology, and the mid-cycle LH surge is its classic example. A sustained high level of estrogen stops inhibiting and instead stimulates gonadotropin release, producing a self-amplifying rise in LH that triggers ovulation. Incorrect. Circadian ACTH/cortisol secretion is a rhythm, not positive feedback.

Incorrect. Inhibition of TSH by high T3/T4 is classic negative feedback.

## answer_d
Hypocalcemia due to calcitonin secretion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Calcitonin lowering calcium is a corrective (negative-feedback) response, and the statement as written is not positive feedback.

## title
Ultra-short-loop feedback

## question
Which of the following defines "the control done by the releasing hormones on themselves"?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Long-loop negative feedback mechanism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Long-loop feedback is the action of a peripheral target-gland hormone back on the hypothalamus and pituitary, not a releasing hormone on itself.

## answer_b
Long-loop positive feedback mechanism

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Long-loop positive feedback again involves the target hormone, not autoregulation of a releasing hormone.

## answer_c
Short-loop feedback mechanism

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Short-loop feedback is a pituitary hormone acting back on the hypothalamus.

## answer_d
Ultra short feedback mechanism

## explanation_d
Correct. Ultra-short-loop feedback is the control a hypothalamic releasing hormone exerts on its own secretion. It is the shortest of the feedback loops, distinct from short-loop (pituitary hormone on hypothalamus) and long-loop (target hormone on hypothalamus/pituitary). This matches the phrase "the control done by the releasing hormones on themselves."

Metabolic action of growth hormone

## question
A metabolic action of growth hormone is:

## subject
endo

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Growth hormone is lipolytic, mobilizing fat for fuel, not lipogenic.

Incorrect. GH increases amino-acid uptake and transport to support protein synthesis.

Decrease glucose production by the liver

## explanation_d
Incorrect. GH increases, not decreases, hepatic glucose production.

Galactorrhea after head injury

## question
The galactorrhea is most likely a result of which substance not reaching the pituitary gland?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 30-year-old woman is diagnosed with galactorrhea from both breasts. She is not pregnant and has never been pregnant. The nipple discharge started two weeks ago, after she was in a car accident. She had a concussion and is still having headaches.

D

## explanation_a

A head injury that damages the pituitary stalk stops dopamine from reaching the lactotrophs, so prolactin is disinhibited and rises, producing galactorrhea in a non-pregnant woman. The failing substance is therefore dopamine.

## title
Stimulus for calcitonin release

## question
Which of the following stimulates calcitonin release to prevent postprandial hypercalcemia?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Hypocalcemia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hypocalcemia suppresses calcitonin; it is a rise in calcium that stimulates it.

## answer_b
β-adrenergic antagonist

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Beta-adrenergic agonism (not antagonism) can stimulate calcitonin.

## answer_c
Gastrin

## explanation_c
Correct. Calcitonin from thyroid parafollicular C cells lowers plasma calcium, and its release is stimulated in anticipation of a meal by gut hormones such as gastrin. This feed-forward signal triggers calcitonin secretion to prevent postprandial hypercalcemia as dietary calcium is absorbed. Hypocalcemia and hypophosphatemia do not stimulate calcitonin.

## answer_d
Hypophosphatemia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hypophosphatemia does not stimulate calcitonin release.

## title
Fibroblast growth factor-23

## question
As regards fibroblast growth factor-23 (FGF23), which of the following is correct?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Has receptors in parathyroid gland

## explanation_a
Correct. FGF23 is a bone-derived phosphaturic hormone that signals through FGF receptors with the co-receptor klotho, and it has receptors in the parathyroid gland where it modulates PTH, as well as in the kidney. Its secretion rises when plasma phosphate rises. In the kidney it decreases sodium-dependent phosphate reabsorption to promote phosphate excretion.

## answer_b
A rise in PO4 decreases FGF23 level in plasma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A rise in phosphate increases, not decreases, FGF23.

## answer_c
Inhibits parathyroid hormone secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. FGF23 modulates but does not simply inhibit PTH; the correct listed statement is its parathyroid receptors.

## answer_d
Increases Na-dependent PO4 reabsorption

## explanation_d
Incorrect. FGF23 decreases sodium-dependent phosphate reabsorption, causing phosphaturia.
