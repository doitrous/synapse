## title
Hormone affecting nervous-system excitability

## question
Which of the following hormones can affect the excitability of the nervous system?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Aldosterone

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Aldosterone regulates sodium and potassium handling in the kidney; it does not set neuromuscular excitability through plasma calcium.

## answer_b
Prolactin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Prolactin drives lactation and suppresses gonadotropins; it has no direct effect on nerve excitability.

## answer_c
Parathyroid

## explanation_c
Correct. Parathyroid hormone is the main regulator of plasma ionized calcium, and ionized calcium sets the threshold for excitation in nerve and muscle. Hypocalcemia increases excitability (tetany) and hypercalcemia decreases it, so by controlling calcium the parathyroid hormone changes the excitability of the nervous system. The other hormones listed lack this calcium-mediated action.

## answer_d
Activin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Activin modulates FSH secretion and gonadal function; it does not influence neural excitability.

Positive feedback control of hormone secretion

## question
Which of the following shows positive feedback control of hormone secretion?

## subject
endo

## status

## owner

A

## answer_a
LH surge as a result of stimulatory effect by high estrogen levels

## explanation_a
Correct. Positive feedback is rare in endocrinology, and the mid-cycle LH surge is its classic example. A sustained high level of estrogen stops inhibiting and instead stimulates gonadotropin release, producing a self-amplifying rise in LH that triggers ovulation. Incorrect. Inhibition of TSH by high T3/T4 is classic negative feedback.

## answer_d
Hypocalcemia due to calcitonin secretion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Calcitonin lowering calcium is a corrective (negative-feedback) response, and the statement as written is not positive feedback.

## title
Ultra-short-loop feedback

## question
Which of the following defines "the control done by the releasing hormones on themselves"?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Long-loop negative feedback mechanism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Long-loop feedback is the action of a peripheral target-gland hormone back on the hypothalamus and pituitary, not a releasing hormone on itself.

## answer_b
Long-loop positive feedback mechanism

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Long-loop positive feedback again involves the target hormone, not autoregulation of a releasing hormone.

## answer_c
Short-loop feedback mechanism

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Short-loop feedback is a pituitary hormone acting back on the hypothalamus.

## answer_d
Ultra short feedback mechanism

## explanation_d
Correct. Ultra-short-loop feedback is the control a hypothalamic releasing hormone exerts on its own secretion. It is the shortest of the feedback loops, distinct from short-loop (pituitary hormone on hypothalamus) and long-loop (target hormone on hypothalamus/pituitary). This matches the phrase "the control done by the releasing hormones on themselves."

A metabolic action of growth hormone is:

## subject
endo

## status

Incorrect. GH increases, not decreases, hepatic glucose production.

## title
Galactorrhea after head injury

## question
The galactorrhea is most likely a result of which substance not reaching the pituitary gland?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 30-year-old woman is diagnosed with galactorrhea from both breasts. She is not pregnant and has never been pregnant. The nipple discharge started two weeks ago, after she was in a car accident. She had a concussion and is still having headaches.

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Gonadotropin-releasing hormone

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Loss of GnRH would impair gonadotropins, not release prolactin to cause galactorrhea.

Oxytocin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Oxytocin causes milk ejection but does not tonically restrain prolactin.

Incorrect. GHRH controls growth hormone, not prolactin, so its loss would not cause galactorrhea.

Dopamine

Correct. Prolactin is unique in being under tonic inhibition by hypothalamic dopamine (prolactin-inhibiting factor). A head injury that damages the pituitary stalk stops dopamine from reaching the lactotrophs, so prolactin is disinhibited and rises, producing galactorrhea in a non-pregnant woman. The failing substance is therefore dopamine.

Pregnancy hormone maintaining the corpus luteum

Which of the following is referred to as the pregnancy hormone that maintains corpus luteum function?

Estriol

## explanation_b

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Expulsion of the fetus is the second stage of labor.

## answer_b
Increased uterine contractions due to stretching of the cervix by the fetal head

## explanation_b
Correct. The first stage of labor is cervical dilatation. Stretching of the cervix by the descending fetal head reflexly increases oxytocin release and uterine contractions (the Ferguson reflex), a positive-feedback loop that advances dilatation. So the first stage shows increased uterine contractions due to cervical stretch by the fetal head.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Labor requires uterine contraction; progesterone-mediated relaxation opposes it and wanes before labor.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Expulsion of the placenta and membranes is the third stage of labor.

## title
Prevention of fetal rejection

## question
The rejection of the fetus by the mother is prevented by:

## subject
obs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Apoptosis of T lymphocytes by placental Fas-ligand

## explanation_a
Correct. The semi-allogeneic fetus is protected from maternal immune attack by several mechanisms, one of which is expression of Fas-ligand at the maternal-fetal interface; it engages Fas on activated maternal T lymphocytes and triggers their apoptosis. So fetal rejection is prevented by apoptosis of T lymphocytes via placental Fas-ligand.

## answer_b
Increased expression of MHC class I and II

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Trophoblast downregulates classical MHC expression; increased MHC would provoke, not prevent, rejection.

## answer_c
Stimulation of B lymphocytes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Stimulating B lymphocytes would not establish tolerance.

## answer_d
Combined binding of maternal and fetal tissues together

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Simple tissue binding does not explain immune tolerance of the fetus.
