## title
Mode of milk secretion

## question
The mode of secretion of milk from the cells of the mammary gland is:

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Merocrine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Merocrine (exocytosis) accounts for the protein fraction of milk only, so "merocrine" alone is incomplete.

## answer_b
Partly merocrine and partly apocrine

## explanation_b
Correct. Milk is secreted by two modes at once. The protein component (casein, lactalbumin) leaves the alveolar cell by MEROCRINE exocytosis, whereas the fat (lipid droplets) is released by the APOCRINE mode, pinching off with a thin envelope of apical cytoplasm and membrane. So the lactating mammary gland uses a combined merocrine-plus-apocrine secretion.

## answer_c
Apocrine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Apocrine covers only the lipid fraction; the proteins are released by merocrine exocytosis, so apocrine alone is incomplete.

## answer_d
Holocrine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Holocrine secretion (whole-cell disintegration, as in sebaceous glands) does not occur in the mammary gland.

Features of syncytiotrophoblast

## subject

Have microvilli

Incorrect. Its apical surface bears abundant microvilli (a brush border) facing the maternal blood, increasing the exchange surface, so this is true.
