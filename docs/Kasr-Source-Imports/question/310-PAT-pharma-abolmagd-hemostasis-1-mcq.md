## title
Parenteral anticoagulant suitable in heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

## question
A patient develops severe thrombocytopenia on unfractionated heparin, diagnosed as heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT). Which of the following is suitable as a parenteral anticoagulant in this condition?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Alteplase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Alteplase is a fibrinolytic (clot buster), not an anticoagulant, and is not used for ongoing anticoagulation in HIT.

## answer_b
Argatroban

## explanation_b
Correct. In HIT, all heparins must be stopped and a non-heparin anticoagulant started. ARGATROBAN is a parenteral DIRECT THROMBIN INHIBITOR that does not cross-react with HIT antibodies, so it safely provides immediate anticoagulation. Its parenteral route and heparin-independent mechanism make it the appropriate choice here (bivalirudin/lepirudin are alternatives).

## answer_c
Rivaroxaban

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Rivaroxaban is an ORAL factor Xa inhibitor; it may be used later but is not the parenteral agent for acute HIT management.

## answer_d
Dabigatran

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dabigatran is an ORAL direct thrombin inhibitor, not a parenteral agent for acute HIT.

## title
Fibrin-specific plasminogen activator

## question
Which of the following is a fibrin-specific plasminogen activator?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Alteplase

## explanation_a
Correct. Alteplase (recombinant tissue plasminogen activator, tPA) preferentially activates plasminogen that is already bound to FIBRIN within a clot, making it relatively FIBRIN-SPECIFIC. This localises fibrinolysis to the thrombus and causes less systemic plasminogen activation than non-selective agents. Reteplase and tenecteplase share this property; streptokinase does not.

## answer_b
Aminocaproic acid

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Aminocaproic acid is an ANTIfibrinolytic (it inhibits plasminogen activation), the opposite of a plasminogen activator.

## answer_c
Streptokinase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Streptokinase activates plasminogen non-selectively throughout the circulation, so it is NOT fibrin-specific and causes a systemic lytic state.

## answer_d
Tranexamic acid

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tranexamic acid is an antifibrinolytic used to STOP bleeding, not a plasminogen activator.

## title
Antiplatelet that reversibly blocks the P2Y12 receptor

## question
The antiplatelet drug that inhibits REVERSIBLY the binding of ADP to P2Y12 receptors on platelets is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Clopidogrel

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Clopidogrel is a thienopyridine prodrug that binds the P2Y12 receptor IRREVERSIBLY for the platelet's lifespan.

## answer_b
Prasugrel

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Prasugrel is also a thienopyridine that irreversibly inhibits P2Y12.

## answer_c
Ticagrelor

## explanation_c
Correct. Ticagrelor is a cyclopentyltriazolopyrimidine that binds the P2Y12 receptor REVERSIBLY (and at a site distinct from ADP). Because inhibition is reversible and not dependent on prodrug activation, it has a faster onset/offset than clopidogrel, which is why it is the answer. Cangrelor is the reversible intravenous counterpart.

## answer_d
Tirofiban

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tirofiban blocks the glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor, not the P2Y12 (ADP) receptor.

## title
Oral anticoagulant acting as a factor Xa inhibitor

## question
Which of the following is an oral anticoagulant that acts as a factor Xa inhibitor?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Dabigatran is an oral anticoagulant but a direct THROMBIN (factor IIa) inhibitor, not a factor Xa inhibitor.

## answer_b
Enoxaparin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Enoxaparin is a low-molecular-weight heparin given PARENTERALLY; it inhibits factor Xa indirectly via antithrombin, and it is not oral.

## answer_c
Fondaparinux

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fondaparinux is a selective factor Xa inhibitor but is given PARENTERALLY (subcutaneously), not orally.

Rivaroxaban

Correct. Rivaroxaban is a direct, ORAL factor Xa inhibitor (a "-xaban"), binding the active site of factor Xa to reduce thrombin generation. Being both oral and a direct Xa inhibitor is exactly the description, so rivaroxaban (like apixaban and edoxaban) is the answer.

## title
Anticoagulant paired with an inappropriate use

## question
One of the following drugs is paired with an INAPPROPRIATE use:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Andexanet alfa: antidote for bleeding from rivaroxaban

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this pairing is appropriate). Andexanet alfa is a recombinant decoy factor Xa that reverses bleeding from factor Xa inhibitors such as rivaroxaban.

## answer_b
Warfarin: oral anticoagulant during pregnancy

## explanation_b
Correct. This is the INAPPROPRIATE pairing. Warfarin crosses the placenta and is TERATOGENIC (fetal warfarin syndrome, CNS and skeletal defects, fetal bleeding), so it is contraindicated in pregnancy; heparin/LMWH, which do not cross the placenta, are used instead. Pairing warfarin with pregnancy use is therefore wrong.

## answer_c
Lepirudin: patients who developed heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this pairing is appropriate). Lepirudin is a direct thrombin inhibitor used as a non-heparin anticoagulant in HIT.

## answer_d
Aminocaproic acid: haemorrhage induced by overdose of streptokinase

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this pairing is appropriate). Aminocaproic acid is an antifibrinolytic used to treat bleeding from excess fibrinolytic (streptokinase) therapy.

## title
False statement about the control of thromboembolic disorders

## question
Which of the following is FALSE regarding the control of thromboembolic disorders?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Anticoagulant therapy is started by heparin and warfarin together

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). Heparin and warfarin are overlapped at the start ("bridging") because warfarin's effect is delayed and initially prothrombotic.

## answer_b
Heparin has a rapid onset and short duration

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). Heparin acts immediately and is short-lived, allowing rapid titration and reversal.

## answer_c
The onset of the anticoagulant effect of warfarin is delayed

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this statement is TRUE). Warfarin's effect is delayed several days until existing clotting factors are cleared.

## answer_d
Warfarin's antidote is protamine sulfate

## explanation_d
Correct. This is the FALSE statement. Protamine sulfate reverses HEPARIN, not warfarin. Warfarin's antidote is VITAMIN K (with fresh frozen plasma or prothrombin complex concentrate for urgent reversal). Attributing protamine to warfarin is the error.
