## title
Arteries forming Little's area

## question
Little's area is a known spot on the anteroinferior nasal septum that is vulnerable to bleeding because it is an anastomotic network of branches of several arteries. All of the following arteries contribute to it EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Superior labial artery

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The septal branch of the superior labial artery (from the facial artery) is one of the four vessels that anastomose in Little's area, so it does contribute.

## answer_b
Greater palatine artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The greater palatine artery is a genuine contributor to the Kiesselbach anastomosis and therefore is not the exception.

## answer_c
Nasolabial artery

## explanation_c
Correct. Little's (Kiesselbach's) area is the anastomosis of the anterior ethmoidal, sphenopalatine, greater palatine and superior labial arteries. There is no artery called the "nasolabial artery" contributing to it — the term is not part of the plexus. It is therefore the odd one out among the listed vessels.

## answer_d
Sphenopalatine artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The septal branch of the sphenopalatine artery is a major contributor to Little's area, so it is not the exception.

## answer_e
Anterior ethmoidal artery

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The anterior ethmoidal artery supplies the anterosuperior septum and is one of the four vessels of the Kiesselbach plexus.

## title
Spread of nasal vestibule furunculosis

## question
Furunculosis of the vestibule of the nose is dangerous because infection may spread to:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The cavernous sinus

## explanation_a
Correct. The nasal vestibule lies in the "dangerous area of the face", which drains through the facial and angular veins into the ophthalmic veins and then the cavernous sinus. Because these veins are valveless, infection from a vestibular furuncle can ascend retrogradely and cause cavernous sinus thrombosis. This is why such furuncles must not be squeezed.

The ethmoid sinus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The ethmoid sinus is not the feared route of spread; the danger from a vestibular furuncle is intracranial venous spread to the cavernous sinus.

The lateral sinus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The lateral (sigmoid) sinus is related to spread from ear and mastoid infection, not from a nasal vestibule furuncle.

## answer_d
The superior sagittal sinus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The superior sagittal sinus is not the characteristic pathway of spread from the nasal vestibule.

## title
Origin of the anterior ethmoidal artery

## question
The anterior ethmoidal artery arises from the:

## subject
mul

## status

## owner

B

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The external carotid supplies most of the nose through the maxillary and facial arteries, but not the ethmoidal vessels.

Internal carotid artery

## explanation_b
Correct. The anterior ethmoidal artery is a branch of the ophthalmic artery, which itself arises from the internal carotid artery. Incorrect. The maxillary artery gives the sphenopalatine and greater palatine arteries, not the anterior ethmoidal artery.

Facial artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The facial artery gives the superior labial (septal) branch, not the anterior ethmoidal artery.

Timing of nasal fracture reduction with oedema

## question
Reduction of a nasal bone fracture associated with oedema should be done:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

C

## explanation_a

## explanation_b
Incorrect. One hour is not long enough for significant oedema to settle.

Incorrect. A displaced nasal fracture should be reduced; leaving it produces a permanent deformity.
