## title
Characteristic feature of the pars nervosa

## question
The following is a characteristic feature of the pars nervosa:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It is formed of groups of nerve cells separated by fenestrated capillaries.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The pars nervosa contains few or no nerve cell bodies; it is made mainly of unmyelinated axons of hypothalamic magnocellular neurons and supporting pituicytes, not groups of nerve cells around fenestrated capillaries.

## answer_b
It has accumulated neurosecretion called Herring bodies.

## explanation_b
Correct. The pars nervosa (posterior pituitary) is the storage and release site for hypothalamic hormones, and the axons reaching it show dilated terminals packed with neurosecretory granules. These accumulations are visible by light microscopy as Herring bodies. They store oxytocin or ADH bound to neurophysin, awaiting release into the fenestrated capillaries of the neurohypophysis.

## answer_c
It is formed of follicles of basophilic cells with granular cytoplasm.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Follicles of basophilic cells with granular cytoplasm describe glandular adenohypophyseal tissue, not the pars nervosa, which contains no follicles.

## answer_d
It has an upward extension known as the pars tuberalis.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pars tuberalis is an upward extension of the pars distalis (adenohypophysis) around the infundibular stalk, not of the pars nervosa.

## title
Stain that identifies mammotrophs

## question
Mammotrophs could be identified by the following stain:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Silver stains demonstrate reticular fibres and argyrophil cells of the diffuse neuroendocrine system, not pituitary mammotrophs.

## answer_b
Orcein.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Orcein is an elastic-fibre stain and is not used to demonstrate the acidophilic mammotrophs of the anterior pituitary.

## answer_c
Orange G.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Orange G selectively stains the somatotrophs (growth-hormone acidophils), not the prolactin-secreting mammotrophs.

## explanation_d

This selective affinity lets the two acidophil populations be told apart in stained sections. Their number and granularity increase markedly during pregnancy and lactation.

## title
Common feature of the diffuse neuroendocrine system

## question
A common feature of the cells of the diffuse neuro-endocrine system (DNES) is that they:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Are endodermal in origin.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. DNES cells arise from several germ layers, many from neural crest and neuroectoderm as well as endoderm, so a single endodermal origin is not their common feature.

## answer_b
Have no secretory granules.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. DNES cells are defined by abundant membrane-bound dense-core secretory granules storing peptides and amines, so being granule-free is the opposite of the truth.

## answer_c
Are rich in sER and lysosomes.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. They are not characterised chiefly by smooth ER and lysosomes; their hallmark is amine-precursor uptake and decarboxylation with dense-core granule storage.

## answer_d
Are considered highly modified neurons.

## explanation_d
Correct. The diffuse neuroendocrine system (formerly called APUD) is a scattered population of amine- and peptide-secreting cells that share key properties with neurons: they take up amine precursors, decarboxylate them, and store the products in dense-core granules for regulated release. Because of this shared biochemistry and secretory behaviour they are regarded as highly modified neurons. Many indeed derive from the neural crest.

## title
Development of the pituitary gland

## question
Regarding the development of the pituitary gland, mark the one correct statement:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The infundibulum arises as a mesodermal diverticulum from the roof of the diencephalon.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The infundibulum is a neuroectodermal, not mesodermal, downgrowth from the floor of the diencephalon, so both the germ layer and the site are wrong here.

## answer_b
The infundibulum forms the anterior lobe of the pituitary gland.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The infundibulum forms the posterior lobe (neurohypophysis) and stalk, not the anterior lobe.

## answer_c
Rathke's pouch is an endodermal diverticulum from the floor of the stomodeum.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Rathke's pouch is an ectodermal diverticulum from the roof of the stomodeum (primitive mouth), not an endodermal outgrowth from its floor.

## answer_d
It is derived from two sources: the infundibulum and Rathke's pouch.

## explanation_d
Correct. The hypophysis has a dual origin: an ectodermal outpocketing of the stomodeal roof (Rathke's pouch) forms the adenohypophysis (anterior lobe, pars intermedia and pars tuberalis), while a neuroectodermal downgrowth from the floor of the diencephalon (the infundibulum) forms the neurohypophysis (posterior lobe and stalk). These two primordia meet and fuse to build the definitive gland. This is why the pituitary has both a glandular and a neural part.

## title
Cells spared in cryptorchidism

## question
In cryptorchidism (undescended testes), the following cells are not affected:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Primary spermatocyte.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Primary spermatocytes belong to the temperature-sensitive germ-cell line and are damaged when the testis remains at core body temperature.

## answer_b
Spermatid.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Spermatids are late germ cells and are among the first to be lost when spermatogenesis fails in an undescended testis.

## answer_c
Leydig cell.

## explanation_c
Correct. In cryptorchidism the seminiferous (germinal) epithelium is exposed to the higher intra-abdominal temperature, which arrests and destroys the temperature-sensitive spermatogenic cells. The interstitial Leydig cells, which secrete testosterone, are relatively resistant to this heat and are spared, so androgen production and secondary sexual characters are usually preserved. This is why an untreated undescended testis causes infertility but not necessarily hypoandrogenism.

## answer_d
Secondary spermatocyte.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Secondary spermatocytes are germ cells formed during meiosis and are also lost when the heat-sensitive spermatogenic line is damaged.

## title
Least likely effect of chronic androgen abuse

## question
Chronic androgen abuse by healthy male athletes is least likely to result in:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Increased erythropoiesis.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Androgens stimulate renal erythropoietin and the bone marrow, so chronic abuse tends to raise the red-cell mass, making increased erythropoiesis an expected effect.

## answer_b
Increased sperm count.

## explanation_b
Correct. Exogenous androgens suppress the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis by negative feedback, lowering GnRH and therefore LH and FSH. Without adequate intratesticular testosterone and FSH support, spermatogenesis declines and the sperm count falls rather than rises. This is why anabolic-steroid abuse is a recognised cause of male infertility and testicular atrophy.

## answer_c
Increased physical endurance.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. By raising haemoglobin and muscle mass, androgen abuse can improve physical endurance, so a rise in endurance is an expected rather than a least-likely result.

## answer_d
Increased muscle mass.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The classic sought-after effect of androgen abuse is anabolic growth of skeletal muscle, so increased muscle mass is expected.

Function of the syncytiotrophoblast

## question
Choose the correct statement regarding the syncytiotrophoblast:

## subject
obs

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

It is responsible for the secretion of placental hormones.

## answer_d
It disappears after the 4th month of pregnancy.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The syncytiotrophoblast persists throughout pregnancy as the exchange and endocrine surface; it is the cytotrophoblast that becomes discontinuous in later pregnancy.

What the pelvic diaphragm separates

The pelvic diaphragm separates the:

## subject

## status

Incorrect. The true pelvis is separated from the false pelvis by the pelvic brim (inlet), not by the pelvic diaphragm.

Incorrect. The ischiorectal fossa lies within the perineum below the pelvic diaphragm; the diaphragm does not separate the perineum from that fossa.

Correct. The pelvic diaphragm, formed by the levator ani and coccygeus muscles with their fasciae, is the muscular floor of the pelvic cavity. It separates the pelvic cavity above from the perineum below. Structures such as the anal canal and the urethra pass through it to reach the perineum.
