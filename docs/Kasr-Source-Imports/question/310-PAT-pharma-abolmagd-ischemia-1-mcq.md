## title
Drug that is a potassium channel opener and nitric oxide donor

## question
Select the drug that is a potassium channel opener as well as a nitric oxide donor:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Diazoxide

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diazoxide opens ATP-sensitive potassium channels but is not a nitric oxide donor; it is used as a vasodilator and to raise blood glucose.

## answer_b
Sodium nitroprusside

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sodium nitroprusside is a nitric oxide donor but not a potassium channel opener.

## answer_c
Hydralazine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hydralazine is a direct arteriolar vasodilator and neither a potassium channel opener nor an NO donor.

## answer_d
Nicorandil

## explanation_d
Correct. Nicorandil has a DUAL mechanism: it opens ATP-sensitive potassium channels (hyperpolarising and relaxing arterial smooth muscle, reducing afterload) AND donates nitric oxide (raising cGMP to dilate veins, reducing preload). This combination of potassium-channel opening and NO donation is unique to nicorandil among the options.

Antianginal for prophylaxis of variant angina that lowers heart rate

## question
Which of the following is an antianginal drug that consistently decreases the heart rate and is used in the prophylaxis of vasospastic (variant) angina?

pharm

Draft

Incorrect. Propranolol lowers heart rate but is CONTRAINDICATED in vasospastic angina, where unopposed alpha tone can worsen coronary spasm.

Adverse effect of nifedipine

## question
Which of the following is an adverse effect of nifedipine?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

Bradycardia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Nifedipine, a dihydropyridine, tends to cause reflex TACHYCARDIA; bradycardia is a feature of verapamil/diltiazem.

Ankle oedema

Peripheral neuropathy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peripheral neuropathy is not a recognised adverse effect of nifedipine.

## title
Combination reducing both preload and afterload

## question
Which of the following combinations of drugs reduces both preload and afterload?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Nitroglycerin and isosorbide dinitrate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Both nitroglycerin and isosorbide dinitrate are nitrates acting mainly on VEINS, so this pair reduces preload but not much afterload.

## answer_b
Hydralazine and isosorbide dinitrate

## explanation_b
Correct. Isosorbide dinitrate is a venodilator that lowers PRELOAD, while hydralazine is an arteriolar dilator that lowers AFTERLOAD; combined they reduce both, which is the basis of the hydralazine-nitrate regimen used in heart failure. Pairing a venodilator with an arteriolar dilator is exactly how both loading conditions are reduced.

Amlodipine and minoxidil

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Amlodipine and minoxidil are both predominantly ARTERIOLAR dilators, reducing afterload without a matched preload effect.

## answer_d
Hydralazine and diazoxide

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hydralazine and diazoxide are both arteriolar dilators, again targeting afterload rather than both.

## title
Mechanism of tolerance to organic nitrates

## question
Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of tolerance to the anti-anginal effects of organic nitrates?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Activation of enzymes responsible for nitrate metabolism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Enzyme induction of nitrate metabolism is not the accepted mechanism of nitrate tolerance.

## answer_b
Compensatory increase in intracellular calcium with prolonged use

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A rise in intracellular calcium is not the classical explanation for nitrate tolerance.

## answer_c
Depletion of endogenous sulfhydryl (SH) groups required for nitrate activation

## explanation_c
Correct. Organic nitrates must be bioactivated by tissue sulfhydryl (SH) groups to release nitric oxide. Continuous exposure DEPLETES these sulfhydryl donors, so less NO is generated and the vasodilator effect wanes, producing tolerance. This is why a daily nitrate-free interval is used to restore responsiveness.

## answer_d
Internalisation of the receptors responsible for nitrate action

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nitrates do not act on a classical membrane receptor that could internalise; they are bioactivated intracellularly.

## title
Application of the low oral bioavailability of nitrates

## question
Organic nitrates have an oral bioavailability of around 10%. Which of the following is most likely to be an application of this property?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Combining nitrates with beta-blockers is a favourable combination

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The nitrate/beta-blocker combination is favourable, but that reflects complementary haemodynamics, not the low oral bioavailability.

## answer_b
Nitrates are given by IV infusion, not IV bolus, in myocardial infarction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The infusion-versus-bolus choice relates to titratability and short half-life, not to first-pass bioavailability.

## answer_c
Nitrates are given sublingually during acute attacks of angina

## explanation_c
Correct. Organic nitrates undergo extensive hepatic first-pass metabolism, giving only about 10% oral bioavailability. Giving them SUBLINGUALLY bypasses the liver, so the drug reaches the circulation rapidly and reliably to abort an acute angina attack. Exploiting a route that avoids first-pass loss is the direct application of this property.

## answer_d
Slow-release preparations of nitrates are available

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Slow-release formulations address short duration of action, not the first-pass bioavailability problem.

## title
Main beneficial antianginal effect of nitrates

## question
Which of the following is considered the main beneficial effect of nitrates as an antianginal drug?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Venodilatation and reduction of preload

## explanation_a
Correct. At therapeutic doses nitrates act predominantly on VEINS, causing venodilatation that pools blood peripherally and reduces venous return and PRELOAD. Lower preload reduces ventricular wall tension and myocardial oxygen demand, which is the main way nitrates relieve angina. Coronary dilation contributes but the dominant benefit is preload reduction.

Bradycardia and reduced cardiac conductivity

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Nitrates do not slow the heart or reduce conduction; that describes beta-blockers or verapamil.

## answer_c
Vasodilatation mainly of the coronary arteries

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Although nitrates can dilate coronaries, their principal antianginal action in typical angina is preload reduction, not direct coronary dilation.

## answer_d
Inhibition of myocardial contractility

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nitrates do not directly depress myocardial contractility.
