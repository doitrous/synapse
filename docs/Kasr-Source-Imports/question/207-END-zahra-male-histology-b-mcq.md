## title
Contents of the sperm head

## question
The head of a mature spermatozoon contains:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
A flattened dense nucleus capped with an acrosomal cap

## explanation_a
Correct. The head of a mature spermatozoon consists of a flattened, highly condensed haploid nucleus capped anteriorly by the acrosome (acrosomal cap), a Golgi-derived sac of hydrolytic enzymes. The condensed chromatin gives the head its dense, flattened profile, and the acrosome covers about the anterior two-thirds of the nucleus. The head therefore carries the genetic material and the enzymes for fertilisation.

## answer_b
The axonemal complex

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The axonemal complex is the microtubular core of the flagellum (tail), not a component of the head.

## answer_c
A pair of centrioles

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The centrioles lie in the neck (connecting piece) between the head and the tail, not within the head.

## answer_d
Mitochondria

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mitochondria form the sheath of the middle piece of the tail; they are not part of the head.

## title
Fate of residual bodies

## question
Residual bodies formed during spermiogenesis are phagocytosed by:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Interstitial (Leydig) cells lie outside the seminiferous tubule and secrete testosterone; they do not phagocytose residual bodies.

## answer_b
The Sertoli cells

## explanation_b
Correct. As the spermatid matures, its excess cytoplasm is pinched off as residual bodies, which are phagocytosed and digested by the surrounding Sertoli cells. This is one of the supportive/phagocytic roles of the Sertoli cell within the seminiferous epithelium. The recycled material contributes to the Sertoli cell's metabolic support of spermatogenesis.

## answer_c
The primary spermatocytes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Primary spermatocytes are dividing germ cells, not phagocytes.

## answer_d
The spermatids

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Spermatids are the cells shedding the residual bodies, not the cells that remove them.

Spermatogenesis in man, from a type-A spermatogonium to a released spermatozoon, takes approximately 74 days (about two and a half months), with further maturation in the epididymis afterwards. Incorrect. Forty-six days underestimates the human spermatogenic cycle, which is close to 74 days.

## title
Largest spermatogenic cell

## question
The largest spermatogenic cell in the seminiferous tubule is the:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Spermatid

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The spermatid is a small, later cell that undergoes metamorphosis into a spermatozoon; it is not the largest.

## answer_b
Secondary spermatocyte

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Secondary spermatocytes are smaller, short-lived cells that quickly complete the second meiotic division and are rarely seen in sections.

## answer_c
Primary spermatocyte

## explanation_c
Correct. Primary spermatocytes are the largest cells of the spermatogenic series. They arise from type-B spermatogonia and are diploid (2n) but hold 4N DNA, because they spend a long prophase of the first meiotic division after replicating their DNA, which makes them large and conspicuous in testicular sections.

## answer_d
Type B spermatogonium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Type-B spermatogonia are smaller basal cells that give rise to the primary spermatocytes; they are not the largest.

## title
Erectile tissue of the corpora cavernosa

## question
The erectile tissue of the corpora cavernosa is formed of:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Mucous-secreting glands

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Erectile tissue contains no mucous glands; it is a vascular sponge.

## answer_b
Vascular (venous) spaces

## explanation_b
Correct. The erectile tissue of the corpora cavernosa is a sponge-like network of endothelium-lined vascular (venous) spaces. These spaces are separated by trabeculae of smooth muscle together with collagen, reticular and elastic fibres, and during erection the helicine arteries fill them with blood while venous drainage is compressed. It is the blood-filled venous spaces, not glands or a single tissue type, that define the erectile tissue.

## answer_c
Smooth muscle fibres only

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Smooth muscle is present in the trabeculae but does not by itself constitute the erectile tissue, which is defined by its venous spaces.

## answer_d
Elastic connective tissue only

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Elastic connective tissue is only one component of the trabeculae, not the whole erectile tissue.

Site of Reinke crystals

## question

## subject

## explanation_a

## answer_b
The head of a spermatozoon

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The sperm head contains condensed nucleus and acrosome, not Reinke crystalloids.

## answer_c
Human Leydig cells

## explanation_c
Correct. Crystals of Reinke are rod-shaped, membrane-free protein crystalloids found in the cytoplasm of adult human interstitial (Leydig) cells, alongside lipid droplets and lipofuscin pigment. They are characteristic of human Leydig cells and help identify them and their tumours. They are not found in Sertoli cells or germ cells.

Type B spermatogonia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Type-B spermatogonia are basal germ cells and do not contain Reinke crystals.
