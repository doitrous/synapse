## title
Releasing a patient's records without consent

## question
A man asked the psychiatrist of his 25-year-old brother to release his brother's records. For which of the following actions can the patient sue the psychiatrist?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Abandonment

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Abandonment is the unilateral ending of a treatment relationship without notice or handover; disclosing records is not abandonment.

## answer_b
Breach of confidentiality

## explanation_b
Correct. Releasing a competent adult patient's records to his brother without the patient's consent is a breach of confidentiality, for which the patient can sue. The 25-year-old is a competent adult whose information may not be disclosed to a relative without his authorisation. The wrong here is unauthorised disclosure, not a failure of skill or care.

## answer_c
Incompetence

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Incompetence is a lack of the expected knowledge or skill; improperly disclosing information is a confidentiality breach, not a competence issue.

## answer_d
Negligence

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Negligence is a failure to meet the standard of care that causes harm; unauthorised disclosure of records is specifically a breach of confidentiality.

## title
Guiding principle of the Hippocratic Oath

## question
According to the Hippocratic Oath, one of the following four principles might be regarded as a "guiding principle".

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Autonomy

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Autonomy — respect for the patient's self-determination — is a modern bioethical principle emphasised long after the Oath, not its guiding commitment.

## answer_b
Beneficence

## explanation_b
Correct. The guiding principle of the Hippocratic Oath is beneficence: the physician's positive commitment to act for the benefit of the sick and to use treatment to help the patient. The Oath is framed around doing good for the patient, which is beneficence. This overarching duty to benefit the patient is what marks it out from the other listed principles.

## answer_c
Justice

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Justice — fair distribution of benefits and burdens — is a principle of modern bioethics, not the guiding theme of the Oath.

## answer_d
Non-maleficence

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Non-maleficence ("first do no harm") is closely associated with Hippocratic teaching, but the guiding principle keyed here is the positive duty of beneficence to benefit the patient.

## title
Ethical principle behind first-come-first-managed triage

## question
After a bus accident, 12 patients were admitted to the emergency room. The doctor decided to treat the patients according to the rule of "first come, first managed". Here the doctor applied which basic ethical principle?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Abandonment

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Abandonment is a failure of duty, not an ethical principle; it does not describe a fair allocation rule.

## answer_b
Beneficence

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Beneficence is acting for the patient's good; the specific issue here is how to allocate care fairly among many patients, which is justice.

## answer_c
Justice

## explanation_c
Correct. Treating patients by a neutral "first come, first managed" rule applies the principle of justice — the fair and impartial distribution of a limited resource (the doctor's time) among competing patients. Justice governs how benefits and burdens are shared out, which is exactly what a queue rule does. That is why this scenario illustrates justice rather than beneficence or non-maleficence.

## answer_d
Non-maleficence

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Non-maleficence is the duty to avoid harm; it does not describe the fair-allocation rule the doctor is using.

## title
Definition of an ethical dilemma

## question
A conflict between two moral principles is defined as:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Assault

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Assault is causing another person to fear imminent unlawful force; it is not a conflict between moral principles.

## answer_b
Dilemma

## explanation_b
Correct. A conflict between two moral principles is defined as an (ethical) dilemma: a situation in which two valid moral principles point to incompatible courses of action, so honouring one means breaching the other. The decision-maker must weigh the competing principles because both cannot be fully satisfied. This tension between valid principles is precisely what a dilemma denotes.

## answer_c
Indecency

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Indecency refers to offensive or obscene conduct, not a clash of moral principles.

## answer_d
Negligence

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Negligence is a failure to meet the standard of care causing harm, not a conflict between two moral principles.
