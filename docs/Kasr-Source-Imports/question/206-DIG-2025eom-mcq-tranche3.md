## title
Basal electrical rhythm of GIT smooth muscle
## question
Which of the following is correct as regards the basal electrical rhythm (BER) of GIT smooth muscles?
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
B
## answer_a
Is shown in the wall of oesophagus.
## explanation_a
Incorrect. The upper oesophagus is striated muscle and the body is largely aperistaltic smooth muscle without the same pacemaker-driven slow-wave activity seen in the stomach and intestine; BER is not classically demonstrated in the oesophageal wall.
## answer_b
Is initiated by pacemaker cells known as interstitial cells of Cajal.
## explanation_b
Correct. Interstitial cells of Cajal, interposed between and within the smooth muscle layers, are the pacemaker cells that generate the rhythmic slow-wave depolarisations (the basal electrical rhythm) of GIT smooth muscle.
## answer_c
Its frequency is the same in all segments of GIT.
## explanation_c
Incorrect. BER frequency is segment-specific (roughly 3/min in the stomach, up to about 12/min in the duodenum, declining toward the terminal ileum), not uniform along the GIT.
## answer_d
Always results in muscle contraction.
## explanation_d
Incorrect. A slow wave sets only the potential timing/rhythm of contraction; an actual contraction requires the wave to reach threshold, usually via added neural or hormonal excitation producing superimposed spike potentials.

## title
Gastrin rise in pernicious anaemia
## question
In pernicious anaemia, why does gastrin secretion rise to high levels?
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## vignette

## correct_answer
A
## answer_a
The acid secreting cells of stomach are destroyed, so no negative feedback effect.
## explanation_a
Correct. Autoimmune destruction of the acid-secreting parietal cells in pernicious anaemia removes the low-pH signal that normally suppresses antral G-cell gastrin release, so gastrin rises unchecked (secondary hypergastrinaemia).
## answer_b
The parietal cells secrete large amount of gastric acid.
## explanation_b
Incorrect. This is the opposite of what happens — the parietal cells are destroyed and acid output falls, which is precisely why the negative feedback on gastrin is lost.
## answer_c
Post ganglionic vagal fibers supplying G cells secrete acetyl choline.
## explanation_c
Incorrect. Vagal cholinergic input is a normal physiological stimulus for gastrin release in general, but it is not the mechanism behind the specific, marked gastrin elevation seen in pernicious anaemia — that is driven by loss of acid-mediated feedback.
## answer_d
Presence of several gastrinomas in the pancreas.
## explanation_d
Incorrect. Multiple gastrinomas describe Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (a primary hypergastrinaemia with inappropriately high acid output), not pernicious anaemia, whose hypergastrinaemia is secondary to low acid output.
