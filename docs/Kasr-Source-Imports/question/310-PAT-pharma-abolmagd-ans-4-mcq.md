## title
Which claimed advantage of beta-1 selective blockers is false

## question
Adrenergic beta-1 selective blockers offer the following advantages EXCEPT:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Lower incidence of bronchospasm

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it IS an advantage). Sparing beta-2 receptors in the airways means less bronchospasm than with non-selective blockers.

## answer_b
Less prone to produce cold hands and feet

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it IS an advantage). Sparing beta-2-mediated vasodilation means less peripheral vasoconstriction, so fewer cold extremities.

## answer_c
Beta-1 selectivity is absolute

## explanation_c
Correct answer. Beta-1 selectivity is only RELATIVE, not absolute: at higher doses these agents also block beta-2 receptors, so the selectivity is lost. Claiming it is absolute is therefore the false statement and the exception.

## answer_d
Less liable to cause hypoglycaemia

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it IS an advantage). Sparing beta-2 receptors preserves adrenaline-driven glycogenolysis, so beta-1 selective agents are less likely to mask or prolong hypoglycaemia.

Atropine substitute mismatched with its use

## question
Which of the following atropine substitutes is NOT matched correctly with its use?

## subject
pharm

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hyoscine butyl bromide in colic

## explanation_a
Correct pairing (so not the answer). Hyoscine butylbromide is a poorly absorbed antimuscarinic antispasmodic used for abdominal colic.

## answer_b
Tiotropium in urinary incontinence

## explanation_b
Correct answer. This is the WRONG match: tiotropium is a long-acting inhaled antimuscarinic used as a bronchodilator in COPD and asthma, NOT for urinary incontinence (which is treated with uroselective antimuscarinics such as tolterodine or solifenacin). Pairing tiotropium with incontinence is therefore incorrect.

## answer_c
Tropicamide for eye fundus examination

## explanation_c
Correct pairing (so not the answer). Short-acting tropicamide is used to dilate the pupil for fundus examination.

## answer_d
Benztropine in Parkinsonism

## explanation_d
Correct pairing (so not the answer). Benztropine, a central antimuscarinic, is used in Parkinsonism.

## title
Agent to confirm a diagnosis of myasthenia gravis

## question
A 44-year-old man has intermittent fatigue and drooping eyelids. Administration of which of the following agents is most appropriate to confirm a diagnosis of myasthenia gravis?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Adrenaline

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adrenaline is a sympathomimetic with no role in diagnosing myasthenia gravis.

## answer_b
Edrophonium

## explanation_b
Correct. Edrophonium is an ultra-short-acting anticholinesterase; in the Tensilon test it transiently raises acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction, and a prompt but brief improvement in muscle strength (for example resolution of ptosis) confirms myasthenia gravis. Its very short duration makes it ideal for a rapid diagnostic challenge. That is why it is the appropriate confirmatory agent.

## answer_c
Bethanechol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bethanechol is a direct muscarinic agonist acting on smooth muscle, not a diagnostic anticholinesterase for the neuromuscular junction.

## answer_d
Physostigmine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Physostigmine is a longer-acting anticholinesterase used therapeutically; edrophonium's brevity makes it the preferred diagnostic agent.

## title
Calculating the apparent volume of distribution

## question
A drug given as a 200 mg single intravenous dose produces a peak plasma concentration of 0.02 mg/ml. The apparent volume of distribution (Vd) is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
3 litres

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This does not satisfy Vd = dose / plasma concentration for the given figures.

## answer_b
4 litres

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 4 litres would require a plasma concentration of 0.05 mg/ml, which is not the case here.

## answer_c
5 litres

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 5 litres would require a concentration of 0.04 mg/ml, not 0.02 mg/ml.

## answer_d
10 litres

## explanation_d
Correct. The apparent volume of distribution is Vd = dose / plasma concentration. Here Vd = 200 mg / 0.02 mg per ml = 10 000 ml = 10 litres. This is the only option consistent with the definition, so the answer is 10 litres.

## title
Calculating a maintenance infusion rate

## question
It is desired to maintain a plasma concentration of 0.2 g/L of an antibiotic. Plasma clearance equals 6 L/hour. Calculate the maintenance infusion rate:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
1.2 g/hour

## explanation_a
Correct. At steady state the maintenance infusion rate equals target concentration multiplied by clearance: rate = Css × CL = 0.2 g/L × 6 L/hour = 1.2 g/hour. This balances the amount cleared per hour, so the answer is 1.2 g/hour.

## answer_b
2.4 g/hour

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 2.4 g/hour is twice the required rate and would drive the concentration above target.

## answer_c
3 g/hour

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 3 g/hour does not equal Css × CL for these values.

## answer_d
5 g/hour

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 5 g/hour greatly exceeds the product of concentration and clearance.

## title
Time to reach steady-state plasma concentration

## question
The half-life of a drug is about 6 hours. Approximately how long will it take for blood levels to reach a steady (plateau) concentration on regular dosing?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
12 hours

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 12 hours is only two half-lives, giving about 75% of steady state, which is not yet the plateau.

## answer_b
24 hours

## explanation_b
Correct. Steady state is reached after roughly four to five half-lives. With a half-life of 6 hours, four half-lives is about 24 hours (approximately 94% of steady state), which is the best approximation of the plateau among the options. Hence about 24 hours.

## answer_c
48 hours

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 48 hours is eight half-lives, far beyond the time actually needed to plateau.

## answer_d
4 days

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 4 days is much longer than the four to five half-lives required.
