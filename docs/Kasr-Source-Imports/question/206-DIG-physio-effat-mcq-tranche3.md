## title
Function lost when all vagal output to the gut is eliminated

## question
A stroke completely eliminates all vagal output to the gastrointestinal tract. Which function is most likely to be totally eliminated?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Gastrin release

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Gastrin release can still be driven by luminal peptides acting directly on antral G cells and by local reflexes, so it is not totally eliminated.

## answer_b
Pancreatic bicarbonate secretion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pancreatic bicarbonate secretion is driven largely by secretin released in response to duodenal acid, a hormonal pathway independent of the vagus.

## answer_c
Primary oesophageal peristalsis

## explanation_c
Correct. The vagus directly innervates the striated muscle of the pharynx and upper oesophagus and coordinates swallowing and primary oesophageal peristalsis. Losing all vagal output abolishes this centrally programmed primary peristalsis. It is therefore the function most completely eliminated.

## answer_d
Secondary oesophageal peristalsis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Secondary oesophageal peristalsis is a local stretch reflex triggered by a retained bolus and can occur through the enteric nervous system without vagal input, so it persists.

## title
Agent that virtually eliminates gastric-phase acid secretion

## question
The gastric phase accounts for about 60 percent of the acid response to a meal. Which substance can virtually eliminate acid secretion during the gastric phase?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Antigastrin antibodies

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Blocking gastrin removes only one stimulatory pathway; histamine- and acetylcholine-driven acid secretion continues.

## answer_b
Atropine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Atropine blocks the muscarinic (vagal) pathway but not the histamine and gastrin pathways, so acid secretion is reduced, not eliminated.

## answer_c
Histamine H2 blocker

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An H2 blocker removes the histamine pathway but leaves acetylcholine- and gastrin-driven secretion, so acid output is only lowered.

## answer_d
Proton pump inhibitor

## explanation_d
Correct. A proton pump inhibitor blocks the parietal-cell H+/K+ ATPase, the final common step of acid secretion. Because every stimulatory pathway ultimately works through this pump, inhibiting it can virtually eliminate acid secretion. The other agents block only one upstream pathway each and cannot abolish secretion.

## title
Agent that both damages the mucosal barrier and stimulates acid

## question
Which substance can both damage the gastric mucosal barrier and stimulate gastric acid secretion?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Bile salts

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bile salts do damage the mucosal barrier, but they have no clinically significant effect to stimulate gastric acid secretion.

## answer_b
Epidermal growth factor

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Epidermal growth factor strengthens and repairs the mucosal barrier rather than damaging it.

## answer_c
Gastrin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Gastrin stimulates acid secretion, but it strengthens (through its trophic effect) rather than damages the mucosal barrier.

## answer_d
Helicobacter pylori

## explanation_d
Correct. Helicobacter pylori produces urease, generating ammonia that is converted to ammonium in the acid environment and damages the surface epithelial cells and the mucosal barrier. It also increases gastric acid secretion, probably by increasing parietal-cell mass. It is therefore the single agent that both damages the barrier and stimulates acid.
