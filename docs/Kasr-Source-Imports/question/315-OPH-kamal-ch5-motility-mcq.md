## title
Extraocular muscle not arising from the orbital apex

## question
All of the following muscles arise from the apex of the orbit, EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Superior oblique muscle

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it does arise at the apex). The superior oblique arises from the body of the sphenoid near the annulus of Zinn at the orbital apex.

## answer_b
Lateral rectus muscle

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it does arise at the apex). The lateral rectus, like the other recti, arises from the annulus of Zinn at the orbital apex.

## answer_c
Inferior oblique muscle

## explanation_c
Correct. The inferior oblique is the exception: it is the only extraocular muscle that arises anteriorly, from the orbital floor near the lacrimal fossa, rather than at the apex. The four recti and the superior oblique all take origin from the annulus of Zinn at the orbital apex, so the inferior oblique is the one that does not.

## answer_d
Inferior rectus muscle

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it does arise at the apex). The inferior rectus is one of the four recti and arises from the annulus of Zinn at the orbital apex.

## title
Action of the superior rectus muscle

## question
What is the action of the superior rectus muscle?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Elevation, intorsion, abduction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The superior rectus adducts, not abducts, as its tertiary action, though intorsion is correct.

## answer_b
Elevation, intorsion, adduction

## explanation_b
Correct. The superior rectus runs above the globe at an angle to the visual axis, so its primary action is elevation with secondary intorsion and tertiary adduction. This is why its elevating action is tested with the eye abducted, where the muscle acts as a pure elevator. The combination elevation-intorsion-adduction distinguishes it from the inferior oblique, which elevates but extorts and abducts.

## answer_c
Elevation, extorsion, adduction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The superior rectus intorts (not extorts); extorsion with elevation belongs to the inferior oblique.

## answer_d
Elevation, extorsion, abduction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The superior rectus intorts and adducts; extorsion and abduction describe the inferior oblique.

## title
Best management of acute esotropia with limited abduction

## question
A 40-year-old male patient presents with a recent onset of esodeviation in the right eye with diplopia that improved on covering either eye. On examination, there is right esotropia 30 prism diopters and limited abduction. What is the best management plan?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Prescribing glasses

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Glasses correct a refractive error; they do not address an acquired sixth-nerve palsy causing the esotropia and limited abduction.

## answer_b
Starting systemic antibiotics

## explanation_b
Incorrect. There is no infective process here; antibiotics have no role in an acute cranial nerve palsy.

## answer_c
Asking for brain MRI

## explanation_c
Correct. Recent-onset esotropia with limited abduction and diplopia relieved by covering either eye is an acquired sixth (abducens) nerve palsy. In an adult this is a neurological sign that requires neuroimaging (brain MRI) to exclude a compressive, ischaemic or intracranial cause before any surgical treatment. Investigating the cause takes priority over intervention on the muscle itself.

## answer_d
Scheduling of urgent extraocular muscle surgery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Muscle surgery is premature and is not done for a recent acquired palsy before the cause is found and a period of observation for recovery has passed.

## title
True statement about amblyopia

## question
Amblyopia:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Is a problem of adolescence.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Amblyopia develops in early childhood, when the visual system is still plastic, not in adolescence.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Amblyopia develops in the early years of visual development and must be treated before, not after, that window; it does not begin only after age 9.

## answer_c
Can be caused by congenital cataract and congenital corneal opacity.

## explanation_c
Correct. Congenital cataract and congenital corneal opacity block a clear image during the critical period of visual development, producing stimulus-deprivation amblyopia. Early removal of the obstruction is essential because the deficit becomes permanent if not treated while the visual system is plastic.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amblyopia is a serious, preventable cause of childhood visual loss that should be detected and treated, not ignored.

Cause of exotropia in an elderly hypertensive

## question
Exotropia in a 70 year old male with uncontrolled hypertension can be due to:

## subject
mul

## status

Trigeminal cranial nerve palsy.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The trigeminal (fifth) nerve is sensory to the eye and motor to muscles of mastication; it does not move the globe, so its lesion does not cause a squint.

Refractive error associated with exophoria

Exophoria is commonly associated with which error of refraction?

mul

Compound astigmatism.

Incorrect. Compound astigmatism is a refractive error of two meridia and is not the recognised association of exophoria.

## title
Diagnosis of exotropia with ptosis and limited adduction

## question
The presentation of exotropia in primary position with ptosis and limited adduction is suggestive of:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Third nerve palsy.

## explanation_a
Correct. Third (oculomotor) nerve palsy paralyses the levator (giving ptosis) and the medial, superior and inferior recti and inferior oblique, so the eye rests down and out (exotropia) with limited adduction, elevation and depression. The combination of ptosis, exotropia and limited adduction is the classic picture. A dilated pupil may accompany it when the pupillary fibres are involved.

## title
True statement about paralytic strabismus

## question
Regarding paralytic strabismus:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Eye deviates to the opposite direction of action of paralyzed muscle

## explanation_a
Correct. In paralytic strabismus the affected muscle cannot pull the eye into its field of action, so the unopposed antagonist deviates the eye in the opposite direction. For example, a lateral rectus palsy leaves the eye deviated inward, away from the muscle's abducting action. This is the true statement.

## answer_b
Limitation of movement in the opposite direction of the paralyzed muscle.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Movement is limited in the direction of the paralysed muscle's action (toward its field), not in the opposite direction.

## answer_c
Diplopia rarely occurs in paralytic squint

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Diplopia is characteristic of and usually prominent in paralytic squint, being worst in the direction of the paralysed muscle's action.

## answer_d
Injury of the nerve trunk is the only cause

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nerve trunk injury is only one cause; paralytic squint also follows nuclear, muscular and neuromuscular-junction lesions and vascular palsies.

## title
Cause of ptosis with mydriasis

## question
Ptosis and mydriasis are seen in:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Facial palsy

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Facial (seventh) nerve palsy weakens eyelid closure (lagophthalmos), not lid elevation, and does not dilate the pupil.

## answer_b
Peripheral neuritis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A non-specific peripheral neuritis does not produce the specific combination of ptosis with a dilated pupil.

## answer_c
Oculomotor palsy

## explanation_c
Correct. Third (oculomotor) nerve palsy paralyses the levator, causing ptosis, and when the parasympathetic pupillary fibres are affected the sphincter pupillae is denervated, giving a fixed dilated pupil (mydriasis). The eye also rests down and out. This ptosis-plus-mydriasis pattern distinguishes it from Horner syndrome.

## answer_d
Sympathetic palsy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sympathetic (Horner) palsy causes ptosis with a small pupil (miosis), not mydriasis.

## title
True statement about esotropia

## question
Esotropia is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Divergent squint

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Esotropia is a convergent (inward) squint; a divergent squint is exotropia.

## answer_b
Latent convergent squint

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Esotropia is a manifest convergent deviation; the latent form is esophoria, not esotropia.

## answer_c
Congenital if it appears after 6 months of age

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Infantile (congenital) esotropia appears within the first 6 months of life; onset after that is acquired, not congenital.

## answer_d
Associated with accommodation and hypermetropia

## explanation_d
Correct. A common form is accommodative esotropia, in which uncorrected hypermetropia drives excess accommodation and the linked convergence turns the eye inward. Correcting the hypermetropia with convex lenses relaxes accommodation and can straighten the eyes. This accommodation-hypermetropia link is the true statement.

## title
Muscle not supplied by the third nerve

## question
Third cranial nerve supplies the following muscles except:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Superior rectus

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it is supplied by the third nerve). The superior rectus is one of the recti innervated by the oculomotor nerve.

## answer_b
Superior oblique

## explanation_b
Correct. The superior oblique is the exception: it is supplied by the fourth (trochlear) nerve, not the third. The oculomotor nerve supplies the superior, inferior and medial recti, the inferior oblique and the levator, so the superior oblique is the one it does not supply. The lateral rectus (abducens) is the other non-oculomotor muscle.

## answer_c
Medial rectus

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it is supplied by the third nerve). The medial rectus is an oculomotor muscle and adducts the eye.

## answer_d
Inferior oblique

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it is supplied by the third nerve). The inferior oblique is innervated by the oculomotor nerve.
