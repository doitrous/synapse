## title
Fracture from falling on the feet from height

## question
Falling from height on the feet causes fracture of:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The vault

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A vault fracture typically follows a direct impact to the top of the head, not an axial force transmitted up the spine from the feet.

## answer_b
The parietal bone

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An isolated parietal fracture results from localised direct trauma to the side of the head, not from a fall onto the feet.

## answer_c
The face or chin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Facial or chin fractures follow a direct blow to the face; they are not the characteristic lesion of a fall landing on the feet.

## answer_d
The base of skull

## explanation_d
Correct. When a person falls from a height and lands on the feet, the force is transmitted axially up the vertebral column to the skull base, driving the spine against the foramen magnum and producing a ring (or hinge) fracture around the base of the skull. This indirect, force-transmission mechanism is why the base — not the vault — is the site of fracture. It is a classic medico-legal marker of a fall from height onto the feet or buttocks.

State of hair in scalds

## subject

## explanation_b
Correct. A scald is an injury from moist heat — hot liquid or steam — so the hair over a scalded area is simply WET and matted, never singed. Incorrect. Hair is not 'eaten up' in scalds; destruction of hair suggests a corrosive or a severe dry burn.

Cause of tattooing around a firearm entry wound

## question
Tattooing present around firearm entry wound is due to:

## subject

Incorrect. Grease from the bullet produces a greyish grease/dirt collar (grease ring), not the punctate tattooing.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hot gases cause burning/searing and, at contact range, the stellate laceration of gas expansion — not tattooing.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Flame produces scorching/singeing and smoke produces soot blackening (smudging) that can be wiped away; true tattooing cannot be wiped off.

Its presence indicates an intermediate (close but not contact) range of fire, which is why it is a key medico-legal sign for estimating firing distance.

## title
Site of hypostasis in a drowned body

## question
The hypostasis in a drowned body generally appears in:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Head and neck

## explanation_a
Correct. In a drowned body the head hangs down while the trunk and limbs float slightly higher in the water (the classic head-down floating posture), so gravity pools the settling blood into the most dependent parts — the HEAD, neck and, to a degree, the upper chest and distal limbs. Because position after death, not the site of injury, determines where hypostasis forms, the drowned body's head-down attitude explains this distribution. This contrasts with a body lying supine on land, where hypostasis appears on the back.

## answer_b
Lower limbs

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lower-limb hypostasis is seen in a body kept upright (e.g. hanging), not in the typical head-down floating drowned body.

## answer_c
Back

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hypostasis on the back is the pattern of a body lying supine on a surface, not a floating drowned body.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hypostasis follows the dependent parts for the body's position, so it is not uniformly distributed over all regions.
