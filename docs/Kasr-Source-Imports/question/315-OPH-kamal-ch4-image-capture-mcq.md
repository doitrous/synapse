## title
Refractive error corrected by a minus cylinder axis 180

## question
A patient corrected with a minus 2 diopter cylinder axis 180 has:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Simple hypermetropic astigmatism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A minus (concave) cylinder corrects myopic, not hypermetropic, astigmatism; a plus cylinder would be used for hypermetropic astigmatism.

## answer_b
Simple myopic astigmatism

## explanation_b
Correct. A pure minus (concave) cylinder with no spherical component corrects simple myopic astigmatism: one meridian is emmetropic and the other is myopic, so only the myopic meridian needs a concave cylinder. Because there is no sphere, it is 'simple' rather than 'compound'. Compound myopic astigmatism would need a sphere plus a cylinder.

## answer_c
Compound myopic astigmatism

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Compound myopic astigmatism has both meridia myopic and needs a spherical lens combined with the cylinder, not a cylinder alone.

## answer_d
Simple myopia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Simple myopia is corrected by a spherical concave lens, not a cylinder; a cylinder implies astigmatism.

## title
False statement about keratoconus

## question
In keratoconus, all the following statements are true EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Munson sign is positive

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it is a true statement). Munson sign, a V-shaped bulging of the lower lid on downgaze, is a classic sign of advanced keratoconus.

## answer_b
Corneal opacity may be seen

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it is a true statement). Corneal opacity from stromal scarring or healed hydrops can be seen in keratoconus.

## answer_c
Corneal topography is diagnostic

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it is a true statement). Corneal topography demonstrating inferior steepening is the most sensitive diagnostic tool, especially in early disease.

Kayser-Fleischer ring is present

## explanation_d
Correct. This is the false statement: a Kayser-Fleischer ring is a corneal copper deposit of Wilson disease, not a feature of keratoconus. The keratoconus-specific corneal iron line is the Fleischer ring, which is different. So a Kayser-Fleischer ring does not belong to keratoconus.

## title
Refractive change in early nuclear cataract

## question
Early senile nuclear cataract is accompanied by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Myopic shift of refraction.

## explanation_a
Correct. Early nuclear sclerosis increases the refractive index of the lens nucleus, which increases its refractive power and produces a myopic shift. This 'index myopia' can transiently improve near vision, the so-called second sight. It is a classic early feature of nuclear cataract.

## answer_b
Hyperopic shift of refraction.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A hyperopic shift is not typical of nuclear cataract; the change is toward myopia.

## answer_c
Night blindness.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Night blindness points to retinal disease such as retinitis pigmentosa, not to early nuclear cataract.

## answer_d
Ocular pain.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Uncomplicated cataract is painless; ocular pain suggests glaucoma or inflammation.

## title
Refractive error characteristic of keratoconus

## question
Which refractive error is characteristic of keratoconus?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Regular astigmatism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Regular astigmatism has perpendicular principal meridia and is correctable by cylinders; keratoconus distorts the cornea irregularly.

## answer_b
Hypermetropia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Keratoconus steepens the cornea and causes myopia, not hypermetropia.

## answer_c
Irregular astigmatism

## explanation_c
Correct. The conical, asymmetric thinning of keratoconus produces irregular astigmatism whose meridia are neither perpendicular nor uniform. This cannot be fully corrected by spectacle cylinders and typically needs rigid or scleral contact lenses. Irregular astigmatism is therefore the hallmark refractive error of keratoconus.

## answer_d
Emmetropia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Emmetropia means no refractive error, which is the opposite of the progressive ametropia of keratoconus.

## title
Meaning of hypermetropia

## question
Hypermetropia means that the refractive system of the eye is:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Weaker than the emmetrope.

## explanation_a
Correct. In hypermetropia parallel rays are focused behind the retina because the eye's refractive power is too weak for its axial length. This usually reflects a short axial length or an insufficiently powerful cornea/lens. A convex (plus) lens is needed to add the missing power.

## answer_b
Stronger than the emmetrope.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An overly strong refractive system focuses light in front of the retina, which is myopia, not hypermetropia.

## answer_c
The cornea is very steep.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A very steep cornea increases power and causes myopia, the opposite of hypermetropia.

## answer_d
The eye has long axial length.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A long axial length causes myopia; hypermetropia is associated with a short axial length.

Current treatment of cataract

## question
The current treatment of cataract is:

## subject
mul

## status

## answer_b
Phacoemulsification without intraocular lens implantation.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Removing the cataract without implanting an IOL leaves the eye markedly hypermetropic (aphakic) and is not the standard.

Correct. Modern cataract treatment is phacoemulsification, in which ultrasonic energy fragments and aspirates the lens through a small incision. ## answer_d
Excimer laser corneal ablation.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Excimer laser ablates the cornea for refractive errors; it does not treat lens opacity.
