## title
Coverings of the testis

## question
The testis is covered with all the following EXCEPT:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Tunica vaginalis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The tunica vaginalis is a genuine covering of the testis: a serous sac derived from the processus vaginalis of the peritoneum that clothes the anterior and lateral surfaces of the organ.

## answer_b
Tunica albuginea

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The tunica albuginea is the tough dense fibrous capsule immediately deep to the tunica vaginalis and is a true covering of the testis.

## answer_c
Tunica propria

## explanation_c
Correct. The testis has three coats from outside in — the tunica vaginalis (serous), the tunica albuginea (dense fibrous capsule) and the tunica vasculosa (inner loose vascular layer). There is no "tunica propria" among the testicular coverings; that term refers to the peritubular boundary tissue of the seminiferous tubule, not a coat of the whole organ, so it is the required exception.

## answer_d
Tunica vasculosa

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The tunica vasculosa is the innermost vascular layer lining the deep aspect of the tunica albuginea and the septa, and is a true covering of the testis.

## title
Cells of the seminiferous tubule

## question
The seminiferous tubule contains all the following cells EXCEPT:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Sertoli cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sertoli (sustentacular) cells are the tall supporting cells that line the seminiferous tubule and form its blood-testis barrier, so they are a true tubular cell.

## answer_b
Leydig cells

## explanation_b
Correct. The seminiferous tubule is lined by the germinal (spermatogenic) epithelium — spermatogonia, spermatocytes and spermatids — together with Sertoli cells. Leydig (interstitial) cells lie OUTSIDE the tubule, in the loose interstitial connective tissue between tubules, where they secrete testosterone. They are therefore not a cell of the seminiferous tubule and are the required exception.

## answer_c
Spermatids

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Spermatids are haploid germ cells in the adluminal compartment of the tubule and are a true tubular cell.

## answer_d
Spermatogonia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Spermatogonia are the diploid stem cells resting on the basal lamina inside the tubule and are a true tubular cell.

## title
Source of androgen-binding protein

## question
Androgen binding protein (ABP) is secreted by:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Interstitial cells of Leydig

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Leydig cells secrete testosterone, not androgen-binding protein; they supply the androgen that ABP then binds.

## answer_b
Type B spermatogonia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Type B spermatogonia are germ cells that differentiate into primary spermatocytes; they have no secretory role for ABP.

## answer_c
Type A spermatogonia

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Type A spermatogonia are the stem/reserve germ cells and do not secrete ABP.

## answer_d
Sertoli cells

## explanation_d
Correct. Androgen-binding protein is a secretory product of the Sertoli cell, made under FSH stimulation. By binding testosterone, ABP keeps a high local androgen concentration inside the seminiferous tubule and carries testosterone into the lumen and along the ducts, which is essential for spermatogenesis.
