## title
Plicae circulares of the small intestine

## question
Choose the correct statement concerning the histology of the small intestine:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Villi are folds of the intestinal mucosa and submucosa

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Villi are finger-like projections of the mucosa only (epithelium plus lamina propria core); it is the plicae circulares, not the villi, that also contain a submucosal core.

## answer_b
Plicae circulares are folds with a mucosal covering and a submucosal core

## explanation_b
Correct. The plicae circulares (valves of Kerckring) are permanent circular folds whose surface is mucosa but whose core contains submucosa; this is what distinguishes them from villi, which are mucosal projections only. These folds greatly increase the absorptive surface of the small intestine, most prominently in the jejunum. Villi, microvilli and plicae circulares together amplify the surface area several hundred-fold.

## answer_c
Microvilli decrease the surface area available for absorption

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Microvilli (the brush border) markedly increase, not decrease, the absorptive surface area of each enterocyte.

## answer_d
Intestinal crypts are simple branched alveolar glands

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The intestinal crypts of Lieberkuhn are simple tubular glands, not branched alveolar glands.

## title
Histology of the anal canal

## question
Regarding the histology of the anal canal, choose the correct statement:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The haemorrhoidal plexuses are arterial and lie in the lamina propria

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The haemorrhoidal plexuses are venous, not arterial, and lie in the submucosa; internal haemorrhoids are dilatations of the submucosal venous plexus above the pectinate line.

## answer_b
The epithelium lining the transitional (colorectal) zone is simple squamous

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The transitional zone is lined by stratified epithelium (transitioning from simple columnar of the rectum to stratified squamous of the anal skin), not simple squamous.

## answer_c
The inner circular layer of the muscularis externa forms the external anal sphincter

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The external anal sphincter is formed of skeletal (voluntary) muscle; it is the thickened inner circular smooth muscle that forms the internal anal sphincter.

## answer_d
The muscularis mucosae breaks up and disappears at the level of the pectinate line

## explanation_d
Correct. At the pectinate (dentate) line the muscularis mucosae breaks up and disappears, one of the histological landmarks of the anorectal junction. Above this line the mucosa has the colorectal pattern with a muscularis mucosae; below it the lining becomes stratified squamous of the anal skin without a muscularis mucosae. This transition coincides with the change in epithelium, blood supply and innervation across the pectinate line.

Crypts of Lieberkuhn

## question
The crypts of Lieberkuhn are:

## subject
gi

## status

## owner

C

## answer_a

Incorrect. Finger-like mucosal projections into the lumen are the villi; the crypts open at the base between the villi and extend downward into the mucosa.

## answer_b
Simple branched alveolar glands

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The crypts are simple tubular glands, not branched alveolar glands.

## answer_c
Simple tubular glands located between the bases of the intestinal villi

## explanation_c
Correct. The crypts (intestinal glands) of Lieberkuhn are simple tubular glands that open on the mucosal surface between the bases of adjacent villi and extend down to the muscularis mucosae. Their location between villus bases is the defining histological feature tested here.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Circular folds of mucosa and submucosa are the plicae circulares, a different structure from the crypts.

## title
Oxyntic (parietal) cells of the stomach

## question
Regarding the oxyntic (parietal) cells of the gastric glands, which of the following is true?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Their cytoplasm shows deep basal basophilia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Parietal cells are strongly acidophilic (eosinophilic) because of their abundant mitochondria; deep basophilia is a feature of the pepsinogen-rich chief (peptic) cells.

## answer_b
They are present mainly at the base of the pyloric glands

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Parietal cells are concentrated in the oxyntic (gastric/fundic) glands of the body and fundus, especially the neck and upper region, not at the base of the pyloric glands.

## answer_c
They contain basal electron-dense (secretory) granules

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Parietal cells do not store basal electron-dense secretory granules; abundant apical granules are a feature of chief cells (zymogen) or basal granules of enteroendocrine cells.

## answer_d
They contain intracellular canaliculi

## explanation_d
Correct. The parietal (oxyntic) cell is characterised by a deep intracellular canaliculus, an invagination of the apical plasma membrane lined by microvilli, together with an associated tubulovesicular system. On stimulation the tubulovesicles fuse with the canalicular membrane, expanding the microvillous surface for hydrochloric acid secretion. This canalicular system is the hallmark ultrastructural feature of the acid-secreting parietal cell.

## subject

## answer_a

## explanation_a

Its main excretory duct is called Wharton's duct

## explanation_d
Correct. The main excretory duct of the submandibular gland is Wharton's duct, which opens at the sublingual caruncle beside the frenulum of the tongue. Recognising the named main ducts is a standard histology/anatomy point: Wharton's duct for the submandibular, Stensen's duct for the parotid. This is the correct statement among the options.

## title
Mesangial cells of the renal corpuscle

## question
Regarding the mesangial cells of the renal corpuscle, which of the following is true?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
They are acidophilic cells rich in ribosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Mesangial cells are modified pericyte-like cells; they are not described as acidophilic ribosome-rich cells (that description fits protein-synthesising cells such as plasma cells, not mesangial cells).

## answer_b
They have a contractile function

## explanation_b
Correct. Mesangial cells are contractile, phagocytic supporting cells: their contraction can alter the glomerular capillary surface area and hence the filtration rate, and they also clear trapped macromolecules and secrete matrix and cytokines. This contractile, regulatory role is their key functional feature. They respond to angiotensin II and other vasoactive agents.

## answer_c
They are the lacis cells located at the urinary pole

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The lacis (extraglomerular mesangial) cells sit at the vascular pole within the juxtaglomerular apparatus, not at the urinary pole; intraglomerular mesangial cells lie among the capillaries.

## answer_d
They are located only between the glomerular capillaries (intraglomerular)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mesangial cells exist in two populations: intraglomerular (between the capillaries) and extraglomerular (lacis cells at the vascular pole), so they are not located only between the glomerular capillaries.

## title
Intercalated cells of the collecting ducts

## question
Regarding the intercalated cells lining the collecting tubules, which of the following is true?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
They show a single central cilium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A single central cilium is a feature of the principal (light) cells of the collecting duct, not the intercalated (dark) cells.

## answer_b
They have numerous basal infoldings like the proximal tubule

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Prominent basal infoldings packed with mitochondria characterise the proximal (and distal) convoluted tubule cells; intercalated cells have abundant mitochondria and apical microplicae rather than the deep basal infoldings of the proximal tubule.

## answer_c
They are of alpha and beta types

## explanation_c
Correct. The intercalated (dark) cells of the collecting duct occur as two functional types: alpha-intercalated cells secrete H+ (via apical H+ ATPase and H+/K+ ATPase) and reabsorb bicarbonate, while beta-intercalated cells secrete bicarbonate and reabsorb H+. This alpha/beta division lets the collecting duct handle either acid or base loads, and is the correct descriptive feature. They are interspersed among the more numerous principal cells.

## answer_d
They have very few organelles

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Intercalated cells are rich in mitochondria and cytoplasmic organelles (reflecting their active ion transport), so 'very few organelles' is wrong.

## title
Histology of the distal convoluted tubule

## question
Regarding the distal convoluted tubule (DCT), which of the following is true?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It is lined by tall pyramidal cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The DCT is lined by low cuboidal cells, not tall pyramidal cells.

## answer_b
Its lining cells have a deeply basophilic cytoplasm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Its cytoplasm is paler and less acidophilic than the proximal tubule and is not deeply basophilic.

## answer_c
Its apical cell surface bears a dense, tall microvillous brush border

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A tall, dense microvillous brush border is the hallmark of the proximal convoluted tubule; the DCT has only sparse, short microvilli, so its lumen appears clearer and wider.

## answer_d
It shows extensive basolateral interdigitations with indistinct cell boundaries

## explanation_d
Correct. The distal convoluted tubule cells show extensive basolateral membrane interdigitations with neighbouring cells (and basal infoldings packed with mitochondria), which makes the lateral cell boundaries indistinct in the light microscope. This basolateral amplification supports active sodium reabsorption. Together with the absence of a prominent brush border and the wider, clearer lumen, it distinguishes the DCT from the proximal tubule.
