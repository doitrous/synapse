## title
Repeatedly practising under the influence of alcohol

## question
A doctor repeatedly treating his patient under the influence of alcohol is doing:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Civil negligence

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Civil negligence is a careless breach of the duty of care that harms a specific patient; repeatedly practising while intoxicated is judged as a disciplinary breach of professional standards rather than as a single negligent clinical act.

## answer_b
Dichotomy

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dichotomy (fee-splitting) is the unethical sharing of fees in return for patient referral; it has nothing to do with practising under the influence of alcohol.

## answer_c
Privileged communication

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Privileged communication refers to legally protected confidential information between doctor and patient; it is not a form of wrongdoing at all.

## answer_d
Professional misconduct

## explanation_d
Correct. Repeatedly treating patients while under the influence of alcohol is professional misconduct — infamous conduct in a professional respect that falls below the standards expected of the profession. It reflects a pattern of unfitness to practise safely and is handled by the medical syndicate's disciplinary process rather than as a single act of civil negligence. Practising while impaired endangers every patient seen and breaches the doctor's ethical duty to be fit for duty.

## title
Physical harassment of a patient during examination

## question
During examination, a female patient experienced verbal and physical harassment by the physician. She filed a complaint against him. The physician can be sued for which of the following?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Assault

## explanation_a
Correct. Verbal and, especially, physical harassment of a patient during examination is an assault — an intentional act that threatens or inflicts unlawful physical contact, for which the physician can be sued in the courts. Assault is a criminal and civil wrong distinct from clinical error, because it is a deliberate violation of the patient's person rather than a lapse of care. The patient's complaint therefore founds a legal action for assault.

## answer_b
Incompetence

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Incompetence is a lack of reasonable skill or knowledge in providing care; harassment is a deliberate wrongful act, not a deficiency of clinical ability.

## answer_c
Negligence

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Negligence is a careless failure to meet the standard of care; harassment is intentional misconduct, not carelessness, so it is not negligence.

## answer_d
Professional misconduct

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although harassment is also unprofessional, the specific ground on which the physician can be sued in court is assault; professional misconduct is dealt with by the syndicate's disciplinary process rather than by a lawsuit.

Senior surgeon persisting with outdated operative technique

## question
Dr. X is alarmed by the behavior of the senior surgeon in the operating room. The surgeon uses out-of-date techniques that prolong operations and result in greater post-operative pain and longer recovery time. This case is an example of:

Medical malpractice.

## explanation_a
Correct. A surgeon who uses out-of-date techniques that prolong operations and cause greater pain and slower recovery is committing medical malpractice — substandard professional treatment that harms patients. The demonstrable harm (added pain, longer recovery) is the injury that makes it malpractice rather than a mere ethical lapse.

Incorrect. Negligence can be one element underlying malpractice, but the best single description of substandard technique that injures patients is medical malpractice; here the pattern of harmful practice is the malpractice itself.

## answer_d
Incompetence.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Incompetence is a lack of skill or knowledge; a senior surgeon who is capable but persists with outdated methods is delivering substandard care (malpractice) rather than simply lacking ability.

Incorrect. Blame is not the first action; the ladder opens with an alarm or caution before blame.

Incorrect. The correct opening step is the alarm, not a warning; this option also misorders the early sanctions.
