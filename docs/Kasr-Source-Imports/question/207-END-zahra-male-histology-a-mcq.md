## title
Location of myoid cells

## question
Concerning myoid cells, which one of the following is correct?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
They are seen in the prostate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Myoid cells are not a feature of the prostatic stroma; the prostate has fibromuscular stroma of ordinary smooth muscle, not the peritubular myoid layer.

## answer_b
They are seen in the testis

## explanation_b
Correct. Myoid (peritubular) cells are flattened, contractile smooth-muscle-like cells that form the peritubular tissue surrounding each seminiferous tubule of the testis. Their rhythmic contraction helps move non-motile spermatozoa and testicular fluid along the tubule toward the rete testis, and they contribute to the wall in which the blood-testis barrier sits.

## answer_c
They control blood flow in the corpora cavernosa

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Blood flow in the corpora cavernosa is controlled by the smooth muscle of the helicine arteries and trabeculae, not by testicular myoid cells.

## answer_d
They control the movement of sperm in the epididymis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Movement of sperm along the epididymis is produced by the smooth muscle of the epididymal wall; myoid cells act around the seminiferous tubule, not in the epididymis.

## title
Origin of the acrosomal vesicle

## question
The acrosomal vesicle is derived from:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The rough endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The rough endoplasmic reticulum synthesises the hydrolytic enzymes, but the membrane-bound acrosomal vesicle itself is assembled downstream in the Golgi.

## answer_b
The smooth endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The smooth endoplasmic reticulum is not the source of the acrosomal vesicle.

## answer_c
The Golgi complex

## explanation_c
Correct. During the Golgi phase of spermiogenesis, pro-acrosomal granules produced by the Golgi complex coalesce into a single membrane-bound acrosomal vesicle that caps the anterior pole of the spermatid nucleus. The mature acrosome is therefore a modified lysosome-like Golgi derivative containing hydrolytic enzymes (hyaluronidase, acrosin) used in fertilisation.

## answer_d
The nucleus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The nucleus condenses and elongates during spermiogenesis but does not give rise to the acrosome.

## title
Definition of spermiogenesis

## question
Spermiogenesis is the process by which:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Spermatogonia differentiate into spermatids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The transformation of spermatogonia through spermatocytes to spermatids is spermatogenesis (spermatocytogenesis and meiosis), not spermiogenesis.

## answer_b
Spermatozoa begin to function

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Functional activation and fertilising capacity are acquired later during epididymal maturation and capacitation, not during spermiogenesis.

## answer_c
Germ cells migrate to the primitive male gonads

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Migration of primordial germ cells to the genital ridge is an embryonic event, unrelated to spermiogenesis.

## answer_d
Spermatids differentiate into spermatozoa

## explanation_d
Correct. Spermiogenesis is the final, non-dividing phase of sperm formation in which the round haploid spermatid undergoes metamorphosis into a mature spermatozoon. It comprises acrosome formation, nuclear condensation and elongation, development of the flagellum, assembly of the mitochondrial sheath and shedding of the residual cytoplasm. No further cell division occurs, which separates it from the earlier spermatogenic divisions.

## title
Features of spermatogonia

## question
Spermatogonia are characterised by the following EXCEPT:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Correct. Spermatogonia are the stem cells of the germinal epithelium and lie in the basal compartment against the basal lamina, not near the lumen. As their progeny mature into spermatocytes and spermatids they move progressively toward the lumen, so a luminal location is the false statement and the required exception.

## answer_b
Diploid cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Spermatogonia are diploid (2n) cells, so this is a true feature.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Spermatogonia are present in the fetal testis and persist as the self-renewing stem-cell pool in the adult, so this is true.

## answer_d
Susceptible to damage by body temperature

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The germ cells, including spermatogonia, are temperature-sensitive, which is why the testis is kept below core temperature in the scrotum; this is a true feature.

## subject

Correct. The prostate is a compound tubulo-alveolar gland: 30-50 branched tubulo-alveolar glands embedded in a fibromuscular stroma, arranged in mucosal, submucosal and main (peripheral) groups, and draining by ducts into the prostatic urethra. Their lumina often contain corpora amylacea.

Incorrect. The prostate is not a simple alveolar gland; its ducts branch and its units are tubulo-alveolar rather than purely alveolar.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 'Branched alveolar' describes glands such as the sebaceous gland, not the compound tubulo-alveolar prostate.

## title
Site of stereocilia in the male tract

## question
Stereocilia occur in which of the following?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Seminiferous tubules

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The seminiferous tubule is lined by the stratified germinal (spermatogenic) epithelium with Sertoli cells; it has no stereocilia.

## answer_b
Straight tubules (tubuli recti)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The straight tubules are lined by a simple epithelium of Sertoli-like cells, without stereocilia.

## answer_c
Seminal vesicles

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The seminal vesicle has a folded pseudostratified/columnar secretory mucosa, not stereocilia.

## answer_d
Epididymis

## explanation_d
Correct. The epididymis is lined by a tall pseudostratified columnar epithelium whose principal cells bear long, branching, non-motile microvilli called stereocilia. These greatly increase the surface area for the absorption of testicular fluid and the addition of maturation factors as sperm pass through, so stereocilia are a hallmark of the epididymal duct.

## answer_e
Rete testis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The rete testis is lined by a simple cuboidal epithelium with single short apical microvilli, not stereocilia.

## title
Order of the male genital duct system

## question
Sperm pass through the male genital ducts in which of the following orders?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Tubuli recti - rete testis - efferent ductules - epididymis

## explanation_a
Correct. After leaving the convoluted seminiferous tubules, sperm enter the straight tubules (tubuli recti), which open into the rete testis in the mediastinum; from the rete testis 10-20 efferent ductules (ductuli efferentes) carry sperm out of the testis into the head of the epididymis. The sequence is therefore seminiferous tubule -> tubuli recti -> rete testis -> efferent ductules -> epididymis.

## answer_b
Tubuli recti - efferent ductules - rete testis - epididymis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The efferent ductules follow the rete testis, not precede it; this order is reversed.

## answer_c
Rete testis - tubuli recti - efferent ductules - epididymis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The tubuli recti drain into the rete testis, so the rete testis cannot come first.

## answer_d
Rete testis - efferent ductules - tubuli recti - epididymis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This scrambles the sequence; the tubuli recti are the first intratesticular duct, not the last.
