## title
Location of the membranous urethra

## question
The membranous part of the male urethra lies within which of the following structures?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The wall of the urinary bladder

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The bladder neck is encircled by the internal urethral sphincter and continues as the prostatic urethra; the membranous urethra lies well below the bladder, beyond the apex of the prostate.

## answer_b
The external urethral sphincter

## explanation_b
Correct. The membranous urethra is the shortest and least dilatable part of the male urethra. It passes through the deep perineal pouch and the perineal membrane, surrounded by the sphincter urethrae (external urethral sphincter), which provides voluntary continence.

## answer_c
The bulb of the penis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The bulb of the penis surrounds the beginning of the spongy (penile) urethra, which lies distal to the membranous part.

## answer_d
The substance of the prostate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The prostate encloses the prostatic urethra, the segment proximal to the membranous urethra; the membranous part begins at the prostatic apex.

## title
Interior of the urinary bladder

## question
Which of the following statements about the interior of the urinary bladder is CORRECT?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The interureteric crest joins the two ureteric orifices

## explanation_a
Correct. The interureteric crest (bar) is a ridge raised by the underlying interureteric muscle that runs transversely between the two ureteric orifices; it marks the upper boundary of the trigone and is a landmark used at cystoscopy.

## answer_b
The apex of the trigone lies at a ureteric orifice

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The apex (lowest angle) of the trigone is at the internal urethral orifice; the two ureteric orifices form the upper lateral angles of the trigone.

## answer_c
The mucosa over the trigone is loosely folded

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Over the trigone the mucosa is smooth and firmly adherent to the underlying muscle, unlike the rugose, loosely attached mucosa lining the rest of the bladder.

## answer_d
The whole bladder lining is a derivative of mesoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The bladder is lined by transitional epithelium (urothelium); most of it is of endodermal origin from the vesical part of the urogenital sinus, with only the trigone region having a mesodermal (mesonephric duct) contribution.

## question
Which of the following contributes to the blood supply of the scrotum?

## subject
gi

## vignette

C

## answer_a
The anterior scrotal skin is supplied by the femoral nerve

## explanation_a
Incorrect, and this concerns innervation rather than blood supply: the anterior scrotal skin is supplied by the ilioinguinal nerve and the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve (both L1), not the femoral nerve.

## answer_b
The posterior scrotal skin is supplied by the subcostal nerve

## explanation_b
Incorrect, and again a nerve rather than an artery: the posterior scrotal skin is supplied by the perineal (posterior scrotal) branches of the pudendal nerve (S3), not the subcostal nerve (T12).

The deep external pudendal artery is among its arteries

## explanation_c
Correct. The scrotum is supplied by the anterior scrotal branches of the superficial and deep external pudendal arteries (from the femoral artery), together with the posterior scrotal branches of the internal pudendal artery and the cremasteric artery.

## answer_d
Its skin is corrugated by the external oblique muscle

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The scrotal skin is thrown into rugae by the dartos muscle, a sheet of smooth muscle in the superficial fascia, not by the external oblique.

## title
Development of the ductus deferens

## question
The ductus (vas) deferens develops from the:

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

Mesonephric (Wolffian) duct

## explanation_a
Correct. The ductus deferens, together with the epididymis, seminal vesicle and ejaculatory duct, is a derivative of the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct, whose persistence and differentiation in the male are driven by testosterone.

Ureteric bud

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The ureteric bud, itself an outgrowth of the mesonephric duct, forms the ureter, renal pelvis, calyces and collecting tubules, not the genital duct.

Paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The paramesonephric duct regresses in the male under the influence of anti-Müllerian hormone; in the female it forms the uterine tubes, uterus and upper vagina.

## answer_d
Metanephric blastema

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The metanephric cap (blastema) forms the nephrons, from glomerulus to distal convoluted tubule, not the genital ducts.

Nerve supply of the testis

## question
Which of the following statements about the testis is CORRECT?

## subject
gi

## status

## answer_a
It develops from paraxial mesoderm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The gonad arises from the intermediate mesoderm of the genital (gonadal) ridge, colonised by primordial germ cells that migrate from the wall of the yolk sac; paraxial mesoderm forms the somites.

## answer_b
It drains its lymph to the inguinal nodes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The testis drains its lymph along the testicular vessels to the para-aortic (lumbar) nodes at L1–L2; only the scrotal skin drains to the superficial inguinal nodes.

Incorrect. The spermatogonia are derived from the primordial germ cells; it is the supporting Sertoli cells that arise from the coelomic (surface) epithelium of the gonadal ridge.

It is supplied by sympathetic fibres arising from the T10 segment

## explanation_d
Correct. The testis receives autonomic (mainly sympathetic) fibres from the T10–T11 segments via the renal and aortic (testicular) plexuses; this is why testicular pain is referred to the periumbilical region, the T10 dermatome.

Rectus abdominis and the arcuate line

## question
Which of the following statements about the rectus abdominis and its sheath is CORRECT?

## subject
gi

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It has three tendinous intersections visible on its posterior surface

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The tendinous intersections are firmly attached to the anterior wall of the rectus sheath and are seen on the anterior surface of the muscle (the "six-pack"); the posterior surface is smooth.

## answer_b
Below the arcuate line, the aponeuroses of all three flat muscles pass in front of it

## explanation_b
Correct. Below the arcuate line (roughly midway between the umbilicus and the pubis) the aponeuroses of the external oblique, internal oblique and transversus abdominis all pass anterior to the rectus, so the posterior surface of the muscle rests directly on the transversalis fascia.

The inferior epigastric vessels lie anterior to it

Incorrect. The inferior epigastric vessels enter the rectus sheath below the arcuate line and ascend behind (deep to) the muscle to anastomose with the superior epigastric vessels.

## answer_d
It lies directly on the transversalis fascia throughout its length

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Only below the arcuate line does the rectus lie directly on transversalis fascia; above that line the posterior wall of the rectus sheath (internal oblique and transversus aponeuroses) intervenes.
