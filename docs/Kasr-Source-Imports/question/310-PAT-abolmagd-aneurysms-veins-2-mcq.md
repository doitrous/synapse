## title
Most common site of aortic aneurysm

## question
The most common site of aortic aneurysm is:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Ascending aorta

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The ascending aorta is the site of syphilitic and Marfan-related aneurysms, but it is not the commonest site of aortic aneurysm overall.

## answer_b
Aorta distal to left subclavian artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The descending thoracic aorta distal to the left subclavian is a less common site than the infrarenal abdominal aorta.

## answer_c
Aorta proximal to renal artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The suprarenal segment (proximal to the renal arteries) is a less common site; most atherosclerotic aneurysms spare it.

## answer_d
Aorta distal to renal artery

## explanation_d
Correct. The commonest site of an (atherosclerotic) aortic aneurysm is the abdominal aorta DISTAL TO the renal arteries — the infrarenal segment, between the renal arteries and the aortic bifurcation. This region bears the heaviest atherosclerotic burden and its media lacks vasa vasorum, making the wall most vulnerable to aneurysmal dilatation. Hence the classic abdominal aortic aneurysm is infrarenal.

## title
Cerebral berry aneurysm — exception (autosomal recessive)

## question
These are for cerebral berry aneurysm except:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
They are commoner in women

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS true). Berry aneurysms are somewhat commoner in women.

## answer_b
They are commoner at the circle of Willis

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this IS true). They cluster at the branch points of the circle of Willis, where the congenital medial defects lie.

## answer_c
They are inherited as autosomal recessive disease

## explanation_c
Correct. This statement is FALSE, so it is the exception: the heritable association of berry aneurysms is with AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT polycystic kidney disease, not an autosomal recessive disease. Berry aneurysms themselves arise from a congenital medial defect and are usually sporadic, but their recognised inherited link is autosomal dominant. Labelling them autosomal recessive reverses the inheritance pattern, which is the trap tested here.

## answer_d
They are the commonest cause of spontaneous subarachnoid haemorrhage

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this IS true). Ruptured berry aneurysms are the commonest cause of spontaneous (non-traumatic) subarachnoid haemorrhage.

## title
False statement about varicose veins (pressure atrophy)

## question
The following is false about varicose veins:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It is produced by prolonged, increased intraluminal venous pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS true). Varicose veins are produced by prolonged, increased intraluminal venous pressure with valvular incompetence.

## answer_b
Esophageal varices complicate portal hypertension

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this IS true). Oesophageal varices are the dilated collateral veins that complicate portal hypertension.

## answer_c
Risk factors include obesity

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this IS true). Obesity (along with pregnancy, prolonged standing and age) is a recognised risk factor.

## answer_d
Causes pressure atrophy on surrounding structures

## explanation_d
Correct. This statement is FALSE, so it is the answer: varicose veins do NOT cause pressure atrophy of surrounding structures. Pressure atrophy of adjacent tissues (e.g. vertebral erosion) is a feature of a pulsatile, high-pressure ARTERIAL aneurysm, not of low-pressure dilated veins. Attributing pressure atrophy to varicose veins confuses venous varices with arterial aneurysms, which is the error tested here.

## title
Most important complication of varicose veins

## question
The most important complication of varicose veins is:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Oedema

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Stasis oedema is a common but comparatively minor complication, not the most important one per this source.

## answer_b
Hemorrhage

## explanation_b
Correct. This source rates HAEMORRHAGE as the most important complication of varicose veins: a thin-walled superficial varix can rupture through the atrophic overlying skin and bleed profusely, which can be dangerous if not controlled. The chronically raised venous pressure and the exposed, poorly supported varix make sudden haemorrhage the feared acute event. Oedema, aneurysm (an arterial lesion) and trophic skin changes are lesser or non-applicable answers.

## answer_c
Aneurysm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An aneurysm is an arterial wall dilatation and is not a complication of veins.

## answer_d
Trophic skin changes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Trophic skin changes (pigmentation, eczema, lipodermatosclerosis) accompany chronic venous insufficiency but are not rated the most important complication here.

## title
Complication of aneurysms (all of the above)

## question
One of the following is a complication of aneurysms:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Rupture

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the single answer. Rupture IS a complication (the major one), but it is not the only one listed.

## answer_b
Mural thrombus

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the single answer. Mural thrombus IS a complication (with risk of thromboembolism), but not the only one.

## answer_c
Pressure on the surrounding structure

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the single answer. Pressure on surrounding structures IS a complication, but not the only one.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. ALL of the above are complications of an aneurysm: rupture with haemorrhage (the most feared), mural thrombosis within the sac (which can embolise), and pressure/erosion of surrounding structures. Because each listed item is a genuine complication, 'all of the above' is the correct answer. These mechanical and thrombotic complications, not neoplastic change, are what an aneurysm produces.
