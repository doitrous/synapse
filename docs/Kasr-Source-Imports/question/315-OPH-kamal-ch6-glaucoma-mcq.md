## title
Field change not seen in open-angle glaucoma

## question
All the following field changes are associated with open-angle glaucoma, EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Nasal step

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it is a glaucomatous field change). A nasal step is a classic early defect of open-angle glaucoma.

## answer_b
Central scotoma

## explanation_b
Correct. A central scotoma is the exception: glaucoma damages the arcuate nerve-fibre bundles first and spares the central (macular) fibres until late, so central vision and acuity are preserved and a central scotoma is not a typical glaucomatous defect. A central scotoma instead suggests a maculopathy or optic-nerve disease. This makes it the field change not associated with glaucoma.

## answer_c
Arcuate scotoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it is a glaucomatous field change). Arcuate (Bjerrum) scotomas follow the arcuate nerve-fibre bundles and are characteristic of glaucoma.

## answer_d
Tubular field

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it is a glaucomatous field change). A constricted tubular field is the end-stage field of advanced glaucoma.

## title
Setting of phacolytic glaucoma

## question
Phacolytic glaucoma occurs in the setting of:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Intumescent cataract

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An intumescent (swollen) cataract causes phacomorphic glaucoma by angle closure, not phacolytic glaucoma.

## answer_b
Open anterior lens capsule

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Phacolytic glaucoma follows leakage through an intact but hypermature capsule; a frankly open (ruptured) capsule with lens material in the anterior chamber gives lens-particle or phaco-antigenic glaucoma.

## answer_c
Long-standing retinal detachment

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A long-standing retinal detachment is not the setting of phacolytic glaucoma.

## answer_d
Hypermature cataract

## explanation_d
Correct. Phacolytic glaucoma is a secondary open-angle glaucoma of a hypermature (Morgagnian) cataract, in which liquefied lens proteins leak through the intact capsule and, with macrophages, clog the trabecular meshwork. The raised pressure resolves after the cataract is removed. So the setting is a hypermature cataract.

## title
Mechanism of glaucoma after ischaemic central retinal vein occlusion

## question
Which mechanism describes secondary glaucoma following ischemic central retinal vein occlusion?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Blood in the trabecular meshwork

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Blood clogging the trabecular meshwork causes haemolytic or ghost-cell glaucoma after vitreous haemorrhage, not the glaucoma of ischaemic vein occlusion.

## answer_b
Rise of IOP following a rise in systemic blood pressure

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Intraocular pressure is not driven by systemic blood pressure in this way; the glaucoma here is structural, from new vessels.

## answer_c
Neo-vessel formation in the anterior chamber angle

## explanation_c
Correct. An ischaemic central retinal vein occlusion releases angiogenic factors that grow new vessels (rubeosis) over the iris and into the anterior-chamber angle, forming a fibrovascular membrane that blocks and later contracts over the trabecular meshwork. This is neovascular glaucoma. The mechanism is neovessel formation in the angle.

## answer_d
Angle recession

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Angle recession is a post-traumatic tearing of the ciliary body, not the mechanism after an ischaemic vein occlusion.

## title
True statement about angle-closure glaucoma

## question
In angle closure glaucoma:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Treatment is essentially surgical.

## explanation_a
Correct. The definitive treatment of angle-closure glaucoma is surgical: a laser or surgical peripheral iridotomy to relieve pupil block and reopen the angle. Medical treatment only lowers the pressure acutely and does not cure the anatomical block, so the management is essentially surgical. This is the true statement.

## answer_b
If medical treatment controls the high intraocular pressure, surgery is not needed.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Even when drops control the pressure, an iridotomy is still needed to remove the underlying pupil block, so medical control does not make surgery unnecessary.

## answer_c
Can be secondary to immature cataract.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Angle closure is precipitated by an intumescent (swollen) cataract, not an immature one.

## answer_d
Is more common in myopic women over 40 years old.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Angle-closure glaucoma is commoner in hypermetropic (not myopic) older women, because hypermetropic eyes have shallow anterior chambers.

## title
True statement about primary open-angle glaucoma

## question
One of the following is TRUE about primary open-angle glaucoma:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It usually presents with headache.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Primary open-angle glaucoma is painless and does not typically present with headache.

## answer_b
Optic disc analysis is mandatory.

## explanation_b
Correct. Optic-disc analysis is mandatory in primary open-angle glaucoma because the disease is asymptomatic until late and cannot be found from symptoms. Assessing the cup-to-disc ratio and neuroretinal rim, with tonometry and perimetry, is how the disease is detected and followed. So disc analysis being mandatory is the true statement.

## answer_c
It requires surgery in most cases.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Most cases are controlled medically; surgery is reserved for progression or intolerance, not the majority.

## answer_d
