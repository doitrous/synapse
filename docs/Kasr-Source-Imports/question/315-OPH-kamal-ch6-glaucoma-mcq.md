## title
Field change not seen in open-angle glaucoma

## question
All the following field changes are associated with open-angle glaucoma, EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Nasal step

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it is a glaucomatous field change). A nasal step is a classic early defect of open-angle glaucoma.

## answer_b
Central scotoma

## explanation_b
Correct. A central scotoma is the exception: glaucoma damages the arcuate nerve-fibre bundles first and spares the central (macular) fibres until late, so central vision and acuity are preserved and a central scotoma is not a typical glaucomatous defect. A central scotoma instead suggests a maculopathy or optic-nerve disease. This makes it the field change not associated with glaucoma.

## answer_c
Arcuate scotoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it is a glaucomatous field change). Arcuate (Bjerrum) scotomas follow the arcuate nerve-fibre bundles and are characteristic of glaucoma.

## answer_d
Tubular field

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it is a glaucomatous field change). A constricted tubular field is the end-stage field of advanced glaucoma.

## title
Setting of phacolytic glaucoma

## question
Phacolytic glaucoma occurs in the setting of:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Intumescent cataract

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An intumescent (swollen) cataract causes phacomorphic glaucoma by angle closure, not phacolytic glaucoma.

## answer_b
Open anterior lens capsule

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Phacolytic glaucoma follows leakage through an intact but hypermature capsule; a frankly open (ruptured) capsule with lens material in the anterior chamber gives lens-particle or phaco-antigenic glaucoma.

## answer_c
Long-standing retinal detachment

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A long-standing retinal detachment is not the setting of phacolytic glaucoma.

## answer_d
Hypermature cataract

## explanation_d
Correct. Phacolytic glaucoma is a secondary open-angle glaucoma of a hypermature (Morgagnian) cataract, in which liquefied lens proteins leak through the intact capsule and, with macrophages, clog the trabecular meshwork. The raised pressure resolves after the cataract is removed. So the setting is a hypermature cataract.

## title
Mechanism of glaucoma after ischaemic central retinal vein occlusion

## question
Which mechanism describes secondary glaucoma following ischemic central retinal vein occlusion?

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Blood in the trabecular meshwork

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Blood clogging the trabecular meshwork causes haemolytic or ghost-cell glaucoma after vitreous haemorrhage, not the glaucoma of ischaemic vein occlusion.

## answer_b
Rise of IOP following a rise in systemic blood pressure

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Intraocular pressure is not driven by systemic blood pressure in this way; the glaucoma here is structural, from new vessels.

## answer_c
Neo-vessel formation in the anterior chamber angle

## explanation_c
Correct. An ischaemic central retinal vein occlusion releases angiogenic factors that grow new vessels (rubeosis) over the iris and into the anterior-chamber angle, forming a fibrovascular membrane that blocks and later contracts over the trabecular meshwork. This is neovascular glaucoma. The mechanism is neovessel formation in the angle.

## answer_d
Angle recession

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Angle recession is a post-traumatic tearing of the ciliary body, not the mechanism after an ischaemic vein occlusion.

## title
Feature not seen in open-angle glaucoma

## question
Open angle glaucoma can present with all of the following, EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
High intraocular pressure.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it is a feature). A raised intraocular pressure is typical of open-angle glaucoma.

## answer_b
Shallow anterior chamber with iris bombe.

## explanation_b
Correct. A shallow anterior chamber with iris bombe is the exception: open-angle glaucoma has a normal, open, deep angle, whereas a shallow chamber and iris bombe are the hallmarks of angle-closure glaucoma. So this feature does not belong to open-angle glaucoma. It is what separates the closed-angle from the open-angle mechanism.

## answer_c
Optic atrophy.

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it is a feature). Progressive optic-nerve damage produces glaucomatous optic atrophy with cupping.

## answer_d
Visual field defect.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (it is a feature). Characteristic visual-field loss is a defining feature of open-angle glaucoma.

## title
True statement about angle-closure glaucoma

## question
In angle closure glaucoma:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Treatment is essentially surgical.

## explanation_a
Correct. The definitive treatment of angle-closure glaucoma is surgical: a laser or surgical peripheral iridotomy to relieve pupil block and reopen the angle. Medical treatment only lowers the pressure acutely and does not cure the anatomical block, so the management is essentially surgical. This is the true statement.

## answer_b
If medical treatment controls the high intraocular pressure, surgery is not needed.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Even when drops control the pressure, an iridotomy is still needed to remove the underlying pupil block, so medical control does not make surgery unnecessary.

## answer_c
Can be secondary to immature cataract.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Angle closure is precipitated by an intumescent (swollen) cataract, not an immature one.

## answer_d
Is more common in myopic women over 40 years old.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Angle-closure glaucoma is commoner in hypermetropic (not myopic) older women, because hypermetropic eyes have shallow anterior chambers.

## title
True statement about congenital glaucoma

## question
Congenital glaucoma:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Should be treated by topical antiglaucoma eye drops only.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Topical drops are only a temporising measure; they do not correct the developmental angle anomaly.

## answer_b
Surgical treatment is the definitive treatment.

## explanation_b
Correct. Congenital glaucoma is caused by a developmental anomaly of the anterior-chamber angle that blocks aqueous outflow, and its definitive treatment is surgical, by goniotomy or trabeculotomy to open the obstructed angle. Drops only temporise before surgery. Early surgery is essential to prevent irreversible blindness.

## answer_c
Is best treated by lens extraction.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The problem is the angle, not the lens, so lens extraction is not the treatment.

## answer_d
Does not cause blindness if not treated.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Untreated congenital glaucoma causes buphthalmos and progresses to irreversible blindness.

## title
Purpose of fundus examination in open-angle glaucoma

## question
Fundus examination is important in open angle glaucoma examination:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
To detect arteriovenous correlation.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Arteriovenous crossing changes belong to hypertensive retinopathy, not to the glaucoma assessment.

## answer_b
To detect optic disc cupping.

## explanation_b
Correct. Fundus examination in glaucoma is done to detect optic-disc cupping, the sign of glaucomatous optic neuropathy, with an increased cup-to-disc ratio and neuroretinal-rim thinning. Following the cupping tracks the disease and its field loss. This is the reason fundoscopy is essential in glaucoma.

## answer_c
To detect any associated retinal tear.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Looking for a retinal tear is part of a peripheral retinal examination, not the purpose of fundoscopy in glaucoma.

## answer_d
To check red reflex.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The red reflex assesses the clarity of the media (for example a cataract), not the optic-disc damage of glaucoma.

## title
Cause not producing secondary open-angle glaucoma

## question
Causes of secondary open angle glaucoma include all of the following, EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Acute iridocyclitis.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (it is a secondary open-angle cause). Acute iridocyclitis raises the pressure with an open angle by clogging the meshwork with inflammatory cells and debris.

## answer_b
Angle recession.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (it is a secondary open-angle cause). Post-traumatic angle recession damages the trabecular meshwork, causing an open-angle glaucoma.

## answer_c
Steroid induced glaucoma.

## explanation_c
Incorrect (it is a secondary open-angle cause). Prolonged topical steroids reduce trabecular outflow, causing steroid-induced open-angle glaucoma.

## answer_d
Phacomorphic glaucoma.

## explanation_d
Correct. Phacomorphic glaucoma is the exception: it is a secondary angle-CLOSURE glaucoma, in which an intumescent (swollen) cataract pushes the iris forward and closes the angle, not an open-angle mechanism. All the other options act with an open angle, so phacomorphic glaucoma is the one that does not belong.
