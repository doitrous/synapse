## title
Hormone acting by the IP3-Ca2+ mechanism

## question
Which of the following hormones acts by the IP3-Ca2+ mechanism?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cholecalciferol

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cholecalciferol (vitamin D) is lipophilic and acts through an intracellular nuclear receptor (the vitamin D receptor) to regulate gene transcription, not through IP3-calcium at the membrane.

## answer_b
Progesterone

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Progesterone is a steroid; it crosses the membrane and binds an intracellular nuclear receptor that regulates transcription, so it uses no membrane second messenger.

## answer_c
Insulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Insulin binds a receptor tyrosine kinase that autophosphorylates and recruits IRS proteins; its signalling is through the tyrosine-kinase cascade, not IP3-calcium.

## answer_d
Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)

## explanation_d
Correct. GnRH is a hydrophilic decapeptide that binds a Gq-coupled receptor on the pituitary gonadotroph. Gq activates phospholipase C, which splits PIP2 into inositol trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol; IP3 releases calcium from intracellular stores and DAG activates protein kinase C, together driving LH and FSH release. This IP3-calcium pathway is the defining second-messenger mechanism of GnRH and separates it from the nuclear-receptor steroids and the tyrosine-kinase insulin among the options.

## title
Hormone with intrinsic lactogenic activity

## question
Which of the following hormones has intrinsic lactogenic activity?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)

## explanation_a
Incorrect. TSH stimulates the thyroid follicular cells to make thyroid hormone; it has no action on the breast and no lactogenic activity.

## answer_b
Melanocyte-stimulating hormone (MSH)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. MSH acts on melanocytes to promote pigmentation; it is not lactogenic.

## answer_c
Growth hormone (GH)

## explanation_c
Correct. Growth hormone belongs to one structurally homologous family with prolactin and human placental lactogen, so it can bind the prolactin receptor and possesses intrinsic lactogenic activity in addition to its somatotropic effects. This family homology is why marked growth-hormone excess can be associated with galactorrhoea even without a rise in prolactin, and it is what makes GH the lactogenic hormone among these options.

## answer_d
Dopamine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dopamine is the tonic hypothalamic inhibitor of prolactin release, the opposite of a lactogenic agent.

## title
Plasma carrier of ADH

## question
ADH circulates in plasma primarily:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Bound to neurophysin I

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Neurophysin I is the carrier peptide paired with oxytocin, not with ADH.

## answer_b
Bound to neurophysin II

## explanation_b
Correct. Antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) is synthesised in hypothalamic magnocellular neurons as part of a larger precursor that also yields its specific carrier peptide, neurophysin II. The hormone is transported down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract, stored in the posterior pituitary and released bound to neurophysin II, which is its principal association in the circulation. The specific ADH-neurophysin II pairing (versus oxytocin-neurophysin I) is the point tested here.

## answer_c
Bound to plasma albumin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Plasma albumin and specific globulins carry lipophilic steroid and thyroid hormones; ADH is associated with its own peptide carrier, neurophysin II.

## answer_d
Not bound to plasma proteins

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ADH is not simply free in plasma; it is released and associated with neurophysin II derived from its common precursor.
