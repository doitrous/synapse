## title
Hormone acting by the IP3-Ca2+ mechanism

## question
Which of the following hormones acts by the IP3-Ca2+ mechanism?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cholecalciferol

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cholecalciferol (vitamin D) is lipophilic and acts through an intracellular nuclear receptor (the vitamin D receptor) to regulate gene transcription, not through IP3-calcium at the membrane.

## answer_b
Progesterone

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Progesterone is a steroid; it crosses the membrane and binds an intracellular nuclear receptor that regulates transcription, so it uses no membrane second messenger.

## answer_c
Insulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Insulin binds a receptor tyrosine kinase that autophosphorylates and recruits IRS proteins; its signalling is through the tyrosine-kinase cascade, not IP3-calcium.

## answer_d
Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)

## explanation_d
Correct. GnRH is a hydrophilic decapeptide that binds a Gq-coupled receptor on the pituitary gonadotroph. Gq activates phospholipase C, which splits PIP2 into inositol trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol; IP3 releases calcium from intracellular stores and DAG activates protein kinase C, together driving LH and FSH release. This IP3-calcium pathway is the defining second-messenger mechanism of GnRH and separates it from the nuclear-receptor steroids and the tyrosine-kinase insulin among the options.

## title
Hormone with intrinsic lactogenic activity

## question
Which of the following hormones has intrinsic lactogenic activity?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)

## explanation_a
Incorrect. TSH stimulates the thyroid follicular cells to make thyroid hormone; it has no action on the breast and no lactogenic activity.

## answer_b
Melanocyte-stimulating hormone (MSH)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. MSH acts on melanocytes to promote pigmentation; it is not lactogenic.

## answer_c
Growth hormone (GH)

## explanation_c
Correct. Growth hormone belongs to one structurally homologous family with prolactin and human placental lactogen, so it can bind the prolactin receptor and possesses intrinsic lactogenic activity in addition to its somatotropic effects. This family homology is why marked growth-hormone excess can be associated with galactorrhoea even without a rise in prolactin, and it is what makes GH the lactogenic hormone among these options.

## answer_d
Dopamine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dopamine is the tonic hypothalamic inhibitor of prolactin release, the opposite of a lactogenic agent.

## title
Plasma carrier of ADH

## question
ADH circulates in plasma primarily:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Bound to neurophysin I

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Neurophysin I is the carrier peptide paired with oxytocin, not with ADH.

## answer_b
Bound to neurophysin II

## explanation_b
Correct. Antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) is synthesised in hypothalamic magnocellular neurons as part of a larger precursor that also yields its specific carrier peptide, neurophysin II. The hormone is transported down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract, stored in the posterior pituitary and released bound to neurophysin II, which is its principal association in the circulation. The specific ADH-neurophysin II pairing (versus oxytocin-neurophysin I) is the point tested here.

## answer_c
Bound to plasma albumin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Plasma albumin and specific globulins carry lipophilic steroid and thyroid hormones; ADH is associated with its own peptide carrier, neurophysin II.

## answer_d
Not bound to plasma proteins

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ADH is not simply free in plasma; it is released and associated with neurophysin II derived from its common precursor.

## title
Thyroid gland size in iodine deficiency

## question
All of the following about the thyroid gland is true, EXCEPT:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Takes up iodide against its electrochemical gradient

## explanation_a
Correct statement (so not the exception). The sodium-iodide symporter concentrates iodide into the follicular cell against its electrochemical gradient by coupling it to the inward sodium gradient (secondary active transport).

## answer_b
Decreases in size when dietary iodine is deficient

## explanation_b
Correct answer (this is the false statement). When dietary iodine is deficient, thyroid hormone output falls, so negative feedback is released and TSH rises. The sustained TSH drive hypertrophies and hyperplases the follicular cells, so the gland ENLARGES into an endemic goitre rather than decreasing in size.

## answer_c
Contains enzymes that oxidise iodide to iodine

## explanation_c
Correct statement (so not the exception). Thyroid peroxidase oxidises trapped iodide to a reactive iodine species before organification.

## answer_d
Contains enzymes that iodinate tyrosine

## explanation_d
Correct statement (so not the exception). Thyroid peroxidase iodinates tyrosyl residues of thyroglobulin (organification) to form MIT and DIT.

## title
Why pre-pubertal castration causes tall stature

## question
Men castrated before puberty grow taller than normal because:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Release of growth hormone is increased

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Castration does not raise growth-hormone secretion; the extra height comes from prolonged growth-plate activity, not from more GH.

## answer_b
Of androgen resistance

## explanation_b
Incorrect. These men are not androgen-resistant; they simply have little androgen to aromatise, so the defect is in estrogen production, not androgen receptor function.

## answer_c
Estradiol in the epiphyseal growth plate takes longer to reach its closure threshold

## explanation_c
Correct. Epiphyseal-plate closure is ultimately driven by estrogen, most of which in males comes from aromatisation of testicular androgen. After pre-pubertal castration there is little androgen to aromatise, so estradiol in the growth plate takes much longer to reach the level that fuses the plates. The epiphyses stay open longer and the long bones keep growing, producing the tall eunuchoid stature.

## answer_d
Of insensitivity to IGF-1

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sensitivity to IGF-1 is preserved; the mechanism is delayed estrogen-driven epiphyseal closure, not altered IGF-1 signalling.

## title
Why sexual precocity causes short adult stature

## question
Patients with sexual precocity are apt to be short as adults because of:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Increased levels of androgens

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Although androgens rise, it is their aromatisation to estrogen, and the estrogen action on the growth plate, that closes the epiphyses; naming androgens alone misses the operative hormone.

## answer_b
The estrogen surge during precocious puberty

## explanation_b
Correct. In precocious puberty the early rise in estrogen (aromatised from the prematurely secreted sex steroids) closes the epiphyseal plates ahead of schedule. Growth is initially fast, giving a tall child, but the premature epiphyseal fusion stops linear growth early, so the final adult height is short. This is the mirror image of the delayed closure seen after pre-pubertal castration.

## answer_c
Deficiency of androgen receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Androgen receptors are not deficient; the short stature results from premature estrogen-driven epiphyseal closure, not receptor loss.

## answer_d
Increased levels of thyroxine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Thyroxine is not the cause; the early epiphyseal fusion is due to the premature estrogen surge.

## title
Effect of high estrogen on the fallopian tube

## question
A high concentration of estrogen in the adult female causes:

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Secretory changes to appear in the endometrial glands

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Secretory transformation of the endometrial glands is a progesterone action of the luteal phase, not an estrogen effect.

## answer_b
Increased motility of the cilia of the fallopian tube

## explanation_b
Correct. A high estrogen level stimulates the ciliated cells of the fallopian tube, increasing the number and coordinated beating of the cilia toward the uterus, and it raises tubal smooth-muscle activity. This estrogen-driven ciliary and muscular activity helps sweep the ovum along the tube after ovulation.

## answer_c
Decreased excitability of uterine muscle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Reduced uterine excitability (myometrial quiescence) is a progesterone effect; estrogen tends to increase, not decrease, uterine excitability.

## answer_d
Marked growth of the acini of the breast prior to lactation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Marked growth of the breast acini before lactation is driven by progesterone (with prolactin), whereas estrogen mainly develops the ductal system.
