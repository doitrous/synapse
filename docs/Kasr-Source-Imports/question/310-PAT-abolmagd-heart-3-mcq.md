## answer_c

## explanation_c
Correct. ARTHRALGIA (joint pain without objective signs of inflammation) is a MINOR criterion, not a major one, so it is the item NOT included among the majors. The minor criteria include arthralgia, fever, raised acute-phase reactants and a prolonged PR interval.

Incorrect. Subcutaneous nodules are a Jones MAJOR criterion, so they are included.

## title
Where the Aschoff nodule is best seen

## question
Aschoff's nodule in rheumatic heart is best seen in:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Valves

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The valves show the warty rheumatic vegetations and later fibrous fusion, but the diagnostic Aschoff nodule is not best seen there.

## answer_b
Endocardium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The endocardium (including MacCallum's patch) is involved, but the Aschoff nodule is characteristically a myocardial lesion.

## answer_c
Pericardium

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The pericardium shows serofibrinous ('bread-and-butter') pericarditis, not the Aschoff nodule.

## answer_d
Myocardium

## explanation_d
Correct. Although rheumatic fever is a PANCARDITIS, the pathognomonic ASCHOFF NODULE is best seen in the MYOCARDIUM, characteristically in the perivascular interstitial connective tissue. It shows central fibrinoid necrosis ringed by activated macrophages (Anitschkow cells), multinucleate Aschoff giant cells and lymphocytes. This myocardial interstitial location is where the diagnostic lesion is identified.

cvs

The 'BREAD-AND-BUTTER' appearance is the gross hallmark of SEROFIBRINOUS (fibrinous) pericarditis: a fibrin-rich exudate coats the visceral and parietal pericardial surfaces, and pulling the inflamed layers apart draws out shaggy fibrin strands like buttered bread being separated. Incorrect. Suppurative (purulent) pericarditis is caused by pyogenic bacteria, not rheumatic fever.

## answer_d
Constrictive pericarditis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Constrictive pericarditis is a late fibrosing/calcifying sequel, not the acute bread-and-butter appearance.

## title
Acute infective endocarditis — the false feature

## question
Acute infective endocarditis does not have the following feature:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Caused by a highly virulent organism, e.g. Staphylococcus aureus

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true feature). Acute infective endocarditis is caused by highly virulent organisms such as Staphylococcus aureus.

## answer_b
Affects a previously injured or abnormal valve

## explanation_b
Correct (the feature it does NOT have). Acute infective endocarditis characteristically attacks PREVIOUSLY NORMAL (healthy) valves, destroying them rapidly. Affecting an already injured or abnormal valve describes the SUBACUTE form, in which low-virulence organisms seed damaged valves. Therefore 'affects a previously injured valve' is not a feature of the acute form and is the answer.

## answer_c
The larger vegetation often causes embolic complication

## explanation_c
Incorrect (a true feature). The large septic vegetations of acute infective endocarditis frequently break off to cause embolic complications and septic infarcts.

## answer_d
Death occurs in days to weeks in 50 to 60% of the patients

## explanation_d
Incorrect (a true feature). Acute infective endocarditis is rapidly progressive and fatal in a high proportion (about 50-60%) of patients within days to weeks.

Characteristic histologic feature of rheumatic fever

## question
The characteristic histologic feature of rheumatic fever is:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Rheumatoid nodule

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The rheumatoid nodule is the lesion of rheumatoid arthritis, not rheumatic fever.

## answer_b
Aschoff nodule

## explanation_b
Correct. The ASCHOFF NODULE (body) is the characteristic (pathognomonic) HISTOLOGIC lesion of rheumatic fever: a focus of central fibrinoid necrosis surrounded by activated macrophages (Anitschkow cells with 'caterpillar' chromatin), multinucleate Aschoff giant cells, lymphocytes and plasma cells. It is found chiefly in the myocardial interstitium. Its presence is diagnostic of rheumatic carditis.

Incorrect. MacCallum's patch is a GROSS lesion (subendocardial thickening of the left atrium), not the characteristic histologic feature.

## answer_d
Peyer's patches

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peyer's patches are normal gut-associated lymphoid tissue and are unrelated to rheumatic fever.

## question
MacCallum's patch is healed rheumatic endocarditis in:

## subject
cvs

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. MacCallum's patch is a left-sided, not right-atrial, lesion.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The right ventricle is not the site of MacCallum's patch.

## answer_c
The posterior wall of the left atrium

## explanation_c
Correct. MacCALLUM'S PATCH is a map-like area of subendocardial thickening (healed rheumatic endocarditis) on the POSTERIOR WALL of the LEFT ATRIUM. Its left-atrial posterior-wall location is the specific fact tested.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Although the left ventricle is on the mitral side, the patch classically lies on the posterior wall of the left ATRIUM, above the mitral valve.

Subacute infective endocarditis — the false feature

## question
The following are characteristic of subacute infective endocarditis except:

## subject
cvs

## status

D

## explanation_a
Incorrect (a true feature). Subacute infective endocarditis characteristically affects the left-sided mitral and aortic valves, which are the valves most often previously damaged.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (a true feature). The subacute form produces large, bulky, brownish but relatively non-destructive vegetations.

Correct (the false feature). Friable vegetations with VALVE PERFORATION and rapid destruction are features of ACUTE infective endocarditis by virulent organisms, NOT of the subacute form.
