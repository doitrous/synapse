## title
Control of post-adenoidectomy bleeding

## question
Post adenoidectomy bleeding can be treated by:

## subject
mul

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Posterior nasal packing.

## explanation_a
Correct. The adenoid bed lies in the postnasal space, so bleeding after adenoidectomy is arrested by a posterior nasal (postnasal) pack that applies direct pressure to the nasopharynx. The pack is placed if simple measures fail and the child is kept under observation. Anterior packing alone does not reach the bleeding bed.

## answer_b
Local nasal steroids.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Local nasal steroids reduce mucosal inflammation over weeks and have no role in stopping active surgical haemorrhage.

## answer_c
Anti inflammatory drugs.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Anti-inflammatory drugs do not control bleeding; some (like NSAIDs) can even worsen it by impairing platelet function.

## answer_d
Hydrogen peroxide mouth gargle.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A hydrogen peroxide mouth gargle cleans the oral cavity but cannot tamponade a bleeding nasopharyngeal adenoid bed.

## answer_e
None of the above.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. There is an effective measure — a postnasal pack — so "none of the above" is wrong.

Effect not caused by adenoid enlargement

## question
Adenoid enlargement may result in any of the following EXCEPT:

## subject
mul

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adenoid hypertrophy obstructs both Eustachian tube openings and causes bilateral secretory otitis media (middle ear effusion), so this is a genuine effect and not the exception.

## explanation_b
Correct. Recurrent epistaxis is NOT a feature of adenoid enlargement; the adenoid is lymphoid tissue that obstructs and infects rather than bleeds. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nasopharyngeal obstruction causes snoring and obstructive sleep apnoea, a true and important effect of adenoid enlargement.

## answer_e
Recurrent acute otitis media.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Eustachian tube obstruction and the adenoid acting as an infection reservoir cause recurrent acute otitis media, so this is a real effect.

## subject
mul

## vignette

C

## answer_a
Radiotherapy is considered the treatment of choice.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (true statement). Radiotherapy is the treatment of choice for nasopharyngeal carcinoma because the tumour is radiosensitive and surgically inaccessible, so this is true and not the exception.

## explanation_b

Naming the facial nerve as the first affected is therefore the incorrect statement.

Incorrect (true statement). Nasopharyngeal carcinoma metastasises early to the cervical lymph nodes, so this is true.

The mass may extend into the nasal cavity.

## explanation_e
Incorrect (true statement). The mass can extend anteriorly into the nasal cavity, so this is a genuine feature.

## subject

Incorrect. Ultrasonography does not image a deep nasopharyngeal mass or its skull-base extension adequately.

B

## explanation_b
Correct. A 15-year-old male with blood-stained nasal discharge, conductive deafness (Eustachian tube obstruction) and a strongly enhancing nasopharyngeal mass on CT is the classic picture of juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma, a highly vascular benign tumour of adolescent boys. ## answer_e
Inverted papilloma.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Inverted papilloma arises from the lateral nasal wall in adults and is not typically an enhancing nasopharyngeal mass in an adolescent.
