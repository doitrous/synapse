## title
Drug that inhibits synthesis of all eicosanoids

## question
Which of the following would inhibit the synthesis of all the members of the eicosanoid family?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Dazoxiben

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Dazoxiben inhibits thromboxane synthase only, blocking thromboxane production but not the other eicosanoids.

## answer_b
Glucocorticoids

## explanation_b
Correct. Glucocorticoids induce lipocortin (annexin-1), which inhibits phospholipase A2, the enzyme that liberates arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids. Because arachidonic acid is the common precursor of ALL eicosanoids, blocking its release stops synthesis of prostaglandins, thromboxanes AND leukotrienes together. This upstream action is why glucocorticoids suppress the entire family.

## answer_c
Montelukast

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Montelukast is a leukotriene-receptor antagonist; it blocks a receptor, not eicosanoid synthesis, and only the leukotriene arm.

## answer_d
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs

## explanation_d
Incorrect. NSAIDs inhibit cyclo-oxygenase, so they block prostaglandins and thromboxanes but leave the lipoxygenase (leukotriene) pathway intact — not all eicosanoids.

## title
Most likely adverse effect of diphenhydramine

## question
A 20-year-old woman is taking diphenhydramine for severe hay fever. Which of the following adverse effects is she most likely to report?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Muscarinic increase in bladder tone

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diphenhydramine has ANTImuscarinic effects, so it tends to reduce bladder tone (risking retention), not increase it.

## answer_b
Nausea

## explanation_b
Incorrect. It is used to relieve nausea in some settings; nausea is not its typical adverse effect.

## answer_c
Nervousness and anxiety

## explanation_c
Incorrect. First-generation antihistamines cause CNS depression, not nervousness or anxiety.

## answer_d
Sedation

## explanation_d
Correct. Diphenhydramine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine that readily crosses the blood-brain barrier and blocks central H1 receptors, producing marked SEDATION and drowsiness. This is its most common adverse effect and the reason such agents are also used as over-the-counter sleep aids. Its antimuscarinic actions add dry mouth and blurred vision.

## title
Main use of epoprostenol (PGI2 analogue)

## question
Epoprostenol, an analogue of PGI2, is sometimes used by intravenous infusion. Which of the following is the main use for this drug?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Treatment of postpartum haemorrhage

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Postpartum haemorrhage is treated with uterotonics such as oxytocin, ergometrine or the prostaglandin analogue misoprostol/carboprost, not with the vasodilator epoprostenol.

## answer_b
Treatment of open-angle glaucoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glaucoma prostaglandin therapy uses topical PGF2-alpha analogues (latanoprost), not intravenous epoprostenol.

## answer_c
Treatment of pulmonary hypertension

## explanation_c
Correct. Epoprostenol is a synthetic prostacyclin (PGI2) analogue and a potent vasodilator and inhibitor of platelet aggregation. Given by continuous intravenous infusion it lowers pulmonary vascular resistance, so its main use is treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension. Its very short half-life is why it must be infused continuously.

## answer_d
Treatment of acute migraine headache

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Acute migraine is treated with triptans or ergot alkaloids (vasoconstrictors), the opposite pharmacology to a vasodilator prostacyclin.

## title
Prostaglandin given before neonatal cardiac surgery

## question
A newborn infant is being prepared for surgical repair of congenital heart disease. Which of the following agents might be administered pre-operatively?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Alprostadil

## explanation_a
Correct. Alprostadil is prostaglandin E1. In duct-dependent congenital heart lesions it is infused to keep the ductus arteriosus OPEN, maintaining pulmonary or systemic blood flow until corrective or palliative surgery can be performed. This ductus-maintaining action is exactly why it is given pre-operatively in the neonate.

## answer_b
Ergotamine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ergotamine is a vasoconstrictor used for migraine; it has no role in maintaining ductal patency and would be harmful.

## answer_c
Misoprostol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Misoprostol is a PGE1 analogue used for gastric protection and obstetric indications; it is not used to keep the neonatal duct open.

## answer_d
Zafirlukast

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Zafirlukast is a leukotriene-receptor antagonist for asthma, unrelated to ductal patency.

Ergot derivative used to suppress lactation

## question
Which of the following is an ergot derivative used in the suppression of lactation?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## correct_answer
A

Bromocriptine

## explanation_a
Correct. Bromocriptine is a semisynthetic ergot derivative and a dopamine (D2) receptor agonist. By stimulating pituitary dopamine receptors it suppresses prolactin secretion, so it is used to suppress lactation and to treat hyperprolactinaemia. Incorrect. Ergotamine is an ergot alkaloid used for migraine (vasoconstriction), not for lactation suppression.

Ergometrine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ergometrine is a uterotonic used to control postpartum haemorrhage, not to suppress lactation.

## answer_d
Ketanserin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ketanserin is a 5-HT2 antagonist and is not an ergot derivative used for lactation.

## title
Most likely indication for itopride

## question
Itopride is used as a prokinetic agent. Which of the following is its most likely indication?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
As a prokinetic for the treatment of gastro-oesophageal reflux

## explanation_a
Correct. Itopride enhances gastrointestinal motility (it blocks dopamine D2 receptors and inhibits acetylcholinesterase, raising acetylcholine in the gut wall), so it is used as a PROKINETIC for functional dyspepsia and gastro-oesophageal reflux, where it speeds gastric emptying. Promoting forward motility is the basis of this indication.

## answer_b
Symptomatic treatment of carcinoid syndrome

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Carcinoid syndrome is treated with somatostatin analogues and serotonin antagonists, not a prokinetic.

## answer_c
Prophylaxis of migraine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Migraine prophylaxis uses agents such as propranolol, topiramate or amitriptyline, not itopride.

## answer_d
Treatment of chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chemotherapy-induced vomiting is controlled by 5-HT3 antagonists (ondansetron); itopride is a motility agent, not a specific antiemetic for this setting.

## title
Antiemetic for chemotherapy-induced vomiting

## question
A 40-year-old patient is about to undergo cancer chemotherapy with a combination that produces severe nausea and vomiting. The antiemetic drug most likely to be included in her regimen is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Bromocriptine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bromocriptine is a dopamine agonist and itself causes nausea; it is not an antiemetic.

## answer_b
Cetirizine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cetirizine is a second-generation H1 antihistamine for allergy, not effective for chemotherapy-induced vomiting.

## answer_c
Cimetidine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cimetidine is an H2 antagonist that reduces gastric acid; it does not control chemotherapy emesis.

## answer_d
Ondansetron

## explanation_d
Correct. Ondansetron is a 5-HT3 (serotonin) receptor antagonist. Chemotherapy triggers serotonin release from gut enterochromaffin cells, which stimulates 5-HT3 receptors on vagal afferents and the chemoreceptor trigger zone; blocking these receptors makes ondansetron highly effective for severe chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting. That mechanism is why it is the antiemetic of choice here.
