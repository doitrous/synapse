## label
Herring bodies are the accumulated neurosecretion of the pars nervosa
## canonical_key
pituitary-histology.pars-nervosa.herring-bodies
## aliases
Neurosecretory bodies of the posterior pituitary
Herring bodies in the neurohypophysis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The pars nervosa (posterior pituitary) is not glandular; it is made of unmyelinated axons of hypothalamic magnocellular neurons and supporting pituicytes. Along these axons the neurosecretory material accumulates in dilated terminals visible by light microscopy as Herring bodies. These bodies store oxytocin or ADH bound to neurophysin, released into the fenestrated capillaries of the neurohypophysis.
## explicit_objective
Identify Herring bodies as the characteristic accumulated neurosecretion of the pars nervosa.
## pitfalls
Mistaking the pars nervosa for glandular follicular tissue, or attributing the pars tuberalis to it.

The pituitary has a dual origin from Rathke's pouch and the infundibulum

## definition
The pituitary gland develops from two primordia that meet and fuse. An ectodermal outpocketing of the stomodeal roof (Rathke's pouch) forms the adenohypophysis (anterior lobe, pars intermedia and pars tuberalis), while a neuroectodermal downgrowth from the floor of the diencephalon (the infundibulum) forms the neurohypophysis (posterior lobe and stalk). This dual origin explains why the gland has both a glandular and a neural part.
## explicit_objective
Explain the dual embryological origin of the pituitary from Rathke's pouch and the infundibulum.
## pitfalls
Assigning the anterior lobe to the infundibulum, or calling Rathke's pouch endodermal.

## label
Cryptorchidism spares the interstitial Leydig cells while damaging germ cells
## canonical_key
cryptorchidism.spared-cells.leydig
## aliases
Heat damage in undescended testis
Leydig cells resistant in cryptorchidism
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
In cryptorchidism the seminiferous epithelium is exposed to the higher intra-abdominal temperature, which arrests and destroys the temperature-sensitive spermatogenic cells (spermatocytes and spermatids). The interstitial Leydig cells, which secrete testosterone, are relatively heat-resistant and are spared. This is why an untreated undescended testis causes infertility but usually preserves androgen production and secondary sexual characters.
## explicit_objective
Recognise that cryptorchidism damages heat-sensitive germ cells while sparing the interstitial Leydig cells.
## pitfalls
Believing Leydig cells fail in cryptorchidism, or that androgen output is lost.
