# Item
## label
The hyalomere’s microtubules and canalicular system carry out the platelet’s shape change and release
## id
CON-HEM-CC292B4D6CC61E
## canonical_key
platelet-hyalomere-structure-function
## definition
The hyalomere is the peripheral, pale zone of the platelet. Its marginal bundle of microtubules holds the resting discoid shape and contracts to produce pseudopodia; its open canalicular system opens the granule contents to the exterior, and its dense tubular system stores the calcium that triggers them.
## explicit_objective
Explain how each structure of the hyalomere seen on electron microscopy produces a step of platelet function.
## pitfalls
Treating the hyalomere as inert because it looks empty. The granules are in the granulomere; the machinery that acts on them is here.

# Item
## label
A platelet is a non-nucleated fragment with a pale peripheral hyalomere and a dark central granulomere
## id
CON-HEM-A2BE134E34EB83
## canonical_key
platelet-two-zones-on-light-microscopy
## definition
A platelet is a non-nucleated cytoplasmic fragment, 2–4 µm across, with a peripheral pale basophilic zone — the hyalomere — and a central dark granular zone, the granulomere, which holds the granules and the few organelles. Its cell coat is thick and rich in glycoprotein, which is what lets it adhere.
## explicit_objective
Identify a platelet and name its two zones and what each contains.
## pitfalls
Calling the granulomere pale and the hyalomere dark. The names say it: hyalo- is the clear one, and it is peripheral.

# Item
## label
Platelets carry three granule types — alpha, delta and lambda — each with different contents
## id
CON-HEM-B000CE18F93F83
## canonical_key
platelet-granule-types-and-contents
## definition
Alpha granules hold clotting factors, fibrinogen and platelet-derived growth factor. Delta (dense) granules hold ADP, ATP, calcium and serotonin. Lambda granules are lysosomes, holding hydrolytic enzymes. All three lie in the granulomere.
## explicit_objective
Name the three platelet granule types and give the contents of each.
## pitfalls
Putting serotonin in the alpha granules. It is in the delta granules with the calcium and the adenine nucleotides, and that grouping is what the question turns on.

Platelets are shed from megakaryocytes along demarcation channels that cut the cytoplasm into platelet ribbons
## id
CON-HEM-2AD9D05B2B6947
## canonical_key
platelet-origin-from-megakaryocyte
## definition
Platelets arise from megakaryocytes in the bone marrow. Invaginations of the megakaryocyte plasma membrane — the demarcation channels — extend through the cytoplasm and into its pseudopodia, dividing it into platelet ribbons that fragment into individual platelets.
## explicit_objective
Give the origin of the platelet and explain how demarcation channels produce it.
## pitfalls
Calling the demarcation channels cell junctions. They are membrane invaginations of one cell, not contacts between two.

## label
The normal platelet count is 150,000–400,000/mm³, and bleeding follows when it falls far below it
## id
CON-HEM-5E948BE9C94615
## canonical_key
platelet-count-and-thrombocytopenia
## definition
The normal platelet count is about 150,000–400,000 per mm³. Thrombocytopenia — a count below roughly 50,000 per mm³, from reduced marrow production or increased destruction — prolongs the bleeding time and causes purpura and excessive bleeding after trauma.
## explicit_objective
State the normal platelet count and the consequences of a low one.
## pitfalls
Confusing the platelet count with the red or white cell counts. Platelets are hundreds of thousands, red cells millions, white cells thousands.
