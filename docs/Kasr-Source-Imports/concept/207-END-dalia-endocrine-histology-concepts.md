# Item
## id
CON-END-6857E313E630C1
## label
Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones directly into the blood
## canonical_key
endocrine-gland.defining-feature.ductless
## aliases
Endocrine glands have no ducts
Endocrine glands are highly vascular ductless glands
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Endocrine glands are DUCTLESS glands: during development they lose their connection to a surface, so they release their hormones directly into the bloodstream rather than through a duct. They are richly vascular, which carries the hormone away. Being ductless is the defining structural feature that separates them from exocrine glands, whose secretion is delivered along a duct; features such as trabeculae or follicles are present in some endocrine glands but not others.
## explicit_objective
State that endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones directly into the blood.
## pitfalls
Calling endocrine glands avascular, or defining them by absence of follicles/trabeculae; the defining feature is that they are ductless (and they are highly vascular).

## label
The adenohypophysis develops from Rathke's pouch (oral ectoderm)
## canonical_key
adenohypophysis.development.rathkes-pouch
## aliases
The anterior pituitary arises from Rathke's pouch
Rathke's pouch is an ectodermal outgrowth of the stomodeum
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The adenohypophysis (anterior pituitary) develops from Rathke's pouch, an ectodermal diverticulum that grows upward from the roof of the primitive mouth (stomodeum). Its oral ectodermal origin explains why the anterior lobe is glandular epithelium arranged in cords. This contrasts with the neurohypophysis (posterior pituitary), which grows down from the diencephalon as a neural (infundibular) process, so the two lobes have quite different embryonic origins and histology.
## explicit_objective
State that the adenohypophysis develops from Rathke's pouch (oral ectoderm).
## pitfalls
Attributing the adenohypophysis to the diencephalon/hypothalamus; that neural origin belongs to the neurohypophysis, whereas the adenohypophysis comes from Rathke's pouch.

## id
CON-END-244BF1AE396342
## label
The hypophyseal portal system links two capillary plexuses by portal veins
## canonical_key
hypophyseal-portal-system.two-capillary-plexuses
## aliases
The hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal system has a primary and a secondary capillary plexus
Portal veins carry releasing hormones from the median eminence to the pars distalis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The hypothalamic-hypophyseal portal system is a vascular link that connects two capillary beds in series by portal veins. The primary (upper) plexus in the median eminence/infundibulum is supplied by the superior hypophyseal artery and picks up the hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones; hypophyseal portal veins then carry that blood to the secondary (lower) plexus in the pars distalis, which drains into veins. This arrangement delivers hypothalamic hormones directly to the anterior pituitary cells without first diluting them in the systemic circulation.
## explicit_objective
Describe the hypophyseal portal system as two capillary plexuses (median eminence and pars distalis) linked by portal veins.
## pitfalls
Treating the portal system as a single capillary bed, or as two arterial (or two venous) beds; it is one arterial-fed and one venous-fed plexus in series.

## label
Adrenal cortical cells are steroid-secreting cells with abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum
## canonical_key
adrenal-cortex-cell.ultrastructure.abundant-smooth-er
## aliases
Adrenal cortex cells have prominent smooth ER, tubular-cristae mitochondria and lipid droplets
Cortical spongiocytes are steroid-secreting cells
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The cells of the adrenal (suprarenal) cortex are steroid-secreting cells, and their conspicuous ultrastructural feature is abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum, together with mitochondria that have tubular cristae and numerous cytoplasmic lipid droplets. The smooth ER and these mitochondria carry the enzymes that convert cholesterol into corticosteroids. Their lipid content gives them a pale, foamy appearance (spongiocytes). Prominent rough ER, by contrast, characterises protein-secreting cells, not the cortical steroid cells.
## explicit_objective
Recognise the adrenal cortical cell as a steroid-secreting cell with abundant smooth ER, tubular-cristae mitochondria and lipid.
## pitfalls
Attributing well-developed rough ER (or absence of lipid) to adrenal cortical cells; as steroid cells they have prominent smooth ER and abundant lipid droplets.

The chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla are modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons that develop from the NEURAL CREST. Because of this origin they are innervated by preganglionic sympathetic fibres and secrete catecholamines (adrenaline and noradrenaline) directly into the blood. State that adrenal medullary chromaffin cells are of neural-crest origin (distinct from the mesodermal cortex).
## pitfalls
Assuming cortex and medulla share an origin; the cortex is mesodermal and the medulla (chromaffin cells) is neural-crest.

## label
Thyroid colloid is mainly the glycoprotein thyroglobulin
## canonical_key
thyroid-colloid.composition.thyroglobulin-glycoprotein
## aliases
The thyroid follicle stores thyroglobulin as colloid
Thyroglobulin is the iodinated glycoprotein precursor of thyroid hormone
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The colloid that fills the thyroid follicle is composed mainly of thyroglobulin, a large iodinated GLYCOPROTEIN synthesised by the follicular cells and stored extracellularly in the follicle lumen. It is the inactive precursor from which the active hormones T3 and T4 are cleaved after the follicular cell re-endocytoses colloid and its lysosomes digest the thyroglobulin. Thyroglobulin is thus a glycoprotein (not a lipoprotein), and it is distinct from thyroxin, the released active hormone.
## explicit_objective
State that thyroid colloid is mainly the glycoprotein thyroglobulin (the stored hormone precursor).
## pitfalls
Calling the colloid a lipoprotein or naming it thyroxin; the colloid is the glycoprotein thyroglobulin, the precursor of thyroxin.

Thyroid follicular cells form a simple epithelium whose height varies from squamous to columnar

thyroid-follicular-epithelium.height-varies-squamous-to-columnar
## aliases
Thyroid follicular epithelium is flat when inactive and columnar when active
The thyroid follicle is lined by a simple epithelium of variable height

The wall of a thyroid follicle is a SIMPLE (single-layered) epithelium of follicular cells whose height varies with functional state: it is flattened (simple squamous) when the gland is inactive and the follicle is distended with colloid, cuboidal in the normally active gland, and simple columnar when strongly stimulated (e.g. by TSH). State that thyroid follicular cells form a simple epithelium whose height varies from squamous to columnar with activity.
## pitfalls
Describing the follicular lining as stratified or pseudostratified; it is a simple epithelium of variable (squamous-to-columnar) height.

CON-END-37454B76FCB7D6
## label
Steroid-secreting adrenal cortical cells do not store their hormone
## canonical_key
adrenal-cortex.steroid-cells.do-not-store-secretion
## aliases
The adrenal cortex synthesises steroids on demand and does not store them
Unlike the thyroid or pituitary, the adrenal cortex has no stored secretion
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Steroid-secreting cells synthesise their hormone on demand from cholesterol and release it immediately by simple diffusion, so they do NOT store a reserve of secretion. The adrenal (suprarenal) cortex is such a tissue and therefore holds no stored hormone. This contrasts with tissues that do store secretion: the pars nervosa stores oxytocin and ADH in axon terminals (Herring bodies), the pars distalis stores peptide hormones in secretory granules, and the thyroid stores a large extracellular reserve as colloid.
## explicit_objective
State that steroid-secreting adrenal cortical cells do not store their hormone (unlike granule- or colloid-storing endocrine tissue).
## pitfalls
Assuming every endocrine tissue stores its hormone; steroid-secreting cells such as the adrenal cortex release on demand and store none.
