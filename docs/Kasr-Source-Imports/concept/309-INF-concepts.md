## label
Facultative parasite can live independently of a host
## canonical_key
parasitology.general.facultative-parasite-lives-independently
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A facultative parasite is normally free-living and is able to exist independently of a host, but it can also live as a parasite when the opportunity arises; this capacity for independent existence is what distinguishes it from an obligate parasite, which cannot survive outside a host.
## explicit_objective
Define a facultative parasite by its ability to exist independently of a host.
## pitfalls
Confusing facultative with obligate: the defining trait is the ability to live WITHOUT a host, not merely the ability to live within one.

## label
Fasciola infective stage: encysted metacercaria on vegetation
## canonical_key
parasitology.fasciola.infective-stage-metacercaria-on-plants
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The infective stage of Fasciola is the encysted metacercaria on aquatic vegetation such as watercress; humans and sheep are infected by ingesting this plant-borne metacercaria, which excysts in the duodenum and the juvenile fluke migrates through the liver to the bile ducts.
## explicit_objective
Identify the encysted metacercaria on vegetation as both the infective stage and the mode of Fasciola infection.
## pitfalls
Fasciola eggs and cercariae are NOT infective to man; only the metacercaria encysted on plants is. Skin penetration by cercariae is the schistosome route, not Fasciola.
