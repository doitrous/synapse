# Item
## id
CON-GYN-2486E175D5F205
## label
The mammary gland secretes milk by a combined merocrine and apocrine mode
## canonical_key
mammary-gland.secretion-mode.merocrine-and-apocrine
## aliases
Milk protein is secreted by merocrine exocytosis and milk fat by the apocrine mode
The lactating mammary gland is not a holocrine gland
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The lactating mammary gland releases milk by two modes at once. The protein fraction (casein, lactalbumin) is secreted by the merocrine mode (exocytosis of secretory vesicles), whereas the fat (lipid droplets) is released by the apocrine mode, budding off with a thin envelope of apical cytoplasm and membrane. It is therefore a combined merocrine-and-apocrine secretion, not a holocrine one (holocrine secretion, as in sebaceous glands, destroys the whole cell).
## explicit_objective
State that the mammary gland secretes milk by a combined merocrine (protein) and apocrine (fat) mode.
## pitfalls
Calling mammary secretion holocrine; the whole cell is not shed — protein leaves by merocrine and fat by apocrine.

## label
The immunoglobulin of the mammary secretion is secretory IgA
## canonical_key
mammary-secretion.immunoglobulin.iga
## aliases
Colostrum and milk are rich in secretory IgA
IgA gives the newborn passive gut immunity
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The immunoglobulin characteristic of colostrum and milk is secretory IgA. Dimeric IgA made by plasma cells in the connective tissue between the alveoli is taken up by the alveolar cells, coupled to a secretory component and released into the milk. Identify secretory IgA as the immunoglobulin of the mammary secretion.

Naming IgG or IgM as the milk immunoglobulin; the dominant milk antibody is secretory IgA.

The Langhans cytotrophoblast layer disappears as term approaches
The late placental barrier is thinned to syncytiotrophoblast, basal lamina and fetal endothelium
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Early in pregnancy the placental barrier includes a complete cytotrophoblast (Langhans layer) beneath the syncytiotrophoblast. As pregnancy advances the cytotrophoblast becomes discontinuous and largely disappears, so in the late months it is no longer a component of the barrier. The remaining layers — syncytiotrophoblast, its basal lamina, thin villous connective tissue and the fetal capillary endothelium — form a thinner barrier that shortens the diffusion distance and favours exchange near term.
## explicit_objective
State that the cytotrophoblast is lost from the placental barrier in late pregnancy.
## pitfalls
Listing the cytotrophoblast as a late-pregnancy barrier layer; it thins out and disappears as term approaches.

The syncytiotrophoblast has abundant lipid droplets for steroid synthesis
The syncytiotrophoblast secretes hCG and grows only by cytotrophoblast fusion
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The syncytiotrophoblast is the outer, multinucleated layer of the chorionic villus. It is a non-mitotic syncytium that grows only by fusion of underlying cytotrophoblast cells, bears abundant apical microvilli facing maternal blood, and is an active endocrine layer secreting hCG, human placental lactogen, progesterone and oestrogens. Because it synthesises steroids, it is RICH in lipid droplets (cholesterol substrate), so it is not devoid of them.
## explicit_objective
State that the syncytiotrophoblast is a non-mitotic, microvillous, hormone-secreting layer rich in lipid droplets.
## pitfalls
Saying the syncytiotrophoblast is devoid of lipid; it is a steroid-secreting layer full of lipid droplets.

As a growing follicle matures, the theca-interna cells differentiate into steroid-secreting cells with the classic ultrastructure — abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum, mitochondria with tubular cristae and lipid droplets.
