# Item
## id
CON-GIT-1AB47117E5DE74
## label
Antral acidification (low pH) inhibits gastrin secretion through somatostatin
## canonical_key
gastrin.regulation.inhibited-by-antral-acidification
## aliases
gastrin negative feedback by acid
low antral pH inhibits gastrin
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A high concentration of hydrochloric acid in the antrum (a fall in antral pH below about 3) is the main negative-feedback signal that switches off gastrin secretion. The low pH stimulates antral D cells to release somatostatin, which acts in a paracrine manner on adjacent G cells to inhibit gastrin release. This loop prevents further acid production once the meal has been buffered and the antrum has become acidic, whereas vagal activity, amino acids/peptides and gastric distension all stimulate gastrin.
## explicit_objective
Explain the negative feedback by which antral acidification inhibits gastrin secretion via somatostatin.
## pitfalls
Thinking the vagus or amino acids reduce gastrin (they stimulate it), or forgetting that the acid effect is mediated by somatostatin from D cells.

## label
Cholecystokinin stimulates, rather than inhibits, small-intestinal and colonic motility
## canonical_key
cholecystokinin.actions.stimulates-gut-motility
## aliases
CCK increases gut motility
cholecystokinin actions
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Cholecystokinin (CCK), released from duodenal I cells in response to fatty acids and the products of protein digestion, contracts the gall bladder, relaxes the sphincter of Oddi and stimulates enzyme-rich pancreatic secretion. It also stimulates the motility of the small intestine and colon, augmenting peristalsis and contributing to the gastrocolic response, so it does not inhibit gut motility. Its release is under positive feedback from the digestion products that it helps to process.
## explicit_objective
State that CCK stimulates, rather than inhibits, small-intestinal and colonic motility.
## pitfalls
Believing CCK inhibits gut motility, or that its secretion is unaffected by fat and protein digestion products.

## label
Salivary secretion is controlled by the autonomic nervous system, not by GI hormones
## canonical_key
githormones.actions.do-not-stimulate-salivary-secretion
## aliases
saliva under autonomic control
GI hormones do not increase salivation
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Gastrointestinal hormones such as gastrin and cholecystokinin drive pancreatic enzyme secretion, gastric acid secretion and gall-bladder emptying, but none of them has increased salivary secretion as a recognised action. Salivary flow is instead governed almost entirely by autonomic reflexes, chiefly the parasympathetic (facial and glossopharyngeal) supply to the salivary glands, with a lesser sympathetic contribution. So among effects attributed to GI hormones, an increase in salivary secretion is the odd one out.
## explicit_objective
Recognise that salivary secretion is under autonomic, not gastrointestinal-hormonal, control.
## pitfalls
Assuming a GI hormone stimulates salivation, or confusing autonomic reflex control of saliva with hormonal control of gastric and pancreatic secretion.

CON-GIT-B033AB9CDFA806
## label
Parietal cells secrete acid via an apical H+/K+ ATPase and a basolateral alkaline tide, not a basolateral H+/Na+ pump
## canonical_key
gastricacidsecretion.mechanism.apical-hk-atpase-not-basolateral-hna
## aliases
parietal cell acid secretion mechanism
alkaline tide proton pump
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The parietal cell generates H+ and HCO3- from carbonic acid; the H+ is pumped into the lumen by the apical H+/K+ ATPase (the proton pump, target of proton-pump inhibitors), while Cl- follows to form HCl and water follows osmotically. The HCO3- leaves across the basolateral membrane in exchange for Cl- (an HCO3-/Cl- exchanger), producing the 'alkaline tide' in gastric venous blood. There is no basolateral hydrogen/sodium pump supplying the secreted H+, so that mechanism does not contribute to acid secretion.
## explicit_objective
Describe parietal-cell acid secretion by the apical H+/K+ ATPase and the basolateral alkaline tide, and reject a basolateral H+/Na+ pump.
## pitfalls
Attributing secreted H+ to a basolateral H+/Na+ pump, or misplacing the H+/K+ ATPase away from the apical (luminal) membrane.

Identify acetylcholine as a stimulant of parietal-cell acid secretion among the gastric secretagogues.

## arabic_label

## definition
The exocrine pancreas secretes roughly 1500 ml (about 1-1.5 litres) of alkaline, enzyme-rich juice per day, far more than the 150-250 ml sometimes offered as a distractor. This juice contains proteolytic enzymes (trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidases, elastase) sufficient for protein digestion, along with amylase and lipase, and a bicarbonate-rich fluid from the duct cells. ## explicit_objective
Recall that daily pancreatic juice output is about 1500 ml, not 150-250 ml.
## pitfalls
Quoting the daily pancreatic volume as 150-250 ml, or forgetting the enteropeptidase-triggered autocatalytic activation of trypsinogen.

## label
Duodenal acid, fat, hypertonicity and distension inhibit gastric emptying, but alkalinity does not
## canonical_key
gastricemptying.inhibitors.duodenal-acid-fat-hypertonicity-not-alkalinity
## aliases

## arabic_label

## definition
Gastric emptying is slowed by duodenal signals through the enterogastric reflex and hormones such as secretin, cholecystokinin and GIP: acid chyme, fat, hypertonic (or markedly hypotonic) contents and duodenal distension all inhibit emptying until the chyme is neutralised and processed. Distinguish duodenal acid, fat, hypertonicity and distension (which inhibit gastric emptying) from alkalinity (which does not).
## pitfalls
Listing alkalinity as an inhibitor of gastric emptying, or overlooking distension and osmolarity as inhibitory signals.

State that the pyloric sphincter contracts (does not relax) during the act of vomiting.
