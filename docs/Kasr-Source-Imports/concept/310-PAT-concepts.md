## label
Causes of secondary hypertension
## canonical_key
hypertension.secondary.causes
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Secondary hypertension is raised blood pressure with an identifiable underlying cause. Its causes are renal (polycystic kidney, chronic glomerulonephritis, renal artery stenosis, diabetic nephropathy), endocrine (Cushing syndrome, phaeochromocytoma, hyperaldosteronism, hyperthyroidism), cardiovascular (coarctation of the aorta) and toxaemia of pregnancy. Hypothyroidism is not a cause of secondary hypertension.
## explicit_objective
List the renal, endocrine, cardiovascular and pregnancy causes of secondary hypertension and exclude hypothyroidism.
## pitfalls
Assuming any thyroid disorder raises pressure: hyperthyroidism can, but hypothyroidism does not cause secondary hypertension.

hypertension.benign.hyaline-arteriolosclerosis

## definition
Benign (long-standing, moderate) hypertension causes hyaline arteriolosclerosis: homogeneous, glassy pink (hyaline) thickening of arteriolar walls from plasma-protein leakage across injured endothelium and increased smooth-muscle matrix, narrowing the lumen. It is the hallmark small-vessel lesion of benign hypertension and of diabetes, and contrasts with the fibrinoid necrosis and hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis of malignant hypertension.

Attribute hyaline arteriolosclerosis (glassy pink arteriolar thickening) to benign hypertension.
