## label
Myoid (peritubular) cells are contractile cells around the seminiferous tubule of the testis
## canonical_key
seminiferous-tubule.myoid-cell.peritubular-contractile-in-testis
## aliases
Peritubular myoid cells surround the seminiferous tubule
Myoid cells help move sperm along the seminiferous tubule
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Myoid (peritubular) cells are flattened, smooth-muscle-like contractile cells that form the peritubular tissue around each seminiferous tubule of the testis. Their rhythmic contraction propels non-motile spermatozoa and testicular fluid along the tubule toward the rete testis, and they form part of the wall that supports the Sertoli-cell blood-testis barrier. They are a testicular feature and are not found in the prostate, corpora cavernosa or epididymis.
## explicit_objective
Identify peritubular myoid cells as contractile cells around the seminiferous tubule of the testis.
## pitfalls
Placing myoid cells in the epididymis or prostate; they belong to the peritubular tissue of the seminiferous tubule.

## definition
During the Golgi phase of spermiogenesis, pro-acrosomal granules produced by the Golgi complex of the spermatid coalesce into a single membrane-bound acrosomal vesicle that caps the anterior pole of the condensing nucleus. State that the acrosome is formed from the Golgi complex during spermiogenesis.

Spermiogenesis is the metamorphosis of spermatids into spermatozoa

Spermiogenesis transforms round spermatids into mature sperm

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Spermiogenesis is the final, non-dividing phase of sperm formation in which the round haploid spermatid undergoes metamorphosis into a mature spermatozoon. It comprises acrosome formation, nuclear condensation and elongation, development of the flagellum, assembly of the mitochondrial sheath around the middle piece and shedding of excess (residual) cytoplasm. It is distinct from spermatogenesis (spermatogonia -> spermatocytes -> spermatids) and from the later epididymal maturation that confers motility.
## explicit_objective
Define spermiogenesis as the metamorphosis of spermatids into spermatozoa.
## pitfalls
Confusing spermiogenesis (spermatid -> spermatozoon) with spermatogenesis (spermatogonium -> spermatid); no cell division occurs in spermiogenesis.

The epididymis is lined by a tall pseudostratified columnar epithelium whose principal cells bear long, branching, non-motile microvilli called stereocilia.
