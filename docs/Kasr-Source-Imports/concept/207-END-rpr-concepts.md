The inferior gluteal nerve is a branch of the sacral plexus
## canonical_key
sacral-plexus.inferior-gluteal-nerve.branch
## aliases
Inferior gluteal nerve origin
Sacral plexus motor branches
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The inferior gluteal nerve arises from the sacral plexus (L5, S1 and S2). It leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis and supplies the gluteus maximus. The femoral, obturator and lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh, by contrast, all come from the lumbar plexus.
## explicit_objective
State that the inferior gluteal nerve is a branch of the sacral plexus.
## pitfalls
Confusing lumbar-plexus nerves (femoral, obturator, lateral cutaneous of thigh) with sacral-plexus nerves (gluteal nerves, sciatic, pudendal).

## definition
Vasectomy divides the ductus deferens, so spermatozoa can no longer pass from the epididymis into the ejaculate. The secretions added downstream of the cut still reach the urethra: the seminal vesicles contribute the largest volume fraction and the prostate its thin milky secretion. The ejaculate therefore continues to contain seminal and prostatic fluids but is free of sperm, which is the basis of the procedure as a method of sterilisation.
## explicit_objective
Predict that the post-vasectomy ejaculate contains seminal and prostatic fluid without sperm.
## pitfalls
Assuming ejaculate volume falls to zero; most of the volume (seminal + prostatic secretion) is unchanged, only the sperm are absent.

## definition
The pectinate (dentate) line divides the anal canal into an upper endodermal part and a lower ectodermal part. Below the line the lining is somatic skin, and its lymph drains to the superficial inguinal lymph nodes. Above the line the visceral part drains upward to the internal iliac and inferior mesenteric nodes, so the pectinate line is a lymphatic watershed.
## explicit_objective
State that the anal canal below the pectinate line drains to superficial inguinal nodes.
## pitfalls
Draining the whole anal canal to one node group; the pectinate line separates inguinal (below) from internal iliac/inferior mesenteric (above) drainage.

Rectal lymph follows the arteries that supply the rectum. State the lymphatic drainage of the rectum to inferior mesenteric and internal iliac nodes.

Sending rectal lymph to inguinal nodes; those drain the lower anal canal, not the rectum.

## definition
External haemorrhoids arise below the pectinate line, from dilated tributaries of the inferior rectal veins. Because this part of the anal canal is lined by skin, they are covered by skin and are painful, being supplied by the somatic inferior rectal nerve. Internal haemorrhoids, by contrast, are dilatations of the superior rectal (portal) tributaries above the line, covered by mucous membrane and painless.
## explicit_objective
Define external haemorrhoids and distinguish them from internal haemorrhoids.
## pitfalls
Swapping the two: mucosa-covered superior-rectal varices are internal, skin-covered inferior-rectal varices are external.
