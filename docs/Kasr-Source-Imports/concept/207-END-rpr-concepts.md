## label
The inferior gluteal nerve is a branch of the sacral plexus
## canonical_key
sacral-plexus.inferior-gluteal-nerve.branch
## aliases
Inferior gluteal nerve origin
Sacral plexus motor branches
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The inferior gluteal nerve arises from the sacral plexus (L5, S1 and S2). It leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis and supplies the gluteus maximus. The femoral, obturator and lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh, by contrast, all come from the lumbar plexus.
## explicit_objective
State that the inferior gluteal nerve is a branch of the sacral plexus.
## pitfalls
Confusing lumbar-plexus nerves (femoral, obturator, lateral cutaneous of thigh) with sacral-plexus nerves (gluteal nerves, sciatic, pudendal).

## label
The deep artery of the penis is a branch of the internal pudendal artery
## canonical_key
internal-pudendal-artery.branches.deep-artery-of-penis
## aliases
Deep artery of the penis origin
Terminal branches of the internal pudendal artery
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The internal pudendal artery is the main artery of the perineum. After giving the inferior rectal and perineal branches it ends by supplying the erectile tissue: the artery of the bulb, the dorsal artery of the penis and the deep artery of the penis (which runs in the corpus cavernosum). The median sacral and superior rectal arteries are not its branches.
## explicit_objective
State that the deep artery of the penis is a terminal branch of the internal pudendal artery.
## pitfalls
Assuming the erectile arteries come from the external iliac; they are terminal branches of the internal pudendal artery.

## label
The ureter is supplied in the abdomen by branches of the gonadal (testicular or ovarian) artery
## canonical_key
ureter.arterial-supply.testicular-or-ovarian-artery
## aliases
Ureteric blood supply
Gonadal artery branch to the ureter
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The ureter has a segmental blood supply along its length. In the abdomen it receives constant branches from the renal and the gonadal (testicular or ovarian) arteries; in the pelvis it is supplied by the vesical and (in the female) uterine arteries. The gonadal artery is therefore a recognised ureteric supplier, whereas the superior mesenteric and external iliac arteries give it no branches.
## explicit_objective
State that the gonadal (testicular/ovarian) artery contributes to the arterial supply of the ureter.
## pitfalls
Forgetting the ureter's supply is segmental and multi-source; the external iliac artery supplies the lower limb, not the ureter.

## label
The deep transverse perineal muscle is a content of the deep perineal pouch
## canonical_key
deep-perineal-pouch.contents.deep-transverse-perinei
## aliases
Contents of the deep perineal pouch
Deep transverse perineal muscle location
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The deep perineal pouch lies between the perineal membrane below and the pelvic diaphragm above. Its contents include the deep transverse perineal muscle and the sphincter urethrae, together with the internal pudendal vessels and the dorsal nerve of the penis/clitoris. The ischiocavernosus, bulb of the vestibule and greater vestibular glands, by contrast, lie in the superficial perineal pouch.
## explicit_objective
Identify the deep transverse perineal muscle as a content of the deep perineal pouch.
## pitfalls
Placing the ischiocavernosus or the greater vestibular glands (superficial pouch) in the deep pouch.

## label
The ischiocavernosus muscle lies in the superficial perineal pouch
## canonical_key
ischiocavernosus.location.superficial-perineal-pouch
## aliases
Ischiocavernosus location
Superficial perineal pouch muscles
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The ischiocavernosus, the bulbospongiosus and the superficial transverse perineal muscle are the three paired muscles of the superficial perineal pouch, the space between the perineal membrane and the deep perineal fascia. The ischiocavernosus covers the crus of the penis or clitoris and helps maintain erection. The deep transverse perineal muscle and sphincter urethrae lie in the deep, not the superficial, pouch.
## explicit_objective
Locate the ischiocavernosus in the superficial perineal pouch.
## pitfalls
Confusing the superficial-pouch muscles (ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus, superficial transverse perinei) with the deep-pouch muscles.

The greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands lie in the superficial perineal pouch in the female
## canonical_key
superficial-perineal-pouch.female-contents.greater-vestibular-glands
## aliases
Bartholin gland location
Greater vestibular glands superficial pouch
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
In the female the superficial perineal pouch contains the crura of the clitoris with the ischiocavernosus, the bulbs of the vestibule with the bulbospongiosus, and the greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands lying behind the bulbs. The sphincter urethrae and deep transverse perineal muscle lie in the deep pouch; the bulbourethral glands are the male homologue and are not present in the female.
## explicit_objective
Locate the greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands in the female superficial perineal pouch.
## pitfalls
Equating the female Bartholin glands with the male bulbourethral glands, which lie in the deep pouch.
