## label
The obturator internus tendon leaves the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen
## canonical_key
pelvic-wall.obturator-internus-tendon.exits-lesser-sciatic-foramen
## aliases
Obturator internus exit from the pelvis
Contents of the lesser sciatic foramen
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The obturator internus arises inside the pelvis from the obturator membrane and surrounding bone. Its tendon turns almost a right angle around the lesser sciatic notch and leaves the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen, then crosses the lateral wall of the ischiorectal fossa to insert into the greater trochanter. The piriformis and the gluteal nerves and vessels, by contrast, leave through the greater sciatic foramen.
## explicit_objective
State that the obturator internus tendon exits the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen.
## pitfalls
Confusing the greater sciatic foramen (piriformis, gluteal vessels/nerves) with the lesser (obturator internus tendon, pudendal nerve, internal pudendal vessels).

The lymphatics of the fundus and upper body of the uterus ascend with the ovarian vessels in the suspensory ligament of the ovary along the posterior abdominal wall to the para-aortic (lumbar) nodes. The fallopian tube is invested by peritoneum (intraperitoneal)
## canonical_key

Mesosalpinx invests the uterine tube

## arabic_label

## definition
The fallopian (uterine) tube lies in the free upper margin of the broad ligament and is almost completely invested by peritoneum, its mesentery being the mesosalpinx. It is therefore an intraperitoneal structure, which is why tubal disease can seed the peritoneal cavity. This contrasts with the extraperitoneal bladder base and lower rectum and the vesicovaginal-septum-covered anterior fornix, which are not peritoneum-covered.
## explicit_objective
State that the fallopian tube is invested by peritoneum and is intraperitoneal.
## pitfalls
Calling the bladder base or lower rectum peritoneum-covered; only the tube (and superior bladder/upper rectum) are.
