## label
Paediatric body water and drug distribution
## canonical_key
pharmacokinetics.pediatric.body-water
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Neonates and infants have a higher proportion of total body water (about 70-80% of body weight versus roughly 60% in adults) and a larger extracellular fluid compartment. This raises the apparent volume of distribution of water-soluble drugs, so weight-based doses of hydrophilic drugs are relatively higher in children. Neonates also have lower plasma albumin and a more permeable blood-brain barrier, while body fat is proportionally low at birth.
## explicit_objective
State that paediatric patients have more total body water than adults, which raises the volume of distribution of water-soluble drugs.
## pitfalls
Confusing more body water with more fat; neonates have LESS fat and a MORE permeable blood-brain barrier, not the reverse.

Patient factors affecting drug dosage
## canonical_key
drug-dosage.factors.age-sex-food
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Drug dose requirements vary with body size, age, sex, renal function and food. Females often need somewhat smaller doses than males because of smaller lean mass; gastric-irritant drugs are given after meals to reduce mucosal irritation; and glomerular filtration falls in the elderly, so renally cleared drugs accumulate and their doses are reduced. Identify correct dosage-modifying factors and recognise the false claim that children have less body water and a less permeable blood-brain barrier.
## pitfalls
The paediatric water/BBB statement is reversed; children have more body water and a more permeable BBB than adults.

## aliases

Vasodilation from circulating acetylcholine versus parasympathetic stimulation
## canonical_key
acetylcholine.vasodilation.uninnervated-muscarinic
## aliases

Circulating acetylcholine raised by an anticholinesterase can act on these uninnervated endothelial receptors to cause vasodilation and hypotension, an effect that stimulating parasympathetic nerves cannot reproduce. Explain why hypotension appears with anticholinesterases but not with parasympathetic nerve stimulation.
## pitfalls
Assuming blood vessels are parasympathetically innervated; the vasodilator response depends on uninnervated endothelial muscarinic receptors.

## canonical_key
pilocarpine.lipid-solubility.miotic
## aliases

## definition
Pilocarpine is a naturally occurring cholinomimetic alkaloid and a tertiary amine, so it is lipid-soluble, crosses the blood-brain barrier and penetrates the cornea. Choosing a quaternary agent (neostigmine) or a rapidly hydrolysed ester (acetylcholine) as a corneal-penetrating miotic.

M2 muscarinic receptors are Gi-coupled and lower cAMP
## canonical_key
muscarinic-m2.gi.decreases-camp
## aliases

Autonomic receptors differ in their signalling. M2 muscarinic receptors couple to the inhibitory Gi protein, which inhibits adenylyl cyclase and so DECREASES intracellular cAMP (and opens potassium channels), underlying vagal slowing of the heart. Identify M2 muscarinic receptors as Gi-coupled receptors that decrease intracellular cAMP.

Confusing the Gs (beta2, raises cAMP) and Gq (alpha1) pathways with the Gi-coupled M2 receptor that lowers cAMP.

Pralidoxime is an oxime that binds the phosphorylated enzyme and removes the phosphate group, REACTIVATING cholinesterase provided it is given before irreversible 'ageing' of the enzyme. This restores acetylcholine breakdown and relieves particularly the nicotinic (skeletal-muscle) features that atropine cannot reverse, which is why it must be given early.
## explicit_objective
State that pralidoxime treats organophosphate poisoning by reactivating cholinesterase before ageing.
## pitfalls
Assigning pralidoxime a sedative, antimuscarinic or anticonvulsant role, or giving it late after enzyme ageing has occurred.

## definition
Atropine is contraindicated where its antimuscarinic effects are hazardous. In (angle-closure) glaucoma, ciliary relaxation and pupillary dilatation crowd the drainage angle and raise intraocular pressure dangerously. In benign prostatic hypertrophy, relaxation of the detrusor and reduced voiding can precipitate acute urinary retention. By contrast, symptomatic bradycardia/heart block and organophosphate poisoning are indications, not contraindications.
## explicit_objective
Identify glaucoma and prostatic outflow obstruction as contraindications to atropine.
## pitfalls
Mistaking atropine's indications (bradycardia, organophosphate poisoning) for contraindications, or forgetting the retention risk in prostatic hypertrophy.

## definition
Antimuscarinic (parasympathetic-depressant) eye drops block the sphincter pupillae and the ciliary muscle, producing mydriasis (pupil dilatation) and cycloplegia (paralysis of accommodation, so the lens cannot focus for near vision). Expecting miosis or a fall in intraocular pressure; antimuscarinics dilate the pupil and can raise pressure.

Choosing a peripheral anticholinesterase (pyridostigmine, echothiophate) or an antimuscarinic (tropicamide) for dementia.
