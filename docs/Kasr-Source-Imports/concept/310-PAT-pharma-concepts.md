# Item

CON-PHA-30D71EED124EB1
## label
Paediatric body water and drug distribution
## canonical_key
pharmacokinetics.pediatric.body-water
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Neonates and infants have a higher proportion of total body water (about 70-80% of body weight versus roughly 60% in adults) and a larger extracellular fluid compartment. This raises the apparent volume of distribution of water-soluble drugs, so weight-based doses of hydrophilic drugs are relatively higher in children. Neonates also have lower plasma albumin and a more permeable blood-brain barrier, while body fat is proportionally low at birth.
## explicit_objective
State that paediatric patients have more total body water than adults, which raises the volume of distribution of water-soluble drugs.
## pitfalls
Confusing more body water with more fat; neonates have LESS fat and a MORE permeable blood-brain barrier, not the reverse.

## label
Patient factors affecting drug dosage
## canonical_key
drug-dosage.factors.age-sex-food
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Drug dose requirements vary with body size, age, sex, renal function and food. Females often need somewhat smaller doses than males because of smaller lean mass; gastric-irritant drugs are given after meals to reduce mucosal irritation; and glomerular filtration falls in the elderly, so renally cleared drugs accumulate and their doses are reduced. Paediatric patients have MORE total body water and a MORE permeable blood-brain barrier than adults, so the claim that they have less of both is false.
## explicit_objective
Identify correct dosage-modifying factors and recognise the false claim that children have less body water and a less permeable blood-brain barrier.
## pitfalls
The paediatric water/BBB statement is reversed; children have more body water and a more permeable BBB than adults.

## label
Therapeutic uses of cholinergic agonists
## canonical_key
cholinergic-agonists.therapeutic-uses
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Cholinergic (parasympathomimetic) agonists stimulate gastrointestinal and bladder smooth muscle and exocrine glands. Their therapeutic uses include postoperative paralytic ileus and non-obstructive urinary retention (bethanechol), glaucoma and dry mouth (pilocarpine), and myasthenia gravis (neostigmine). They are contraindicated in bronchial asthma (bronchoconstriction), peptic ulcer (increased acid secretion) and after acute myocardial infarction.
## explicit_objective
List paralytic ileus and urinary retention as therapeutic uses of cholinergic agonists and recognise asthma and peptic ulcer as contraindications.
## pitfalls
Choosing asthma or peptic ulcer as a use; both are contraindications to cholinergic agonists.

## label
Vasodilation from circulating acetylcholine versus parasympathetic stimulation
## canonical_key
acetylcholine.vasodilation.uninnervated-muscarinic
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Most resistance blood vessels receive no parasympathetic innervation, yet their endothelium bears M3 muscarinic receptors that release nitric oxide. Circulating acetylcholine raised by an anticholinesterase can act on these uninnervated endothelial receptors to cause vasodilation and hypotension, an effect that stimulating parasympathetic nerves cannot reproduce. Bradycardia, miosis and increased gastric acid secretion, by contrast, occur with both parasympathetic nerve stimulation and anticholinesterases.
## explicit_objective
Explain why hypotension appears with anticholinesterases but not with parasympathetic nerve stimulation.
## pitfalls
Assuming blood vessels are parasympathetically innervated; the vasodilator response depends on uninnervated endothelial muscarinic receptors.

## label
Pilocarpine as a lipid-soluble miotic
## canonical_key
pilocarpine.lipid-solubility.miotic
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Pilocarpine is a naturally occurring cholinomimetic alkaloid and a tertiary amine, so it is lipid-soluble, crosses the blood-brain barrier and penetrates the cornea. This allows it to be applied topically as a miotic that contracts the ciliary muscle and lowers intraocular pressure in glaucoma. Acetylcholine and methacholine are rapidly hydrolysed charged esters, and neostigmine is a lipid-insoluble quaternary compound, so none of them is suitable for corneal penetration.
## explicit_objective
Identify pilocarpine as the lipid-soluble parasympathomimetic suitable for use as a miotic.
## pitfalls
Choosing a quaternary agent (neostigmine) or a rapidly hydrolysed ester (acetylcholine) as a corneal-penetrating miotic.

## canonical_key
cholinergic.urinary-retention.exclude-obstruction
## aliases

## arabic_label

## definition
Postoperative non-obstructive urinary retention can be treated with the cholinomimetic bethanechol, but mechanical bladder-outlet obstruction must be excluded first. State that mechanical obstruction must be excluded before treating urinary retention with a cholinergic agonist.

## label
Atropine as drug of choice in organophosphate poisoning
## canonical_key
atropine.organophosphate-poisoning.drug-of-choice
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Atropine is the muscarinic antagonist of choice in organophosphate (cholinesterase-inhibitor) poisoning because it reverses the life-threatening muscarinic effects: bronchorrhoea, bronchospasm, bradycardia and glandular secretions. It is titrated to drying of secretions. Its more selective substitutes are designed for organ-selective use (eye, gut, bladder, airway) and so are not chosen as the systemic antidote.
## explicit_objective
Identify organophosphate poisoning as the setting where atropine is preferred over its more selective substitutes.
## pitfalls
Selecting organ-selective substitutes (tropicamide, tiotropium) that are chosen to limit systemic effect, the opposite of an antidote's requirement.

Hyoscine (scopolamine) for motion sickness
## canonical_key
hyoscine.motion-sickness
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Hyoscine (scopolamine) is a tertiary-amine antimuscarinic with strong central penetration and is the most effective drug for preventing motion sickness, classically delivered as a transdermal patch behind the ear before travel. Its action on central vestibular muscarinic pathways underlies this benefit and its sedative effect, distinguishing it from the more peripherally acting atropine.

Identify hyoscine as the antimuscarinic of choice for motion sickness.

Choosing atropine, which is less effective for motion sickness and less centrally sedative than hyoscine.

CON-PHA-026FE3FD6BFBCD
## label
Nicotinic effects of organophosphates are not blocked by atropine
## canonical_key
organophosphate.nicotinic-effects.atropine-resistant
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Atropine blocks only muscarinic receptors, so in organophosphate poisoning it relieves secretions, bronchospasm, bradycardia and gut hypermotility but does not reverse the effects at the skeletal-muscle neuromuscular junction, which are mediated by NICOTINIC receptors. Accumulated acetylcholine there causes fasciculations, weakness and eventually depolarising paralysis, which persist despite atropine and require a cholinesterase reactivator (pralidoxime) and respiratory support.
## explicit_objective
State that the skeletal-muscle (nicotinic) effects of organophosphates are not blocked by atropine.
## pitfalls
Expecting atropine to relieve muscle fasciculations or paralysis; only the muscarinic features respond to it.

## label
Atropine to limit muscarinic effects of neostigmine in myasthenia
## canonical_key
neostigmine.myasthenia.atropine-cover
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Neostigmine treats myasthenia gravis by raising acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction to improve muscle strength, but the same rise stimulates muscarinic receptors and causes salivation, colic, sweating and bradycardia. Atropine, a muscarinic antagonist, is co-administered to block these unwanted muscarinic effects while leaving the beneficial nicotinic action at the muscle end-plate intact.

Identify atropine as the agent used to counter the muscarinic side effects of neostigmine in myasthenia gravis.
## pitfalls
Choosing an immunosuppressant (azathioprine) or a stimulant when the aim is to block neostigmine's muscarinic side effects.
