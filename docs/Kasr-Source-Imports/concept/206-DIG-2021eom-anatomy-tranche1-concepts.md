# Item

CON-GIT-2F7221C01F32AF
## label
Early appendicitis pain is visceral and referred to the periumbilical (T10) region
## canonical_key
appendicitis.referredpain.periumbilical-visceral
## aliases
periumbilical referral of appendicitis
midgut visceral pain referral to umbilicus
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The appendix is a midgut derivative, so its visceral afferent pain fibres travel with sympathetic nerves to the T10 spinal segment. Early appendicitis therefore produces vague, poorly localised pain referred to the T10 dermatome around the umbilicus. Only when inflammation later involves the parietal peritoneum does somatic pain localise to McBurney's point in the right iliac fossa.
## explicit_objective
Explain why early appendicitis pain is felt at the umbilicus and later shifts to the right iliac fossa.
## pitfalls
Assuming pain is localised to the right iliac fossa from the outset, or attributing the periumbilical pain to a T10 somatic rather than visceral origin.

## label
The ureter enters the pelvis by crossing the bifurcation of the common iliac artery
## canonical_key
ureter.relations.crosses-common-iliac-bifurcation
## aliases
ureter crosses common iliac bifurcation
ureter pelvic brim landmark
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The abdominal ureter descends retroperitoneally on the medial part of the psoas major and enters the pelvis by crossing in front of the bifurcation of the common iliac artery at the pelvic brim, over the sacroiliac joint. This is a reliable surgical landmark for finding the ureter. It is also one of the three sites of normal ureteric narrowing where stones tend to impact.
## explicit_objective
Locate the ureter as it crosses the common iliac bifurcation at the pelvic brim.
## pitfalls
Confusing the crossing of the common iliac artery with the point where the ureter is crossed by the gonadal vessels higher up, or thinking the IVC lies lateral to the right ureter.

The inferior mesenteric artery supplies the hindgut: the distal (left) third of the transverse colon, the descending and sigmoid colon and the upper rectum, via its left colic, sigmoid and superior rectal branches. Define the colonic territory of the inferior mesenteric artery and the splenic-flexure watershed.

The uncinate process is the hook-like inferior extension of the head of the pancreas that projects behind the superior mesenteric vessels, so the superior mesenteric artery and vein lie anterior to it. Describe the relation of the pancreatic uncinate process to the superior mesenteric vessels.
