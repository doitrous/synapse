# Item
## id
CON-GIT-E77F76B7DAC349
## label
Daily faecal loss of bile acids is the only route of cholesterol elimination; conjugated bile salts are water-soluble
## canonical_key
bile-acids.excretion.faecal-loss-eliminates-cholesterol
## aliases
bile acid cholesterol excretion
enterohepatic conservation of bile acids
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Bile salts are amphipathic and, especially when conjugated with glycine or taurine, water-soluble, which lets them emulsify fat; taurine conjugates are more soluble than glycine conjugates. Most of the pool is actively reabsorbed in the terminal ileum and recycled, so only small amounts are lost in the stool each day. That faecal loss, however, is the body's only route for eliminating cholesterol, from which bile acids are made, so bile-acid conservation is tightly linked to cholesterol balance. Interrupting reabsorption (ileal resection or bile-acid binders) increases the loss and lowers plasma cholesterol.
## explicit_objective
State that daily faecal loss of bile acids is the only route of cholesterol elimination and that conjugated bile salts are water-soluble.
## pitfalls
Thinking bile salts are water-insoluble, absorbed mainly by passive diffusion, or that glycine conjugates are more soluble than taurine conjugates.

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-0E1E1155AAE0F9
## label
Osmotic diarrhoea raises the stool osmotic gap and stops with fasting, unlike secretory diarrhoea
## canonical_key
diarrhea.osmotic.increased-stool-osmotic-gap-stops-with-fasting
## aliases
osmotic diarrhoea stool osmotic gap
osmotic versus secretory diarrhoea
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Osmotic diarrhoea occurs when poorly absorbed, osmotically active solutes draw water into the lumen, so the stool osmotic gap is increased and the diarrhoea ceases with fasting or on stopping the solute. Common causes are lactase and other disaccharidase deficiencies, magnesium-containing antacids or laxatives, and non-absorbable sugars such as sorbitol. Secretory diarrhoea, by contrast, results from increased crypt-cell secretion and inhibited electroneutral NaCl absorption; its stool osmotic gap is normal and it persists during fasting. The fasting response and the osmotic gap therefore separate the two.
## explicit_objective
State that osmotic diarrhoea raises the stool osmotic gap and stops with fasting, unlike secretory diarrhoea.
## pitfalls
Attributing an increased osmotic gap or fasting-responsive diarrhoea to crypt secretion, bacterial toxins, or a colon-only process (those describe secretory diarrhoea).

## label
Short-chain fatty acids are generated and absorbed in the colon and support colonic sodium absorption and mucosal growth
## canonical_key
colon.short-chain-fatty-acids.bacterial-product-absorbed
## aliases
colonic short-chain fatty acids
SCFA absorption colon
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Short-chain fatty acids are products of bacterial fermentation of undigested complex carbohydrate, and the colon is the major site for both their generation and their absorption. Once absorbed they exert trophic effects on the colonic mucosa and promote sodium absorption from the colon. A high-fibre diet increases their production, which is one way fibre supports colonic function. They are not the fat-digestion products handled by the small intestine.
## explicit_objective
State that short-chain fatty acids are generated and absorbed in the colon and support colonic sodium absorption and mucosal growth.
## pitfalls
Placing short-chain fatty acid absorption in the small intestine rather than the colon.

## label
Most gut water absorption is in the jejunum, driven passively by active sodium absorption, with the colon reclaiming a small fraction
## canonical_key
small-intestine.water-absorption.jejunum-major-site-passive
## aliases
jejunum water absorption
gut fluid handling
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The gut handles roughly 9 to 12 litres of fluid daily, most of it derived from gastrointestinal secretions rather than ingested drink. Osmotic equilibration of chyme occurs in the duodenum, and most water and electrolyte absorption then takes place in the jejunum. Water movement is passive, following the active absorption of sodium. The colon has a limited capacity, reclaiming only a few litres a day, so small-intestinal absorption dominates.
## explicit_objective
State that most gut water absorption is in the jejunum, driven passively by active sodium absorption, with the colon reclaiming a small fraction.
## pitfalls
Assuming most fluid comes from ingested drink, that colon and small intestine absorb equally, or that osmotic equilibration occurs in the stomach.

CON-GIT-4D54528A539047
## label
Loss of gastric reservoir delivers a hypertonic load rapidly, causing reactive hypoglycaemia and fluid shifts (dumping syndrome)
## canonical_key
dumping-syndrome.postgastrectomy.hypoglycemia-and-hypertonic-load
## aliases
dumping syndrome
post-gastrectomy hypoglycaemia
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
When the stomach's reservoir function and control of emptying are lost, for example after partial gastrectomy or a gastrojejunal anastomosis, a hypertonic meal is delivered rapidly into the intestine. Osmotic movement of water into the gut lumen causes hypovolaemia and hypotension, giving early weakness and dizziness. The rapid glucose load also triggers a large insulin surge that produces reactive hypoglycaemia, accounting for the later weakness, dizziness and sweating. These features together define dumping syndrome.
## explicit_objective
Explain that loss of gastric reservoir function delivers a hypertonic load rapidly, causing reactive hypoglycaemia and fluid shifts in dumping syndrome.
## pitfalls
Attributing dumping symptoms to raised blood pressure, glucagon or CCK, or to sustained hyperglycaemia rather than reactive hypoglycaemia.

## label
Common bile duct obstruction raises conjugated (direct) bilirubin, the post-hepatic jaundice pattern
## canonical_key
jaundice.obstructive.conjugated-bilirubin-bile-duct-obstruction
## aliases
obstructive jaundice
conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
When bilirubin has already been conjugated in the liver but cannot be excreted because the common bile duct is obstructed, the conjugated (direct) fraction is reabsorbed into the blood and rises. This post-hepatic pattern contrasts with the mixed rise of both fractions seen in hepatocellular disease and with unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia from overproduction or impaired uptake. Alkaline phosphatase, excreted in bile, typically rises with obstruction. Recognising a predominantly conjugated rise points to a cholestatic or obstructive cause.
## explicit_objective
State that common bile duct obstruction raises conjugated (direct) bilirubin, the post-hepatic jaundice pattern.
## pitfalls
Confusing the conjugated-predominant obstructive pattern with the mixed hepatocellular rise or with unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia.

## label
Neutral NaCl absorption (coupled Na+-H+ and Cl--HCO3- exchange) is the major mechanism of small-intestinal sodium absorption
## canonical_key
small-intestine.sodium-absorption.neutral-nacl-major-mechanism
## aliases
neutral NaCl absorption
small intestinal sodium uptake
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Sodium absorption is the primary absorptive event of the small intestine and is required for the absorption of water and other electrolytes. Although several pathways exist, the major mechanism is neutral NaCl absorption, in which apical Na+-H+ exchange is coupled to Cl--HCO3- exchange so that no net charge is transferred. Nutrient-coupled sodium uptake (for example with glucose) contributes strongly after a meal, and electrogenic sodium transport predominates in the colon rather than the small intestine. Solvent drag moves some sodium with water but is a minor route.
## explicit_objective
State that neutral NaCl absorption (coupled Na+-H+ and Cl--HCO3- exchange) is the major mechanism of small-intestinal sodium absorption.
## pitfalls
Choosing electrogenic transport (colonic), Na+-K+ cotransport, or solvent drag as the major small-intestinal sodium mechanism.

## label
Secretin-driven pancreatic bicarbonate secretion is the major factor neutralising acid and protecting the duodenal mucosa
## canonical_key
duodenum.mucosal-protection.pancreatic-bicarbonate-neutralises-acid
## aliases
duodenal mucosal protection
pancreatic bicarbonate neutralisation
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Unlike the gastric lining, the duodenal mucosa has little endogenous barrier of its own, so it depends on neutralising the acid emptied into it. Hydrogen ions entering the proximal duodenum release secretin, which stimulates pancreatic bicarbonate secretion; this pancreatic output matches gastric acid output and neutralises it. This secretin-pancreatic bicarbonate axis is therefore the major factor protecting the duodenal mucosa. Duodenal (Brunner gland) and biliary bicarbonate contribute only modestly by comparison.
## explicit_objective
State that secretin-driven pancreatic bicarbonate secretion is the major factor neutralising acid and protecting the duodenal mucosa.
## pitfalls
Crediting an endogenous duodenal barrier, or duodenal, hepatic or biliary bicarbonate, as the major protective factor instead of pancreatic bicarbonate.
