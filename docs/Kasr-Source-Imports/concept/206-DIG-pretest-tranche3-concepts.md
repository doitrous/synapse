# Item
## id
CON-GIT-5AC52EF838815E
## label
SGLT1 mediates apical Na+-dependent glucose and galactose uptake; GLUT-2 mediates basolateral exit
## canonical_key
enterocyte.glucose-absorption.sglt1-apical-cotransporter
## aliases
SGLT1 glucose transporter
glucose-galactose malabsorption
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Dietary glucose and galactose are absorbed across the enterocyte apical brush border by the sodium-dependent cotransporter SGLT1, a secondary active transport driven by the inward sodium gradient set up by the basolateral Na+/K+ ATPase. Glucose then leaves the cell across the basolateral membrane by facilitated diffusion through GLUT-2. Because SGLT1 carries both glucose and galactose, a congenital defect produces glucose-galactose malabsorption with severe, potentially fatal osmotic diarrhoea, relieved by removing both sugars from the diet. Fructose is handled separately, entering apically through GLUT-5.
## explicit_objective
State that SGLT1 mediates apical Na+-dependent glucose and galactose uptake and GLUT-2 mediates basolateral exit.
## pitfalls
Confusing apical SGLT1 with basolateral GLUT-2, or with the fructose transporter GLUT-5 or the renal SGLT2.

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-C100ABF3159A9D
## label
Medium-chain triglycerides are water-soluble and pass directly into the portal blood without chylomicron packaging
## canonical_key
fat.medium-chain-triglycerides.portal-blood-not-chylomicron
## aliases
medium-chain triglycerides
MCT portal absorption
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Medium-chain triglycerides (fatty acids of 6 to 12 carbons) are much more water-soluble than long-chain fats and are hydrolysed more rapidly. Inside the enterocyte they are not resynthesised into triglyceride or packaged into chylomicrons; instead they are released directly into the portal venous blood. Because their uptake needs neither extensive emulsification nor micelle formation, they are readily absorbed and are useful nutritionally in many malabsorptive diseases. This contrasts with long-chain fats, which leave the cell as chylomicrons in the lymph.
## explicit_objective
State that medium-chain triglycerides are water-soluble and pass directly into the portal blood without chylomicron packaging.
## pitfalls
Assuming medium-chain fats follow the long-chain route (triglyceride resynthesis, chylomicrons, lymph) or require emulsification.

CON-GIT-E6D0092CCB2424
## label
Plasma iron is carried on transferrin; absorption is slow, ferrous-form dependent, and stored as ferritin
## canonical_key
iron.transport.plasma-bound-transferrin
## aliases
iron transport transferrin
iron absorption ferrous
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
In plasma, iron circulates bound to the beta-globulin transferrin, and excess iron is stored, mainly in hepatocytes, combined with apoferritin as ferritin. The rate of iron absorption is very slow, only a few milligrams per day, and is tightly regulated to body need rather than dietary intake. Iron is taken up in the reduced ferrous (Fe2+) state, so ferrous compounds are more effective than ferric ones in treating deficiency. Apical uptake uses the divalent metal transporter, while ferroportin exports iron across the basolateral membrane.
## explicit_objective
State that plasma iron is carried on transferrin, that absorption is slow and ferrous-form dependent, and that it is stored as ferritin.
## pitfalls
Believing iron is absorbed rapidly or in the ferric form, or that ferroportin is the apical uptake transporter.

CON-GIT-4B103F8F192456
## label
VIP and nitric oxide are the NANC inhibitory neurotransmitters relaxing gut smooth muscle; their loss causes achalasia
## canonical_key
enteric-nerves.inhibitory-neurotransmitter.vip-and-nitric-oxide
## aliases
NANC inhibitory neurotransmitters
achalasia inhibitory neurons
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Relaxation of gastrointestinal smooth muscle is produced by nonadrenergic, noncholinergic (NANC) enteric nerves whose principal inhibitory transmitters are vasoactive intestinal peptide and nitric oxide. These mediate receptive relaxation and the deglutitive relaxation of sphincters such as the lower oesophageal sphincter. Degeneration of these inhibitory neurons leaves unopposed excitation, so the sphincter fails to relax on swallowing, producing the raised pressure and 'bird's beak' oesophagus of achalasia. Acetylcholine, substance P and dopamine are excitatory, and somatostatin is a paracrine secretion rather than a relaxant transmitter.
## explicit_objective
State that VIP and nitric oxide are the NANC inhibitory neurotransmitters relaxing gut smooth muscle, whose loss causes achalasia.
## pitfalls
Choosing acetylcholine, substance P or dopamine (excitatory), or somatostatin (paracrine), as the inhibitory relaxant.

## label
Enteropeptidase activates trypsinogen to trypsin, which then activates the other pancreatic proenzymes
## canonical_key
pancreas.trypsinogen-activation.enteropeptidase
## aliases
enterokinase trypsinogen
pancreatic proenzyme cascade
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Pancreatic proteases are secreted as inactive zymogens to protect the gland. In the duodenum, enteropeptidase (enterokinase) released from the mucosal cells cleaves trypsinogen to active trypsin. Trypsin then activates the remaining proenzymes, including chymotrypsinogen and the procarboxypeptidases, in an amplifying cascade that drives luminal protein digestion. Enteropeptidase is heavily glycosylated, which protects it from being digested itself.
## explicit_objective
State that enteropeptidase activates trypsinogen to trypsin, which then activates the other pancreatic proenzymes.
## pitfalls
Thinking chymotrypsin, procarboxypeptidase or alkaline pH activates trypsinogen; the initiating step is enteropeptidase.

CON-GIT-7FDAE8F856A5C0
## label
Hepatocellular (infectious) hepatitis raises both conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin, unlike obstructive jaundice
## canonical_key
jaundice.hepatocellular.direct-and-indirect-bilirubin-rise
## aliases
hepatocellular jaundice bilirubin
mixed hyperbilirubinaemia
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Infectious hepatitis injures hepatocytes, impairing uptake, conjugation and excretion of bilirubin while damaged cells regurgitate pigment back into the blood. Total serum bilirubin therefore rises with both the conjugated (direct) and unconjugated (indirect) fractions raised, usually about equally. This mixed pattern distinguishes hepatocellular jaundice from obstruction, in which conjugated bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase rise predominantly. Liver disease also decreases bile-acid synthesis, so an isolated bile-acid rise is not characteristic.
## explicit_objective
State that hepatocellular (infectious) hepatitis raises both conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin, unlike obstructive jaundice.
## pitfalls
Expecting an isolated alkaline phosphatase or conjugated-only rise (that is obstruction), or a fall in bilirubin.

## label
Colonic gas is produced mainly by bacterial fermentation of undigested carbohydrate
## canonical_key
colon.gas.bacterial-fermentation-undigested-carbohydrate
## aliases
intestinal gas
flatus bacterial fermentation
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Most of the gut's gas lies in the colon and is produced by bacterial fermentation of undigested material, yielding carbon dioxide, hydrogen and methane. Foods rich in indigestible carbohydrate, such as the oligosaccharides in beans, markedly increase this production. Swallowed atmospheric air, by contrast, collects mostly in the stomach, and diffusion from blood contributes only some of the nitrogen. Gas formed in the small intestine from acid-bicarbonate interaction does not accumulate because it is reabsorbed or passed onward.
## explicit_objective
State that colonic gas is produced mainly by bacterial fermentation of undigested carbohydrate.
## pitfalls
Attributing colonic gas chiefly to swallowed air, blood diffusion, or acid-bicarbonate CO2 rather than bacterial fermentation.

CON-GIT-D2359C24D2AFA7
## label
Long-chain fat is exported from enterocytes as chylomicrons into the lymph, while short/medium-chain fat enters portal blood
## canonical_key
fat.long-chain.chylomicrons-into-lymph
## aliases
chylomicron formation
long-chain fat lymphatic transport
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Long-chain fats are absorbed as monoglycerides and free fatty acids, then resynthesised into triglyceride within the enterocyte. The triglyceride is packaged, with cholesterol, phospholipid and apoprotein, into chylomicrons, which are extruded into the intestinal lacteals and carried in the lymph to the systemic circulation. Short- and medium-chain fatty acids, being water-soluble, bypass this route and pass directly into the portal blood. This division explains why long-chain fat malabsorption is tied to defects in chylomicron handling or lymphatic drainage.
## explicit_objective
State that long-chain fat is exported from enterocytes as chylomicrons into the lymph, while short/medium-chain fat enters portal blood.
## pitfalls
Answering monoglycerides, free triglyceride, or free fatty acids for the lymph-exported form; the particle is the chylomicron.
