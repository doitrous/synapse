## label
GnRH acts on a Gq-coupled receptor using the IP3-DAG-calcium second messenger system
## canonical_key
gnrh.receptor.ip3-calcium-gq
## aliases
Gonadotropin-releasing hormone signals through phospholipase C, IP3 and intracellular calcium
GnRH uses the IP3-Ca2+ pathway, not cyclic AMP or a nuclear receptor
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) is a hydrophilic decapeptide that binds a G-protein-coupled receptor on the pituitary gonadotroph. The receptor couples to Gq, which activates phospholipase C to split membrane PIP2 into inositol trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol; IP3 releases calcium from intracellular stores and, with DAG-activated protein kinase C, drives LH and FSH secretion. This IP3-calcium mechanism distinguishes GnRH from the lipophilic steroids cholecalciferol and progesterone, which act on intracellular nuclear receptors, and from insulin, which acts through an intrinsic receptor tyrosine kinase.
## explicit_objective
Identify GnRH as a hormone that signals through the Gq / phospholipase C / IP3-calcium pathway rather than cyclic AMP, a nuclear receptor or a tyrosine kinase.
## pitfalls
Assuming every peptide hormone raises cyclic AMP: GnRH (like TRH and oxytocin) uses the Gq-IP3-calcium arm of GPCR signalling, not the Gs-cAMP arm.

CON-END-A707E662F5EC8D
## label
Growth hormone has intrinsic lactogenic (prolactin-like) activity
## canonical_key
growthhormone.lactogenic.intrinsic-activity
## aliases
GH shares structural homology with prolactin and can itself promote lactation
Among TSH, MSH, GH and dopamine, growth hormone is the one with lactogenic activity
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Growth hormone, prolactin and human placental lactogen belong to one structurally homologous hormone family, so growth hormone can bind the prolactin receptor and possesses intrinsic lactogenic activity in addition to its somatotropic effects. Thyroid-stimulating hormone and melanocyte-stimulating hormone have no lactogenic action, and dopamine is in fact the tonic inhibitor of prolactin release; therefore growth hormone is the hormone with intrinsic lactogenic activity. This overlap is why very high growth-hormone states can be associated with galactorrhoea even without a rise in prolactin.
## explicit_objective
Recognise growth hormone as the anterior-pituitary hormone with intrinsic lactogenic activity, reflecting its structural homology with prolactin.
## pitfalls
Treating lactogenesis as prolactin-exclusive: growth hormone belongs to the same hormone family and binds the prolactin receptor, giving it intrinsic lactogenic activity.

CON-END-36019EAEAF1832
## label
ADH circulates in plasma bound to neurophysin II
## canonical_key
adh.carrier.neurophysin-ii
## aliases
Vasopressin is transported and stored bound to neurophysin II
Oxytocin pairs with neurophysin I, ADH with neurophysin II
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) is synthesised in the hypothalamic supraoptic and paraventricular neurons as part of a larger precursor that also yields its specific carrier peptide, neurophysin II. The hormone travels down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract, is stored in the posterior pituitary and released bound to neurophysin II, which is its principal association in plasma. This is distinct from oxytocin, which is packaged with neurophysin I, and unlike steroid or thyroid hormones ADH is not carried on plasma albumin or globulins.
## explicit_objective
State that ADH (vasopressin) is associated with neurophysin II, the carrier peptide derived from its own precursor, rather than neurophysin I or plasma albumin.
## pitfalls
Swapping the two carriers: neurophysin II accompanies ADH/vasopressin, whereas neurophysin I accompanies oxytocin.
