## label
The lacrimal nerve (V1's most lateral branch) supplies skin of the upper lateral eyelid
## canonical_key
lacrimal-nerve-upper-eyelid-skin
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The lacrimal nerve is the most lateral of the three branches of the ophthalmic division (V1) of the trigeminal nerve. It runs along the upper border of lateral rectus, through the lacrimal gland, and its terminal cutaneous twigs supply the skin of the lateral part of the upper eyelid and adjoining conjunctiva -- distinct territory from the more medial supraorbital and supratrochlear (frontal nerve) branches, which cover the middle/medial upper eyelid and forehead.
## explicit_objective
Localise the lacrimal nerve's cutaneous territory to the lateral upper eyelid, distinguishing it from the supraorbital/supratrochlear (medial/central) and zygomaticofacial (cheek) territories.
## pitfalls
Assuming any V1 branch covers the whole upper eyelid uniformly -- the three V1 cutaneous branches (lacrimal, supraorbital, supratrochlear) split the upper eyelid/forehead by position, lateral to medial.

CON-NEU-AFE75BD9FFF1F1
## label
Hypoglossal nerve injury paralyses ipsilateral genioglossus, deviating the protruded tongue toward the injured side
## canonical_key
hypoglossal-nerve-tongue-deviation-injured-side
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The hypoglossal nerve (CN XII) loops through the floor of the submandibular triangle on its way to the tongue, making it the named structure most exposed to a penetrating neck wound there. On protrusion, each side's genioglossus normally pulls the tongue forward and slightly to the opposite side, so healthy bilateral action keeps protrusion straight. When one hypoglossal nerve is injured, the unopposed healthy-side genioglossus pushes the tongue toward the weak (injured) side, so the direction of deviation on protrusion localises the lesion to that same side's hypoglossal nerve.
## explicit_objective
State that hypoglossal nerve injury deviates the protruded tongue toward the injured (weak) side, and use this to localise a lesion from the observed direction of deviation.
## pitfalls
Concluding the tongue deviates AWAY from the injured side -- it is the unopposed healthy-side genioglossus that wins, pushing the tongue toward the weak side, not away from it.

## label
The facial nerve exits the skull through the stylomastoid foramen before entering the parotid gland
## canonical_key
facial-nerve-stylomastoid-foramen
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
After leaving the brainstem and traversing the facial canal within the temporal bone, the facial nerve (CN VII) exits the skull through the stylomastoid foramen, immediately posterior to the styloid process. It then enters the parotid gland and divides into five terminal branches (temporal, zygomatic, buccal, marginal mandibular, cervical); the marginal mandibular branch supplies only the lower lip muscles (not the upper lip, which is buccal/zygomatic territory), and the facial nerve also supplies the posterior (not anterior) belly of digastric.
## explicit_objective
State that the facial nerve exits the skull via the stylomastoid foramen, giving five terminal branches within the parotid gland, and supplies the posterior belly of digastric.
## pitfalls
Confusing the facial nerve's five parotid branches with four, or crediting it with the anterior belly of digastric (that belongs to the mylohyoid nerve, CN V3) or the whole lip (marginal mandibular is lower lip only).

## label
The abducent nerve supplies only lateral rectus, producing pure horizontal abduction
## canonical_key
abducent-nerve-lateral-rectus
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The abducent nerve (CN VI) supplies a single extraocular muscle, lateral rectus, which abducts the eye. Because only one muscle is involved, an isolated abducent nerve palsy produces a pure horizontal deviation -- a medially deviated, non-abducting eye -- without affecting elevation, depression or eyelid position, unlike an oculomotor (CN III) palsy which affects multiple muscles and the lid.
## explicit_objective
State that the abducent nerve supplies lateral rectus only, and predict the resulting pure horizontal deficit of an abducent nerve palsy.
## pitfalls
Attributing superior rectus, inferior oblique or the superior tarsal muscle to the abducent nerve -- these are oculomotor (CN III) or sympathetic territory, not abducent.

## label
Lateral pterygoid is the sole jaw-opening muscle among the four muscles of mastication
## canonical_key
lateral-pterygoid-jaw-opening
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Of the four muscles of mastication (masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid, lateral pterygoid), lateral pterygoid alone opens (depresses) the mandible, by pulling the condyle and articular disc forward as the mouth opens. The other three (masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid) all elevate/close the jaw. A lateral pterygoid palsy classically deviates the jaw TOWARD the weak side on opening, because the healthy contralateral muscle pulls the mandible over unopposed.
## explicit_objective
Identify lateral pterygoid as the jaw-opening muscle of mastication, and predict jaw deviation toward the weak side on opening if it is paralysed.
## pitfalls
Assuming any muscle of mastication opens the jaw -- three of the four (masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid) are elevators/closers; only lateral pterygoid opens it.

## label
The ansa cervicalis's inferior root (descendens cervicalis) arises from C2 and C3
## canonical_key
ansa-cervicalis-inferior-root
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The ansa cervicalis is a nerve loop embedded in the anterior wall of the carotid sheath, formed by two roots: a superior root (descendens hypoglossi), a C1 branch that joins and travels with the hypoglossal nerve before descending independently, and an inferior root (descendens cervicalis), which arises directly from the C2 and C3 anterior rami of the cervical plexus and descends to join the superior root. The completed loop supplies the infrahyoid strap muscles (sternohyoid, sternothyroid, inferior belly of omohyoid), while superior belly omohyoid is supplied separately by the superior root itself.
## explicit_objective
State that the ansa cervicalis's inferior root arises from C2-C3, distinguishing it from the superior root's C1-via-hypoglossal origin.
## pitfalls
Confusing which root is which -- the SUPERIOR root travels with the hypoglossal nerve (C1 fibres), while the INFERIOR root (C2-C3) arises independently from the cervical plexus.

The cavernous sinus lies adjacent to the pituitary gland and is the classic site of septic dural sinus thrombosis
## canonical_key
cavernous-sinus-thrombosis
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases
