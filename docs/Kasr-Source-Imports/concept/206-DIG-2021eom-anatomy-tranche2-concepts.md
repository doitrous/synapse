## label
The common hepatic artery gives the gastroduodenal and right gastric arteries
## canonical_key
commonhepaticartery.branches.right-gastric-and-gastroduodenal
## aliases
common hepatic artery branches
gastroduodenal and right gastric arteries origin
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The common hepatic artery runs to the right along the upper border of the pancreas and divides into the gastroduodenal artery, which descends behind the first part of the duodenum, and the hepatic artery proper, which passes to the liver in the free edge of the lesser omentum. The right gastric artery arises from the hepatic artery proper (or occasionally the common hepatic artery) and runs along the lesser curvature to anastomose with the left gastric artery. The right gastric and gastroduodenal arteries are thus the vessels derived from the common hepatic stem, while the left gastric and splenic arteries are the other two direct coeliac branches.
## explicit_objective
List the gastroduodenal and right gastric arteries as vessels derived from the common hepatic artery.
## pitfalls
Assigning the left gastric or splenic artery to the common hepatic artery, or confusing the hepatic artery proper (its continuation) with a co-branch.

CON-GIT-27360ED690D3EB
## label
The appendicular artery is an end artery whose occlusion causes gangrene of the appendix
## canonical_key
appendicularartery.endartery.gangrene-on-occlusion
## aliases
appendicular artery end artery
appendix gangrene ileocolic artery
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The appendix is supplied by the appendicular artery, a branch of the ileocolic artery (from the superior mesenteric artery), which reaches the appendix in the free edge of the mesoappendix. The appendicular artery is a functional end artery with no effective collateral anastomosis, so thrombosis of it during acute appendicitis rapidly causes ischaemia, gangrene and perforation. Occlusion of the parent ileocolic artery removes the same sole supply.
## explicit_objective
Identify the appendicular artery (from the ileocolic artery) as the end artery whose occlusion causes appendicular gangrene.
## pitfalls
Attributing appendicular supply to the right, middle or left colic arteries, or forgetting that the appendicular artery is an end artery with no anastomosis.

## label
The right suprarenal gland is pyramidal (triangular) in shape and lies behind the inferior vena cava
## canonical_key
rightsuprarenalgland.shape.pyramidal-triangular
## aliases
right adrenal gland pyramidal
right suprarenal gland shape and relations
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The right suprarenal (adrenal) gland is pyramidal or triangular in shape and caps the upper pole of the right kidney, in contrast to the more elongated, semilunar left gland. Anteriorly it is related to the inferior vena cava and the bare area of the liver, and posteriorly to the diaphragm. Its hilum faces anteromedially and transmits the short right suprarenal vein, which drains directly into the inferior vena cava.
## explicit_objective
Recognise the pyramidal shape of the right suprarenal gland and its relation to the inferior vena cava.
## pitfalls
Calling the right gland semilunar (that is the left gland), or thinking the suprarenal gland reaches the renal hilum or relates anteriorly to the stomach.

## label
The greater curvature forms from the fast-growing dorsal border of the embryonic stomach
## canonical_key
stomach.development.dorsal-border-becomes-greater-curvature
## aliases
greater curvature dorsal border development
stomach rotation greater curvature
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The developing stomach is a foregut dilatation whose dorsal (originally posterior) border grows much faster than its ventral border. This differential growth balloons the dorsal border out to form the greater curvature, while the slower ventral border becomes the lesser curvature. The stomach then rotates 90 degrees clockwise about its longitudinal axis, carrying the greater curvature to the left and the lesser curvature to the right, and drawing the dorsal mesogastrium out to form the greater omentum and the lesser sac behind it.
## explicit_objective
Explain that the fast-growing dorsal border of the embryonic stomach becomes the greater curvature.
## pitfalls
Thinking the stomach is partly midgut, reversing the axis of the 90-degree rotation, or attributing the greater curvature to the ventral border.

## label
The main pancreatic duct forms from the ventral duct plus the distal part of the dorsal duct
## canonical_key
mainpancreaticduct.formation.distal-dorsal-plus-ventral-duct
## aliases
main pancreatic duct of Wirsung formation
accessory duct of Santorini
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
As the ventral and dorsal pancreatic buds fuse, their duct systems anastomose. The main pancreatic duct (of Wirsung) is formed by the entire ventral pancreatic duct together with the distal part of the dorsal pancreatic duct, and it opens with the bile duct at the major duodenal papilla. The proximal part of the dorsal duct persists as the accessory duct of Santorini, which drains at the minor duodenal papilla; failure of the ducts to fuse produces pancreas divisum.
## explicit_objective
Describe the main pancreatic duct as the ventral duct plus the distal dorsal duct.
## pitfalls
Attributing the whole main duct to the dorsal duct alone or the ventral duct alone, or forgetting that the proximal dorsal duct persists as the accessory duct.

## label
The ureteric bud forms the collecting system of the kidney, including the major calyces
## canonical_key
uretericbud.derivatives.collecting-system-major-calyces
## aliases
ureteric bud derivatives
major calyces from ureteric bud
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The ureteric bud is an outgrowth of the mesonephric duct that gives rise to the collecting system of the kidney: the ureter, renal pelvis, major and minor calyces, and the collecting tubules and ducts, formed by its repeated dichotomous branching. The nephron proper — Bowman's capsule and the proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle and distal convoluted tubule — develops instead from the surrounding metanephric mesenchyme (blastema) induced by the bud. Reciprocal induction between the two tissues is essential for normal kidney development.
## explicit_objective
Distinguish ureteric-bud derivatives (collecting system, major calyces) from nephron structures of metanephric origin.
## pitfalls
Assigning nephron segments (Bowman's capsule, proximal or distal convoluted tubule) to the ureteric bud instead of the metanephric mesenchyme.

## label
In the male the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct gives rise to the seminal vesicle
## canonical_key
mesonephricduct.male-derivatives.seminal-vesicle
## aliases
Wolffian duct male derivatives
seminal vesicle from mesonephric duct
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
In the male, testosterone from the fetal testis maintains the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct, which gives rise to the epididymis, the ductus (vas) deferens, the ejaculatory duct and the seminal vesicle, the last budding from the lower end of the duct. The efferent ductules and small remnants such as the paradidymis derive from mesonephric tubules, not the duct itself. The paramesonephric (Mullerian) duct regresses, leaving the appendix of the testis and the prostatic utricle as its only male remnants.
## explicit_objective
Identify the seminal vesicle as a male derivative of the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct.
## pitfalls
Confusing mesonephric-tubule derivatives (efferent ductules, paradidymis) or paramesonephric remnants (prostatic utricle) with mesonephric-duct derivatives.

## label
The hindgut gives rise to the descending and sigmoid colon, rectum and upper anal canal
## canonical_key
hindgut.derivatives.sigmoid-colon
## aliases
hindgut derivatives
sigmoid colon from hindgut
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The hindgut gives rise to the distal (left) third of the transverse colon, the descending colon, the sigmoid colon, the rectum and the upper part of the anal canal down to the pectinate line. Consistent with this common embryological origin, all these structures are supplied by the inferior mesenteric artery, the artery of the hindgut. The caecum, appendix, ascending colon and proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon are midgut derivatives, and the gall bladder is a foregut derivative.
## explicit_objective
List the sigmoid colon among the hindgut derivatives supplied by the inferior mesenteric artery.
## pitfalls
Assigning the ascending colon or caecum (midgut) or the gall bladder (foregut) to the hindgut.
