## label
The basal electrical rhythm (slow waves) of GIT smooth muscle is initiated by pacemaker interstitial cells of Cajal
## canonical_key
basalelectricalrhythm.pacemaker.interstitial-cells-of-cajal
## aliases
BER pacemaker cells
Interstitial cells of Cajal - GIT slow waves
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC), specialised pacemaker cells interposed between the longitudinal and circular smooth muscle layers and within the circular layer itself, generate rhythmic, spontaneous depolarisation-repolarisation cycles known as the basal electrical rhythm (BER) or slow waves. BER frequency varies by GIT segment (roughly 3/min in the stomach, up to about 12/min in the duodenum, declining toward the terminal ileum), it does not by itself guarantee muscle contraction, and it is present throughout the stomach, small intestine and large intestine.
## explicit_objective
Attribute the GIT basal electrical rhythm to pacemaker interstitial cells of Cajal, and recognise that BER frequency differs by segment and does not by itself produce contraction.
## pitfalls
Assuming BER frequency is uniform along the GIT (it is segment-specific, fastest in the duodenum) or that a slow wave always triggers contraction — contraction requires the wave to reach threshold, usually via added neural/hormonal excitation, producing superimposed spike potentials.

## label
In pernicious anaemia, gastrin rises because autoimmune destruction of the acid-secreting parietal cells removes the antral G cell's normal negative feedback
## canonical_key
gastrin.pernicious-anemia.loss-of-negative-feedback
## aliases
Secondary hypergastrinaemia - pernicious anemia
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Gastrin release from antral G cells is normally suppressed by the low luminal pH (below roughly 2) produced by parietal-cell acid secretion. Pernicious anaemia destroys the gastric body's parietal (and chief) cells through autoimmune atrophic gastritis, so acid output collapses, luminal pH stays persistently high, and G cells are never switched off — producing markedly elevated fasting serum gastrin (secondary hypergastrinaemia) alongside the vitamin B12 deficiency that follows loss of intrinsic factor.
## explicit_objective
Explain why chronic atrophic (autoimmune) gastritis in pernicious anaemia produces hypergastrinaemia through loss of acid-mediated negative feedback, not through a gastrin-secreting tumour.
## pitfalls
Confusing this physiological secondary hypergastrinaemia (low acid, high gastrin, from feedback loss) with Zollinger-Ellison syndrome's primary hypergastrinaemia (a gastrinoma driving inappropriately high acid output despite high gastrin) — the acid output points in opposite directions in the two conditions.

## label
Fat and glucose in the duodenum stimulate GIP secretion from K cells — they do not inhibit it
## canonical_key
gip.duodenaltrigger.fat-and-glucose-stimulate-not-inhibit-secretion
## aliases
Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide secretion trigger
Gastric inhibitory peptide secretion trigger
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP, also called gastric inhibitory peptide) is released from K cells of the duodenal and proximal jejunal mucosa in direct response to luminal glucose and fat. As an incretin, GIP potentiates glucose-stimulated insulin release from pancreatic beta cells and inhibits their apoptosis, while also mildly inhibiting gastric acid secretion and motility — an inhibitory action on the stomach, not an inhibitory relationship with the very duodenal nutrients that trigger its own release.
## explicit_objective
State that duodenal fat and glucose stimulate, not inhibit, GIP secretion from K cells, distinguishing GIP's triggers from its separate inhibitory actions on the stomach.
## pitfalls
Assuming that because GIP is historically named "gastric inhibitory peptide," fat in the duodenum must inhibit its own secretion — the inhibitory action targets gastric acid/motility, while duodenal fat and glucose are GIP's stimulus, not its brake.

## label
VIP is released from cholinergic VIP-releasing enteric nerves and acts as a major inhibitory neurotransmitter of GI smooth muscle
## canonical_key
vip.release.cholinergic-vip-releasing-nerves
## aliases
Vasoactive intestinal peptide release
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) is a neuropeptide released from a subset of enteric neurons classically described as cholinergic VIP-releasing nerves (non-adrenergic, non-cholinergic inhibitory neurons of the enteric nervous system). It relaxes GI smooth muscle (including the lower oesophageal sphincter and gastric fundus during receptive relaxation) and dilates GI blood vessels, and it stimulates intestinal and pancreatic secretion of water and electrolytes, augmenting secretin-stimulated pancreatic bicarbonate output.
## explicit_objective
Identify VIP as a product of cholinergic VIP-releasing enteric nerves that relaxes GI smooth muscle and dilates GI vasculature, rather than a hormone that contracts muscle or vasoconstricts.
## pitfalls
Reversing VIP's actions — it relaxes (not contracts) GI smooth muscle and vasodilates (not vasoconstricts) GI blood vessels; a VIP-secreting tumour (VIPoma) causes watery diarrhoea precisely because of this relaxant, secretory profile.

Reduced motilin secretion during pregnancy contributes to the constipation and dyspepsia of pregnancy
## canonical_key
motilin.deficiency.pregnancy-constipation-dyspepsia

## arabic_label

## definition
Motilin is released cyclically from enterochromaffin-like (Mo) cells of the duodenal and jejunal mucosa during the fasting state, triggering the migrating motor complex that sweeps residual contents and bacteria through the small intestine between meals. Progesterone-driven smooth-muscle relaxation in pregnancy reduces motilin secretion and slows the migrating motor complex, contributing (together with mechanical compression by the gravid uterus) to the constipation, dyspepsia and delayed gastric emptying commonly reported in pregnancy.
## explicit_objective
Link reduced motilin secretion in pregnancy to the resulting constipation and dyspepsia via a slowed migrating motor complex.
## pitfalls
Attributing pregnancy constipation/dyspepsia solely to mechanical compression by the uterus — the hormonal (progesterone-mediated fall in motilin and smooth-muscle tone) component is the specific physiological mechanism this question tests.

## canonical_key
saliva.function.ph-buffering-except

Saliva buffering function
## arabic_label

## definition
Saliva's three defined protective/mechanical functions are protecting the oral mucosa against pathogenic bacteria (via lysozyme, IgA and mechanical washing), assisting swallowing and speech through lubrication, and cooling hot food/drink before it reaches the oesophagus. ## pitfalls
Treating salivary pH as a single fixed high number — resting saliva sits closer to neutral, and only rises toward alkaline values with increased flow, so describing salivary buffers as keeping the oral pH fixed "high at 7.8" misstates the function.

Salivary secretion reaching the mouth is hypotonic and rich in bicarbonate and potassium relative to plasma
## canonical_key
saliva.composition.rich-in-bicarbonate
## aliases
Ductal modification of saliva
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Saliva is formed in two stages: an isotonic primary secretion by acinar cells, then striated-duct cells reabsorb Na+ and Cl- while secreting K+ and HCO3- (both active processes), so the final saliva reaching the mouth is hypotonic to plasma, poor in sodium and chloride, but rich in bicarbonate and potassium.

State that final salivary secretion is hypotonic and rich in bicarbonate (and potassium), not rich in sodium or isotonic to plasma.

Assuming ductal modification makes saliva resemble plasma (isotonic) — ductal Na+/Cl- reabsorption exceeds K+/HCO3- secretion, so the net effect is a hypotonic, bicarbonate-rich fluid; this modification is reduced, and saliva becomes more isotonic and higher in Na+, only at very high flow rates.

## label
The "alkaline tide" — gastric HCl secretion raises gastric venous blood pH via parallel HCO3- secretion into the blood
## canonical_key
gastricacidsecretion.alkaline-tide.hco3-into-venous-blood
## aliases
Alkaline tide mechanism
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The parietal cell's apical H+/K+ ATPase secretes H+ into the gastric lumen while, on its basolateral (blood-facing) side, a Cl-/HCO3- exchanger secretes the HCO3- generated by carbonic anhydrase-catalysed CO2 hydration into the gastric venous blood. This bicarbonate efflux — the alkaline tide — transiently raises gastric venous, and after a large meal even systemic, blood pH, and is a direct consequence of active acid secretion rather than of the proton pump itself or of its inhibition.
## explicit_objective
Explain the alkaline tide as parallel basolateral HCO3- secretion accompanying apical H+ secretion by the parietal cell, raising gastric venous blood pH.
## pitfalls
Confusing the alkaline tide with the mechanism of acid secretion itself (the proton pump) or with inhibition of the H+/K+ ATPase — the alkaline tide is the physiological consequence (basolateral HCO3- efflux) of active acid secretion, not the secretory pump or its blockade.

## canonical_key

## arabic_label

The exocrine pancreas secretes lipase, amylase and the proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin) as its major digestive enzymes; lipase alone hydrolyses dietary triglycerides into monoglycerides and free fatty acids for micellar solubilisation and absorption. ## pitfalls
Assuming any pancreatic enzyme deficiency causes steatorrhoea equally — fat malabsorption specifically tracks lipase loss, since amylase and the proteases digest carbohydrate and protein, not fat.

## definition
The migrating motor complex (MMC) is a cyclical, multiphasic pattern of motility (quiescence, then irregular contractions, then an intense burst of peristaltic activity) that recurs roughly every 90-120 minutes during the fasting state, initiated by motilin and coordinated by the enteric nervous system, sweeping from the stomach through the small intestine to the ileocaecal valve. Its principal physiological role is housekeeping — clearing residual food debris, secretions and bacteria from the small intestine between meals — and it is specifically a fasting-state phenomenon, ceasing (not starting) with food intake.
## explicit_objective
Attribute the migrating motor complex's main physiological role to preventing small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth between meals, and correctly place its activity in the fasting (not fed) state.
## pitfalls
Describing the MMC as a single peristaltic wave (it is a multiphasic, recurring cycle) or as initiated by food and stopping during fasting — the true pattern is the reverse: the MMC is a fasting-state phenomenon that is suppressed by feeding.
