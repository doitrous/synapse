Plicae circulares are permanent folds with a mucosal surface and a submucosal core
## canonical_key
plicae-circulares.small-intestine.mucosal-fold-submucosal-core
## aliases
plicae circulares submucosal core

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The plicae circulares (valves of Kerckring) are permanent circular folds of the small-intestinal wall whose surface is mucosa but whose core contains submucosa, which distinguishes them from villi, which are projections of mucosa only. They are most prominent in the jejunum and, together with the villi and the microvillous brush border, greatly amplify the absorptive surface area.
## explicit_objective
Distinguish plicae circulares (mucosa with a submucosal core) from villi (mucosa only).
## pitfalls
Confusing plicae circulares with villi; thinking villi contain submucosa, or that microvilli reduce the surface area.

At the pectinate (dentate) line of the anal canal the muscularis mucosae breaks up and disappears, marking the junction between the upper colorectal-type mucosa and the lower anal skin. ## label
Cholecystokinin is a hormonal stimulus of the pancreas, not a constituent of pancreatic juice
## canonical_key

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Pancreatic juice contains digestive zymogens and enzymes (trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, procarboxypeptidases, lipase and amylase) in a bicarbonate-rich fluid. Cholecystokinin is not a component of the juice; it is a hormone released by the duodenal I cells that stimulates the acinar cells to secrete enzyme-rich juice and contracts the gall bladder. CCK therefore acts on the pancreas rather than being contained in its secretion.
## explicit_objective
Recognise cholecystokinin as a hormonal stimulus of the pancreas, not a constituent of pancreatic juice.
## pitfalls
Listing CCK among the pancreatic-juice enzymes, or forgetting that trypsinogen and chymotrypsinogen are secreted as inactive zymogens.

Trypsin hyposecretion impairs protein digestion in pancreatic insufficiency

## definition
Trypsin is the principal pancreatic protease and the activator of the other pancreatic zymogens (chymotrypsinogen, proelastase, procarboxypeptidases). When a damaged or insufficient pancreas hyposecretes its enzymes, loss of trypsin most directly impairs protein digestion, both through its own proteolysis and by failing to activate the downstream proteases. Lipase deficiency instead causes fat maldigestion (steatorrhoea), and amylase deficiency affects starch.
## explicit_objective
Explain that pancreatic trypsin hyposecretion impairs protein digestion.
## pitfalls
Attributing protein maldigestion to lipase or amylase; forgetting that trypsin activates the other pancreatic zymogens.
