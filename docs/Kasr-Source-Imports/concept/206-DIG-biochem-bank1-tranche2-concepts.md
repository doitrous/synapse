# Item
## id
CON-GIT-27465BBE1D340E
## label
GLUT4 is the insulin-dependent facilitative glucose transporter of muscle and adipose tissue
## canonical_key
glut4.transporter.adipose-insulin-facilitative
## aliases
insulin-responsive glucose transporter GLUT4
GLUT4 in muscle and fat
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
GLUT4 is a facilitative glucose transporter expressed mainly in skeletal muscle, cardiac muscle and adipose tissue. It moves glucose into the cell by facilitated diffusion down its concentration gradient. Its distinguishing feature is insulin dependence: insulin triggers translocation of GLUT4-containing vesicles to the plasma membrane, raising surface transporter number and hence glucose uptake, which is why all three statements (adipose expression, glucose facilitation, insulin dependence) are true of it.
## explicit_objective
State that GLUT4 is the insulin-dependent facilitative glucose transporter of muscle and adipose tissue.
## pitfalls
Confusing GLUT4 with the insulin-independent SGLT1/GLUT2 of gut and kidney, or thinking insulin changes its intrinsic activity rather than its membrane abundance.

## label
Dietary pentoses are not utilised because no specific kinase phosphorylates them
## canonical_key
pentose.dietary-utilization.no-specific-kinase
## aliases
dietary pentose metabolism
why xylose and arabinose are not used
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Dietary pentoses such as arabinose and xylose are absorbed from the gut but cannot be metabolised because the body lacks a specific kinase to phosphorylate them. Without phosphorylation they cannot enter metabolic pathways and are excreted largely unchanged. The pentose the body does need (ribose for nucleotides) is generated endogenously from glucose by the pentose phosphate pathway rather than obtained from the diet.
## explicit_objective
Explain that dietary pentoses are not utilised owing to the absence of a specific phosphorylating kinase.
## pitfalls
Assuming dietary pentoses are simply not absorbed, or that endogenous pentose availability is the reason they are unused.

## label
Cellulose is indigestible dietary fibre that prevents constipation
## canonical_key
cellulose.dietary-fibre.prevents-constipation
## aliases
dietary fibre and bowel transit
cellulose as roughage
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Cellulose is a beta-1,4-linked glucose polymer that humans cannot digest because they lack cellulase. As insoluble dietary fibre it is not absorbed; instead it adds bulk and retains water in the stool, speeding intestinal transit and preventing constipation. This distinguishes it from digestible carbohydrates (starch, maltose, fructose) that are broken down and absorbed for energy.
## explicit_objective
Identify cellulose as indigestible dietary fibre that adds faecal bulk and prevents constipation.
## pitfalls
Treating cellulose as an energy source, or confusing it with digestible alpha-1,4/1,6-linked starch.

## label
Carbohydrate digestion begins in the mouth with salivary amylase
## canonical_key
carbohydrate.digestion-start.mouth-salivary-amylase
## aliases
salivary amylase ptyalin
where starch digestion starts
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Carbohydrate digestion begins in the mouth, where salivary alpha-amylase (ptyalin) starts hydrolysing starch into maltose and dextrins during chewing. The acidic stomach then inactivates this enzyme, and digestion resumes in the small intestine with pancreatic amylase and completes at the brush border with disaccharidases. Thus the mouth, not the stomach or intestine, is where carbohydrate digestion is initiated.
## explicit_objective
State that carbohydrate digestion starts in the mouth via salivary amylase.
## pitfalls
Naming the stomach (where salivary amylase is inactivated) or the small intestine (where digestion is completed) as the starting site.

CON-GIT-130366224DFA25
## label
Absorbed dietary fat is transported as chylomicrons
## canonical_key
chylomicron.transport.dietary-lipid
## aliases
exogenous lipid transport
chylomicron as dietary fat carrier
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
After the products of fat digestion are absorbed, the enterocyte re-esterifies fatty acids and monoacylglycerols into triacylglycerol and packages it, with cholesterol and apolipoproteins, into chylomicrons. Chylomicrons enter the intestinal lymphatics (lacteals) and reach the blood via the thoracic duct, delivering exogenous (dietary) fat to peripheral tissues. They are therefore the transport form of absorbed dietary fat, distinct from luminal micelles and from the albumin-bound free fatty acids released by adipose tissue.
## explicit_objective
State that absorbed dietary fat is transported in the circulation as chylomicrons.
## pitfalls
Confusing the intraluminal micelle (delivery to the mucosa) or the fatty acid-albumin complex (adipose-derived free fatty acids) with the blood transport form of dietary fat.

CON-GIT-440EFB9ADE609A
## label
Triacylglycerol synthesis adds a fatty acyl-CoA to diacylglycerol via DGAT
## canonical_key
triacylglycerol.synthesis.fatty-acyl-coa
## aliases
DGAT final step of TAG synthesis
diacylglycerol acyltransferase
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
In triacylglycerol synthesis, glycerol-3-phosphate is progressively acylated to phosphatidic acid and then dephosphorylated to diacylglycerol. The final, committed step is catalysed by diacylglycerol acyltransferase (DGAT), which transfers a third fatty acid from an activated fatty acyl-CoA onto the diacylglycerol to yield triacylglycerol. The acyl donor added at this step is therefore fatty acyl-CoA, not free glycerol, glycerol-3-phosphate or the two-carbon acetyl-CoA.
## explicit_objective
Identify fatty acyl-CoA as the acyl donor added to diacylglycerol by DGAT to form triacylglycerol.
## pitfalls
Choosing glycerol-3-phosphate (the upstream backbone) or acetyl-CoA (the two-carbon unit for fatty-acid synthesis) as the group added at the final esterification step.

## label
Bile salts emulsify dietary fat in the intestine
## canonical_key
bile.function.fat-emulsification
## aliases
bile salt emulsification of fat
amphipathic bile salts
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Bile salts are amphipathic steroid derivatives that emulsify dietary fat, dispersing large fat globules into a fine emulsion of small droplets. This greatly increases the surface area available to pancreatic lipase and, together with the products of digestion, forms mixed micelles that ferry fatty acids and monoacylglycerols to the enterocyte brush border. Emulsification, not enzyme catalysis, is their defining digestive role; pancreatic lipase itself is anchored and activated by colipase.
## explicit_objective
State that bile salts emulsify dietary fat to aid its digestion and absorption.
## pitfalls
Calling bile salts the direct activator of lipase (that is colipase) or an inhibitor of lipid absorption.

CON-GIT-C074B8AE72D015
## label
In a micelle, hydrophilic heads face the water and hydrophobic tails point inward
## canonical_key
micelle.orientation.hydrophilic-heads-outward
## aliases
micelle amphipathic orientation
fatty acid micelle structure
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Fatty acids and other amphipathic lipids in water spontaneously form micelles in which the polar hydrophilic heads (for a fatty acid, the carboxylic-acid groups) face the aqueous solvent, while the non-polar hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails are packed together toward the interior, away from water. This orientation minimises unfavourable contact between the hydrocarbon chains and water and is the basis for how mixed micelles solubilise the products of fat digestion.
## explicit_objective
Describe micelle orientation: hydrophilic heads outward to the solvent, hydrophobic tails inward.
## pitfalls
Reversing the orientation (hydrocarbon chains facing the water), which is energetically impossible in an aqueous micelle.
