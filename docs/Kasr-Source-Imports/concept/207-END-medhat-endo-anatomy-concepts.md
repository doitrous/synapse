## label
A pituitary tumour compresses the optic chiasma to cause bitemporal hemianopia
## canonical_key
pituitary-tumor.visual-field.bitemporal-hemianopia
## aliases
Bitemporal hemianopia in pituitary adenoma
Chiasmatic compression by a sellar mass
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The pituitary gland lies directly below the optic chiasma. An enlarging pituitary adenoma grows upward and presses on the decussating nasal retinal fibres in the chiasma. Because these fibres carry the temporal halves of each visual field, their compression produces a bitemporal (heteronymous) hemianopia. A lesion behind the chiasma would instead give a homonymous hemianopia, and cerebellar signs are unrelated to a sellar mass.
## explicit_objective
Explain why a pituitary tumour classically causes a bitemporal hemianopia.
## pitfalls
Confusing bitemporal (chiasmatic) with homonymous (retrochiasmatic) hemianopia.

## label
The anterior pituitary is supplied by the hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal circulation
## canonical_key
pituitary-gland.anterior-lobe.hypophyseal-portal-supply
## aliases
Hypophyseal portal system
Blood supply of the adenohypophysis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The superior hypophyseal arteries first break up into a capillary plexus in the median eminence and upper infundibulum. Blood from this plexus is collected into hypophyseal portal veins that descend the stalk and open into a second sinusoidal capillary bed in the anterior lobe. The adenohypophysis therefore receives most of its blood indirectly through this portal circulation, which also carries hypothalamic releasing hormones to it. The posterior lobe, by contrast, is supplied directly by the inferior hypophyseal artery.
## explicit_objective
Describe the hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal supply of the anterior pituitary.
## pitfalls
Assuming the superior hypophyseal artery supplies the anterior lobe directly rather than via portal veins.

The pituitary gland lies below the diaphragma sellae
## canonical_key
pituitary-gland.relation.below-diaphragma-sellae
## aliases

Relations of the pituitary gland
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The pituitary gland sits in the hypophyseal fossa of the body of the sphenoid and is roofed by the diaphragma sellae, a fold of dura pierced by the infundibulum. The gland therefore lies below the diaphragma sellae, with the hypothalamus above it, the sphenoid body (and sphenoid air sinus) below, and the cavernous sinus with the internal carotid artery on each side. Stating that the gland lies above the diaphragma sellae reverses this relation and is wrong.
## explicit_objective
State that the pituitary lies below the diaphragma sellae, with the sphenoid body below and the hypothalamus above.
## pitfalls
Reversing the gland and its dural roof, i.e. placing the gland above the diaphragma sellae.

The left suprarenal gland is semilunar (crescentic) in outline and is the larger, more medially placed of the two glands.
