## label
The vagus coordinates primary oesophageal peristalsis, whereas secondary peristalsis is a local reflex
## canonical_key
esophagus.peristalsis.vagus-primary-not-secondary
## aliases
primary versus secondary oesophageal peristalsis
vagal control of swallowing
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Primary oesophageal peristalsis is the continuation of the swallowing wave: it is centrally programmed in the swallowing centre and carried by the vagus, which directly innervates the striated muscle of the pharynx and upper oesophagus and coordinates the wave down the oesophagus. Secondary peristalsis is a local reflex triggered by distension when a bolus is left behind; it is organised largely by the enteric nervous system and can occur without vagal input. Consequently, complete loss of vagal output (for example after a brain-stem stroke) abolishes primary peristalsis while secondary peristalsis persists. Other functions such as gastrin release and pancreatic bicarbonate secretion also continue because they have hormonal or local drives.
## explicit_objective
State that the vagus coordinates primary oesophageal peristalsis, whereas secondary peristalsis is a local reflex that survives loss of vagal output.
## pitfalls
Assuming vagotomy abolishes secondary peristalsis (it does not) or that it eliminates gastrin release and pancreatic bicarbonate secretion.

# Item

CON-GIT-9F6FFFE753ED44
## label
Helicobacter pylori both damages the gastric mucosal barrier and stimulates acid secretion
## canonical_key
hpylori.gastricmucosa.barrier-damage-and-acid
## aliases
Helicobacter pylori barrier and acid
H. pylori urease ammonia
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Helicobacter pylori is a gram-negative bacterium with high urease activity that generates ammonia from urea; in the acid stomach the ammonia becomes ammonium, which damages the surface epithelial cells and the gastric mucosal barrier. Uniquely among the common ulcerogenic agents, H. pylori also increases gastric acid secretion, probably by increasing parietal-cell mass and by disturbing the somatostatin-gastrin balance. It therefore both weakens the barrier and raises the acid load, a combination that promotes ulceration. By contrast, bile salts damage the barrier without stimulating acid, while epidermal growth factor and gastrin strengthen rather than damage it.
## explicit_objective
State that Helicobacter pylori both damages the gastric mucosal barrier (via ammonia) and increases gastric acid secretion.
## pitfalls
Choosing bile salts (damage the barrier but do not stimulate acid) or gastrin/EGF (strengthen the barrier) instead of H. pylori.
