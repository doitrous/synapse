# Item
## id
CON-END-164082D53CE4FD
## label
The LH surge driven by high estrogen is an example of positive feedback
## canonical_key
hormone-feedback.positive.lh-surge-estrogen
## aliases
Positive feedback in endocrine control
Estrogen-triggered LH surge
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Most endocrine axes use negative feedback, where the target-gland hormone inhibits its own trophic hormone. Positive feedback is rare: the mid-cycle LH surge is the classic example, in which a sustained high level of estrogen stops inhibiting and instead stimulates gonadotropin release, driving a self-amplifying rise in LH that triggers ovulation. Circadian ACTH/cortisol secretion and the inhibition of TSH by T3/T4 are negative-feedback or rhythm phenomena, not positive feedback.
## explicit_objective
Identify the estrogen-driven LH surge as the classic example of endocrine positive feedback.
## pitfalls
Mistaking circadian rhythms or T3/T4 inhibition of TSH (negative feedback) for positive feedback.

## label
Ultra-short feedback is the control a releasing hormone exerts on its own secretion
## canonical_key
hormone-feedback.ultra-short-loop.releasing-hormone-self
## aliases
Ultra-short loop feedback
Autofeedback of releasing hormones
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Endocrine feedback loops are classified by length. Long-loop feedback is the action of a peripheral target-gland hormone back on the hypothalamus and pituitary; short-loop feedback is the action of a pituitary hormone back on the hypothalamus. Ultra-short-loop feedback is when a hypothalamic releasing hormone regulates its own secretion, i.e. the control done by the releasing hormones on themselves.
## explicit_objective
Define ultra-short-loop feedback as a releasing hormone acting on its own secretion.
## pitfalls
Confusing ultra-short (releasing hormone on itself) with short-loop (pituitary hormone on hypothalamus) or long-loop feedback.

Hypothalamic neurons synthesize the releasing and inhibiting hormones and store them in the median eminence until release into the hypophyseal portal system. ## explicit_objective
Recognize somatostatin as a hypothalamic hormone that is both synthesized and stored in the hypothalamus.
## pitfalls
Choosing ADH, which is made in the hypothalamus but stored in the posterior pituitary, not the hypothalamus.

## label
Prolactin causes anovulation and amenorrhea during lactation
## canonical_key
prolactin.action.lactational-anovulation-amenorrhea

Lactational amenorrhea
Prolactin and the ovary
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
High prolactin suppresses GnRH pulsatility, lowering LH and FSH and thus causing anovulation and amenorrhea; this underlies the natural lactational amenorrhea of breastfeeding.
