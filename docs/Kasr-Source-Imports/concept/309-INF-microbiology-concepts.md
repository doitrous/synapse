## label
Obligate aerobes require oxygen for growth
## canonical_key
bacteria.oxygen-requirement.obligate-aerobe-definition
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
An obligate (strict) aerobe is a micro-organism that can grow only in the presence of molecular oxygen, which it uses as the terminal electron acceptor of aerobic respiration; it cannot obtain energy by fermentation or anaerobic respiration.
## explicit_objective
Define an obligate aerobe as an organism that requires oxygen for growth, and distinguish it from facultative anaerobes, microaerophiles and aerotolerant anaerobes.
## pitfalls
Confusing the four oxygen classes: facultative anaerobes grow with OR without oxygen, microaerophiles need low oxygen tensions, and aerotolerant anaerobes tolerate but do not use oxygen; only the obligate aerobe strictly requires it.

Phenotypic variation is a temporary, non-heritable change in a bacterial character in response to the environment (for example loss of flagella on exposure to phenol), with no change in the genome; it reverses when the stimulus is removed, unlike genotypic variation.

Distinguish phenotypic (environmental, reversible, non-heritable) variation from genotypic variation caused by mutation or gene transfer.
## pitfalls
Mutation, transformation and transduction all change the genotype and are heritable; only an environment-driven, reversible change with an unchanged genome (e.g. flagellar loss in phenol) is a phenotypic variation.
