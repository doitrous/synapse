## label
Obligate aerobes require oxygen for growth
## canonical_key
bacteria.oxygen-requirement.obligate-aerobe-definition
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
An obligate (strict) aerobe is a micro-organism that can grow only in the presence of molecular oxygen, which it uses as the terminal electron acceptor of aerobic respiration; it cannot obtain energy by fermentation or anaerobic respiration.
## explicit_objective
Define an obligate aerobe as an organism that requires oxygen for growth, and distinguish it from facultative anaerobes, microaerophiles and aerotolerant anaerobes.
## pitfalls
Confusing the four oxygen classes: facultative anaerobes grow with OR without oxygen, microaerophiles need low oxygen tensions, and aerotolerant anaerobes tolerate but do not use oxygen; only the obligate aerobe strictly requires it.

## label
Generalized transduction occurs during the lytic cycle of a bacteriophage
## canonical_key
bacteria.gene-transfer.generalized-transduction-lytic-cycle
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Generalized transduction is bacteriophage-mediated transfer of bacterial DNA: during the lytic cycle a phage fragments the host chromosome and, by packaging error, encloses a random piece of host DNA in a phage head, which then delivers it to a new recipient cell.
## explicit_objective
State that generalized transduction is phage-mediated gene transfer occurring during the lytic cycle, and separate it from transformation and conjugation.
## pitfalls
Transduction is not transformation (uptake of naked DNA by a competent recipient) nor conjugation (DNA transfer through a sex pilus); and it produces a genotypic (heritable) change, not a transient phenotypic one.

The bacterial capsule is an anti-phagocytic virulence factor
## canonical_key
bacteria.virulence.capsule-is-antiphagocytic
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The polysaccharide (or polypeptide) capsule surrounding some bacteria is a major anti-phagocytic virulence factor: it masks surface structures and prevents opsonin/complement deposition, so phagocytes cannot readily engulf the organism unless specific anti-capsular antibody is present.
## explicit_objective
Identify the capsule as an anti-phagocytic virulence factor and contrast it with spreading/tissue enzymes and exotoxins.
## pitfalls
Collagenase, hyaluronidase and lecithinase are spreading/tissue-damaging enzymes and exotoxins act on host targets; none is primarily anti-phagocytic. The capsule is the classic anti-phagocytic structure.

## label
Phenotypic bacterial variation is non-heritable and reversible
## canonical_key
bacteria.variation.phenotypic-non-heritable-vs-genotypic
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Phenotypic variation is a temporary, non-heritable change in a bacterial character in response to the environment (for example loss of flagella on exposure to phenol), with no change in the genome; it reverses when the stimulus is removed, unlike genotypic variation.
## explicit_objective
Distinguish phenotypic (environmental, reversible, non-heritable) variation from genotypic variation caused by mutation or gene transfer.
## pitfalls
Mutation, transformation and transduction all change the genotype and are heritable; only an environment-driven, reversible change with an unchanged genome (e.g. flagellar loss in phenol) is a phenotypic variation.

## label
Bacteria adhere to host surfaces by means of pili (fimbriae)
## canonical_key
bacteria.adhesion.pili-mediate-attachment-to-host
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Pili (fimbriae) are short, hair-like surface appendages whose adhesin tips bind specific host-cell receptors, mediating attachment of bacteria to mucosal and other host surfaces - the essential first step of colonization.
## explicit_objective
Identify pili/fimbriae as the adhesion organelles that attach bacteria to host surfaces, distinct from flagella.
## pitfalls
Flagella provide motility, not adhesion; the cell wall and cytoplasmic membrane are structural. Attachment to host surfaces is the job of pili/fimbriae (common pili), while the sex pilus mediates conjugation.

In prokaryotes the mesosome is the functional equivalent of mitochondria
## canonical_key
bacteria.structure.mesosome-functional-equivalent-of-mitochondria
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Prokaryotes lack mitochondria; instead, mesosomes - invaginations of the cytoplasmic membrane - increase membrane surface area and are the site of the respiratory-chain enzymes, making them the functional counterpart of the mitochondrion for energy generation.
## explicit_objective
State that the mesosome is the prokaryotic functional equivalent of the mitochondrion.
## pitfalls
Ribosomes are for protein synthesis, endospores are dormant survival forms and inclusion granules are storage depots; none carries out respiration. The membrane-derived mesosome hosts the respiratory enzymes.
