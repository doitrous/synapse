# Item

CON-GIT-4C82FBBF96540B
## label
Motilin initiates the migrating motor complex in fasting, and erythromycin is a motilin-receptor agonist
## canonical_key
motilin.mmc.initiates-interdigestive-migrating-motor-complex
## aliases
motilin
erythromycin prokinetic
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Motilin is a gut peptide hormone released cyclically during the interdigestive (fasting) period that initiates the migrating motor complex, the sweeping contractions that clear residue from stomach to ileum between meals. It stimulates contraction both directly on smooth muscle and through excitatory enteric nerves. The macrolide antibiotic erythromycin acts as a motilin-receptor agonist, so it triggers strong gut contractions; nausea, cramping and diarrhoea are common consequences and the effect is exploited clinically as a prokinetic.
## explicit_objective
State that motilin initiates the migrating motor complex in fasting and that erythromycin is a motilin-receptor agonist.
## pitfalls
Confusing motilin (interdigestive MMC) with gastrin, CCK or secretin, which act during digestion; or forgetting erythromycin's prokinetic mechanism.

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-8746FE15C9B072
## label
Pancreatic lipase is the major lipolytic enzyme, and its deficiency causes steatorrhoea
## canonical_key
pancreas.fat-digestion.lipase-major-lipolytic-enzyme
## aliases
pancreatic lipase
steatorrhoea in pancreatitis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Fat digestion is completed in the proximal small intestine mainly by pancreatic lipase, the major lipolytic enzyme secreted by pancreatic acinar cells. Full activity needs the cofactor colipase, bile salts, fatty acids and an alkaline pH. When acinar secretion of lipase falls (for example in pancreatitis) triglycerides are not hydrolysed and pass into the stool as excess fat, producing steatorrhoea. Amylase and the proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin) digest carbohydrate and protein and do not account for fat malabsorption; colipase is only a cofactor.
## explicit_objective
Identify pancreatic lipase as the major lipolytic enzyme whose deficiency causes steatorrhoea.
## pitfalls
Attributing steatorrhoea to amylase or proteases, or treating colipase (a cofactor) as the primary determinant of fat digestion.

CON-GIT-221496F0500999
## label
Resting lower oesophageal sphincter tone is primarily myogenic, with neural and hormonal modulation
## canonical_key
esophagus.les.tone-primarily-myogenic
## aliases
lower oesophageal sphincter tone
LES pressure genesis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The lower oesophageal sphincter is a high-pressure zone between the oesophageal body and the gastric fundus that limits reflux of gastric contents. Its resting tone arises chiefly from the intrinsic (myogenic) properties of the sphincter's circular smooth muscle. Excitatory vagal input and hormones modulate this baseline, and inhibitory enteric nerves release nitric oxide to relax it during swallowing, but the principal source of the resting pressure is myogenic. Loss of tone (from alcohol, caffeine, chocolate, smoking or certain drugs and hormones) permits reflux and heartburn.
## explicit_objective
State that resting lower oesophageal sphincter tone is primarily myogenic, with neural and hormonal modulation.
## pitfalls
Attributing resting sphincter pressure mainly to vagal/parasympathetic input, circulating gastrin, or nitric oxide (which relaxes the sphincter).

## label
Zollinger-Ellison syndrome is a gastrinoma that secretes gastrin autonomously, driving acid hypersecretion
## canonical_key
gastrinoma.zollinger-ellison.gastrin-hypersecretion-acid
## aliases
gastrinoma
Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Zollinger-Ellison syndrome is caused by a gastrinoma, a tumour that secretes large amounts of gastrin free of the normal feedback by intestinal signals and gastric pH. The high circulating gastrin drives parietal-cell acid hypersecretion, giving the triad of refractory peptic ulcer disease, raised basal and maximal acid output, and an elevated serum gastrin. It may occur within multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1, and proton pump inhibitors are the treatment of choice. Somatostatin and secretin inhibit acid, and gastrin-releasing peptide and histamine are not the hormone measured to be elevated.
## explicit_objective
Explain that a gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome) secretes gastrin autonomously, driving acid hypersecretion.
## pitfalls
Choosing histamine (a local paracrine amplifier), gastrin-releasing peptide, secretin or somatostatin instead of gastrin as the elevated plasma hormone.

Gut bacteria synthesise folate, vitamin K and some B vitamins, but folic acid is absorbed by the host in significant quantity
## canonical_key
folate.absorption.bacterial-synthesis-host-absorbs
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Several vitamins, including folic acid, vitamin K and some of the B complex, are synthesised by intestinal bacteria. Of these, folic acid is the one the host absorbs from the colon in significant quantities; dietary intake of the others remains necessary because bacterial product is not efficiently absorbed. This distinguishes folate as both produced in and absorbed from the intestine, whereas vitamin K, though bacterially synthesised, depends substantially on dietary supply in this teaching framing.
## explicit_objective
State that gut bacteria synthesise folate, vitamin K and some B vitamins, but folic acid is absorbed by the host in significant quantity.
## pitfalls
Choosing vitamin K because it is bacterially synthesised, overlooking that the bank's discriminator is significant host absorption (folic acid).

CON-GIT-536B2FFD064DC4
## label
Cholecystokinin contracts the gallbladder and relaxes the sphincter of Oddi to deliver bile after a meal
## canonical_key
cck.gallbladder.contraction-and-sphincter-oddi-relaxation
## aliases
cholecystokinin gallbladder
sphincter of Oddi relaxation
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Cholecystokinin is released from the upper small intestine in response to dietary fat and partially digested protein. It contracts gallbladder smooth muscle both directly and through a vagovagal reflex, while inhibitory enteric nerves relax the sphincter of Oddi; together these deliver concentrated bile into the duodenum for fat digestion. Vagal cholinergic input also promotes gallbladder contraction and is blocked by atropine. A fat-rich meal is therefore the strongest stimulus to emptying, and gallbladder contraction is coupled with sphincter relaxation, not simultaneous contraction.
## explicit_objective
State that cholecystokinin contracts the gallbladder and relaxes the sphincter of Oddi to deliver bile after a meal.
## pitfalls
Thinking fat or duodenal amino acids inhibit the gallbladder, that atropine stimulates it, or that the sphincter of Oddi contracts together with the gallbladder.

## label
Villous atrophy in coeliac disease reduces the absorptive surface area, causing generalised malabsorption
## canonical_key
celiac.malabsorption.reduced-absorptive-surface-area
## aliases
gluten-sensitive enteropathy
coeliac villous atrophy
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Gluten-sensitive enteropathy (coeliac disease) causes autoimmune destruction of small-intestinal villi, sharply reducing the mucosal absorptive surface area. The surviving villous-tip cells also have impaired or absent membrane transporters, so absorption of nutrients, minerals, electrolytes and water all fall together. This produces bulky diarrhoea, weight loss, microcytic anaemia (iron), and low calcium and albumin. The defect is in the small-intestinal mucosa itself, not in motility, migrating motor complexes, the enterohepatic circulation, or gastric emptying.
## explicit_objective
Explain that villous atrophy in coeliac disease reduces the absorptive surface area, causing generalised malabsorption.
## pitfalls
Attributing coeliac malabsorption to altered motility, migrating motor complexes, bile recirculation, or gastric emptying instead of loss of absorptive surface.

## label
Lactase deficiency leaves lactose unabsorbed, causing osmotic diarrhoea and bacterial fermentation
## canonical_key
lactase.deficiency.osmotic-diarrhea
## aliases
lactose intolerance
lactase deficiency
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Lactase is a brush-border enzyme that hydrolyses lactose into glucose and galactose. When it is deficient, unabsorbed lactose remains in the lumen and osmotically draws water into the gut, causing diarrhoea and cramps; colonic bacteria ferment the sugar to fatty acids, carbon dioxide and hydrogen, producing bloating and an abnormal hydrogen breath test. The diarrhoea is osmotic, not secretory, and ceases when lactose is withdrawn. Only lactose is malabsorbed: general carbohydrate absorption, mucosal surface area and pancreatic secretion are normal.
## explicit_objective
Explain that lactase deficiency leaves lactose unabsorbed, causing osmotic diarrhoea and bacterial fermentation (bloating).
## pitfalls
Calling the diarrhoea secretory, blaming reduced surface area or pancreatic secretion, or generalising to all carbohydrate when only lactose is affected.
