# Item
## id
CON-GIT-2DD5354E7071AF
## label
SGLT1 is the insulin-independent sodium-glucose cotransporter of the intestinal and renal apical membrane
## canonical_key
sglt1.cotransporter.intestinal-renal-apical
## aliases
sodium-glucose cotransporter 1
SGLT1 secondary active glucose transport
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
SGLT1 is a secondary-active cotransporter located on the apical (luminal) membrane of small-intestinal enterocytes and renal proximal tubular cells. It carries glucose (and galactose) into the cell against their concentration gradient by coupling their uptake to the downhill entry of sodium; the favourable sodium gradient is maintained by the basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase. Transport by SGLT1 is insulin independent and does not occur in muscle or adipose tissue, where glucose entry uses the facilitative GLUT4 transporter instead.
## explicit_objective
Recognise SGLT1 as the insulin-independent, secondary-active sodium-glucose cotransporter of intestine and kidney that is absent from muscle and fat.
## pitfalls
Placing SGLT1 in muscle or adipose tissue, calling it insulin dependent, or thinking glucose moves down its gradient through it (it moves against its gradient, driven by the sodium gradient).

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-EE98FED5AED1BA
## label
Dietary fructose is absorbed by the facilitative transporter GLUT5
## canonical_key
fructose.absorption.glut5-facilitated
## aliases
GLUT5 fructose transporter
intestinal fructose absorption
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Fructose is absorbed across the apical membrane of enterocytes by GLUT5, a facilitative transporter with high specificity for fructose. Uptake is sodium independent and proceeds by facilitated diffusion down the fructose concentration gradient, so it needs neither ATP nor a sodium gradient. Fructose then exits the enterocyte across the basolateral membrane through GLUT2 into the portal blood. This distinguishes fructose absorption from the sodium-coupled, secondary-active uptake of glucose and galactose by SGLT1.
## explicit_objective
State that dietary fructose is absorbed by the facilitative, sodium-independent transporter GLUT5.
## pitfalls
Assigning fructose absorption to SGLT1 (sodium-coupled, for glucose/galactose) or to GLUT4 (insulin-responsive, in muscle and fat), or assuming fructose uptake needs energy.

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-40FAC96E9D1BD3
## label
Muscle glucose uptake is insulin-enhanced facilitated diffusion through GLUT4
## canonical_key
muscle.glucose-uptake.insulin-glut4
## aliases
GLUT4 insulin-responsive glucose transport
insulin-stimulated muscle glucose uptake
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Glucose enters skeletal muscle (and adipose tissue) by facilitated diffusion through GLUT4, moving down its concentration gradient without expending ATP. Insulin enhances this uptake by triggering translocation of GLUT4 transporters from intracellular storage vesicles to the plasma membrane, raising the number of transporters and hence the rate of glucose entry. The process is not active transport and is not coupled to sodium, contrasting with the sodium-dependent SGLT1 uptake of intestine and kidney.
## explicit_objective
Explain that muscle glucose uptake is insulin-enhanced facilitated diffusion via recruitment of GLUT4 to the membrane.
## pitfalls
Calling muscle glucose uptake active or energy dependent, or coupling it to sodium (that is SGLT1 in gut and kidney); forgetting that insulin acts by moving GLUT4 to the surface rather than by changing the gradient.
