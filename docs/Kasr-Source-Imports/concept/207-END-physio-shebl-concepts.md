## id
CON-END-AB812888FA4AB4
## label
Pro-opiomelanocortin is cleaved in pituitary corticotrophs to yield ACTH
## canonical_key
pro-opiomelanocortin.precursor.acth
## aliases
POMC is the parent peptide of ACTH, MSH and beta-endorphin
ACTH is derived from pro-opiomelanocortin
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) is a large precursor polypeptide synthesised by the corticotrophs of the anterior pituitary (and the pars intermedia). Enzymatic cleavage of POMC produces adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) along with melanocyte-stimulating hormone, beta-lipotropin and beta-endorphin. Cortisol, growth hormone, prolactin and TSH are not POMC products: cortisol is an adrenal steroid, while GH, prolactin and TSH are transcribed from their own genes.
## explicit_objective
Identify ACTH as the anterior-pituitary hormone derived from cleavage of pro-opiomelanocortin.
## pitfalls
Confusing the precursor with the target-gland steroid it stimulates: POMC yields ACTH, whereas cortisol is the downstream adrenal steroid, not a POMC cleavage product.

CON-END-7B2DA0D46E8EAB
## label
Growth hormone acts through the JAK2-STAT signalling pathway
## canonical_key
growth-hormone.signal-transduction.jak2-stat
## aliases
GH receptor is a cytokine-family receptor coupled to JAK2
Growth hormone signalling is mediated by Janus kinase 2 and STAT proteins
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The growth-hormone receptor is a single-membrane-pass receptor of the cytokine-receptor family that has no intrinsic enzymatic activity. Growth hormone binds and dimerises the receptor, activating the associated Janus kinase 2 (JAK2). JAK2 phosphorylates the receptor and STAT transcription factors, which then dimerise and move to the nucleus to change gene transcription. This distinguishes GH from lipophilic thyroid hormones (nuclear receptors) and from vasopressin and glucagon (G-protein-coupled, second-messenger) signalling.
## explicit_objective
State that growth hormone signals through JAK2 activation and STAT phosphorylation.
## pitfalls
Assuming every peptide hormone uses cyclic AMP; the GH receptor recruits JAK2-STAT rather than generating a diffusible second messenger.

Growth hormone is protein-anabolic by increasing amino-acid transport into cells
## canonical_key
growth-hormone.protein-anabolism.amino-acid-transport
## aliases
GH raises cellular amino-acid uptake and protein synthesis
Growth hormone produces positive nitrogen balance
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Growth hormone has a direct anabolic effect on protein metabolism: it increases the active transport of amino acids across cell membranes, enhances their incorporation into protein and reduces protein breakdown, giving a positive nitrogen balance. Its skeletal-growth effect on epiphyseal cartilage is largely indirect through hepatic somatomedins, while on carbohydrate and fat it is diabetogenic (decreases glucose utilisation) and lipolytic (mobilises fat), not lipogenic.
## explicit_objective
Describe growth hormone's protein-anabolic action via increased amino-acid transport into cells.
## pitfalls
Attributing GH's growth of cartilage to a direct action; the epiphyseal effect is mediated by somatomedins, whereas the amino-acid-transport/protein effect is a direct GH action.

## label
Somatomedins circulate bound to carrier proteins, giving a long half-life and slow tissue release
## canonical_key
somatomedins.plasma-transport.binding-proteins-long-half-life
## aliases
IGF-binding proteins prolong the action of somatomedins
Insulin-like growth factors are released slowly from plasma carrier proteins
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Somatomedins (insulin-like growth factors) are produced by the liver under growth-hormone drive and circulate almost entirely bound to specific IGF-binding proteins. This tight binding prolongs their plasma half-life and releases the active factor slowly to the tissues, so their growth-promoting action (stimulating collagen and chondroitin-sulphate synthesis and cartilage growth) is sustained rather than pulsatile. They also exert negative feedback that inhibits, not stimulates, growth-hormone secretion.
## explicit_objective
Explain that somatomedins circulate bound to carrier proteins, giving a long half-life and slow release to tissues.
## pitfalls
Thinking somatomedins act briefly like a rapidly cleared peptide; their binding proteins give a long half-life, and they inhibit rather than stimulate GH.

## id
CON-END-01389CD9E41784
## label
ADH concentrates urine by V2-receptor-driven insertion of aquaporin-2 channels
## canonical_key
adh.mechanism.aquaporin-2-insertion
## aliases
Vasopressin inserts aquaporin-2 water channels in the collecting duct
ADH raises collecting-duct water permeability through V2 receptors and cyclic AMP
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Antidiuretic hormone binds V2 receptors on the basolateral membrane of collecting-duct principal cells, activating a Gs / cyclic-AMP cascade. This causes preformed aquaporin-2 water channels to be inserted into the apical membrane, increasing water permeability so water is reabsorbed down the medullary osmotic gradient and a concentrated urine is formed. The vascular V1 receptor (phospholipase C, vasoconstriction) and the ACTH-potentiating V1b receptor are separate pathways; renal prostaglandin E2 opposes ADH rather than being produced by it.
## explicit_objective
Describe the V2-receptor / cyclic-AMP / aquaporin-2 mechanism of the antidiuretic action of ADH.
## pitfalls
Confusing the V2 renal water action with the V1 vascular action; only the V2 pathway inserts aquaporin-2 to reabsorb water.

## id
CON-END-0D30110930BD3D
## label
Cervical and vaginal stretch stimulates oxytocin release in the Ferguson reflex
## canonical_key
oxytocin.secretion-stimulus.cervical-dilation-ferguson-reflex
## aliases
Cervical dilation triggers oxytocin secretion during labour
Ferguson reflex is oxytocin positive feedback in parturition
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Stretch of the cervix and upper vagina during labour generates afferent neural signals that reach the hypothalamic magnocellular neurons and trigger oxytocin release from the posterior pituitary. The released oxytocin increases uterine contraction, which dilates the cervix further and drives yet more oxytocin release - a positive-feedback loop known as the Ferguson reflex. Suckling is the separate stimulus for the milk-ejection reflex; a rise in prolactin or in extracellular fluid volume is not a stimulus for oxytocin secretion.
## explicit_objective
Identify cervical/vaginal stretch as the stimulus for oxytocin release in the Ferguson positive-feedback reflex.
## pitfalls
Mistaking a downstream effect (milk ejection, uterine contraction) for the stimulus; the trigger is cervical/vaginal stretch, not the effect it produces.
