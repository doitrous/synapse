# Item
## id
CON-AND-5106D48BA0EABF
## label
The testis is covered by tunica vaginalis, tunica albuginea and tunica vasculosa
## canonical_key
testis.coverings.exclude-tunica-propria
## aliases
The testis has three coats: serous tunica vaginalis, fibrous tunica albuginea and vascular tunica vasculosa
Tunica propria is not a covering of the testis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The testis is enclosed by three coverings. The outermost is the tunica vaginalis, a serous sac derived from the processus vaginalis that covers the anterior and lateral surfaces. Deep to it is the tunica albuginea, a tough dense fibrous capsule that thickens posteriorly as the mediastinum testis and sends septa into the gland. The innermost is the tunica vasculosa, a loose vascular layer lining the albuginea and septa. The term "tunica propria" denotes the peritubular boundary tissue of the seminiferous tubule, not a coat of the whole organ, so it is not one of the testicular coverings.
## explicit_objective
List the three coverings of the testis and recognise that "tunica propria" is not among them.
## pitfalls
Counting "tunica propria" as a testicular coat; it is the peritubular tissue of the seminiferous tubule, not a covering of the organ.

# Item
## id
CON-AND-93A502EB184C54
## label
Leydig cells lie in the interstitium, not within the seminiferous tubule epithelium
## canonical_key
seminiferous-tubule.cell-content.excludes-leydig-cells
## aliases
The seminiferous tubule contains Sertoli cells, spermatogonia, spermatocytes and spermatids
Leydig cells are interstitial, between the seminiferous tubules
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The seminiferous tubule is lined by the germinal (spermatogenic) epithelium — spermatogonia on the basal lamina, then spermatocytes and spermatids toward the lumen — supported by the tall Sertoli cells that also form the blood-testis barrier. The interstitial (Leydig) cells are NOT part of this epithelium; they lie in the loose vascular connective tissue between the tubules, where they secrete testosterone under LH stimulation. So the tubular cell population is spermatogenic cells plus Sertoli cells, while Leydig cells are an extratubular, interstitial population.
## explicit_objective
State that Leydig cells are interstitial and are not part of the seminiferous tubule epithelium.
## pitfalls
Placing Leydig cells inside the seminiferous tubule; they are interstitial cells between the tubules.

# Item
## id
CON-AND-98D8EE26BD1C22
## label
Androgen-binding protein is secreted by Sertoli cells
## canonical_key
androgen-binding-protein.source.sertoli-cell
## aliases
Sertoli cells make ABP under FSH stimulation
ABP concentrates testosterone inside the seminiferous tubule
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Androgen-binding protein (ABP) is a glycoprotein secreted by the Sertoli cell under follicle-stimulating-hormone stimulation. By binding testosterone (supplied by the neighbouring Leydig cells), ABP maintains a high local androgen concentration within the seminiferous tubule and carries testosterone into the tubular lumen and along the excurrent ducts. This sustained intratubular androgen level is required for the progression of spermatogenesis, which is why ABP is a Sertoli-cell product rather than a Leydig-cell or germ-cell product.
## explicit_objective
Identify androgen-binding protein as a Sertoli-cell secretion that concentrates testosterone in the seminiferous tubule.
## pitfalls
Attributing ABP to Leydig cells; Leydig cells make testosterone, whereas ABP is a Sertoli-cell secretion that binds it.
