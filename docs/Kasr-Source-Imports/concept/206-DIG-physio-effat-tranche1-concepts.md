# Item
## id
CON-GIT-A8144604EB8CA2
## label
The duodenocolic (gastrocolic) reflex produces the post-prandial urge to defecate
## canonical_key
reflex.duodenocolic.defecation-urge
## aliases
gastrocolic reflex
duodenocolic reflex
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
When the stomach and duodenum are distended by a meal, afferent signals evoke the gastrocolic and duodenocolic reflexes, which increase the motility of the colon and drive mass movements in the descending and sigmoid colon. These mass movements propel colonic contents into the rectum; rectal distension then initiates the defecation reflex, giving the characteristic urge to defecate soon after eating. The reflex is mediated partly by the autonomic nervous system and partly by gastrointestinal hormones (e.g. gastrin, cholecystokinin). It is distinct from the enterogastric reflex (duodenum inhibiting the stomach) and the intestino-intestinal reflex (protective bowel-wide relaxation on over-distension).
## explicit_objective
Identify the duodenocolic (gastrocolic) reflex as the cause of the post-prandial urge to defecate.
## pitfalls
Confusing it with the enterogastric reflex (opposite direction, slows gastric emptying) or the rectosphincteric reflex (triggered by rectal, not duodenal, distension).

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-2C9513DBEB4208
## label
Vitamin B12 is absorbed in the terminal ileum bound to intrinsic factor
## canonical_key
absorption.vitamin-b12.terminal-ileum
## aliases
cyanocobalamin absorption site
B12 terminal ileum intrinsic factor
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Dietary vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin) is bound in the stomach to intrinsic factor, a glycoprotein secreted by gastric parietal cells. The intrinsic-factor-B12 complex travels to the terminal ileum, where it attaches to specific brush-border receptors (cubam) and is absorbed almost exclusively at that site. Because absorption depends on both intrinsic factor and an intact terminal ileum, either loss of parietal cells (pernicious anaemia, gastrectomy) or resection/disease of the terminal ileum (Crohn disease, surgery) causes vitamin B12 deficiency and, over time, megaloblastic anaemia. Other nutrients are absorbed more proximally, so ileal loss selectively threatens B12 (and bile-salt) uptake.
## explicit_objective
State that vitamin B12 is absorbed in the terminal ileum bound to intrinsic factor, so ileal resection causes B12 deficiency.
## pitfalls
Assuming B12 is absorbed in the duodenum or jejunum like most nutrients, or forgetting that intrinsic factor is required for its ileal uptake.

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-71487BE84D9403
## label
GIP is the gastrointestinal hormone released by all three macronutrients
## canonical_key
hormone.gip.released-by-all-macronutrients
## aliases
glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide foodstuffs
gastric inhibitory peptide release
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP, historically gastric inhibitory peptide) is released from K cells of the duodenal and proximal jejunal mucosa in response to all three major foodstuffs — fat, carbohydrate and protein. This broad responsiveness makes GIP the incretin that links essentially any meal to insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells. It contrasts with the other gastrointestinal hormones, each of which responds to only some nutrients: cholecystokinin to fat and protein, gastrin mainly to protein, and motilin largely between meals. GIP therefore stands out as the single hormone released by fat, carbohydrate and protein together.
## explicit_objective
Recall that GIP is the gastrointestinal hormone released by all three macronutrients (fat, carbohydrate and protein).
## pitfalls
Choosing CCK (fat and protein only) or gastrin (protein only); assuming that because GIP is named "gastric inhibitory peptide" it is not released by nutrients.
