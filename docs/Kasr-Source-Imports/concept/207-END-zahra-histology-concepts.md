The supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei secrete oxytocin and vasopressin, not endorphin
## canonical_key
hypothalamic-magnocellular-nuclei.secretion.oxytocin-vasopressin-not-endorphin
## aliases
Magnocellular hypothalamic neurons make the posterior-pituitary hormones
Posterior pituitary hormones are synthesised in the hypothalamus
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The magnocellular neurons of the supraoptic and paraventricular hypothalamic nuclei synthesise the two posterior-pituitary hormones, oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin, ADH). Their axons descend as the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract and release these hormones from dilated terminals (Herring bodies) in the pars nervosa. Beta-endorphin is an opioid peptide cleaved from pro-opiomelanocortin in anterior-pituitary corticotrophs and arcuate neurons, so it is not a product of the magnocellular nuclei.
## explicit_objective
List the hormones produced by the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei and identify endorphin as the odd one out.
## pitfalls
Assuming every hypothalamic peptide is made by the magnocellular nuclei; endorphin belongs to the POMC/corticotroph pathway.

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The adrenal medulla is a modified sympathetic ganglion. Its principal cells are the chromaffin cells, modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons that store and secrete catecholamines and give the chromaffin reaction; scattered among them are true sympathetic ganglion cells. Both populations are present, so the medulla is made of chromaffin cells and sympathetic ganglion cells, not glial or steroid-secreting cells.
## explicit_objective
Identify the two neural cell populations of the adrenal medulla.
## pitfalls
Assuming the medulla contains only chromaffin cells; sympathetic ganglion cells are also present.

## definition
Mineralocorticoids, chiefly aldosterone, are steroid hormones secreted by the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal cortex. Their secretion is controlled mainly by the renin-angiotensin system: angiotensin II stimulates the zona glomerulosa to release aldosterone, with a rise in plasma potassium as a second stimulus. They are steroid, not protein, in nature, and are not the ACTH-dependent products of the deeper cortical zones.
## explicit_objective
State that angiotensin II is the main regulator of mineralocorticoid secretion.
## pitfalls
Attributing aldosterone control to ACTH or placing it in the zona fasciculata; it is angiotensin-II-driven from the glomerulosa.
