## label
The supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei secrete oxytocin and vasopressin, not endorphin
## canonical_key
hypothalamic-magnocellular-nuclei.secretion.oxytocin-vasopressin-not-endorphin
## aliases
Magnocellular hypothalamic neurons make the posterior-pituitary hormones
Posterior pituitary hormones are synthesised in the hypothalamus
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The magnocellular neurons of the supraoptic and paraventricular hypothalamic nuclei synthesise the two posterior-pituitary hormones, oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin, ADH). Their axons descend as the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract and release these hormones from dilated terminals (Herring bodies) in the pars nervosa. Beta-endorphin is an opioid peptide cleaved from pro-opiomelanocortin in anterior-pituitary corticotrophs and arcuate neurons, so it is not a product of the magnocellular nuclei.
## explicit_objective
List the hormones produced by the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei and identify endorphin as the odd one out.
## pitfalls
Assuming every hypothalamic peptide is made by the magnocellular nuclei; endorphin belongs to the POMC/corticotroph pathway.

## id
CON-END-24F545A2BC1416
## label
Pituicytes of the pars nervosa are glial (astrocyte-like) supporting cells
## canonical_key
pars-nervosa.pituicyte.glial-cell
## aliases
Pituicytes are the glial cells of the posterior pituitary
Pituicytes do not secrete hormone
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The only resident cell of the pars nervosa is the pituicyte, a branched glial cell related to astrocytes. Pituicytes give structural and metabolic support to the unmyelinated hypothalamic axons that store and release vasopressin and oxytocin; they do not themselves synthesise these hormones. They are confined to the pars nervosa and are not found in the pars distalis.
## explicit_objective
Identify pituicytes as glial support cells of the pars nervosa.
## pitfalls
Believing pituicytes secrete vasopressin; the hormones come from hypothalamic neurons, pituicytes only support their axons.

## canonical_key
pars-nervosa.composition.no-neuronal-cell-bodies

The posterior pituitary has no neuron cell bodies

## definition
The pars nervosa (posterior pituitary) is neural tissue composed of the unmyelinated axons of hypothalamic magnocellular neurons, their dilated storage terminals called Herring bodies, glial pituicytes and fenestrated capillaries. The neuronal cell bodies themselves lie in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus, so the pars nervosa itself contains no nerve cell bodies.
## explicit_objective
State that the pars nervosa holds axons, Herring bodies and pituicytes but not neuron cell bodies.
## pitfalls
Confusing the storage terminals (Herring bodies) with neuron cell bodies, which are actually in the hypothalamus.

Recognise orange G as a selective stain for pars distalis acidophils.

The pars intermedia, the thin zone between the pars distalis and the pars nervosa, contains basophilic cells that cleave pro-opiomelanocortin to melanocyte-stimulating hormone (MSH).
