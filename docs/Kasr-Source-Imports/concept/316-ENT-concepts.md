## label
Traumatic tympanic membrane perforation is managed by keeping the ear dry for spontaneous healing
## canonical_key
otolaryngology.traumatic-tympanic-membrane-perforation.management-keep-dry
## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A traumatic tympanic membrane perforation usually heals spontaneously within weeks; management is to keep the ear dry, avoid ear drops and syringing, and reserve myringoplasty for a perforation that fails to heal.
## explicit_objective
State that a traumatic tympanic membrane perforation is managed conservatively by keeping the ear dry until spontaneous healing.
## pitfalls
Syringing or ear washing forces water and organisms into the middle ear and can convert a clean traumatic perforation into suppurative otitis media; immediate myringoplasty is not indicated.

Cerebrospinal fluid otorrhoea is a clear, watery discharge from the ear that increases on straining, coughing or bending because these raise CSF pressure; it follows a breach of dura and temporal bone, usually after trauma or surgery.

## definition
Meniere’s disease, due to endolymphatic hydrops, presents with recurrent episodic vertigo, fluctuating sensorineural hearing loss and tinnitus (often with aural fullness); attacks last minutes to hours and recur over years.
## explicit_objective
Recognise the vertigo–SNHL–tinnitus triad of Meniere’s disease.
## pitfalls
Vestibular neuronitis gives vertigo with normal hearing, and an acoustic neuroma gives steady progressive unilateral loss rather than the episodic attacks of Meniere’s disease.

## canonical_key

## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Labyrinthitis inflames the whole membranous labyrinth, so it causes vertigo together with sensorineural hearing loss, and this combination localises the lesion to the labyrinth rather than to the vestibular nerve alone.
## explicit_objective
Recognise that vertigo with sensorineural hearing loss indicates labyrinthitis.
## pitfalls
Vestibular neuritis gives vertigo with normal hearing, cerebellar lesions give ataxia without hearing loss, and benign paroxysmal positional vertigo gives brief positional vertigo without hearing loss.
