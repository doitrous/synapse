# Item
## id
CON-GIT-BD6F8A06B226B6
## label
The inferior epigastric artery runs vertically in the extraperitoneal connective tissue just medial to the deep inguinal ring and is the vessel ligated to reduce a hernia at this site
## canonical_key
inferiorepigastricartery.relations.hernia-reduction-landmark
## aliases
Inferior epigastric artery course
Hernia reduction landmark artery
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The inferior epigastric artery arises from the external iliac artery just above the inguinal ligament and runs superomedially in the extraperitoneal fat toward the rectus sheath, raising the lateral umbilical fold of peritoneum. Its position just medial to the deep inguinal ring (and lateral to the site of a direct inguinal hernia) makes it the key landmark separating indirect hernias (lateral to the artery) from direct hernias (medial to it), and it is the vessel a surgeon must identify and preserve or ligate when operating in this region, including during hernia reduction.
## explicit_objective
Identify the inferior epigastric artery as the vessel running vertically in the extraperitoneal tissue medial to the bowel/deep ring at hernia surgery, distinguishing it from the deep and superficial circumflex iliac arteries (which run laterally, toward the anterior superior iliac spine) and the superficial epigastric artery (a subcutaneous, not extraperitoneal, vessel).
## pitfalls
Confusing the inferior epigastric artery with the superficial epigastric artery — the superficial vessel runs in the subcutaneous fat of the abdominal wall, not in the extraperitoneal connective tissue deep to it, and plays no role in the indirect/direct hernia landmark distinction.

## id
CON-GIT-48E8D82563B8E3
## label
The right kidney normally lies at a lower level than the left kidney, displaced inferiorly by the liver
## canonical_key
kidney.position.right-lower-than-left
## aliases
Kidney vertical position
Right versus left kidney level
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Because the right lobe of the liver occupies space above it, the right kidney sits roughly one vertebral level lower than the left kidney. The left kidney's hilum, unlike the right, is separated from the peritoneum of the lesser sac (not by the duodenum, which relates instead to the right kidney's anterior surface and hilum region). At the renal hilum, the standard antero-posterior order of structures is renal vein (most anterior), renal artery, then renal pelvis/ureter (most posterior) — so the renal vein lies anteriorly, not the renal artery. The renal (perinephric) fascia is firmly adherent and cannot be easily stripped from the kidney; it is the internal fibrous renal capsule that strips off cleanly in health.
## explicit_objective
State that the right kidney lies lower than the left (due to the liver), and correct the three paired distractors: which kidney's hilum relates to the duodenum (right, not left), which structure is anterior at the hilum (vein, not artery), and which fascial layer strips easily (the capsule, not the perinephric fascia).
## pitfalls
Assuming the two kidneys sit at the same vertebral level, or reversing the duodenum-hilum relation (it is the right kidney, via the second part of the duodenum) and the vein/artery antero-posterior order at the hilum (vein is anterior, artery is middle, pelvis/ureter is posterior).

CON-GIT-42AF0282F5C42E
## label
The ureter passes deep to the gonadal vessels as it descends on the posterior abdominal wall
## canonical_key
ureter.relations.crossed-by-gonadal-vessels
## aliases
Ureter and gonadal vessels crossing
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
As the ureter runs down the posterior abdominal wall on the medial edge of psoas major, it is crossed anteriorly (i.e. it passes deep/posterior to) the gonadal vessels (testicular or ovarian). This is distinct from its relation to the genitofemoral nerve, which lies on the surface of psoas major alongside the ureter without crossing deep to it, and from the common iliac artery, which the ureter crosses anteriorly at the pelvic brim (the ureter is superficial to, not deep to, the common iliac artery at that point).
## explicit_objective
State that the ureter passes deep to the gonadal vessels, and distinguish this crossing from its relations to the genitofemoral nerve (runs alongside, not crossed) and the common iliac artery (the ureter crosses anterior to it, not deep to it).
## pitfalls
Reversing which vessel is superficial at the ureter's crossings — the gonadal vessels cross anterior to (superficial to) the ureter in the abdomen, while at the pelvic brim it is the ureter itself that lies anterior to (superficial to) the common iliac vessels.

## label
The aortic hiatus of the diaphragm transmits the aorta, azygos vein and thoracic duct
## canonical_key
aortichiatus.contents.aorta-azygos-thoracicduct
## aliases
Aortic hiatus contents
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The aortic hiatus, at the level of T12 behind the median arcuate ligament, is the most posterior and lowest of the three great diaphragmatic openings and is not surrounded by muscle fibres (unlike the oesophageal and caval openings), which is why it does not dilate with diaphragmatic contraction. It transmits the aorta, the azygos vein and the thoracic duct. An aneurysm here therefore threatens the azygos vein and thoracic duct, not the vagus nerve or oesophagus, which pass instead through the oesophageal hiatus at T10.
## explicit_objective
Name the aortic hiatus's three contents (aorta, azygos vein, thoracic duct) and distinguish them from the oesophageal hiatus's contents (oesophagus, vagal trunks), which an aortic-hiatus aneurysm cannot compress.
## pitfalls
Assigning the vagus nerve or oesophagus to the aortic hiatus — those structures pass through the separate oesophageal hiatus at T10, two vertebral levels above the aortic hiatus at T12.

## label
The third (horizontal) part of the duodenum lies directly anterior to the inferior vena cava, so a tumour anterior to the IVC most likely compresses it
## canonical_key
inferiorvenacava.relations.duodenum-third-part-anterior
## aliases
IVC anterior relations
Tumour anterior to IVC
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The inferior vena cava ascends retroperitoneally just to the right of the aorta. Structures lying directly anterior to it in the upper retroperitoneum include the third (horizontal) part of the duodenum (crossing it, as it also crosses the aorta) and, more superiorly, the head of the pancreas and the epiploic foramen's posterior boundary. A mass anterior to the IVC in this region is therefore best placed as compressing the third part of the duodenum, rather than the right sympathetic trunk (which lies posterior/lateral to the IVC, not anterior to it), a lumbar artery (a posterior branch of the aorta, on the opposite side of the great vessels from an anterior IVC mass), or the left renal artery (which crosses behind, not in front of, the IVC on its way to the left kidney).
## explicit_objective
Identify the third part of the duodenum as the structure directly anterior to the IVC most likely compressed by an anteriorly sited mass, distinguishing it from structures that lie posterior to the IVC (right sympathetic trunk, lumbar arteries) or cross behind it (left renal artery).
## pitfalls
Assuming any nearby retroperitoneal structure could be compressed regardless of its anteroposterior relation to the IVC — only structures that lie anterior to the IVC (like the third part of the duodenum) are at risk from an anteriorly sited mass; posterior structures are shielded by the vessel itself.

## label
The suprarenal glands receive direct arterial branches from the aorta, the inferior phrenic arteries and the renal arteries
## canonical_key
suprarenalgland.bloodsupply.aorta-inferiorphrenic-renal
## aliases
Suprarenal gland blood supply
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Each suprarenal (adrenal) gland has a triple direct arterial supply: the superior suprarenal artery from the inferior phrenic artery, the middle suprarenal artery arising directly from the abdominal aorta, and the inferior suprarenal artery from the renal artery. Rapid occlusion of all three sources (aorta, inferior phrenic and renal arteries) produces suprarenal ischaemia; the splenic, superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric arteries play no direct role in suprarenal perfusion.
## explicit_objective
Name the three direct arterial sources of the suprarenal gland (aorta, inferior phrenic artery, renal artery) and exclude the splenic and mesenteric arteries, which do not supply it directly.
## pitfalls
Substituting the splenic artery or either mesenteric artery for one of the three genuine suprarenal sources — none of those vessels give a direct suprarenal branch, even though they lie in the same general retroperitoneal region.

## label
The superior and middle rectal veins form a portal-caval anastomosis at the anorectal junction
## canonical_key
portocavalanastomosis.pairs.superior-middle-rectal-veins
## aliases
Anorectal portocaval anastomosis
Rectal venous anastomosis
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
At the anorectal junction, the superior rectal vein (portal system, draining via the inferior mesenteric vein) anastomoses with the middle and inferior rectal veins (systemic system, draining via the internal iliac and internal pudendal veins), forming one of the four classic portosystemic anastomoses. This is distinct from other venous pairs that do not form portosystemic links: the hepatic veins and IVC are both entirely systemic; the left and right gastric veins are both entirely portal; and the inferior and superficial epigastric veins are both entirely systemic.
## explicit_objective
Identify the superior and middle rectal veins as a genuine portal-systemic pair, and reject the three distractor pairs, each of which links two veins from the same (not opposite) venous system.
## pitfalls
Assuming any two named abdominal or pelvic veins that happen to be near each other form a portosystemic anastomosis — a true anastomosis specifically links one portal-system vein with one systemic-system vein; pairing two veins from the same system is never correct.

## label
A duplicated (double) ureter results from premature splitting of the ureteric bud before it reaches the metanephric blastema
## canonical_key
uretericbud.duplication.double-ureter
## aliases
Double ureter embryology
Ureteric bud duplication
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The ureteric bud, an outgrowth of the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct, induces the metanephric blastema (metanephros) to form the definitive kidney, while itself giving rise to the ureter, renal pelvis, calyces and collecting ducts. If the ureteric bud splits prematurely (bifid or duplicated bud) before or while penetrating the metanephric blastema, a double (duplex) ureter results, sometimes with two separate renal pelves draining one kidney. This is distinct from failure of the paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct, which produces female genital tract anomalies, and from primary metanephros or mesonephric duct failure, which produce renal agenesis or other anomalies rather than ureteric duplication specifically.
## explicit_objective
Identify premature splitting of the ureteric bud as the embryological basis of a duplicated ureter, distinguishing it from mesonephric duct, paramesonephric duct and metanephros anomalies, which produce different malformations.
## pitfalls
Attributing ureteric duplication to the paramesonephric duct (a female genital tract structure, unrelated to ureter formation) or to the metanephros itself (the induced tissue, not the branching structure that becomes the ureter).
