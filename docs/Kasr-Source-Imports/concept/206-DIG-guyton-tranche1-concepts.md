# Item
## id
CON-GIT-0FFFF9747E1622
## label
Basal saliva has bicarbonate as its highest-concentration ion and potassium as its most enriched relative to plasma
## canonical_key
saliva.ionic-composition.high-potassium-bicarbonate-low-sodium-chloride
## aliases
salivary ionic composition
saliva potassium bicarbonate
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Salivary acini secrete a primary fluid resembling plasma, but as it passes along the ducts sodium is actively reabsorbed and potassium and bicarbonate are actively secreted. The result under basal conditions is a hypotonic saliva rich in potassium and bicarbonate and low in sodium and chloride. By absolute concentration bicarbonate is highest (about 50 to 70 mEq/L versus potassium about 30 mEq/L), whereas relative to plasma potassium is the most enriched (about sevenfold, compared with bicarbonate about threefold). Sodium and chloride fall to roughly 10 to 15 percent of their plasma values.
## explicit_objective
State that basal saliva is rich in potassium and bicarbonate and low in sodium and chloride, with bicarbonate highest by absolute concentration and potassium most enriched relative to plasma.
## pitfalls
Confusing highest absolute concentration (bicarbonate) with highest concentration relative to plasma (potassium), or expecting high salivary sodium and chloride.

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-735ED99B44A69F
## label
Gastric acid is needed to form and activate pepsin, so achlorhydria most impairs protein digestion
## canonical_key
achlorhydria.protein-digestion.pepsin-needs-acid
## aliases
achlorhydria protein digestion
pepsin requires acid
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Achlorhydria is failure of the stomach to secrete hydrochloric acid, diagnosed when gastric pH does not fall below 4 after stimulation. Acid is needed both to convert pepsinogen to pepsin and to provide the acidic medium pepsin requires to work. Without acid, gastric protein digestion by pepsin is lost, so protein digestion is the most impaired. Carbohydrate, fat and nucleic acid digestion depend on salivary, pancreatic and brush-border enzymes and are largely spared.
## explicit_objective
Explain that gastric acid is needed to form and activate pepsin, so achlorhydria most impairs protein digestion.
## pitfalls
Assuming achlorhydria mainly impairs carbohydrate or fat digestion, which rely on acid-independent enzymes.

# Item

CON-GIT-0791E91A5EA804
## label
Pepsinogen is secreted by gastric chief (peptic) cells and activated to pepsin by acid
## canonical_key
stomach.pepsinogen.secreted-by-chief-cells-activated-by-acid
## aliases
pepsinogen chief cells
peptic cell pepsinogen
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Pepsinogen, the inactive precursor of pepsin, is secreted mainly by the peptic (chief) cells of the gastric (oxyntic) glands. Contact with hydrochloric acid, or with pepsin already formed, cleaves pepsinogen to active pepsin. Pepsin is a proteolytic enzyme that begins protein digestion in the stomach and can digest collagen and other connective tissue in meat. It is a product of the stomach's gastric glands, not of the pancreas or duodenum.
## explicit_objective
State that pepsinogen is secreted by gastric chief (peptic) cells and activated to pepsin by acid.
## pitfalls
Attributing pepsinogen to pancreatic acinar or ductal cells or to duodenal epithelium rather than gastric chief cells.

# Item
## id
CON-GIT-51DC789A5CB834
## label
CCK, released by fat and protein, is the only hormone that physiologically inhibits gastric emptying by relaxing the orad stomach
## canonical_key
cck.gastric-emptying.only-hormone-inhibiting-emptying
## aliases
CCK gastric emptying
cholecystokinin orad relaxation
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Cholecystokinin, released from the small intestine by dietary fat and protein, is the only gastrointestinal hormone that inhibits gastric emptying under normal physiological conditions. It does so by relaxing the orad stomach, increasing its compliance so the stomach can hold more without a rise in luminal pressure, which slows delivery of chyme to the duodenum. This keeps the stomach full longer, so fatty, protein-rich meals feel more satisfying, and CCK additionally acts on brain feeding centres to reduce further eating. Other hormones slow emptying only at pharmacological doses.
## explicit_objective
State that CCK, released by fat and protein, is the only hormone that physiologically inhibits gastric emptying by relaxing the orad stomach.
## pitfalls
Choosing GIP, gastrin, motilin or secretin as the physiological inhibitor of gastric emptying instead of CCK.

## id
CON-GIT-ACADA7F1C2C1A8
## label
The palatopharyngeal folds form a slit that selects adequately chewed boluses during the pharyngeal phase of swallowing
## canonical_key
swallowing.pharyngeal-phase.palatopharyngeal-folds-select-bolus-size
## aliases
pharyngeal phase of swallowing
palatopharyngeal folds bolus selection
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
During the pharyngeal phase of swallowing, coordinated by the brain-stem swallowing centre, the palatopharyngeal folds on each side of the pharynx are pulled medially to form a sagittal slit. This slit acts as a selective gate, admitting food that has been chewed small enough while impeding larger objects. At the same time the soft palate rises to close the posterior nares, the vocal cords approximate and the epiglottis covers the larynx to protect the airway, and the upper oesophageal sphincter relaxes to admit the bolus. The palatopharyngeal folds are specifically the structure that judges bolus size.
## explicit_objective
State that the palatopharyngeal folds form a slit that selects adequately chewed boluses during the pharyngeal phase of swallowing.
## pitfalls
Crediting the epiglottis, larynx, soft palate or upper oesophageal sphincter (airway protection or passage) with selecting bolus size.

## id
CON-GIT-1DA7616AE63593
## label
Cystic fibrosis is a defect of CFTR chloride transport, reducing exocrine fluid secretion and thickening mucus
## canonical_key
cystic-fibrosis.cftr.chloride-channel-secretion
## aliases
cystic fibrosis CFTR
chloride channel exocrine secretion
## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Cystic fibrosis is caused by defective chloride transport across the apical membrane of exocrine epithelial cells. The CFTR protein is a cyclic-AMP-regulated chloride channel that also regulates other ion channels; moving chloride out of the cell draws water and drives exocrine fluid secretion. When CFTR is absent or nonfunctional, chloride and therefore water secretion fall, so mucus in the pancreas, lungs and liver becomes thick and sticky and blocks the ducts. The primary disruption is thus in chloride, not sodium or calcium, transport.
## explicit_objective
State that cystic fibrosis is a defect of CFTR chloride transport, reducing exocrine fluid secretion and thickening mucus.
## pitfalls
Naming sodium, calcium, potassium or phosphate as the primary transport defect instead of chloride (CFTR).
