Hypothalamo-pituitary hormone signalling and transport: GnRH, growth hormone and ADH

## summary
Hormones of the hypothalamo-pituitary axis differ in how they signal and how they travel. GnRH is a peptide that works through a Gq-coupled receptor, activating phospholipase C to make IP3 and diacylglycerol and to release intracellular calcium, so it does not use cyclic AMP or a nuclear receptor. Growth hormone belongs to the same structural family as prolactin and human placental lactogen, which gives it intrinsic lactogenic activity in addition to its somatotropic effects. Antidiuretic hormone is made in hypothalamic magnocellular neurons together with its own carrier peptide, neurophysin II, and circulates bound to it, whereas oxytocin pairs with neurophysin I. Together these points cover the receptor class, hormone-family homology and plasma-carrier facts most easily confused on this material.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups three discriminating facts about hypothalamo-pituitary hormones: the IP3-calcium signalling of GnRH, the intrinsic lactogenic activity of growth hormone, and the transport of ADH on neurophysin II.
### Mechanism
GnRH binds a Gq-coupled receptor; phospholipase C splits PIP2 into IP3 and DAG, IP3 releases stored calcium and PKC is activated, driving LH/FSH release. Growth hormone binds a cytokine-family receptor and, through its homology with prolactin, can also engage the prolactin receptor, giving intrinsic lactogenic activity. ADH is cleaved from a precursor that yields neurophysin II, is stored in the posterior pituitary and released bound to that carrier; oxytocin is packaged with neurophysin I.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: the Gq-IP3-calcium arm of GPCR signalling versus the Gs-cAMP arm and versus nuclear or tyrosine-kinase receptors; hormone-family homology (GH/prolactin/hPL) as the basis of lactogenic overlap; and the specific pairing of ADH with neurophysin II versus oxytocin with neurophysin I.
### Clinical significance
These points underlie the pharmacology of GnRH analogues (pulsatile versus continuous dosing of gonadotroph GPCRs), the galactorrhoea that can accompany very high growth-hormone states, and the interpretation of neurophysin/ADH co-secretion in disorders of water balance.

See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors here are built from those reversals (GnRH raising cyclic AMP, lactogenesis being prolactin-exclusive, ADH riding on albumin or swapping neurophysins with oxytocin).
