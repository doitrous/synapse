## title
Histology of the lactating mammary gland: secretion, immunoglobulin and duct system
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The mammary gland is a compound tubulo-alveolar gland of 15-20 lobes, each drained by a lactiferous duct that opens on the nipple and widens into a lactiferous sinus. In lactation the alveolar cells secrete milk by two modes at once: the protein fraction leaves by merocrine exocytosis and the fat by the apocrine mode, so it is neither purely merocrine nor holocrine. The dominant immunoglobulin of colostrum and milk is secretory IgA, synthesised by plasma cells in the connective tissue between the alveoli and transcytosed into the milk by the alveolar cells, giving the newborn passive gut immunity.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the fresh mammary histology facts tested by the Dr Kandeel female set: the combined merocrine-plus-apocrine mode of milk secretion, the identity of the milk immunoglobulin (secretory IgA), its source (interstitial plasma cells), and the lobar drainage by lactiferous ducts.
### Mechanism
Prolactin-driven alveolar cells package casein into merocrine vesicles and bud off fat by the apocrine mode. Interstitial plasma cells make dimeric IgA that the alveolar cells transcytose into the lumen with a secretory component. Milk drains through intralobular and interlobular ducts to each lobe's lactiferous duct and sinus.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: secretion is merocrine (protein) + apocrine (fat), not holocrine; milk immunoglobulin = IgA; IgA made by interstitial plasma cells, not by the alveolar cells; each lobe drained by a lactiferous duct.
### Clinical significance
Secretory IgA in colostrum and milk is a key route of passive immunity for the newborn, and the lactiferous-duct/sinus system is the anatomical basis of milk storage and of duct-centred breast pathology.

ART-GYN-207END-KANDEEL-PLACENTA-DECIDUA-HISTOLOGY
## title
Histology of the placenta and decidua: barrier layers, trophoblast and decidual regions
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The definitive placenta interlocks a fetal part, the chorion frondosum (villous chorion), with a maternal part, the decidua basalis. The pregnant endometrium (decidua) has three regions: basalis (under the conceptus), capsularis (over it) and parietalis (lining the rest of the cavity) — there is no decidua vaginalis. Each chorionic villus is covered by an outer syncytiotrophoblast, a non-mitotic hormone-secreting syncytium rich in lipid droplets and microvilli, over a cytotrophoblast that thins and disappears as term approaches. The placental (materno-fetal) barrier is formed of the syncytiotrophoblast (and early cytotrophoblast), the trophoblastic basal lamina, villous connective tissue and the fetal capillary endothelium — a continuous, non-fenestrated endothelium — and it thins in late pregnancy as the cytotrophoblast is lost.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the fresh placental and decidual histology facts tested by the Dr Kandeel female set: the layers of the placental barrier (with a continuous fetal endothelium), the loss of the cytotrophoblast from the late-pregnancy barrier, the syncytiotrophoblast as a non-mitotic lipid-rich secretory layer, the maternal (decidua basalis) and fetal (chorion) parts of the placenta, and the three regions of the decidua.
### Mechanism
Chorionic villi bathed by maternal blood in the intervillous space exchange gases and nutrients across the placental barrier; the syncytiotrophoblast secretes hCG and steroids, and the barrier thins near term as the cytotrophoblast regresses, shortening the diffusion distance.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: fetal capillary endothelium continuous, not fenestrated; cytotrophoblast absent from the late-pregnancy barrier; syncytiotrophoblast non-mitotic and rich in lipid; maternal placenta = decidua basalis; fetal placenta = chorion; decidua regions = basalis/capsularis/parietalis (no vaginalis).
### Clinical significance
Thinning of the barrier and loss of the cytotrophoblast near term optimise materno-fetal exchange, while the syncytiotrophoblast is the source of the hCG used to detect and monitor pregnancy.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes (a fenestrated fetal endothelium, a cytotrophoblast in the late barrier, a chorion as the maternal part, a decidua vaginalis).

ART-GYN-207END-KANDEEL-OVARY-UTERUS-HISTOLOGY
## title
Histology of the ovary, endometrium and vagina: surface, theca, corpus luteum, ovulation and glands
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The ovary is covered by a simple cuboidal "germinal epithelium" (a misnomer — it does not make the ova) over the tunica albuginea; its follicles and their derivatives all lie in the cortex, while the medulla holds only vessels, nerves and connective tissue. In a maturing follicle the theca-interna cells differentiate into steroid-secreting cells (smooth ER, tubular-cristae mitochondria, lipid) that supply androgen for granulosa aromatisation. At ovulation the secondary oocyte is extruded with its zona, corona radiata and cumulus, while the theca interna stays behind and luteinises into the corpus luteum — which also lies in the cortex. In the uterus, secretory-phase endometrial glands become coiled and sacculated under progesterone; and the vagina is lined by a glycogen-rich stratified squamous non-keratinised epithelium and is glandless.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the fresh ovarian, endometrial and vaginal histology facts tested by the Dr Kandeel female set: the simple cuboidal germinal epithelium of the ovary, the steroid-cell ultrastructure of the theca interna, the cortical location of the corpus luteum, the retention of the theca interna at ovulation, the coiled sacculated glands of the secretory endometrium, and the stratified squamous non-keratinised lining of the vagina.
### Mechanism
Follicles grow in the cortex; theca interna makes androgen for granulosa aromatisation; at ovulation the cumulus-oocyte complex is expelled and the residual theca and granulosa luteinise. Progesterone remodels the endometrial glands into a secretory, glycogen-rich, coiled pattern; vaginal glycogen supports the acidic protective flora.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: germinal epithelium = simple cuboidal (misnomer); theca interna = steroid-secreting; corpus luteum and follicles in the cortex, not the medulla; theca interna retained at ovulation; secretory-phase glands coiled/sacculated, not straight; vaginal epithelium stratified squamous non-keratinised, not transitional.
### Clinical significance
The cortical location of follicles and corpus luteum orients ovarian histology and surgery; the coiled secretory glands underlie the implantation window; and the glycogen-rich vaginal epithelium underpins the lactobacillus-driven acidic pH that protects against infection.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes (a germ-cell-forming germinal epithelium, a protein-secreting theca interna, a medullary corpus luteum, straight secretory glands, a transitional vaginal lining).
