Histology of the endocrine glands: pituitary, thyroid and suprarenal

## summary
Endocrine glands are ductless, highly vascular glands that release hormones directly into the blood. The pituitary has two embryologically distinct lobes: the adenohypophysis from Rathke's pouch (oral ectoderm) and the neurohypophysis from the diencephalon; the anterior lobe is reached by the hypophyseal portal system, two capillary plexuses (median eminence and pars distalis) linked by portal veins. The thyroid follicle is lined by a simple epithelium whose height varies from squamous (inactive) to columnar (active) and is filled with colloid — mainly the glycoprotein thyroglobulin, the stored hormone precursor. The suprarenal gland is two organs in one: a mesodermal cortex of steroid-secreting cells (abundant smooth ER, tubular-cristae mitochondria, lipid) that release on demand without storing hormone, and a neural-crest medulla of chromaffin cells that secrete catecholamines.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the fresh endocrine-gland histology facts tested by the Dr Dalia set: the ductless nature of endocrine glands, the Rathke's-pouch origin of the adenohypophysis, the two-plexus hypophyseal portal system, the steroid-cell ultrastructure of the adrenal cortex, the neural-crest origin of the medullary chromaffin cells, the glycoprotein (thyroglobulin) composition of thyroid colloid, the variable height of the thyroid follicular epithelium, and the non-storing nature of the steroid-secreting cortex.
### Mechanism
Hypothalamic releasing hormones reach the anterior pituitary through the portal veins; anterior pituitary cells store peptide hormones in granules. Thyroid follicular cells store colloid extracellularly and later cleave T3/T4 from thyroglobulin. Adrenal cortical cells synthesise steroids on demand from cholesterol using smooth ER and tubular-cristae mitochondria, while neural-crest chromaffin cells release catecholamines.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: endocrine glands ductless (not avascular); adenohypophysis from Rathke's pouch (not diencephalon); portal system = two plexuses in series; adrenal cortex cells rich in smooth ER (steroid type); chromaffin cells neural-crest (medulla) vs mesodermal cortex; thyroid colloid = glycoprotein thyroglobulin; follicular epithelium simple, squamous-to-columnar; steroid cortex stores no hormone.
### Clinical significance
The oral-ectoderm origin of Rathke's pouch underlies craniopharyngiomas; the portal delivery of hypothalamic hormones is the anatomical basis of neuroendocrine control; the on-demand release by the steroid cortex explains why adrenal insufficiency shows no stored reserve, whereas the thyroid's large colloid store buffers hormone output.
