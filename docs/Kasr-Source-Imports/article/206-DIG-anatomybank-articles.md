## summary
This article gathers the gross-anatomy and developmental facts tested in the 206 DIG Anatomy department question bank, spanning the gut tube and the urogenital structures the module's anatomy course treats together. It covers the vertebral origin of the rectum and the puborectalis sling, the medial relation of the descending duodenum and the ligament of Treitz, the anterior versus posterior relations of the stomach, and the tributaries of the inferior vena cava. It then works through the male genital and urinary structures: the base of the bladder and its seminal-vesicle relation, the openings of the ejaculatory ducts, the origin of the vas deferens, the internal spermatic fascia, the parts of the male urethra, the bladder apex and trigone, the ureteric constrictions and the right-versus-left ureter relations, and the embryology of the glomeruli, the urogenital sinus, cryptorchidism and the lower anal canal. Each linked concept carries the precise claim and the distractors the exam options are built from.

### Definition

### Mechanism
The abdominal gut tube is organised by its embryological divisions and their fixed and mobile parts: the retroperitoneal duodenum curves around the pancreatic head and becomes the mobile jejunum at the ligament of Treitz, while the rectum begins where the sigmoid mesocolon ends in front of S3 and the anorectal junction is slung forward by the puborectalis. The urinary and genital organs share a common developmental origin: the ureteric bud builds the collecting system while the metanephric cap forms the nephrons, the urogenital sinus is partitioned into vesical, pelvic and phallic parts that form the bladder and the segments of the male urethra, and the mesonephric duct gives the vas deferens, seminal vesicle and ejaculatory duct. Venous blood from the gut reaches the inferior vena cava only after traversing the portal system and liver, arriving through the hepatic veins.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one discriminator: the rectum begins at S3 (not S1); the pancreatic head is the medial relation of the descending duodenum; the puborectalis, not a ligament, forms the anorectal sling; the pancreas lies behind, not in front of, the stomach; the hepatic veins are IVC tributaries while the gut is not; the seminal vesicle rests on the bladder base and secretes fluid rather than storing sperm; the ejaculatory ducts open into the prostatic urethra; the vas begins at the tail of the epididymis at the lower testicular pole; the glomeruli arise from the metanephric cap; the bladder apex continues as the median umbilical ligament and its trigone bears the internal urethral orifice at its inferior apex; the pelvi-ureteric junction is the upper ureteric constriction; the left (not right) ureter relates to the inferior mesenteric vessels; the prostatic urethra is the widest and most dilatable part; the membranous urethra derives from the pelvic urogenital sinus; cryptorchidism is arrested descent; and the sacrum lies posterior to the anal canal.
### Clinical significance
These facts underlie common clinical scenarios: identification of the duodenojejunal flexure and the level of a rectal lesion in surgery, the role of the puborectalis in continence and its release in defaecation, the palpation of the seminal vesicles and prostate per rectum, the impaction of ureteric calculi at the pelvi-ureteric junction, the increased malignancy and infertility risk of an undescended testis, the urachal cyst or fistula that persists at the bladder apex, and the safe suprapubic puncture of a full bladder above its peritoneal reflection.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors in this cluster's questions are built from exactly those reversals - for example placing the rectal origin at S1, the epididymal tail at the upper pole, the sacrum anterior to the anal canal, or the inferior mesenteric vessels medial to the right ureter.

## summary
This article gathers a second set of gross-anatomy and developmental facts tested in the 206 DIG Anatomy department question bank, drawn from two further past papers (exam C and the 2020 paper). It covers the posterior abdominal wall (the branches and termination of the abdominal aorta, the origin of the inferior phrenic arteries, the drainage of the testicular veins, and the genitofemoral nerve on the front of psoas major), the peritoneal anatomy of the lesser sac, the caecum and the variable appendix, the external oblique of the anterolateral wall, the porta hepatis, the anterior relations of the left kidney, and the posterior relations of the third part of the duodenum. It then works through the male urinary and genital structures and their development: the smooth-muscle internal urethral sphincter, the medial relation of the vas to the seminal vesicle, the coverings of the testis, the timing of testicular descent, the changing blood supply of the ascending kidney, the nature of a congenital hydrocele, and the anal valves at the pectinate line. Each linked concept carries the precise claim and the distractors the exam options are built from.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the tested facts of its cluster; each linked concept holds one atomic statement, and this overview ties them into a single picture of posterior-abdominal-wall, peritoneal, hepatobiliary and urogenital gross anatomy. Read the individual concepts for the exact claim and the distractor each exam option is built from.
### Mechanism
The posterior abdominal wall is organised around the aorta and inferior vena cava and the lumbar plexus on and around psoas major: the aorta gives four pairs of lumbar arteries and the paired inferior phrenic arteries before ending at L4, the right testicular vein reaches the cava directly while the left ascends to the left renal vein, and the genitofemoral nerve alone runs on the front of psoas. The peritoneum folds the gut into sacs and fixes some organs while leaving others mobile: the lesser sac is bounded on the left by the splenic ligaments and opens on the right at the epiploic foramen, the caecum is peritonealised but without a mesentery, and the third part of the duodenum is crossed in front by the superior mesenteric vessels. The urinary and genital organs share a developmental history: the metanephros ascends on a changing blood supply and rotates, the testis descends through the abdominal wall gathering its fascial coverings and reaching the scrotum by term, and a patent processus vaginalis leaves a congenital hydrocele.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one discriminator: the aorta gives four pairs of lumbar arteries and ends at L4; the inferior phrenic arteries spring from the aorta; the splenic ligaments form the left (not right) border of the lesser sac; the caecum is covered by peritoneum on all surfaces despite having no mesentery; the appendix is variable, most often retrocaecal; the external oblique arises from the lower eight ribs; the internal urethral sphincter is smooth involuntary muscle at the bladder neck; the right testicular vein drains to the IVC and the left to the left renal vein; the testis reaches the scrotum in the ninth month; the pancreas relates to the bare anterior area of the left kidney; the ascending kidney is supplied by successive transient arteries; a congenital hydrocele is fluid in a patent processus vaginalis; the tunica albuginea is the intrinsic testicular capsule rather than an acquired covering; the ampulla of the vas lies medial to the seminal vesicle; the porta hepatis transmits the portal triad but not the hepatic veins; the genitofemoral nerve lies on the front of psoas; the anal valves mark the pectinate line; and the superior mesenteric vessels cross the third part of the duodenum anteriorly.
### Clinical significance
These facts underlie common clinical scenarios: the left-sided predominance of varicocele from the right-angled left testicular drainage, the superior mesenteric artery syndrome from anterior compression of the horizontal duodenum, the variable presentation of appendicitis with the mobile appendicular tip, the prevention of retrograde ejaculation by the internal urethral sphincter, the accessory renal arteries left by transient fetal vessels, the patent processus vaginalis behind congenital hydrocele and hernia, and the surgical importance of the portal triad in the free edge of the lesser omentum at the porta hepatis.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors in this cluster's questions are built from exactly those reversals - for example calling the splenic ligaments the right border of the lesser sac, draining both testicular veins into the cava, placing the vas lateral to the seminal vesicle, listing the hepatic veins among porta hepatis contents, or making the superior mesenteric artery a posterior relation of the duodenum.
