## title
Anterior pituitary physiology: POMC, growth hormone signalling and somatomedins
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
The anterior pituitary secretes its hormones from distinct cell lines. Corticotrophs make the large precursor pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC), which is cleaved to ACTH together with MSH, beta-lipotropin and beta-endorphin; cortisol is the downstream adrenal steroid, not a POMC product. Growth hormone binds a cytokine-family receptor that has no intrinsic kinase activity: hormone binding activates Janus kinase 2 (JAK2), which phosphorylates STAT transcription factors. GH is directly protein-anabolic, raising amino-acid transport into cells and building protein for a positive nitrogen balance, while being diabetogenic and lipolytic. Its skeletal-growth effect is largely indirect through hepatic somatomedins (insulin-like growth factors), which circulate bound to IGF-binding proteins that give a long half-life and slow tissue release, and which feed back to inhibit GH.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the anterior-pituitary physiology facts tested by the Dr Shebl set: POMC as the precursor of ACTH, the JAK2-STAT signalling of growth hormone, GH's protein-anabolic action through amino-acid transport, and the plasma transport of somatomedins on binding proteins.
### Mechanism
POMC is enzymatically cleaved in corticotrophs to release ACTH and related peptides. Growth hormone dimerises its receptor and activates JAK2, which phosphorylates STATs that alter transcription; GH also increases active amino-acid uptake and protein synthesis directly, whereas cartilage growth is driven indirectly by liver-derived somatomedins bound to carrier proteins.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: precursor (POMC/ACTH) versus target-gland steroid (cortisol); JAK-STAT versus cyclic-AMP or nuclear signalling; the direct protein-anabolic action of GH versus its indirect skeletal action; and the long half-life of protein-bound somatomedins with their inhibitory feedback on GH.
### Clinical significance
These points underlie the interpretation of GH-axis disorders: ectopic ACTH from POMC-processing tumours, the growth failure of GH-receptor / post-receptor (JAK-STAT) defects such as Laron syndrome, and the use of IGF-1 measurement as a stable integrated index of GH activity because of its binding-protein-prolonged half-life.

## title
Posterior pituitary physiology: ADH aquaporin-2 action and the oxytocin Ferguson reflex
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
The posterior pituitary stores and releases two hypothalamic hormones. Antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) binds V2 receptors on collecting-duct principal cells, raising cyclic AMP and inserting preformed aquaporin-2 water channels into the apical membrane so water is reabsorbed and a concentrated urine is formed; its vascular V1 action and ACTH-potentiating V1b action are separate pathways. Oxytocin release is driven neurally: stretch of the cervix and vagina during labour signals the hypothalamic magnocellular neurons to release oxytocin, which strengthens uterine contraction and dilates the cervix further, a positive-feedback loop called the Ferguson reflex. Suckling is the separate trigger for the milk-ejection reflex.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the posterior-pituitary physiology facts tested by the Dr Shebl set: the V2-receptor / aquaporin-2 mechanism of ADH's antidiuretic action, and the cervical-stretch stimulus for oxytocin release in the Ferguson reflex.
### Mechanism
ADH signals through V2 receptors and cyclic AMP to insert aquaporin-2 channels, increasing collecting-duct water permeability. Oxytocin secretion is a neuroendocrine reflex: cervical/vaginal stretch afferents reach the magnocellular neurons and evoke oxytocin release, which acts on uterine smooth muscle in a self-reinforcing loop.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: the renal V2 water pathway (aquaporin-2) versus the vascular V1 pathway; and the stimulus for oxytocin (cervical stretch) versus its effects (uterine contraction, milk ejection).
### Clinical significance
These points underlie the physiology of urine concentration and its failure in central versus nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, and the obstetric use of the Ferguson reflex and exogenous oxytocin to augment labour.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors here are built from those reversals (ADH acting through V1/PGE2, oxytocin being triggered by its own effects or by prolactin/volume).
