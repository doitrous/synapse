## summary
This article gathers the gastrointestinal-physiology facts tested in the first physiology tranche of the 2021 206 DIG End-of-Module paper: the regulation of gastric acid and gastrin (acetylcholine as a secretagogue, antral-acid negative feedback, the parietal-cell proton pump and alkaline tide, and NSAID impairment of mucosal defence), the GI hormones (cholecystokinin's stimulation of gut motility, and the autonomic rather than hormonal control of saliva), pancreatic secretion (its 1500 ml daily volume and the disturbed function of acute pancreatitis), and motility (lower oesophageal sphincter tone, the factors that inhibit gastric emptying, and the mechanics of vomiting). ### Definition
This article groups the tested facts of its cluster; each linked concept holds one atomic statement, and this overview ties them into a single picture of gastrointestinal secretion and motility. Read the individual concepts for the exact claim and the distractor each exam option is built from.
### Mechanism
Gastric acid secretion is driven by acetylcholine, gastrin and histamine acting on the parietal cell, whose apical H+/K+ ATPase pumps H+ into the lumen while HCO3- leaves basolaterally as the alkaline tide; a low antral pH shuts off gastrin through somatostatin, and prostaglandins protect the mucosa (so NSAIDs, which block them, impair healing). The GI hormones gastrin and cholecystokinin coordinate acid, pancreatic and biliary secretion and gut motility, whereas saliva is under autonomic control. Motility is tuned by the enterogastric reflex and hormones (secretin, CCK, GIP) that inhibit gastric emptying in response to duodenal acid, fat, hypertonicity and distension, by the tone of the lower oesophageal sphincter, and by the coordinated reflex of vomiting.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one discriminator: acetylcholine stimulates acid secretion; a low antral pH inhibits gastrin; the H+/K+ ATPase is apical and there is no basolateral H+/Na+ pump; NSAIDs do not aid ulcer healing; CCK stimulates gut motility; saliva is autonomic, not hormonal; the pancreas secretes about 1500 ml/day; acute pancreatitis raises serum amylase with premature enzyme activation and steatorrhoea; gastric acidity raises lower oesophageal sphincter tone; alkalinity does not inhibit gastric emptying; and the pyloric sphincter contracts during vomiting.
### Clinical significance
These mechanisms underlie the rational treatment of peptic ulcer disease (acid suppression, H. pylori eradication, avoiding NSAIDs), the diagnosis of acute pancreatitis by a raised serum amylase with steatorrhoea, the heartburn of pregnancy from a progesterone-lowered sphincter tone, and the fluid and electrolyte consequences of prolonged vomiting.
