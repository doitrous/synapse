## summary
This Guyton & Hall GI review set probes the secretory and motor physiology of the gut. Saliva is distinctive in its ionic composition: under basal conditions bicarbonate has the highest absolute concentration while potassium is the ion most concentrated relative to plasma, and ductal handling leaves sodium and chloride low. In the stomach, chief (peptic) cells secrete pepsinogen, which needs gastric acid to become active pepsin, so achlorhydria impairs protein digestion. Motility is tightly patterned: the pharyngeal phase of swallowing screens bolus size, cholecystokinin is the one hormone that physiologically slows gastric emptying, and vomiting is a coordinated brain-stem act. Exocrine gland secretion depends on CFTR chloride transport, which fails in cystic fibrosis.

### Mechanism
Salivary acini secrete a plasma-like primary fluid; as it flows along the ducts sodium is reabsorbed and potassium and bicarbonate are secreted, giving saliva high potassium and bicarbonate and low sodium and chloride. Pepsinogen from chief cells is cleaved to pepsin by hydrochloric acid, which pepsin also needs as its working medium. Swallowing's pharyngeal phase pulls the palatopharyngeal folds into a slit that admits only adequately chewed boluses; cholecystokinin relaxes the orad stomach to raise compliance and slow emptying; and CFTR chloride channels drive the fluid secretion of exocrine glands.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one discriminator: bicarbonate (absolute) versus potassium (relative to plasma) in saliva; chief cells and acid for pepsinogen/pepsin; the palatopharyngeal folds for bolus selection; CCK as the sole physiological inhibitor of gastric emptying; and chloride transport for exocrine secretion.
### Clinical significance
Achlorhydria (for example atrophic gastritis) impairs protein digestion; delayed gastric emptying and receptive-relaxation physiology underlie post-prandial fullness; and cystic fibrosis, from defective CFTR chloride transport, thickens exocrine secretions and blocks pancreatic and other ducts.
