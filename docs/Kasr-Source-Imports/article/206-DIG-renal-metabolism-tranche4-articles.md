## title
Energy metabolism, thermoregulation and the control of food intake
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
Basal metabolic rate is the resting, post-absorptive energy expenditure per unit body surface area; it falls with age as muscle mass is lost and rises in the cold. Cold defence is layered: immediate shivering (about 4-5 fold) and cutaneous vasoconstriction, brown-fat uncoupled thermogenesis, and, if cold is prolonged, a thyroid-hormone-driven rise in metabolic rate. Body temperature is defended around a hypothalamic set-point that prostaglandin E2 raises during fever. Food intake is balanced between the lateral-hypothalamic feeding center and ventromedial satiety center, with GLP-1, peptide YY and insulin as satiety signals and ghrelin as the fasting hunger signal. The specific dynamic action of food is the post-meal rise in metabolic rate, greatest for protein (~30%).

## title
Renal tubular function: filtration markers, transport directions and segmental handling of Na+, K+ and glucose
## subject
renal
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
The nephron handles solutes by filtration, reabsorption (lumen to blood), secretion (blood to lumen) and net excretion. Inulin clearance equals GFR, while creatinine clearance slightly overestimates it because creatinine is additionally secreted. The juxtaglomerular granular cells are the intrarenal baroreceptors and renin source, distinct from the NaCl-sensing macula densa. Sodium is reabsorbed most in the proximal tubule (~65%), with aldosterone controlling only the distal principal-cell segment, while ANP is the natriuretic hormone. Hydrogen is secreted (not reabsorbed); potassium is secreted by principal cells and reabsorbed by intercalated cells mainly during depletion; proximal glucose exits basolaterally by GLUT-2 facilitated diffusion; and obligatory proximal water reabsorption uses aquaporin-1.

## title
Urine concentration, water balance and acid-base: the countercurrent system, SIADH and metabolic acidosis
## subject
renal
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
The renal medullary osmotic gradient is created by the loop of Henle acting as a countercurrent multiplier, then preserved against washout by the vasa recta acting as passive countercurrent exchangers and by sluggish medullary blood flow. In SIADH, excess ADH maximises collecting-duct water reabsorption so urine is inappropriately concentrated (high osmolarity) while plasma is diluted, producing hyponatremia and modest volume expansion with little edema. On the acid-base side, salicylate (aspirin) poisoning causes a high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis, in contrast to Cushing syndrome, Conn syndrome and persistent vomiting, which all cause metabolic alkalosis.
