ART-END-207END-PITUITARY-GH-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Hypothalamic-pituitary control and growth hormone
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
The hypothalamus governs the anterior pituitary through releasing and inhibiting hormones stored in the median eminence, and the whole system is disciplined by feedback loops of different length: long-loop (target hormone on hypothalamus/pituitary), short-loop (pituitary hormone on hypothalamus) and ultra-short-loop (a releasing hormone on itself). Negative feedback dominates, the estrogen-driven LH surge being the rare positive-feedback exception. Growth hormone is secreted in pulses, stimulated by hypoglycemia, exercise and amino acids and inhibited by aging, hyperglycemia, somatostatin and its own product; metabolically it is diabetogenic, sparing glucose in muscle while its mediator somatomedin C (IGF-1) drives chondrocyte differentiation and long-bone growth. Failure or excess along this axis produces recognizable syndromes: achondroplasia from an autosomal FGFR-3 mutation and Simmond's disease from adenohypophyseal atrophy.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the hypothalamic-pituitary and growth-hormone facts tested by the Physiology section (Q60-67); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview ties them together.
### Mechanism
Hypothalamic hormones reach the anterior pituitary through the hypophyseal portal system and are held in check by long-, short- and ultra-short-loop feedback. Growth hormone secretion is set by metabolic and stress signals and acts partly directly and partly through hepatic somatomedin C on the epiphyseal plate.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: the LH surge is positive feedback; ultra-short-loop feedback is a releasing hormone on itself; hypoglycemia raises GH while aging and exogenous GH lower it; somatostatin is made and stored in the hypothalamus; GH lowers muscle glucose oxidation; somatomedin C differentiates chondrocytes into osteogenic cells; achondroplasia is an autosomal FGFR-3 mutation; and Simmond's disease is adenohypophyseal atrophy.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the interpretation of dynamic pituitary testing, the diagnosis of acromegaly and dwarfism, the management of hypopituitarism, and the genetic counseling of skeletal dysplasias.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; the distractors in these questions are built from reversed feedback loops and swapped GH actions.

ART-END-207END-POSTERIOR-PITUITARY-PROLACTIN-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Posterior pituitary, prolactin and water balance
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
Prolactin is unusual in being under tonic inhibition by hypothalamic dopamine, so anything that interrupts dopamine delivery -- such as a stalk injury -- disinhibits it and causes galactorrhea; high prolactin in turn suppresses GnRH to give the anovulation and amenorrhea of lactation. The posterior pituitary stores two hypothalamic peptides: oxytocin, which contracts smooth muscle in labor, the breast and the male vas deferens, and ADH (vasopressin), which conserves water by inserting aquaporin-2 in the collecting duct, thereby concentrating urine while diluting plasma. When the collecting duct cannot respond to ADH the result is nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, in which ADH is high or normal but the kidney is resistant, urine stays dilute and plasma sodium rises.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the prolactin, oxytocin and ADH/water-balance facts tested by the Physiology section (Q68-72); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview connects them.
### Mechanism
Dopamine inhibits prolactin, while oxytocin and ADH are synthesized in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary. ADH acts through aquaporin-2; nephrogenic DI is end-organ resistance to it.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: stalk injury causes galactorrhea by loss of dopamine; prolactin causes lactational anovulation and amenorrhea; oxytocin contracts the vas deferens; ADH has opposite effects on urine and plasma osmolality; and nephrogenic DI shows increased/normal ADH with a resistant kidney.
### Clinical significance
These facts explain the workup of hyperprolactinemia and infertility, the physiology of labor and lactation, and the differentiation of central from nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; distractors here swap prolactin for oxytocin actions and central for nephrogenic DI.

ART-END-207END-THYROID-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Thyroid hormone synthesis, actions and disorders
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
Thyroxine synthesis needs iodide, thyroglobulin and TSH within the follicle, while thyroid-binding globulin only transports the finished hormone in plasma. Once released, thyroid hormones are calorigenic, raising oxygen consumption in most tissues (heart, muscle, gut) but not in a handful of exceptions such as the adult brain, gonads, spleen and anterior pituitary; metabolically they increase glycolysis, insulin secretion and free fatty acids yet lower plasma cholesterol by upregulating LDL clearance. Disorders map onto the axis: Graves' disease is autoimmune stimulation of the TSH receptor beyond TRH control, and hyperthyroidism is graded as primary (high T3/T4, low TSH), secondary (high TSH) or tertiary (excess TRH driving high TSH and high T3/T4).
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the thyroid-physiology facts tested by the Physiology section (Q73-77); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview ties them together.
### Mechanism
TSH drives every step of follicular hormone synthesis from iodide and thyroglobulin; released hormone acts on nuclear receptors to raise metabolic rate and modulate lipids; autoimmune or hierarchical lesions produce the different hyperthyroid pictures.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: TBG transports but does not synthesize T4; thyroid hormones lower plasma cholesterol; they do not raise anterior-pituitary oxygen consumption; Graves' is a TSH-receptor autoantibody escaping TRH control; and tertiary hyperthyroidism shows high T3/T4 with high TSH.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie thyroid function-test interpretation, the diagnosis of Graves' disease and the localization of hyperthyroidism to thyroid, pituitary or hypothalamus.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; distractors here assume thyroid hormone raises cholesterol and apply the primary-disease TSH pattern to tertiary disease.

ART-END-207END-CALCIUM-BONE-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Calcium, phosphate and bone-mineral physiology
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
Plasma ionized calcium sets neuromuscular excitability, and parathyroid hormone -- regulated directly by the calcium-sensing receptor and independent of the pituitary -- is its master controller, so PTH can change nervous-system excitability through calcium. Calcium homeostasis is a job for the gut, kidney, bone, liver and skin, but not the spleen, and vitamin D links several of them: calcitriol induces intestinal calbindin D to boost calcium absorption. Precipitation of calcium and phosphate above their solubility product is held back by inorganic pyrophosphate, calcitonin release is stimulated by gastrin to prevent postprandial hypercalcemia, and the phosphaturic hormone FGF23 -- raised by high phosphate -- acts on receptors in the parathyroid gland and the kidney.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the calcium, phosphate and bone-mineral facts tested by the Physiology section (Q59, Q78-83); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview connects them.
### Mechanism
PTH, calcitriol, calcitonin and FGF23 coordinate calcium and phosphate handling across gut, bone and kidney; ionized calcium feeds back on the parathyroids and sets membrane excitability.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: PTH changes nervous excitability via plasma calcium; PTH is independent of the pituitary; the spleen is not a calcium-metabolism organ; pyrophosphate inhibits calcium-phosphate precipitation; calcitriol acts through calbindin D; gastrin stimulates calcitonin; and FGF23 has parathyroid-gland receptors.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the assessment of tetany and hypercalcemia, the treatment of osteoporosis and rickets, and the understanding of mineral-bone disease in chronic kidney disease.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; distractors here treat the parathyroids as pituitary-controlled and reverse the phosphate-FGF23 relationship.

ART-END-207END-ADRENAL-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Adrenal cortex, glucocorticoids, aldosterone and volume regulation
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
Glucocorticoids are catabolic and hyperglycemic: cortisone raises blood glucose by gluconeogenesis and insulin resistance, which secondarily increases insulin secretion, and it is strongly anti-inflammatory through induction of lipocortin and inhibition of the mediators of inflammation. Chronic excess (Cushing syndrome) brings diabetes, central obesity, osteoporosis and hypertension -- never hypotension -- while ACTH and cortisol follow a circadian rhythm peaking in the early morning. The mineralocorticoid limb, aldosterone, retains sodium in exchange for potassium and hydrogen, so hyperaldosteronism gives hypernatremia, hypokalemia, hypertension and metabolic alkalosis, not acidosis. When the extracellular fluid volume falls, vasopressin, aldosterone and angiotensin II all rise together to defend it.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the adrenal-cortex and volume-regulation facts tested by the Physiology section (Q84-88, Q90); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview connects them.
### Mechanism
Cortisol and aldosterone act on glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid receptors to reprogram metabolism, inflammation and renal electrolyte handling, and the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone and vasopressin systems defend circulating volume.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: glucocorticoids raise insulin indirectly via hyperglycemia; Cushing has hypertension not hypotension; cortisone inhibits inflammatory mediators; hyperaldosteronism causes alkalosis not acidosis; ACTH peaks early morning; and a fall in ECF volume stimulates vasopressin, aldosterone and angiotensin II.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the diagnosis and monitoring of Cushing and Conn syndromes, the timing of cortisol testing, steroid side-effects, and the physiology of shock and heart failure.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; distractors here reverse the blood-pressure and acid-base changes of adrenal steroid excess.

## id
ART-END-207END-PANCREAS-GLUCOSE-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Pancreatic islets, insulin signaling and glucose homeostasis
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
Insulin is the master anabolic hormone. It signals through a receptor tyrosine kinase rather than cAMP, is co-secreted with C-peptide in a 1:1 ratio (making C-peptide a marker of endogenous secretion), and promotes protein synthesis, glycogenesis and lipogenesis while inhibiting ketogenesis -- which is why its absence causes ketoacidosis. Glucose disposal is not entirely insulin-dependent: during exercise, muscle contraction activates 5'-AMP-kinase and translocates GLUT4 to the membrane, lowering blood glucose independently of insulin. The incretin GLP-1, released from gut L cells after a meal, augments glucose-dependent insulin secretion and suppresses glucagon.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the insulin, incretin and glucose-homeostasis facts tested by the Physiology section (Q89, Q91-94); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview ties them together.
### Mechanism
Insulin's tyrosine-kinase receptor drives anabolic metabolism; contraction-mediated AMPK/GLUT4 offers an insulin-independent route; and incretins amplify meal-stimulated insulin release.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: insulin uses a receptor tyrosine kinase, not cAMP; exercise lowers glucose via AMPK and GLUT4 translocation; insulin and C-peptide are equimolar; insulin inhibits ketogenesis; and GLP-1 is an incretin that raises insulin.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the interpretation of C-peptide, the rationale for exercise in diabetes, incretin-based therapies, and the pathophysiology of diabetic ketoacidosis.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; distractors here list ketogenesis as an insulin action and misattribute exercise glucose lowering to gluconeogenesis.

ART-AND-207END-MALE-REPRODUCTIVE-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Male reproductive physiology: Sertoli cells, sperm maturation and testosterone
## subject
androl
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
The seminiferous epithelium is organized by Sertoli cells, whose tight junctions form the blood-testis barrier that isolates developing germ cells. Sertoli cells secrete inhibin, activin and aromatase, and it is their inhibin that feeds back on the pituitary to suppress FSH. Spermatozoa leave the testis immotile and infertile, acquiring motility and fertilizing capacity only during transit through the epididymis. Leydig-cell testosterone drives the male secondary sexual characteristics -- penile and scrotal growth, body hair, epiphyseal closure and laryngeal enlargement that deepens the voice -- so a high-pitched voice is not one of its effects.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the male reproductive-physiology facts tested by the Physiology section (Q95-99); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview connects them.
### Mechanism
Sertoli cells build the barrier and endocrine feedback of the testis, the epididymis completes sperm maturation, and Leydig-cell testosterone masculinizes the body.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: the blood-testis barrier is Sertoli-Sertoli tight junctions; Sertoli cells secrete activin, inhibin and aromatase; inhibin mediates FSH negative feedback; sperm mature in the epididymis; and testosterone deepens rather than raises voice pitch.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the understanding of male infertility, hormone assays (inhibin B, testosterone), and the effects of androgen excess or deficiency.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; distractors here swap Sertoli and Leydig functions and place sperm maturation in the testis.

ART-GYN-207END-FEMALE-REPRO-PREGNANCY-PHYSIOLOGY
## title
Female reproductive, pregnancy and labor physiology
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
Female reproductive physiology runs from puberty to labor. Menarche, the first menstruation, is a late pubertal event, and delayed puberty follows hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal failure (panhypopituitarism, Turner, Kallmann) -- early gonadotropins would instead cause precocity. Mid-cycle, rising estrogen switches to positive feedback and triggers the ovulatory LH surge, and estrogen also protects the premenopausal heart by raising HDL. In pregnancy, hCG from the trophoblast maintains the progesterone-secreting corpus luteum, high estrogen and progesterone suppress lactation until delivery, placental Fas-ligand induces apoptosis of maternal T cells to prevent fetal rejection, and in the first stage of labor cervical stretch reflexly increases uterine contractions.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the female reproductive, pregnancy and labor facts tested by the Physiology section (Q100-102, Q104-108); each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview ties them together.
### Mechanism
The hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis governs puberty and the cycle through negative and positive feedback, while the placenta drives the endocrine and immune adaptations of pregnancy and the reflexes of labor.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: menarche is the first menstruation; early gonadotropins do not cause delayed puberty; the ovulatory LH surge is positive feedback; estrogen raises HDL; hCG maintains the corpus luteum; high estrogen/progesterone suppress antepartum lactation; the first stage of labor is cervical-stretch-driven contraction; and placental Fas-ligand causes T-cell apoptosis.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the assessment of puberty and amenorrhea, ovulation timing, menopausal cardiovascular risk, pregnancy testing, and the management of labor.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field; distractors here confuse pubertal milestones, reverse estrogen's lipid effect and mislabel the stages of labor.
