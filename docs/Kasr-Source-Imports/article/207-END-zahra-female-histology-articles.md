## title
Histology of the ovary: follicles, zona pellucida, oogenesis and the corpus luteum
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
Ovarian follicles mature through defined histological stages. The primordial follicle is a primary oocyte, arrested in prophase of meiosis I, wrapped in a single layer of flat follicular cells and is the only follicle present before puberty; when activated, the follicular cells become cuboidal (unilaminar primary follicle) and the glycoprotein zona pellucida appears, formed by both the oocyte and the granulosa cells. Corona radiata processes cross the zona pellucida to meet oocyte microvilli at gap junctions. When granulosa-secreted fluid forms an antrum the follicle is a secondary (antral) follicle; the zona pellucida is the layer that separates the oocyte from the follicular cells. Meiosis I completes just before ovulation and meiosis II only after fertilisation. After ovulation the corpus luteum forms, and if pregnancy does not occur it regresses into the fibrous corpus albicans.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the ovarian histology facts tested by the Dr Zahra female set: the flat-versus-cuboidal follicular cells of primordial versus primary follicles, the composition and position of the zona pellucida, corona radiata gap junctions, the antrum that defines the secondary follicle, the timing of the two meiotic divisions, and the origin of the corpus albicans.
### Mechanism
Follicle growth is a stepwise change in the oocyte and its follicular cells; the zona pellucida mediates oocyte-follicle communication through gap junctions; meiotic arrest is released by the LH surge before ovulation and by fertilisation for meiosis II; the corpus luteum fibroses into a corpus albicans when not rescued by hCG.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: flat (primordial) versus cuboidal (primary) follicular cells; zona pellucida = glycoprotein from oocyte + granulosa; corona-oocyte gap junctions; antrum defines the secondary follicle; meiosis I before ovulation, meiosis II after fertilisation; corpus albicans from the corpus luteum.
### Clinical significance
These stages underlie ultrasound follicle tracking and assisted reproduction, the arrest of follicles in polycystic ovary syndrome, and the identification of the corpus luteum and corpus albicans in ovarian histology.

## title
Histology of the female genital tract: fallopian tube, endometrium and vagina
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The fallopian tube is lined by a simple columnar epithelium of ciliated cells, which move the ovum toward the uterus, and non-ciliated peg (secretory) cells, which nourish the ovum and activate sperm; its mucosa is highly folded, especially in the ampulla. The endometrium is supplied by two arterial sets: straight (basal) arteries to the non-shed basal zone that regenerates the lining, and coiled spiral arteries to the functional zone that thickens and sheds at menstruation when the spiral arteries constrict. The vagina is lined by non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium and has no glands; it is kept moist by cervical mucus and transudate from its vascular, elastic-rich lamina propria, and its acidic pH comes from lactobacilli acting on epithelial glycogen.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the female-tract histology facts tested by the Dr Zahra female set: the ciliated-and-peg-cell epithelium of the oviduct, the spiral-versus-straight arterial supply of the endometrium, and the glandless but vascular vaginal wall.
### Mechanism
Ciliary beating and peg-cell secretion move and support the ovum; spiral-artery constriction sheds the functional endometrium while the straight-artery basal zone regenerates it; the vagina relies on cervical mucus and vascular transudate rather than its own glands.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: oviduct = simple columnar ciliated + peg cells; spiral arteries -> functional zone, straight arteries -> basal zone; vagina has no glands but a vascular lamina propria.
### Clinical significance
These points underlie tubal transport and ectopic pregnancy, the mechanism of menstrual shedding and regeneration, and the reliance of vaginal moisture and pH on cervical secretion and epithelial glycogen.

See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors here are built from those reversals (a stratified oviduct lining, spiral arteries in the basal zone, glands in the vaginal wall).
