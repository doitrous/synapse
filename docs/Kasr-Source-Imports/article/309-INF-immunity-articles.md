## summary
This article groups the general-immunology facts tested by the paper: the immunoglobulins (IgA gives mucosal local immunity and works by neutralization; IgM acts mainly by complement activation), the complement system (the alternative pathway is driven by factors B, D and properdin), the cytokines (interferon-gamma activates NK cells; IL-2 promotes T- and B-cell proliferation), the humoral basics (thymus-independent antigens activate B cells directly; antibodies are the main defence against extracellular bacteria) and the types of immunity (maternal transplacental antibody is naturally acquired passive immunity; antitoxic serum is artificial passive immunity).
## sections
### Definition
The cluster covers the core vocabulary of immunology examined in 309 INF: antibody classes and functions, the complement pathways, key cytokines, the T-dependent/independent distinction, the antibody defence against extracellular bacteria, and the active/passive-natural/artificial classification of immunity.
### Mechanism
Secretory IgA neutralises pathogens at mucosal surfaces; pentameric IgM activates classical complement; the alternative pathway amplifies complement on microbial surfaces via factors B, D and properdin. IFN-gamma activates macrophages and NK cells while IL-2 expands lymphocytes. Thymus-independent antigens signal B cells directly (IgM, no memory/switching); antibodies neutralise, opsonise and fix complement against extracellular bacteria. Passive immunity is preformed antibody - natural across the placenta, artificial as antitoxic serum.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: IgA (mucosal, neutralization) versus IgG/IgM roles; IgM (complement activation) versus ADCC/neutralization; alternative pathway (innate, factors B/D/P) versus classical/lectin; IFN-gamma (NK/Th1) versus Th2/IgE effects; IL-2 (proliferation) versus interferon actions; T-independent (direct, IgM, no memory) versus T-dependent responses; antibodies versus cytotoxic cells for extracellular bacteria; and passive-natural (placental) versus passive-artificial (antitoxin) versus active immunity.
### Clinical significance
These facts explain vaccine and antibody-therapy design (active vs passive immunisation), why mucosal (IgA) immunity matters, how complement deficiencies predispose to infection, and how cytokines are used and targeted therapeutically.
