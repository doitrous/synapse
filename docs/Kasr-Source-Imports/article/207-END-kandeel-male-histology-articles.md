## title
Histology of the testis: coverings, seminiferous epithelium, Sertoli and Leydig cells
## subject
androl
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The testis is enclosed by three coats — the serous tunica vaginalis, the dense fibrous tunica albuginea and the inner vascular tunica vasculosa; "tunica propria" is not one of them. Its parenchyma is a mass of seminiferous tubules lined by the germinal epithelium (spermatogonia on the basal lamina, then spermatocytes and spermatids) supported by tall Sertoli cells. The spermatogenic lineage runs from diploid type A and type B spermatogonia through the diploid primary spermatocyte and the meiotic divisions to the haploid spermatid. Sertoli cells secrete androgen-binding protein, inhibin and anti-Mullerian hormone but not testosterone; androgen-binding protein, made under FSH, concentrates Leydig-derived testosterone inside the tubule. Leydig (interstitial) cells lie between the tubules, have acidophilic steroid-secreting cytoplasm, and secrete testosterone.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the testicular histology facts tested by the Dr Kandeel male set that are not already in the corpus: the three coverings of the testis, the cellular content of the seminiferous tubule (and the interstitial position of Leydig cells), the spermatogonial lineage (type A dark/pale and type B), the chromosome content of primary spermatocytes and the products of meiosis, the acidophilic steroid-cell cytology of Leydig cells, and the secretions and non-dividing nature of Sertoli cells.
### Mechanism
Germ cells mature from the basal lamina toward the lumen; Sertoli cells support them, form the blood-testis barrier and secrete androgen-binding protein, inhibin and anti-Mullerian hormone. Type A spermatogonia (dark reserve, pale renewing) give type B spermatogonia, which differentiate into primary spermatocytes; the first meiotic division halves the chromosome number to give secondary spermatocytes, and the second gives spermatids. Leydig cells in the interstitium supply the testosterone that androgen-binding protein binds.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: three coats (vaginalis/albuginea/vasculosa, not "propria"); Leydig cells interstitial, not tubular; type B spermatogonia give primary spermatocytes; primary spermatocyte diploid; meiosis-I product = secondary spermatocyte with 23 dyad chromosomes; Leydig cytoplasm acidophilic (steroid-secreting) not basophilic; Sertoli cells do not divide after puberty and do not secrete testosterone.
### Clinical significance
Sertoli-cell androgen-binding protein and the blood-testis barrier explain why local intratubular testosterone is far higher than in blood, and why disruption of Sertoli support arrests spermatogenesis; the interstitial Leydig cells are the endocrine source of testosterone that drives male secondary sexual characters.

See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes (a "tunica propria" coat, Leydig cells inside the tubule, ABP from Leydig cells, dividing Sertoli cells).

## title
Histology of the male genital ducts, accessory glands and penis
## subject
androl
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
After the seminiferous tubules, sperm pass through the tubuli recti to the rete testis — an anastomosing network in the mediastinum testis lined by simple cuboidal epithelium — then the efferent ductules and the epididymis, and finally the vas deferens, which is lined by pseudostratified columnar epithelium with stereocilia and wrapped in a thick three-layered muscular coat. The accessory glands add secretions: each seminal vesicle is a coiled tubular gland with a folded columnar-lined mucosa secreting a yellowish alkaline fructose-rich fluid; the prostate is a fibromuscular tubulo-alveolar gland whose thin milky (non-mucous) secretion is rich in acid phosphatase and PSA; and the paired bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands secrete lubricating mucus into the penile urethra. The male urethra changes lining along its course, being transitional (urothelium) in the prostatic part. The penis contains three erectile bodies; the ventral corpus spongiosum transmits the penile urethra and forms the glans.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the fresh male duct, gland, urethra and penis histology facts tested by the Dr Kandeel set: the epithelium of the vas deferens; the location and lining of the rete testis; the epithelium of the prostatic urethra; the secretions and gland types of the prostate, seminal vesicle and bulbourethral glands; the involvement of the inner prostatic glands in benign prostatic hyperplasia; and the identity of the corpus spongiosum.
### Mechanism
Sperm are conveyed and matured along the excurrent duct system and mixed with accessory-gland secretions to form semen. Each segment has a characteristic epithelium (cuboidal rete, stereociliated pseudostratified vas) and the glands contribute distinct fluids (fructose-rich alkaline seminal-vesicle fluid, thin acid-phosphatase-rich prostatic fluid, mucous Cowper's secretion). The prostatic urethra carries urothelium continuous with the bladder.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: vas deferens = pseudostratified columnar with stereocilia; rete testis = mediastinal, simple cuboidal; prostatic urethra = transitional; prostate secretion = thin milky, not mucous; seminal vesicle = folded columnar, not simple cuboidal; Cowper's glands open into the penile urethra; benign prostatic hyperplasia = inner mucosal/submucosal glands; corpus spongiosum = ventral, transmits the urethra, forms the glans.
### Clinical significance
The inner-gland origin of benign prostatic hyperplasia explains its early obstructive urinary symptoms as the periurethral glands compress the prostatic urethra, in contrast to the outer-gland origin of prostatic carcinoma; the uniform erectile spaces of the corpus spongiosum keep the urethra open during erection.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes (a mucous prostate, a simple cuboidal seminal vesicle, the urethra in the corpora cavernosa, BPH in the main glands).
