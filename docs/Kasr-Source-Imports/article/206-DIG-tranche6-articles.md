## title
Histology of the hepatocyte and the urinary system
## subject
renal
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The hepatocyte is a large polygonal cell with acidophilic vacuolated cytoplasm and a pale nucleolated nucleus. The urinary tract (ureter, bladder, upper urethra) is lined by urothelium whose surface umbrella cells and tight junctions form the blood-urine barrier; the ureter has a two-layered muscularis in its upper two-thirds and three layers in its lower third, and the membranous urethra pierces the urogenital diaphragm within the external sphincter. The nephron shows the three-layered glomerular filtration barrier (fenestrated endothelium, negatively charged basement membrane, podocyte slit diaphragms), acidophilic proximal convoluted tubule cells with brush border and basal infoldings enclosing parallel mitochondria, and the juxtaglomerular apparatus whose macula densa has crowded apical nuclei and an infranuclear Golgi.
## sections
### Definition

### Mechanism
Filtration is size- and charge-selective: the fenestrated endothelium holds back cells, the glomerular basement membrane laminae rarae carry fixed negative charge that repels anionic proteins, and podocyte foot processes packed with actin microfilaments form the slit diaphragms. Reabsorption is dominated by the proximal convoluted tubule, whose basal infoldings and parallel mitochondria power the Na+/K+-ATPase, while the macula densa senses tubular NaCl to regulate GFR and renin release. Urine is then conveyed and stored behind an impermeable urothelial barrier sealed by umbrella-cell junctional complexes.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one discriminator: hepatocyte cytoplasm is acidophilic and vacuolated (not columnar with basal nuclei); the urothelial barrier is the umbrella-cell layer; the macula densa is marked by an infranuclear Golgi; the GBM laminae rarae are negatively charged; the PCT has indistinct boundaries with parallel basal mitochondria; podocytes are the visceral (not parietal) layer; the bladder and ureter are lined by transitional epithelium; the detrusor has three muscle layers; the upper ureter has two muscle layers; and the membranous urethra runs through the urogenital diaphragm.
### Clinical significance
These features underlie the reading of liver and renal biopsies, the charge-selective proteinuria of glomerular disease, urothelial (transitional cell) carcinoma of the bladder and ureter, and the vulnerability of the membranous urethra to rupture in perineal (straddle) injury.

## title
Liver biochemistry: lipid metabolism and detoxification
## subject
fnd
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Biochemistry
## summary
Fatty liver is a triacylglycerol imbalance: it arises when deposition (increased fatty acid delivery or synthesis, decreased fatty acid oxidation) exceeds removal as VLDL. Lipotropic factors (choline, methionine, inositol and essential fatty acids) promote the phospholipid synthesis needed for VLDL export and so protect against fatty liver; endogenous choline itself is made by SAM-dependent methylation, which depends on methionine, folate and vitamin B12. Defective dietary lipid absorption causes steatorrhoea, over-mobilisation of adipose fat overwhelms hepatic VLDL synthesis, and phase I detoxification of xenobiotics proceeds by cytochrome-P450-catalysed hydroxylation.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the tested facts of its cluster; each linked concept carries the atomic statement, and this overview ties them into one picture. Read the individual concepts for the precise claim and the pitfalls that the exam options probe.
### Mechanism
The liver esterifies fatty acids to triacylglycerol and exports them packaged in VLDL; anything that raises triacylglycerol input or lowers VLDL output leaves fat stranded in hepatocytes as steatosis. Phospholipid (lecithin) synthesis is rate-limiting for VLDL assembly, which is why lipotropic factors that feed phospholipid synthesis are protective, and why choline synthesis via phosphatidylethanolamine methylation ties fatty-liver risk to the methionine/folate/B12 methyl-donor supply. Cytochrome P450 mono-oxygenases of the smooth ER use O2 and NADPH to hydroxylate xenobiotics, raising their polarity for phase II conjugation and excretion.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one fact: decreased fatty acid oxidation is a cause of fatty liver; folate, methionine and B12 (not alcoholism, the exam exception) feed choline synthesis; failed lipid absorption gives steatorrhoea; essential fatty acids are a lipotropic factor; hepatic triacylglycerol is exported as VLDL; and cytochrome P450 catalyses xenobiotic hydroxylation.
### Clinical significance
This cluster underlies non-alcoholic and nutritional fatty liver, the fat-soluble-vitamin deficiency of malabsorptive steatorrhoea, the rationale for lipotropic and adequate-protein nutrition, and the cytochrome-P450 basis of drug metabolism and drug-drug interactions.
