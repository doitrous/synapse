### Definition
This article groups the tested facts of its cluster; each linked concept holds one atomic statement, and this overview ties them into a single regional picture of abdominal anatomy. Read the individual concepts for the exact claim and the distractor each exam option is built from.
### Mechanism
The gut is supplied segmentally by its embryological arteries: the coeliac trunk (foregut), the superior mesenteric artery (midgut: small intestine, and colon to the proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon) and the inferior mesenteric artery (hindgut: left third of the transverse colon to the upper rectum), with the splenic flexure a watershed and the duodenum a dual-supplied foregut-midgut junction. Venous blood from the gut, spleen and pancreas returns through the portal system (superior mesenteric, splenic and inferior mesenteric veins) to the liver, which receives about 70% of its inflow from the portal vein; systemic veins such as the suprarenal vein bypass this route. The inguinal canal is floored by the inguinal ligament (anterior superior iliac spine to pubic tubercle), reinforced medially by the conjoint tendon, guarded laterally at the deep ring by the inferior epigastric artery, and traversed by the ilioinguinal nerve to the superficial ring.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one discriminator: appendicitis pain is periumbilical because the appendix is midgut (T10 visceral); the ureter crosses the common iliac bifurcation at the pelvic brim; the superior mesenteric vessels lie anterior to the uncinate process; the second part of the duodenum overlies the right kidney hilum; the short gastric arteries come from the splenic artery; the abdominal aorta gives four pairs of lumbar arteries; the common bile and pancreatic ducts form the ampulla of Vater; the sigmoid colon ends at S3; and the duodenum runs from L1 to L2 and is largely retroperitoneal.
### Clinical significance
These relations underlie the diagnosis of appendicitis by migrating pain, the identification and protection of the ureter in pelvic surgery, the unresectability of uncinate-process pancreatic cancer that encases the superior mesenteric vessels, gallstone impaction at the ampulla causing pancreatitis, the distinction of direct from indirect inguinal hernias by the inferior epigastric artery, and portosystemic anastomoses in portal hypertension.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors in this cluster's questions are built from exactly those reversals — for example, giving the liver 70% of its inflow from the hepatic artery, placing the short gastric arteries on the lesser curvature, or calling the duodenum completely peritonealised.
