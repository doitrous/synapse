## title
Endocrine gland histology and pituitary development
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
This article groups the endocrine-gland histology and pituitary-development facts tested by the 2023 EOM paper. The posterior pituitary (pars nervosa) is a neural structure whose axon terminals store neurosecretion as Herring bodies, while the anterior pituitary acidophils are subdivided by stain: carmine marks the prolactin-secreting mammotrophs and orange G marks the somatotrophs. The scattered diffuse neuroendocrine system behaves as highly modified neurons, and the gland itself arises from two primordia, Rathke's pouch and the infundibulum.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of the endocrine-gland histology and pituitary-development items in the Histology section; each linked concept carries one atomic claim and this overview ties them together.
### Mechanism
The neurohypophysis stores hypothalamic hormones in axon terminals seen as Herring bodies. Adenohypophyseal acidophils are told apart by tinctorial affinity (carmine mammotrophs vs orange-G somatotrophs). DNES cells take up and decarboxylate amine precursors and store products in dense-core granules. Developmentally, an ectodermal Rathke's pouch forms the adenohypophysis and a neuroectodermal infundibulum forms the neurohypophysis.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: Herring bodies belong to the pars nervosa; carmine identifies mammotrophs; DNES cells are highly modified neurons; and the pituitary has a dual (Rathke's pouch plus infundibulum) origin.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the interpretation of pituitary histology and adenomas (prolactinoma vs somatotroph adenoma), neuroendocrine tumours of the DNES, and congenital pituitary anomalies such as craniopharyngioma arising from Rathke's pouch remnants.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors are built by swapping the acidophil stains and by mislabelling the germ layer or lobe of pituitary development.

## title
Male reproductive histology and androgen physiology
## subject
androl
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
This article groups the male reproductive items tested by the 2023 EOM paper: the differential vulnerability of testicular cells and the endocrine consequences of exogenous androgens. In cryptorchidism the heat-sensitive germ cells are destroyed while the interstitial Leydig cells are spared, so fertility fails but androgen output persists. Conversely, exogenous androgen abuse suppresses the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, lowering gonadotropins and reducing spermatogenesis despite anabolic effects.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of the male reproductive histology and physiology items; each linked concept carries one atomic claim.
### Mechanism
Germ cells require the cooler scrotal temperature, so intra-abdominal position (cryptorchidism) arrests them while the interstitial Leydig cells resist. Exogenous androgens act by negative feedback on the hypothalamus and pituitary, cutting GnRH, LH and FSH and thereby intratesticular testosterone and spermatogenesis.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: cryptorchidism spares Leydig cells; and androgen abuse lowers, not raises, the sperm count.
### Clinical significance
These principles explain infertility with preserved virilisation in undescended testes, the timing of orchidopexy, and anabolic-steroid-induced infertility and testicular atrophy in athletes.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors assume Leydig-cell failure in cryptorchidism or a rise in sperm count with androgen use.

## title
Female reproductive and placental histology
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
This article groups the female reproductive and placental histology items tested by the 2023 EOM paper. Ovarian hilar cells are steroidogenic Leydig-cell homologues in the hilum that contain Reinke's crystalloids and secrete androgens. In the placenta the outer syncytiotrophoblast is the endocrine and exchange layer that secretes the placental hormones and persists throughout pregnancy.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of the ovarian and placental histology items; each linked concept carries one atomic claim.
### Mechanism
Hilar cells sit in the ovarian hilum, contain Reinke's crystalloids and secrete androgens like testicular Leydig cells. The syncytiotrophoblast, in direct contact with maternal blood, synthesises hCG, hPL, progesterone and estrogens and mediates maternofetal exchange across its microvillous border.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: hilar cells are Leydig homologues with Reinke's crystalloids; and the syncytiotrophoblast, not the cytotrophoblast, is the placental endocrine layer.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie virilising ovarian hilar-cell tumours and the endocrine assays (hCG, hPL) used to monitor placental function in pregnancy.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors place hilar cells in the cortex or the endocrine role in the pale cytotrophoblast.

## title
Pelvic floor and perineum anatomy
## subject
fnd
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Anatomy
## summary
This article groups the pelvic-wall and perineum anatomy items tested by the 2023 EOM paper. The ischiorectal fossa is bounded laterally by the obturator internus and its fascia, in which the pudendal canal runs, and medially by the levator ani and external anal sphincter. The pelvic diaphragm (levator ani and coccygeus) forms the pelvic floor and separates the pelvic cavity from the perineum below.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of the pelvic-floor and perineum boundary items; each linked concept carries one atomic claim.
### Mechanism
The wedge-shaped ischiorectal fossa is walled laterally by obturator internus fascia (carrying Alcock's canal) and medially by the sloping levator ani. The muscular pelvic diaphragm roofs the perineum and is pierced by the anal canal and urethra.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: the lateral wall of the ischiorectal fossa is obturator internus and its fascia; and the pelvic diaphragm separates the pelvis from the perineum.
### Clinical significance
These boundaries are essential for draining ischiorectal abscesses, understanding pudendal nerve blocks in Alcock's canal, and appreciating pelvic-floor support and prolapse.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors assign the lateral fossa wall to the levator ani or confuse the pelvic diaphragm with the pelvic brim or perineal membrane.

## title
Female pelvic anatomy: ovary, ligaments and blood supply
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Anatomy
## summary
This article groups the female pelvic anatomy items tested by the 2023 EOM paper. The suspensory ligament of the ovary is a true ovarian ligament transmitting the ovarian vessels, which themselves arise from the abdominal aorta just below the renal arteries. Within the broad ligament, the mesosalpinx is the upper fold that encloses and supports the uterine tube.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of the ovarian ligament, ovarian blood supply and broad-ligament items; each linked concept carries one atomic claim.
### Mechanism
The ovary is suspended by the suspensory (infundibulopelvic) ligament carrying the ovarian artery, which descends from its high aortic origin at about L2. The broad ligament is subdivided into mesometrium (uterus), mesovarium (ovary) and mesosalpinx (uterine tube).
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: the suspensory ligament is a true ligament of the ovary; the ovarian artery arises from the abdominal aorta below the renal arteries; and the mesosalpinx supports the uterine tube.
### Clinical significance
These relations guide ligation of the ovarian vessels during oophorectomy, the retroperitoneal course of the ureter near the ovarian vessels, and tubal surgery within the mesosalpinx.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors call the broad ligament an ovarian ligament, or place the ovarian artery on the internal iliac or renal artery.

## title
Endocrine physiology: hormone chemistry, signalling, vitamin D and islets
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
This article groups the general endocrine-physiology items tested by the 2023 EOM paper. Thyroid hormones are amine hormones derived from tyrosine, and G-protein-coupled receptors signal by releasing a GTP-bound alpha subunit from the beta-gamma dimer. Vitamin D is activated in two steps, 25-hydroxylation in the liver then 1-alpha-hydroxylation in the kidney to the active calcitriol, and pancreatic alpha cells secrete glucagon.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of hormone-chemistry, receptor-signalling, vitamin-D-metabolism and islet items; each linked concept carries one atomic claim.
### Mechanism
Hormones fall into amine (tyrosine-derived thyroid hormones and catecholamines), steroid and peptide classes. GPCRs exchange GDP for GTP on Galpha, which dissociates to drive effectors. Cholecalciferol is 25-hydroxylated in the liver and 1-alpha-hydroxylated in the kidney to 1,25-(OH)2-D. Islet alpha cells release glucagon in response to hypoglycaemia.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: thyroid hormones are tyrosine amines; GPCR activation dissociates the GTP-bound alpha subunit; the liver performs 25-hydroxylation; 1,25-(OH)2-D is the active vitamin; and alpha cells secrete glucagon.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the classification of hormones for assay and therapy, the pharmacology of GPCR-targeted drugs, the interpretation of 25-OH-D as vitamin D status, and the counter-regulatory role of glucagon in hypoglycaemia.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors group thyroid hormones with steroids, dissociate the beta subunit, or place 25-hydroxylation in the kidney.

## title
Pituitary and thyroid physiology: growth hormone and thyroid hormones
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
This article groups the anterior-pituitary and thyroid physiology items tested by the 2023 EOM paper. Growth hormone is restrained by an IGF-1-driven long-loop feedback that raises hypothalamic somatostatin. Thyroid hormones are built in a fixed order (iodide trapping, oxidation and iodination, then coupling), and their deficiency in the adult produces myxoedema with coarse skin and non-pitting oedema.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of the growth-hormone and thyroid-hormone physiology items; each linked concept carries one atomic claim.
### Mechanism
IGF-1 released by the liver under GH feeds back on the hypothalamus to raise GHIH (somatostatin) and inhibit GH. Thyroid follicular cells trap iodide, oxidise and organify it onto thyroglobulin tyrosines, then couple iodotyrosines to T3 and T4. Deficiency of thyroid hormone lowers metabolic rate and deposits glycosaminoglycans, producing myxoedema.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: IGF-1 long-loop feedback raises somatostatin to inhibit GH; thyroid synthesis runs iodide-pump then oxidation/iodination then coupling; and hypothyroidism gives coarse skin with non-pitting oedema.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the diagnosis of GH excess and deficiency, the pharmacology of antithyroid drugs that block peroxidase, and the recognition of myxoedema and cretinism.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors confuse the GH feedback loops, place coupling first in thyroid synthesis, or expect weight loss in hypothyroidism.

## title
Calcium, adrenal and pancreatic physiology
## subject
endo
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Physiology
## summary
This article groups the calcium-homeostasis, adrenal and pancreatic physiology items tested by the 2023 EOM paper. Plasma calcium (~10 mg/dl) circulates in diffusible and protein-bound forms, and parathyroid hormone is phosphaturic, giving hypophosphataemia while raising calcium. Cortisol raises blood glucose through hepatic gluconeogenesis, and skeletal muscle depends on insulin (GLUT4) for glucose uptake.
## sections
### Definition
A grouping of the calcium-distribution, parathyroid, cortisol and insulin items; each linked concept carries one atomic claim.
### Mechanism
Plasma calcium exists as ionised/complexed (diffusible) and protein-bound (non-diffusible) fractions. PTH inhibits proximal-tubular phosphate reabsorption (phosphaturia, hypophosphataemia) while raising calcium. Cortisol drives hepatic gluconeogenesis from mobilised amino acids and fatty acids, and insulin translocates GLUT4 to let glucose enter muscle and fat.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: plasma calcium has diffusible and non-diffusible forms at ~10 mg/dl; PTH causes hypophosphataemia; cortisol drives gluconeogenesis; and muscle glucose uptake is insulin-dependent.
### Clinical significance
These principles underlie the interpretation of corrected calcium and phosphate in parathyroid disease, steroid-induced hyperglycaemia and muscle wasting, and the insulin dependence that defines diabetic hyperglycaemia in muscle and fat.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls; distractors call the bone pool readily exchangeable, expect hypophosphaturia with PTH, or label neurons and renal cells insulin-dependent.
