### Definition
This article groups the tested facts of its cluster; each linked concept holds one atomic statement, and this overview ties them into a single picture of gut blood supply and development. Read the individual concepts for the exact claim and the distractor each exam option is built from.
### Mechanism
The gut is supplied segmentally by the arteries of its embryological divisions: the coeliac trunk (foregut) via the common hepatic artery, which yields the gastroduodenal and right gastric arteries; the superior mesenteric artery (midgut), whose ileocolic branch gives the end-arterial appendicular artery; and the inferior mesenteric artery (hindgut). These arterial territories mirror the developmental origins of the gut tube — foregut, midgut and hindgut — which also determine how the stomach rotates to form its curvatures, how the pancreatic ducts fuse, and how the ureteric bud and mesonephric duct build the urogenital drainage systems.
### Key determinants
Each item turns on one discriminator: the common hepatic artery gives the gastroduodenal and right gastric arteries (not the left gastric or splenic); the appendicular artery is an end artery, so its occlusion causes gangrene; the right suprarenal gland is pyramidal and lies behind the inferior vena cava; the greater curvature forms from the fast-growing dorsal border of the stomach; the main pancreatic duct is the ventral duct plus the distal dorsal duct; the ureteric bud forms the major calyces and collecting system; the seminal vesicle is a mesonephric-duct derivative; and the sigmoid colon is a hindgut derivative.
### Clinical significance
These facts underlie the surgical anatomy of the coeliac branches during gastric and pancreatic surgery, the rapid gangrene and perforation of the appendix in appendicitis because its artery is an end artery, the direct caval drainage of the right suprarenal vein, the anomaly of pancreas divisum when the ducts fail to fuse, and the developmental basis of duplex or ectopic ureters and of Wolffian-versus-Mullerian duct remnants.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors in this cluster's questions are built from exactly those reversals — for example, assigning nephron tubules to the ureteric bud, calling the right suprarenal gland semilunar, or placing the sigmoid colon in the midgut.
