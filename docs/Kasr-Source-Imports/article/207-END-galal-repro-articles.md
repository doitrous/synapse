## title
Female pelvic viscera and their lymphatic drainage
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Anatomy
## summary
The uterus, cervix, uterine tube, ovary and vagina share a drainage pattern set by their embryonic position. The gonadal organs (ovary and uterine fundus) drain high along the ovarian vessels to the para-aortic nodes, while the cervix drains laterally through the parametrium to the internal iliac nodes. The uterine tube lies intraperitoneally in the mesosalpinx, and the posterior vaginal fornix opens against the rectouterine pouch, giving a route for culdocentesis.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the female pelvic-viscera facts tested by the Dr Galal bank: fundal and ovarian lymph drainage to the para-aortic nodes, first-echelon internal iliac drainage of the cervix, the intraperitoneal (peritoneum-covered) fallopian tube, and the posterior-fornix route to the rectouterine pouch.
### Mechanism
The high para-aortic drainage of the ovary and uterine fundus follows the ovarian vessels down which these organs descended; the internal iliac drainage of the cervix follows the parametrial course of the uterine vessels; the peritoneal covering of the tube follows its position in the free edge of the broad ligament; and the fornix-to-pouch relation follows the peritoneal reflection onto the posterior vaginal wall.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: para-aortic (gonadal) versus internal iliac (cervical) versus inguinal (vulval) drainage; intraperitoneal tube versus extraperitoneal bladder base and lower rectum; and the posterior (rectouterine) versus anterior (uterovesical) fornix.
### Clinical significance
These pathways underlie the staging of cervical and ovarian carcinoma, the interpretation of a peritoneal spread of tubal disease, and the technique of culdocentesis through the posterior fornix.

## title
Pelvic wall, perineum and the perineal pouches
## subject
fnd
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Anatomy
## summary
The bony pelvis is pierced by the greater and lesser sciatic foramina, through which the obturator internus tendon (lesser) and the gluteal vessels and nerves (greater) pass. The internal iliac artery supplies the gluteal region through its superior and inferior gluteal branches. The perineum is divided into deep and superficial perineal pouches by the perineal membrane, which forms the floor of the deep pouch; the membranous urethra pierces the urogenital diaphragm, and the external anal sphincter lies immediately behind the perineal body.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the pelvis-and-perineum facts tested by the Dr Galal bank: the obturator internus tendon at the lesser sciatic foramen, the gluteal arteries from the internal iliac, the membranous urethra through the urogenital diaphragm, the external anal sphincter behind the perineal body, and the perineal membrane as the floor of the deep perineal pouch.
### Mechanism
The right-angled exit of the obturator internus tendon follows the shape of the lesser sciatic notch; the gluteal supply follows the divisions of the internal iliac; the fixed membranous urethra follows its investment by the urogenital diaphragm; and the pouch relations follow the position of the perineal membrane between the two pouches.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: greater versus lesser sciatic foramen contents; internal iliac versus common/external iliac branches; prostatic versus membranous versus spongy urethra; and the perineal membrane as floor of the deep pouch versus roof of the superficial pouch.
### Clinical significance
These relations underlie the vulnerability of the membranous urethra in straddle injury, the risk to the external anal sphincter in a median episiotomy, and the surgical anatomy of the perineal pouches.

## title
Gross anatomy and lymphatic drainage of the breast
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Anatomy
## summary
The breast lies on the deep pectoral fascia over the pectoralis major and serratus anterior, separated by the loose retromammary space that lets it move on the chest wall. It is supplied by the internal thoracic, lateral thoracic, thoraco-acromial and posterior intercostal arteries. Most of its lymph drains to the axillary nodes, but the medial quadrants drain to the internal thoracic (parasternal) nodes, a pathway that can carry tumour into the thorax and across the midline.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the breast facts tested by the Dr Galal bank: the bed of the breast on pectoralis major and serratus anterior, the internal thoracic drainage of the medial quadrants, and the arteries that do (and do not) supply the gland.
### Mechanism
The mobility of the breast follows the retromammary space over the pectoral fascia; the medial drainage follows the internal thoracic vessels beside the sternum; and the arterial supply follows the perforating and axillary branches that reach the chest wall.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: axillary (lateral) versus parasternal (medial) lymph drainage; the true chest-wall breast arteries versus the shoulder (circumflex humeral) arteries; and the correct rib/nipple levels of the gland.
### Clinical significance
These pathways underlie the spread of breast carcinoma to the parasternal and contralateral nodes, the early sign of skin/muscle tethering, and the arterial anatomy relevant to breast surgery.

## title
Development of the genital system
## subject
gyn
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Embryology
## summary
The gonads and genital ducts arise on the posterior wall from the genital ridges, colonised by primordial germ cells that migrate from the yolk sac along the dorsal mesentery of the hindgut. The paramesonephric (Mullerian) ducts form the uterine tubes, uterus, cervix and upper vagina, whereas the mesonephric ducts and urogenital sinus give the trigone, male duct vestiges and the lower vagina.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the genital-development facts tested by the Dr Galal bank: the migration of primordial germ cells to the genital ridge and the paramesonephric-duct origin of the uterine tube.
### Mechanism
Germ-cell arrival induces the primary sex cords in the genital ridge; the fate of the ducts follows the presence or absence of testis-determining signals, with the paramesonephric ducts persisting in the female to form the tube, uterus and upper vagina.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: yolk-sac origin and dorsal-mesentery route of the germ cells; paramesonephric (tube/uterus/upper vagina) versus mesonephric (trigone/paradidymis) versus urogenital-sinus (lower vagina) derivatives; and the differing cord features of the developing testis and ovary.
### Clinical significance
These origins underlie the pattern of Mullerian duct anomalies (double uterus, agenesis) and the explanation of intersex and gonadal-dysgenesis states.
