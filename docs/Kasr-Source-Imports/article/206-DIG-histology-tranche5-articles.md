## title
Histology of the oral cavity, tongue and oesophagus
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner

## topic

## summary
The oral mucosa is masticatory (keratinized) over the gingiva and hard palate and lining (non-keratinized) over the cheeks, soft palate and pharynx. The tongue carries four papillae: filiform (keratinized, no taste buds), fungiform (few apical taste buds), foliate (lateral ridges) and circumvallate (deep furrow, many taste buds, served by the serous glands of von Ebner). Taste buds hold dark peripheral sustentacular (supporting) cells, pale central gustatory (receptor) cells and basal stem cells. The oesophagus is lined by non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, and its muscularis externa grades from striated (upper third) through mixed (middle third) to smooth muscle (lower third), with a myenteric plexus between the muscle layers and an adventitia that becomes a serosa only below the diaphragm.

## title
Histology of the stomach
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The gastric mucosa is a simple columnar surface epithelium with gastric pits leading into glands. Fundic/oxyntic glands contain oxyntic (parietal) cells (deeply acidophilic, many mitochondria, intracellular canaliculi, acid and intrinsic factor) and chief (peptic) cells (basal basophilic rough ER, apical electron-dense zymogen granules, pepsinogen), together with mucous neck cells and enteroendocrine cells. Enteroendocrine cells secrete basally, so they show an infranuclear Golgi and basal dense-core granules, unlike the apically secreting exocrine cells. The pylorus has deep gastric pits, branched coiled mucous glands, a less-folded mucosa and G cells but no parietal cells. A peptic ulcer, promoted by NSAID loss of protective prostaglandins, is defined by erosion of the mucosa.

## title
Histology of the small and large intestine
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The intestinal epithelium is dominated by enterocytes, tall columnar absorptive cells with an apical microvillous brush border. Goblet cells increase in number from the duodenum to the distal colon, where lubricating mucus is greatest. In the small intestine, Paneth cells at the crypt base secrete antibacterial products and M (microfold) cells of the follicle-associated epithelium transport luminal antigens over Peyer's patches. The large intestine has long straight crypts, an incomplete outer longitudinal muscle gathered into three teniae coli and a continuous inner circular layer; the vermiform appendix is recognised by abundant lymphoid follicles filling the lamina propria and submucosa.

### Mechanism
Enterocytes bear an apical microvillous brush border. Goblet-cell density rises from duodenum to distal colon. M cells over Peyer's patches transport antigens, while Paneth cells secrete antibacterial products at the crypt base. The appendix is defined by abundant submucosal lymphoid follicles; the teniae coli are the incomplete longitudinal layer, the circular layer being continuous.

## title
Histology of the salivary glands, pancreas, gall bladder and liver
## subject
gi
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The major salivary glands run acini into intercalated, then striated (intralobular), then interlobular ducts; the parotid is pure serous (basophilic base, apical zymogen granules, interdigitating membranes), the sublingual mixed and mucous-predominant with serous demilunes and no pure serous acini, and the striated ducts show basal acidophilic mitochondrial striations. The exocrine pancreas is a serous gland whose intercalated ducts begin inside the acinus as centroacinar cells; the endocrine islets of Langerhans are dominated by insulin-secreting beta cells (~70%). The gall bladder has a columnar absorptive lining, irregular smooth muscle and no submucosa. In the liver, the classic hexagonal lobule has a central vein and corner portal triads outlined by thin CT septa, with hepatocyte plates separated by sinusoids that contain the phagocytic Kupffer cells.

### Definition

### Mechanism
The parotid is pure serous with interdigitating cell membranes; the sublingual is mixed, mucous-predominant with demilunes and no pure serous acini; striated ducts show basal mitochondrial striations. Centroacinar cells are the intra-acinar start of the pancreatic intercalated duct, and islet beta cells are the most numerous (~70%, insulin). The gall bladder has columnar lining, irregular muscle and no submucosa; the classic hepatic lobule is central-vein-centred with portal triads at its corners, and Kupffer cells are sinusoidal phagocytes.
