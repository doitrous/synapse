## title
Histology of the testis: spermatogenesis, spermiogenesis and the spermatozoon
## subject
androl
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
The seminiferous tubule is wrapped by contractile peritubular myoid cells and lined by a stratified germinal epithelium supported by Sertoli cells. Spermatogonia are the diploid stem cells lying against the basal lamina; from them arise the primary spermatocytes, the largest cells of the series (diploid but 4N in DNA during their long meiotic prophase), then the short-lived secondary spermatocytes and the haploid spermatids. Spermatogenesis in man takes about 74 days. Spermiogenesis is the final non-dividing metamorphosis of a round spermatid into a spermatozoon: the acrosome is assembled from the Golgi complex and caps the condensed flattened nucleus of the head, a flagellum forms with a mitochondrial sheath around the axoneme in the middle piece, and excess cytoplasm is shed as residual bodies phagocytosed by Sertoli cells. Adult human interstitial (Leydig) cells are identified by their rod-shaped Reinke crystals.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the testicular histology facts tested by the Dr Zahra male set: peritubular myoid cells, the basal location of spermatogonia, the primary spermatocyte as the largest germ cell, the ~74-day duration of spermatogenesis, the definition of spermiogenesis, the Golgi origin of the acrosome, the structure of the sperm head and middle piece, Sertoli-cell clearance of residual bodies, and Reinke crystals of Leydig cells.
### Mechanism
Germ cells mature from the basal lamina toward the lumen while Sertoli cells support them, form the blood-testis barrier and phagocytose residual bodies. Spermiogenesis remodels the spermatid: Golgi-derived acrosome, nuclear condensation, flagellum with a mitochondrial energy segment.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: spermatogonia basal (not luminal); primary spermatocyte largest; acrosome from Golgi (not rER or nucleus); head = nucleus + acrosome; middle piece = axoneme + mitochondrial sheath; residual bodies cleared by Sertoli cells; Reinke crystals in Leydig cells.
### Clinical significance
The long fixed duration of spermatogenesis explains the weeks-long delay before testicular insults change the ejaculate, while Reinke crystals help identify Leydig-cell tumours and myoid-cell contraction underlies transport of immotile testicular sperm.

## title
Histology of the male excurrent ducts and accessory glands
## subject
androl
## status
Draft
## owner
Claude
## topic
Histology
## summary
Sperm leave the testis along an ordered duct system: seminiferous tubule to straight tubules (tubuli recti), then the rete testis, the efferent ductules, and the epididymis. The epididymis is lined by a tall pseudostratified columnar epithelium bearing long non-motile stereocilia that absorb testicular fluid and add maturation factors. The prostate is a compound tubulo-alveolar gland whose alveoli often contain lamellated corpora amylacea. The erectile tissue of the corpora cavernosa is a network of venous spaces separated by trabeculae of smooth muscle, collagen, reticular and elastic fibres.
## sections
### Definition
This article groups the male duct-and-gland histology facts tested by the Dr Zahra male set: the order of the genital duct system, the stereociliated epithelium of the epididymis, the tubulo-alveolar nature of the prostate and its corpora amylacea, and the erectile tissue of the corpora cavernosa.
### Mechanism
The excurrent ducts convey and concentrate sperm; stereocilia give the epididymis a large absorptive/secretory surface; prostatic alveoli accumulate glycoprotein concretions; erection fills the trabeculated venous spaces of the corpora cavernosa.
### Key determinants
Discriminators tested here: duct order (tubuli recti -> rete testis -> efferent ductules -> epididymis); stereocilia mark the epididymis; prostate = compound tubulo-alveolar with corpora amylacea; erectile tissue = venous spaces and fibromuscular trabeculae.
### Clinical significance
The duct order underlies the sites of obstruction in infertility, the epididymis is the site of sperm maturation and storage, corpora amylacea are a normal age-related prostatic finding, and the trabecular smooth muscle of the corpora cavernosa is the target of erectile physiology and its drugs.
### Common misconceptions
See each concept's pitfalls field for the specific reversal a hurried reader makes; the distractors here are built from those reversals (rete testis before the tubuli recti, stereocilia in the seminiferous tubule, the prostate as a purely tubular gland).
