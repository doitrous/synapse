## summary
Twelve End Foundation 2 Batch 43 microbiology facts: the 0.2-8 micrometre bacterial size range; streptodornase as a DNAase spreading-factor enzyme; the greater-than-5-micrometre particle-size cutoff for contact/droplet versus true airborne spread; the chain arrangement of Streptococcus pyogenes; the survival function of bacterial spores under nutrient, heat or dryness stress; cell wall M protein as the basis of streptococcal serotyping; this exam's own printed key for infective endocarditis's causative organism; exfoliative toxins as the cause of staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome; the bilayer structure of the Gram-negative outer membrane; the capsule's antiphagocytic function; plasmid structure; and the coagulase test dividing staphylococcal species.

## sections
### Definition
Bacteria span roughly 0.2 to 8 micrometres in size. Streptodornase is a Streptococcus pyogenes DNAase enzyme. Respiratory particles above about 5 micrometres settle quickly and spread by contact or droplet routes, while droplet nuclei under 5 micrometres stay airborne. Streptococcus pyogenes is a Gram-positive coccus arranged in chains. A bacterial spore is a dormant, resistant survival structure formed under adverse conditions. Cell wall M protein is the antigenically variable streptococcal surface protein used for serotyping. This exam's own printed key names Group A streptococci as a cause of infective endocarditis. Exfoliative toxins are the Staphylococcus aureus toxins responsible for scalded skin syndrome. The Gram-negative outer membrane is a phospholipid/lipopolysaccharide bilayer. The bacterial capsule's primary virulence function is antiphagocytic. A plasmid is a small circular extrachromosomal DNA molecule. The coagulase test divides staphylococci into coagulase-positive Staph. aureus and coagulase-negative species.

### Mechanism
Streptodornase degrades the extracellular DNA that accumulates in pus from lysed leukocytes, reducing its viscosity and helping the organism spread through tissue, a different action from streptokinase's fibrin-lysing mechanism. The greater-than-5-micrometre particle-size threshold matters because larger particles are too heavy to remain suspended in air for long, so they settle onto surfaces or travel only short distances as droplet spray, while smaller droplet nuclei evaporate and stay airborne on currents. A bacterial spore forms when the vegetative cell dehydrates its cytoplasm and lays down a thick, resistant coat around its genetic material, entering a metabolically inactive state it can maintain indefinitely until conditions improve and it germinates back into a vegetative cell; sporulation is a survival mechanism, not a means of increasing cell number. M protein projects from the streptococcal cell wall, is antiphagocytic, and its antigenic variability, more than 150 distinct types, is what serotyping schemes track. Exfoliative toxins are serine proteases that cleave desmoglein-1, a desmosomal adhesion protein, within the epidermis, causing the epidermal layers to separate and produce widespread superficial blistering. The Gram-negative outer membrane's bilayer places phospholipid on its inner leaflet and lipopolysaccharide on its outer leaflet, is studded with porin channels, and exists only in Gram-negative bacteria. The capsule coats the bacterial surface and interferes with recognition and engulfment by host phagocytes, letting the organism survive longer in tissue, so encapsulated strains are typically more virulent than unencapsulated ones. A plasmid replicates independently of the bacterial chromosome and, though non-essential for viability, commonly carries accessory genes such as antibiotic resistance. Coagulase, an enzyme that clots plasma, is produced by Staphylococcus aureus but not by the coagulase-negative staphylococci such as S. epidermidis and S. saprophyticus, making it a key laboratory identification marker.

### Key determinants
What determines whether a respiratory particle transmits by the airborne route versus contact/droplet is its size relative to the roughly 5-micrometre cutoff, not the identity of the pathogen it carries. What determines the streptococcal chain arrangement, as opposed to the staphylococcal cluster, is the plane and completeness of cell division after binary fission. What determines whether a Staphylococcus aureus infection produces scalded skin syndrome rather than food poisoning or toxic shock is which toxin class the strain secretes, exfoliative toxin, enterotoxin or toxic shock syndrome toxin respectively, since each targets a different structure. What determines M-protein-based serotyping is the antigenic diversity of the M protein's variable region, not the streptococcal capsule. What determines whether a staphylococcal isolate is S. aureus rather than a coagulase-negative species is the coagulase test result, a single, decisive laboratory marker. What determines a capsule's protective value is its ability to block phagocyte recognition, independent of whether its own composition is polysaccharide or, in the anthrax exception, protein.

### Clinical significance
Recognising the roughly 5-micrometre particle-size threshold underpins infection-control precautions: droplet/contact precautions suffice for larger-particle pathogens, while true airborne pathogens require airborne (negative-pressure, N95) precautions. Streptodornase and other streptococcal spreading-factor enzymes explain why streptococcal skin and soft-tissue infection tends toward diffuse spread rather than the walled-off abscesses staphylococci typically produce. Recognising staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome and distinguishing it from other blistering conditions guides prompt antistaphylococcal treatment in the affected (typically paediatric) population. The M protein's antiphagocytic and antigenically variable nature explains both Streptococcus pyogenes's virulence and why prior infection with one M type does not protect against a different one, relevant to recurrent streptococcal pharyngitis and post-streptococcal complications. The coagulase test is a routine, rapid bedside/laboratory step in identifying a staphylococcal isolate and predicting its likely virulence. R-plasmids carrying antibiotic-resistance genes are a major mechanism of the spread of resistance between bacteria, of direct relevance to antibiotic stewardship.

## summary
Three End Foundation 2 Batch 43 autonomic pharmacology facts: isoprenaline as a non-selective beta1/beta2 agonist; the mechanism of indirect-acting cholinomimetics (inhibiting acetylcholine hydrolysis rather than binding the receptor); and atropine's competitive, surmountable block of muscarinic receptors.

## sections
### Definition
Isoprenaline (isoproterenol) is a synthetic catecholamine that stimulates both beta1 and beta2 adrenoceptors with no significant alpha activity. Indirect-acting cholinomimetics are drugs that potentiate acetylcholine's own action rather than binding cholinergic receptors themselves. Atropine is a competitive (surmountable) antagonist at muscarinic acetylcholine receptors.

### Mechanism
Isoprenaline's beta1 stimulation raises heart rate and contractility, while its beta2 stimulation produces bronchodilation and vasodilation; because it has no alpha activity, it does not raise peripheral vascular resistance the way adrenaline can. Indirect-acting cholinomimetics, such as neostigmine, physostigmine and pyridostigmine, work one step upstream of the receptor: they inhibit acetylcholinesterase, the enzyme that normally hydrolyses acetylcholine at the synapse, so endogenous acetylcholine accumulates and its effect on both muscarinic and nicotinic receptors is potentiated. Atropine occupies the same muscarinic receptor site as acetylcholine, direct-acting muscarinic agonists (pilocarpine, carbachol) and the acetylcholine an anticholinesterase has caused to accumulate; because the binding is competitive rather than irreversible, a sufficiently high agonist concentration can still displace atropine and restore muscarinic signalling.

### Key determinants
What determines isoprenaline's effect profile is its receptor selectivity: beta1-plus-beta2 with no alpha, distinct from adrenaline's broader alpha/beta1/beta2 profile and from a cardioselective beta1 agonist such as dobutamine. What determines whether a cholinomimetic is classed as direct- or indirect-acting is whether it binds the receptor itself or instead spares endogenous acetylcholine from breakdown. What determines whether a muscarinic antagonist's block is competitive or not is whether it can be displaced by raising the agonist concentration, which is exactly the property atropine has and an irreversible antagonist lacks.

### Clinical significance
Isoprenaline's combined beta1/beta2 action once made it useful for bradyarrhythmias and bronchospasm, though its lack of receptor selectivity (and resulting risk of tachyarrhythmia) has limited its modern use in favour of more selective agents. Indirect-acting anticholinesterases are used to reverse neuromuscular blockade, treat myasthenia gravis and manage glaucoma or Alzheimer's disease, depending on the specific agent's tissue access and reversibility. Atropine's competitive antagonism is exploited clinically to reverse excessive muscarinic stimulation, from organophosphate poisoning to bradycardia, and because the block is competitive rather than irreversible, its effect can in principle be overcome by a large enough dose of the agonist it is opposing.

## summary
Nine End Foundation 2 Batch 43 general pathology facts: secondary-intention wound healing's wide gap and heavy granulation-tissue reliance; hepatocytes as the classic stable-cell example; remodeling as the maturing step of fibrous tissue formation; mucin as the deposit behind the gastric-carcinoma signet ring cell; gout as a purine metabolism disorder; hemochromatosis as an iron metabolism disorder; staphylococci's lack of causal relationship to cellulitis; compensatory hyperplasia as the bone marrow's response to acute hemorrhage; and Barrett oesophagus as chronic-reflux-driven squamous-to-intestinal metaplasia.

## sections
### Definition
Secondary intention wound healing applies to a wide, gaping, contaminated or tissue-deficient wound. Stable cells are normally quiescent but retain the capacity to re-enter the cell cycle when stimulated, with hepatocytes as the classic example. Remodeling is the maturation phase of fibrous tissue (scar) formation. Gastric carcinoma's signet ring cells accumulate intracytoplasmic mucin. Gout is a disorder of purine metabolism producing hyperuricaemia and urate crystal deposition. Hemochromatosis is a disorder of iron metabolism. Staphylococci are causally linked to abscess, boil, carbuncle and furuncle, but not to cellulitis. Acute hemorrhage produces compensatory hyperplasia of the bone marrow. Barrett oesophagus is chronic-reflux-driven squamous-to-intestinal metaplasia of the distal oesophagus.

### Mechanism
Because a wide or contaminated wound's edges cannot be closely apposed, secondary intention healing must first fill the defect with a larger volume of granulation tissue before epithelialisation and wound contraction can close it, taking longer than primary intention's clean, closely apposed incision. Stable cells such as hepatocytes sit in G0 under normal conditions but mount a substantial proliferative response after injury or resection, unlike labile cells (continuously dividing) or permanent cells (unable to divide after maturity, such as neurons). Fibrous tissue formation proceeds from blood clot formation through an inflammatory phase and granulation tissue formation to remodeling, in which type III collagen is progressively replaced by stronger type I collagen and fibres reorganise along lines of tension, increasing the scar's tensile strength over weeks to months. Gastric carcinoma signet ring cells form when a malignant cell accumulates a large mucin vacuole that pushes its nucleus to the cell periphery, producing the ring-with-a-stone appearance; this differs from the lipid-filled signet ring appearance of a fat-laden adipocyte, where the deposited substance is fat, not mucin. In gout, overproduction or underexcretion of purines raises serum uric acid, and monosodium urate crystals precipitate in and around joints, provoking acute inflammation. In hemochromatosis, excess iron absorption or intake deposits iron, largely as haemosidrin, in the liver, pancreas, heart, joints and skin. Streptococcus pyogenes's spreading-factor enzymes (hyaluronidase, streptokinase, streptodornase) favour the diffuse tissue invasion of cellulitis, a pattern staphylococci, which favour localised abscess formation, do not typically produce. After acute hemorrhage, erythropoietin drives an increase in erythroid precursor numbers in the bone marrow to replace lost red cell mass. In Barrett oesophagus, repeated acid-reflux injury to the distal oesophageal squamous epithelium drives its replacement by a more acid-resistant columnar, intestinal-type epithelium containing goblet cells, visible endoscopically as salmon-coloured mucosa extending upward from the gastro-oesophageal junction.

### Key determinants
What determines whether a wound heals by primary or secondary intention is the width of the gap between its edges and the degree of contamination or tissue loss, not merely whether it was surgically created. What determines a cell population's regenerative classification (labile, stable or permanent) is its baseline proliferative activity and its capacity to re-enter the cell cycle after injury. What determines the substance deposited in a signet ring cell is the tissue context, mucin in gastric carcinoma, lipid in a fat-laden adipocyte. What determines gout versus hemochromatosis as the diagnosis is which metabolic pathway, purine or iron, is deranged, read from the clinical and laboratory picture (uric acid versus iron studies). What determines whether chronic reflux produces Barrett oesophagus rather than simple oesophagitis is whether the injury is severe and sustained enough to drive a durable change in epithelial cell type (metaplasia) rather than only reversible inflammation.

### Clinical significance
Recognising secondary-intention healing's slower course and greater granulation-tissue burden guides wound-care decisions, from debridement to delayed closure. Hepatocytes' stable-cell regenerative capacity is why partial hepatectomy and toxic liver injury can be followed by substantial hepatic regrowth, a property exploited in living-donor liver transplantation. Understanding fibrous tissue remodeling explains why a scar's strength continues to increase for months after a wound appears closed, and why premature stress on a healing wound risks dehiscence. Recognising gout and hemochromatosis as distinct metabolic disorders, purine versus iron, guides the correct diagnostic work-up (serum urate versus iron studies) and treatment (urate-lowering therapy versus phlebotomy or chelation). Recognising that cellulitis is not a staphylococcal disease helps target empirical antibiotic choice toward covering Streptococcus pyogenes. Bone marrow compensatory hyperplasia after hemorrhage is the physiological basis for reticulocytosis seen on a peripheral smear during recovery from acute blood loss. Recognising Barrett oesophagus matters because it is a premalignant lesion that raises the risk of oesophageal adenocarcinoma, guiding surveillance endoscopy and biopsy in patients with long-standing reflux disease.

## summary
Nine End Foundation 2 Batch 43 parasitology facts: Hymenolepis nana as the intestinal cestode capable of autoinfection; the confirmed Heterophyes heterophyes life-cycle hosts (snail, fish, human, cat/dog) against a copepod, which belongs to a different fluke's cycle; Trichomonas vaginalis's fomite (shared-towel) transmission route alongside its sexual route; Giardia lamblia's steatorrhoea; physical/environmental versus chemical arthropod control methods; Anopheles as the exclusive vector of human malaria; propagative transmission as multiplication alone inside a vector; Ascaris lumbricoides as a cause of mechanical intestinal obstruction; and complete (holometabolous) metamorphosis's marked immature-versus-adult difference.

## sections
### Definition
Hymenolepis nana is the one intestinal cestode among those tested here able to complete its life cycle inside a single human host, a process called autoinfection. Heterophyes heterophyes moves through a snail first intermediate host, a brackish-water fish second intermediate host, and humans or fish-eating mammals as definitive/reservoir hosts, never a copepod. Trichomonas vaginalis, a trophozoite-only protozoan, spreads chiefly by sexual contact but can also spread by shared damp towels or fomites. Giardia lamblia coats the duodenal mucosa and produces steatorrhoea, a light-coloured greasy stool. Arthropod control divides into physical/environmental methods (source reduction, barriers) and chemical methods (insecticides). Anopheles mosquitoes are the sole vector of human malaria. Propagative transmission is multiplication of a parasite alone inside its vector, without any change in form. Ascaris lumbricoides, a large roundworm, can mechanically obstruct the bowel in heavy infection. Complete metamorphosis describes insects whose immature and adult stages differ markedly in form, habitat and behaviour.

### Mechanism
H. nana's autoinfection begins when an ingested or already-hatched egg releases an oncosphere that penetrates a small-intestinal villus, develops into a cysticercoid larva within the villus, then re-emerges into the lumen to mature into an adult worm whose own eggs can hatch again in the same bowel, requiring no external host and no new exposure. Heterophyes heterophyes's cercariae, released from the snail, seek out and encyst as metacercariae in the flesh of a brackish-water fish; humans and fish-eating mammals become infected by eating this fish raw or undercooked, and no stage of the parasite ever uses a copepod. Trichomonas vaginalis trophozoites, lacking a resistant cyst stage, can nonetheless survive briefly outside the body in a moist, warm environment such as a damp shared towel, allowing incidental transfer to a new host's genital mucosa. Giardia trophozoites attach in large numbers along the duodenal and proximal jejunal brush border, physically interfering with the mucosal surface area available for fat digestion and absorption, so unabsorbed fat passes through as pale, greasy stool. Physical arthropod control acts by removing breeding habitat (stagnant water, sewage) or mechanically excluding the arthropod (nets, screens), while chemical control (insecticides such as DDT) acts through direct toxicity to the arthropod's nervous system. Anopheles females inject Plasmodium sporozoites directly into the bloodstream during a blood meal, completing the sporogonic cycle that only this genus among mosquitoes supports; other mosquito-borne pathogens use Culex or Aedes instead. In propagative transmission, the parasite simply increases in number within the vector without developmental change, contrasted with cyclodevelopmental transmission (form change alone), cyclopropagative transmission (both), mechanical transmission (neither) and transovarian transmission (passage to the vector's own offspring). A heavy Ascaris burden can twist into a tangled bolus of worms large enough to occlude the narrow lumen of the small bowel, especially in children. Complete (holometabolous) metamorphosis proceeds through egg, larva, pupa and adult stages that look and live differently at each stage, unlike incomplete (hemimetabolous) metamorphosis's egg-nymph-adult sequence, where the nymph already resembles a small adult.

### Key determinants
What determines whether a parasite is capable of autoinfection is whether it can complete a full developmental cycle inside one host without leaving the body, a property H. nana has and its close relatives (which require an arthropod intermediate host) lack. What determines a fluke's exact intermediate-host sequence is the species itself, not a generic "trematode" rule, since Heterophyes heterophyes's snail-then-fish sequence differs from Diphyllobothrium latum's copepod-then-fish sequence. What determines whether Trichomonas vaginalis transmission counts as fomite spread rather than sexual spread is the route of contact, not the organism's biology, which is unchanged either way. What determines steatorrhoea versus dysentery in a protozoal gut infection is whether the organism causes malabsorption (Giardia) or mucosal ulceration (Entamoeba histolytica). What determines whether an arthropod control method is physical or chemical is its mechanism of action, not its target or its effectiveness. What determines which mosquito genus is the relevant vector for a given disease is the specific pathogen-vector pairing evolved for that disease, not simply which mosquito is locally common. What determines propagative versus cyclopropagative vector transmission is whether the parasite develops (changes form) inside the vector in addition to multiplying. What determines whether Ascaris causes obstruction rather than anaemia is worm burden and bulk, in contrast to hookworm's blood-feeding mechanism of harm. What determines complete versus incomplete metamorphosis is whether the immature stage resembles the adult in form and habitat.

### Clinical significance
Recognising H. nana's capacity for autoinfection explains why its infections can intensify and persist without reinfection from outside, guiding both treatment (a full course, since intraluminal autoinfection can outlast a single dose) and hygiene counselling. Knowing Heterophyes heterophyes's fish-borne route guides dietary counselling (thoroughly cooking brackish-water fish) in endemic areas. Recognising Trichomonas vaginalis's fomite route, alongside its sexual route, informs both partner treatment and simple hygiene advice (not sharing damp towels) during an outbreak. Recognising steatorrhoea as a Giardia-pattern symptom, distinct from dysenteric bleeding, guides the correct stool workup and treatment choice. Choosing the correct arthropod control method, physical source reduction versus chemical insecticide, matters for vector-control programme design and for avoiding unnecessary chemical exposure. Recognising Anopheles as the only relevant vector focuses malaria-control interventions (nets, indoor residual spraying) specifically on this genus rather than mosquitoes generally. Understanding propagative transmission is foundational to understanding how vector-borne bacterial diseases such as plague amplify within their arthropod vector. Recognising Ascaris-related bowel obstruction as a surgical emergency in a heavily infected (often paediatric) patient is important for timely intervention. Recognising complete metamorphosis explains why larvicidal measures (targeting the aquatic larval stage) are a mainstay of mosquito control, since the larva is both accessible and biologically distinct from the flying adult.
