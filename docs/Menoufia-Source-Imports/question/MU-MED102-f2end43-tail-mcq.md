# Item

## id
QST-MUMED102-F2END43-F2END43-Q50

## title
Autoinfection among intestinal cestodes

## question
Autoinfection is a mode of infection of which one of the following parasites.

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Ascaris lumbricoides.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides is acquired by ingesting embryonated eggs from soil after a period of external development; it has no internal cycle that allows the same host to re-infect itself, so autoinfection is not described for this nematode.

## answer_b
Dipylidium caninum.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dipylidium caninum requires an intermediate host (the flea) in which the cysticercoid larva develops before a new host can be infected by swallowing the flea; the human cannot become re-infected from eggs passed by their own worm without that intermediate step, so it is not an autoinfecting parasite.

## answer_c
Heterophyes heterophyes.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Heterophyes heterophyes requires two intermediate hosts in sequence, a snail and then a brackish-water fish, before its metacercariae become infective; a human cannot re-infect themselves directly, so autoinfection does not apply.

## answer_d
Hymenolepis diminuta.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hymenolepis diminuta, unlike its close relative H. nana, requires an arthropod intermediate host (a grain beetle or flea) for its cysticercoid stage to develop; humans are accidental hosts infected by swallowing the insect, and no internal re-infection cycle exists.

## answer_e
Hymenolepis nana.

## explanation_e
Correct. Hymenolepis nana is unique among the tapeworms tested here in being able to complete its entire life cycle inside a single human host: an egg hatching in the intestine releases an oncosphere that penetrates the villus, develops into a cysticercoid, and re-emerges into the lumen to mature into an adult, whose eggs can then hatch again within the same bowel without ever leaving the body. This internal, self-perpetuating cycle is autoinfection, and it is why H. nana infections can persist and intensify without any new external exposure.

## title
Reservoir host definition

## question
It is an animal that harbors the mature stage of the parasite and acts as a source of infection:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Accidental host.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An accidental host is one a parasite does not normally use at all, infecting it only occasionally and often without completing its normal development there; the stem instead describes an animal that regularly harbours the parasite's mature stage and serves as a recognised source of human infection.

## answer_b
Definitive host.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A definitive host harbours the parasite's adult or sexual stage, but the term on its own does not require that host to be a non-human animal serving as a source of infection for humans; the stem's emphasis on "source of infection" for a separate species is the reservoir host's defining feature, which this exam's own key credits.

## answer_c
Intermediate host.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An intermediate host is where the parasite's larval developmental stages occur, not where the mature (adult) stage is harboured; this option describes the wrong stage of the life cycle.

## answer_d
Reservoir host.

## explanation_d
Correct. A reservoir host is a non-human animal that harbours the same parasite species that infects humans, carrying its mature stage, without itself necessarily being diseased, and by doing so serves as an additional source from which humans can become infected. This is exactly the two-part description the stem gives: harbouring the mature stage, and acting as a source of infection.

## answer_e
Vector.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A vector is an arthropod (or other organism) that transmits a parasite between hosts, often biologically supporting part of its development, but a vector is not itself typically described as harbouring the parasite's mature stage as a source of infection in the reservoir sense.

## title
Schistosomes as the non-hermaphrodite trematode

## question
Most trematodes are hermaphrodites except. .....

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Fasciola hepatica.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fasciola hepatica, like most flukes, is hermaphroditic, with each adult worm carrying both male and female reproductive organs.

## answer_b
Fashiola gigantica.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fasciola gigantica shares the same hermaphroditic reproductive pattern as its close relative F. hepatica; it is not the exception the stem asks for.

## answer_c
Heterophyes heterophyes.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Heterophyes heterophyes is hermaphroditic like the great majority of trematodes; it is not the exception.

## answer_d
Paragonimus westermani.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Paragonimus westermani is likewise hermaphroditic; it follows the general trematode pattern rather than departing from it.

## answer_e
Schistosomes.

## explanation_e
Correct. Schistosoma species are the classic exception to trematode hermaphroditism: they have separate sexes (are dioecious), and the slender female resides within a longitudinal groove on the male's ventral surface, the two worms pairing in copula for the duration of adult life. This dioecious pairing is a frequently tested distinguishing feature of the genus.

## title
Heterophyes heterophyes life-cycle host chain

## question
Heterophyes heterophyes has a complex life cycle involving several hosts. Which of the following is NOT a part of this life cycle?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cat.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cats and dogs act as reservoir hosts for Heterophyes heterophyes, becoming infected by eating the same raw or undercooked brackish-water fish that transmits the parasite to humans; the cat is part of this life cycle.

## answer_b
Cyclop

## explanation_b
Correct. A cyclop (copepod) is the first intermediate host used by parasites such as Diphyllobothrium latum, not by Heterophyes heterophyes. H. heterophyes instead uses a snail as its first intermediate host and a brackish-water fish (classically mullet) as its second intermediate host; a copepod plays no role anywhere in its life cycle, making it the one host not part of this chain.

## answer_c
Fish.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A brackish/coastal-water fish, classically the mullet, is the second intermediate host of Heterophyes heterophyes, where cercariae released from the snail encyst as metacercariae; humans acquire infection by eating this fish raw or undercooked.

## answer_d
Human.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Humans are the definitive host of Heterophyes heterophyes, harbouring the adult intestinal fluke after eating infected raw or undercooked fish.

## answer_e
Snail.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A snail is the first intermediate host of Heterophyes heterophyes, where the miracidium develops through its larval stages before cercariae are released into the water to seek the fish second intermediate host.

## title
Trichomonas vaginalis fomite transmission

## question
Choose the right statement according to Trichomonas vaginalis:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Both trophozoites and cysts are infected.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Trichomonas vaginalis exists only as a trophozoite; it has no cyst stage in its life cycle, so "both trophozoites and cysts" misstates its biology.

## answer_b
Dogs are intermediate host for T. Vaginalis.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Trichomonas vaginalis has no animal reservoir or intermediate host; it is transmitted directly between humans, chiefly by sexual contact, with no dog or other animal host involved.

## answer_c
Infection occurs by eating green salad contaminated with cysts

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Trichomonas vaginalis is not transmitted by ingestion of food and has no cyst stage to survive on contaminated salad; this describes a faeco-oral protozoan route such as Giardia's, not T. vaginalis.

## answer_d
It is transmitted via penetration of the skin.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Trichomonas vaginalis does not have a skin-penetrating infective stage; it is a fragile trophozoite transmitted by direct mucosal contact, not by penetrating intact skin.

## answer_e
Using infected contaminated towels help in transmission.

## explanation_e
Correct. Trichomonas vaginalis trophozoites can survive for a limited time outside the body in a moist, warm environment, unlike many protozoa that require a resistant cyst stage to survive externally. Because of this, sharing contaminated damp towels, underwear or other fomites is recognised as a non-sexual route that can help transmit infection. This adds to, rather than replaces, the classic sexual route of transmission.

## title
Giardia lamblia and steatorrhoea

## question
If the patient noticed that his stool became light-coloured and greasy, what is the probable causative protozoon?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Entamoeba coli.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Entamoeba coli is a non-pathogenic commensal of the large bowel and does not cause malabsorption or steatorrhoea.

## answer_b
Entamoeba histolytica.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Entamoeba histolytica classically causes bloody, mucus-containing dysentery from colonic ulceration, not the pale, greasy, foul-smelling stool of fat malabsorption.

## answer_c
Giardia lamblia.

## explanation_c
Correct. Giardia lamblia trophozoites attach to and coat the duodenal and jejunal mucosa, interfering with fat digestion and absorption. The result is steatorrhoea, a light-coloured, greasy, foul-smelling stool that reflects unabsorbed fat. This is a classic presentation of giardiasis, often accompanied by bloating and abdominal cramps.

Heterophyes heterophyes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Heterophyes heterophyes is an intestinal fluke that can cause diarrhoea and abdominal pain in heavy infections, but light-coloured greasy stool (steatorrhoea) is not its classic presenting feature.

## answer_e
Trichomonas vaginalis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Trichomonas vaginalis is a urogenital, not intestinal, parasite and does not cause gastrointestinal malabsorption or steatorrhoea.

## title
Arthropod control: physical versus chemical methods

## question
All of the following is physical method for arthropod control except.....

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Elimination of stagnant water.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Eliminating stagnant water removes mosquito breeding sites through environmental modification, a physical/environmental control method, not a chemical one.

## answer_b
Proper disposal of sewage and garbage.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Proper disposal of sewage and garbage denies arthropods breeding and feeding material through sanitation, a physical/environmental method.

## answer_c
Using of bed nets.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bed nets are a physical barrier that mechanically prevents arthropods from reaching and biting a host.

## answer_d
Using of door & window screens

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Door and window screens are a physical barrier method, mechanically excluding flying arthropods from indoor spaces.

## answer_e
Using of insecticides like DDT and arsenical compounds.

## explanation_e
Correct. Insecticides such as DDT and arsenical compounds control arthropods by chemical toxicity, killing or repelling them through a poisoning action. This is fundamentally different from physical or environmental methods, which remove breeding sites or mechanically bar arthropods from reaching a host. Because every other option here works by elimination, sanitation or a physical barrier, the insecticide option is the one method that is chemical rather than physical.

## title
Propagative transmission definition

## question
Only multiplication of the parasite takes place inside the vector is called...............

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cyclodevelopmental transmission.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cyclodevelopmental transmission describes the parasite undergoing developmental change (a change in form or stage) inside the vector, without multiplying in numbers, the opposite pattern to the one described.

## answer_b
Cyclopropagative transmission.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cyclopropagative transmission describes the parasite both developing (changing form) and multiplying inside the vector, combining both processes, not multiplication alone.

## answer_c
Mechanical transmission.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mechanical transmission describes a vector simply carrying a parasite on its body parts or mouthparts without the parasite developing or multiplying inside it at all.

## answer_d
Propagative transmission.

## explanation_d
Correct. Propagative transmission describes a vector in which the parasite only multiplies in number, without undergoing any developmental change in form, before being passed on to a new host. This is exactly the pattern the stem describes: multiplication alone, with no cyclical change in the organism's morphology. Yersinia pestis multiplying within the flea gut is a classic example of this purely propagative pattern.

## answer_e
Transovarian transmission.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Transovarian transmission describes a pathogen passing from an infected arthropod to its own offspring through the egg, a route of transmission to the next generation of vector rather than a description of what happens to the parasite inside a single vector.

Organisms live independent on the host, but may occasionally be parasitic under certain conditions, they are:

## subject

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ectoparasite.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An ectoparasite is classified by where it lives (on the host's external surface), not by whether it can also live independently of a host.

## answer_b
Facultative parasite.

## explanation_b
Correct. A facultative parasite is an organism capable of a free-living existence that can also become parasitic when the opportunity or the right conditions arise. It does not depend on a host to complete its life cycle, unlike an obligate parasite. Free-living amoebae such as Naegleria fowleri and Acanthamoeba are classic examples, normally living independently in soil or water but capable of causing serious human infection under the right circumstances.

## answer_c
Obligatory parasite.

Incorrect. An obligatory (obligate) parasite cannot complete its life cycle without a host and has no free-living alternative, the opposite of the independent-but-occasionally-parasitic pattern described.

## answer_d
Permanent parasite.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A permanent parasite remains on or in its host for its entire parasitic life once established, a description of duration of host residence, not of a free-living-versus-parasitic alternative.

## answer_e
Specific parasite.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A specific parasite is one restricted to a particular host species, a description of host range, not of whether it can also live independently.

## question
A 40 years old male with 3 years history of acid reflux, came to hospital with increased severity of acid reflux. ## correct_answer
C

Transformation of squamous epithelium to transitional epithelium.

## explanation_a

## answer_b
Transformation of columnar epithelium to squamous epithelium.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This reverses the actual direction of change and names the wrong destination tissue; chronic reflux does not convert columnar epithelium to squamous, and squamous is not the metaplastic outcome here.

Transformation of transitional epithelium to columnar epithelium.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Transitional-to-columnar metaplasia describes a different anatomical context (such as parts of the urinary tract) and is not the change produced by oesophageal reflux.

## answer_e
Transformation of intestinal epithelium to squamous epithelium.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. This reverses the true direction of change; chronic reflux converts squamous epithelium toward an intestinal (columnar) phenotype, not the other way around.
