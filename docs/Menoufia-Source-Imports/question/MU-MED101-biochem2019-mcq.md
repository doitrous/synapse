## title
Which of the following is a tripeptide?

## question
Which of the following is a tripeptide?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Anserine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Anserine (beta-alanyl-N-methylhistidine) is a dipeptide, not a tripeptide -- two amino acid residues, not three.

## answer_b
Oxytocin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Oxytocin is a nonapeptide hormone -- nine amino acid residues, released by the posterior pituitary -- far larger than a tripeptide.

## answer_c
Glutathione

## explanation_c
Correct. Glutathione is the standard tripeptide example: it is built from three amino acids, glutamic acid, cysteine and glycine, linked by peptide bonds (with an unusual gamma-linkage between the glutamate and cysteine residues). This three-residue composition is exactly what makes it a tripeptide, distinguishing it from the dipeptide, larger oligopeptide and single amino acid named among the other options.

## answer_d
Kallidin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Kallidin is a decapeptide kinin -- ten amino acid residues -- not a tripeptide.

## answer_e
Glycine

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Glycine is a single free amino acid, not a peptide at all -- no peptide bond links it to any other residue.

## subject

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Valine is a purely glucogenic branched-chain amino acid, not ketogenic.

## answer_b
Cysteine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cysteine is glucogenic (converted to pyruvate), not ketogenic.

## answer_c
Leucine

## explanation_c
Correct. Leucine and lysine are the only two amino acids that are purely ketogenic, with no glucogenic pathway at all -- their carbon skeletons are degraded entirely to acetyl-CoA and/or acetoacetyl-CoA (ketone-body precursors), so they cannot be used for net gluconeogenesis. This makes leucine one of the two named exceptions among the amino acids, most of which are glucogenic or mixed.

## answer_d
Threonine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Threonine is glucogenic only, not ketogenic.

## answer_e
Histidine

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Histidine is a glucogenic amino acid, not ketogenic.

## title
The primary structure of protein represents

## question
The primary structure of protein represents

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Linear sequence of amino acids joined by peptide bond

## explanation_a
Correct. The primary structure of a protein is its linear amino-acid sequence, held together by peptide bonds formed during protein biosynthesis; the sequence of any given protein is specific to that protein and is the first of the four structural orders (primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary) from which the higher orders subsequently fold.

## answer_b
3-dimensional structure of protein

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A protein's overall 3-dimensional fold of a single chain is its tertiary structure, not its primary structure.

## answer_c
Helical structure of protein

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A helical (or beta-sheet) folding pattern is a form of secondary structure, not primary structure.

## answer_d
Sub unit structure of protein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The assembly of multiple separate polypeptide chains into one functional protein is quaternary structure, not primary structure.

## answer_e
Alpha helix

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The alpha helix is one specific example of secondary structure, not the primary structure.

## title
Amino acid carrying a net positive charge at physiological pH

## question
Which out of the following amino acids carries a net positive charge at the physiological pH?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Valine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Valine has a neutral, nonpolar side chain and carries no net charge at physiological pH.

## answer_b
Leucine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Leucine has a neutral, nonpolar side chain and carries no net charge at physiological pH.

## answer_c
Isolucine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Isoleucine has a neutral, nonpolar side chain and carries no net charge at physiological pH.

## answer_d
Lysine

## explanation_d
Correct. Lysine's side chain carries a primary amino group with a pKa around 10.5, keeping it protonated and positively charged across the whole physiological pH range. Lysine, arginine and histidine make up the three basic amino acids, but only lysine and arginine are reliably fully protonated at pH 7.4, which is why lysine residues so often sit on a protein's surface interacting with negatively charged phosphate backbones or forming salt bridges with acidic residues elsewhere in the protein.

## answer_e
Aspartic acid

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Aspartic acid is an acidic amino acid -- its side-chain carboxyl group is ionised at physiological pH, giving it a net negative charge, the opposite of what the question asks for.
