# Item

QST-MUMED105-END43-Q01

## title
Character of cardiac muscle fibres

## question
Which one of the followings is a character of cardiac muscle fibers?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Contain few mitochondria

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cardiac muscle fibres are packed with mitochondria (up to 40% of cell volume), reflecting their continuous, fatigue-resistant workload — the opposite of "few mitochondria," which better describes a cell built for brief, anaerobic bursts rather than lifelong rhythmic contraction.

## answer_b
Has intercalated disk

## explanation_b
Correct. Intercalated discs are step-like junctions crossing cardiac muscle fibres at the Z-lines, joining one myocyte to the next end to end. Their transverse portion (fascia adherens and desmosomes) mechanically anchors neighbouring cells, while their lateral portion (gap junctions) electrically couples them, letting cardiac muscle act as a functional syncytium. No other listed option is a genuine cardiac-muscle-fibre feature.

## answer_c
Nuclei are multiple & peripheral

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cardiac muscle fibres typically have a single, central nucleus (occasionally two), not multiple peripheral nuclei — multiple peripheral nuclei is instead the hallmark of skeletal muscle fibres, which are large multinucleated syncytial cells formed by myoblast fusion.

## answer_d
Spindle shaped fibers

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cardiac muscle fibres are branching and roughly cylindrical, not spindle-shaped — a spindle shape (tapering at both ends, single central nucleus) is instead characteristic of smooth muscle cells.

## answer_e
Regenerates by satellite cell mitosis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cardiac muscle fibres are terminally differentiated and essentially do not regenerate by mitosis of any kind, satellite-cell-mediated or otherwise; satellite-cell-mediated regeneration is a skeletal muscle property, not a cardiac one, which is why myocardial infarction heals by fibrous scarring rather than muscle regrowth.

## title
Aging pigment in cardiac muscle fibres

## question
With advance of age, cardiac muscle fibers shows an increase in which one of followings?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ribosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ribosome content reflects a cell's protein-synthesis activity, not its age; cardiac myocytes do not show a specific age-related rise in ribosome number the way they do with lipofuscin.

## answer_b
Lipofuscin pigment

## explanation_b
Correct. Lipofuscin is a yellowish-brown, autofluorescent lipid-protein pigment that accumulates in long-lived, post-mitotic cells like cardiac myocytes as a byproduct of lysosomal degradation of damaged organelles. Because cardiac muscle essentially never divides, this residue is never diluted by cell turnover and instead builds up steadily across a lifetime, making it a recognised "wear and tear" marker of age in histological sections.

## answer_c
Intercalated discs

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Intercalated discs are a structural, junctional feature present throughout life at a roughly constant density; they are not an accumulating age marker the way a pigment residue is.

## answer_d
T-tubules

## explanation_d
Incorrect. T-tubules are a fixed structural feature of the excitation-contraction coupling apparatus, not a substance that progressively accumulates with age in the way lipofuscin does.

## answer_e
Purkinge fibers

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Purkinje fibres are a distinct cell population of the conducting system, not a substance or feature whose "amount" within ordinary cardiac muscle fibres increases with age.

## title
Characteristic of Purkinje muscle fibres

## question
Which one of the followings is a characteristic of purkinje muscle fibers?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Intercalated discs are present

## explanation_a
Incorrect. While Purkinje fibres do have some junctional contacts, the option's phrasing does not capture what specifically distinguishes them from ordinary cardiac muscle on a slide — their pale staining is the classic distinguishing histological feature examiners test.

## answer_b
Diad tubular system

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The diad tubular system is a feature of ordinary working cardiac muscle's excitation-contraction apparatus; Purkinje fibres are instead notable for having comparatively few T-tubules and a sparse contractile apparatus, since their job is conduction, not contraction.

## answer_c
small quantities of glycogen

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Purkinje fibres contain abundant, not small, quantities of glycogen — glycogen is in fact so dense that it dissolves out during routine tissue processing, leaving the characteristic pale, vacuolated appearance.

## answer_d
Pale staining

## explanation_d
Correct. Purkinje fibres are modified cardiac muscle cells of the peripheral conducting system that stain pale because they contain few myofibrils (concentrated peripherally) and abundant glycogen (which dissolves during processing, producing a vacuolated look). This pale staining, together with their larger size, is what lets them be recognised histologically against ordinary, more darkly-staining working myocardium.

## answer_e
smaller than ordinary cardiac muscle fibers

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Purkinje fibres are larger, not smaller, than ordinary cardiac muscle fibres — their greater diameter is part of what supports their high conduction velocity.

## title
Location of T-tubules in cardiac muscle

## question
What is the location of T-tubules in cardiac muscle?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
A-I junction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The A-I junction is where T-tubules are located in skeletal muscle, not cardiac muscle — this is a classic distractor built on the skeletal-muscle rule being wrongly applied to cardiac tissue.

## answer_b
Intercalated disk

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The intercalated disc is a distinct cell-to-cell junction at the Z-line, a different structure from the T-tubule, which is an invagination of the sarcolemma running into the fibre's interior, not a junction between cells.

## answer_c
Z-line

## explanation_c
Correct. T-tubules are invaginations of the sarcolemma that carry the surface action potential deep into the muscle fibre. In cardiac muscle, they are located at the Z-lines, one per sarcomere. They are also wider in calibre than skeletal muscle's own T-tubules, which instead sit at the A-I junction.

## answer_d
A band

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The A band is the region of overlapping thick and thin filaments in the sarcomere; it is not where T-tubules open into the fibre in either cardiac or skeletal muscle.

## answer_e
I band

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The I band is the region containing only thin filaments; T-tubules run near the Z-line at the I band's centre, but the anatomically precise, tested location is the Z-line itself, not the I band as a whole.

## title
Cardiac muscle tubular system

## question
Which one of the followings is true concerning the tubular system in cardiac muscle cells?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Consists of two terminal cisternae of rough endoplasmic reticulum & one T-tubule

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This option misnames the organelle (rough endoplasmic reticulum, not sarcoplasmic reticulum) and misstates the count (two cisternae, describing skeletal muscle's triad) — cardiac muscle uses sarcoplasmic reticulum specifically, and only one cisterna per T-tubule.

## answer_b
Consists of one terminal cisterna of sarcoplasmic reticulum & one T-tubule

## explanation_b
Correct. Cardiac muscle's sarcoplasmic reticulum is less well developed than skeletal muscle's. Each T-tubule couples with only one terminal cisterna of sarcoplasmic reticulum. This forms a diad, rather than skeletal muscle's triad of one T-tubule flanked by two cisternae.

## answer_c
Consists of two terminal cisternae of sarcoplasmic reticulum & one T-tubule

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "Two terminal cisternae" describes skeletal muscle's triad arrangement, not cardiac muscle's diad — cardiac muscle has only one cisterna per T-tubule junction.

## answer_d
Consists of one terminal cisterna of rough endoplasmic reticulum & one T-tubule

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This option correctly gives the single-cisterna count but misnames the organelle: the relevant membrane system is sarcoplasmic reticulum (specialised for calcium storage/release), not rough endoplasmic reticulum (specialised for protein synthesis).

## answer_e
Consists of one terminal cisterna of sarcoplasmic reticulum & one of rough endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Rough endoplasmic reticulum is not part of the calcium-handling tubular system at all; the diad is formed by one T-tubule and one sarcoplasmic-reticulum cisterna, not by two different reticulum types.

## title
Structure medial to the femoral artery

## question
Which one of the following structures lies immediately medial to the femoral artery at the base of the femoral triangle?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Femoral vein

## explanation_a
Correct. At the base of the femoral triangle, the femoral nerve, artery and vein sit in that order from lateral to medial ("NAVEL": Nerve, Artery, Vein, Empty space, Lymphatics). The structure immediately medial to the femoral artery is therefore the femoral vein. Both vessels share the femoral sheath, with the vein enclosed in its own medial compartment.

## answer_b
Femoral nerve

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The femoral nerve lies lateral to the femoral artery, not medial to it — it is the most lateral of the three main neurovascular structures at this level and sits outside the femoral sheath entirely.

## answer_c
Lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh runs well lateral to the femoral triangle's vessels, entering the thigh near the anterior superior iliac spine, far from the artery's medial border.

## answer_d
Lymph node of cloquet

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The lymph node of Cloquet (Rosenmüller) sits within the femoral canal, medial to the femoral vein — one compartment further medial than the vein itself, not immediately medial to the artery.

## answer_e
Lacunar ligament

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The lacunar ligament forms the medial boundary of the femoral ring, well medial to the femoral vein and canal; it is not the structure immediately adjacent to the femoral artery.

## title
Origin of the peroneal artery

## question
The peroneal artery is a branch of which of the following arteries?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Anterior tibial artery

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The anterior tibial artery is a separate terminal branch of the popliteal artery running in the anterior compartment; it does not give off the peroneal artery, which arises from the posterior tibial artery instead.

## answer_b
Posterior tibial artery

## explanation_b
Correct. The popliteal artery divides into the anterior tibial artery and the posterior tibial artery. A short distance below its own origin, the posterior tibial artery gives off the peroneal (fibular) artery as its largest branch. This artery runs along the fibula, supplying the lateral and deep posterior compartments of the leg and the fibula's own nutrient artery.

## answer_c
Popliteal artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The popliteal artery gives rise to the anterior and posterior tibial arteries directly, not to the peroneal artery — the peroneal artery is one step further down the branching sequence, from the posterior tibial artery.

## answer_d
Femoral artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The femoral artery lies proximal to the knee and becomes the popliteal artery; it does not directly give off the peroneal artery, which only arises after two further divisions in the leg.

## answer_e
Profunda femoris artery

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The profunda femoris artery is a thigh vessel supplying the adductor and hamstring muscles; it has no direct continuity with the leg's peroneal artery.

## title
Direction of tricuspid regurgitation

## question
A 45-year-old female is admitted to the hospital with swelling (edema) of the lower limbs. Ultrasound examination reveals an incompetent tricuspid valve. Into which area will regurgitation of blood occur in this patient?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pulmonary trunk

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The pulmonary trunk is downstream of the right ventricle, in the direction of normal forward flow — regurgitation through an incompetent tricuspid valve moves backward, upstream, not further forward into the pulmonary circulation.

## answer_b
Left atrium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The left atrium is on the opposite (systemic, left-heart) side of the circulation from the tricuspid valve, which sits between the right atrium and right ventricle; it cannot receive tricuspid regurgitant flow.

## answer_c
Ascending aorta

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The ascending aorta is downstream of the left ventricle, entirely unconnected to the right-sided tricuspid valve's own inflow/outflow path.

## answer_d
Right atrium

## explanation_d
Correct. The tricuspid valve normally seals the right atrioventricular orifice during ventricular systole so the right ventricle ejects forward into the pulmonary trunk. When it is incompetent, some ventricular blood is instead forced backward into the right atrium — the chamber immediately proximal to the valve — producing systemic venous congestion and the peripheral oedema seen in this patient.

## answer_e
Left ventricle

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The left ventricle is anatomically and functionally separate from the right-sided tricuspid valve (separated by the interventricular septum); it cannot receive tricuspid regurgitant flow.

Venous return.

Correct. Preload is the degree of ventricular wall stretch just before contraction, produced by end-diastolic filling. How much the ventricle fills depends on how much blood flows back to the heart. Venous return is therefore preload's primary determinant.

Stroke volume.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Stroke volume is an OUTPUT of the heart's pumping action, partly determined by preload itself (via Frank-Starling), not the determinant of preload — the causal direction here runs from venous return to preload to stroke volume, not the reverse.

## subject

## answer_a
Decreases intracellular calcium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Amrinone raises, not decreases, intracellular calcium in cardiac myocytes -- the rise in cAMP from its phosphodiesterase III inhibition increases calcium entry through L-type channels, which is what produces its positive inotropic effect.

## answer_b
Decreases cardiac contractility

## explanation_b

## explanation_c

Activates phosphodiesterase

Inhibit phosphodiesterase IV enzyme

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Amrinone inhibits phosphodiesterase III specifically, not phosphodiesterase IV; PDE III is the cardiac/vascular-smooth-muscle isoform responsible for amrinone's inotropic and vasodilator effects.

Positive inotropic effect

The superior and inferior gluteal arteries are branches of which of the following?

## answer_b
Popliteal artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The popliteal artery is the femoral artery's own continuation behind the knee, well distal to and unrelated to the gluteal arteries' pelvic origin.

External iliac artery

Internal iliac artery

Common iliac artery

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The common iliac artery is the parent trunk that divides into the internal and external iliac arteries; the gluteal arteries branch specifically from the internal iliac artery, one step further along, not directly from the common iliac artery.

## title
Effect of sympathetic stimulation on the heart

## question
Which of the following is the effect of sympathetic stimulation to the heart?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Increase in the mean systemic filling pressure.

## explanation_a
Correct. Sympathetic stimulation constricts the systemic veins (venoconstriction), reducing their capacitance and shifting blood out of the low-pressure venous reservoir toward the heart. Because mean systemic filling pressure (MSFP) is set by blood volume relative to vascular capacitance, this venoconstriction raises MSFP even though total blood volume has not changed.

## answer_b
Decrease in the heart rate.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sympathetic stimulation increases, not decreases, heart rate, via beta1-adrenergic action on the SA node; a decrease in heart rate is instead a parasympathetic (vagal) effect.

## answer_c
Collapse of the veins entering the chest.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sympathetic venoconstriction shifts blood toward the chest, increasing venous filling there, not causing the veins entering the chest to collapse.

## answer_d
Decrease in the afterload.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sympathetic stimulation increases, not decreases, afterload, through arteriolar vasoconstriction raising systemic vascular resistance.

## title
Gynecomastia and hyperkalemia side effects

## question
Gynecomastia and hyperkalemia in males are side effects of:

## subject
cvs

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Thiazide diuretic

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Thiazide diuretics characteristically cause hypokalemia, not hyperkalemia, and are not associated with gynecomastia.

## answer_b
Spironolactone

## explanation_b
Correct. Spironolactone, an aldosterone-receptor antagonist, is potassium-sparing and can cause clinically significant hyperkalemia through this mechanism. Mannitol

## explanation_c

Amiloride

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amiloride is a potassium-sparing diuretic that can cause hyperkalemia, but it works by directly blocking collecting-tubule sodium channels rather than by aldosterone-receptor antagonism, and it lacks spironolactone's anti-androgenic/progestogenic activity, so it is not associated with gynecomastia.

## answer_e
Acetazolamide

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Acetazolamide, a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, tends to cause metabolic acidosis and is not associated with hyperkalemia or gynecomastia.

## title
Thrombus type with grossly identifiable lines of Zahn

## question
Which of the following is most likely to have grossly identifiable lines of Zahn:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
arterial Thrombus

## explanation_a
Correct. Lines of Zahn are alternating pale platelet-fibrin layers and darker erythrocyte-rich layers formed in flowing blood. Their lamination is most grossly conspicuous in a thrombus formed under the fast, high-pressure flow of the arterial circulation. That fast flow produces well-organised, clearly visible layers.

## answer_b
capillary Thrombus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Capillary thrombi are far too small in scale for grossly identifiable lamination to be visible.

## answer_c
postmortem Clot

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A postmortem clot forms after death in static, non-flowing blood and characteristically lacks lines of Zahn altogether, instead showing gravity-dependent separation into a dark red ('currant jelly') layer and a paler ('chicken fat') layer -- the opposite of an antemortem, flowing-blood thrombus.

## answer_d
pulmonary congestion.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pulmonary congestion is a passive vascular engorgement, not a thrombus, and has no lines of Zahn to identify.

## answer_e
venous Thrombus

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Venous thrombi form under the much slower, lower-pressure flow of the venous system, so while lines of Zahn can still be present, they are typically far less grossly distinct than in an arterial thrombus formed under fast flow.
