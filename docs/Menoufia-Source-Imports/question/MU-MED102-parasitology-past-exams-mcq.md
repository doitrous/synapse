# Item

QST-MUMED102-PARASITOLOGY-PAST-EXAMS-PARA-PAST-Q01

## title
Transmission with change in form but no multiplication

## question
The organism undergoes cyclical changes, but it does not multiply inside the arthropod. This is called ...... transmission.

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Transporting

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Transporting (mechanical) transmission means the arthropod merely carries the organism on its body surface or in its gut without the organism developing or multiplying at all, the way a housefly carries bacteria on contaminated legs. Here the organism undergoes cyclical (developmental) changes, which mechanical transmission does not involve.

## answer_b
Trans-ovarian

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Trans-ovarian transmission describes passage of the organism from an infected female arthropod to her own offspring through the egg, a route seen with some tick-borne pathogens. It is defined by the generational route of passage, not by whether the organism changes form without multiplying inside a single host arthropod.

## answer_c
Cyclo-developmental

## explanation_c
Correct. Cyclodevelopmental transmission describes a parasite that undergoes a morphological or developmental change inside the vector but does not increase in number there — exactly the pattern described, cyclical changes without multiplication. This is the pattern seen, for example, with microfilariae developing into infective larvae inside a mosquito.

## answer_d
Cyclo-propagative

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cyclo-propagative transmission requires the organism to both change form and multiply inside the vector, as Plasmodium does during sporogony in Anopheles. The stem explicitly excludes multiplication, ruling this option out.

## answer_e
Propagative

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Propagative transmission means the organism simply increases in number inside the vector without any change in form, as Yersinia pestis does inside a flea. The stem describes cyclical (developmental) change, not simple multiplication, so propagative does not fit.

QST-MUMED102-PARASITOLOGY-PAST-EXAMS-PARA-PAST-Q02

## title
Gravid segments of pseudophyllidean cestodes

## question
Regarding the gravid segments of pseudophyllidea, which of the following is true?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
They are absent.

## explanation_a
Correct. Pseudophyllidean cestodes such as Diphyllobothrium latum do not shed discrete gravid proglottids the way cyclophyllidean tapeworms do. Instead their uterus opens directly onto the segment surface through a uterine pore, releasing operculated eggs continuously into the lumen rather than releasing whole detached gravid segments. True gravid segments are therefore absent from this life cycle.

## answer_b
They contain mature genital organs.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Mature genital organs are a feature of proglottids generally, including the mature (not gravid) segments of pseudophyllideans, but since discrete gravid segments are not shed at all in this order, describing 'their' contents is not the relevant distinguishing fact tested here.

## answer_c
They can be detected in stool.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Because gravid segments are not shed, it is operculated eggs — not segments — that are detected in stool examination for pseudophyllidean infection, which is precisely the opposite of what this option claims.

## answer_d
They can be considered as diagnostic stages.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Since gravid segments are absent in this order, they cannot serve as a diagnostic stage; the diagnostic stage for pseudophyllidean infection is instead the operculated egg passed directly through the uterine pore.

## answer_e
They are broader than longer.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. 'Broader than longer' describes the shape of gravid proglottids in some cyclophyllidean tapeworms (such as Taenia saginata's segments, which are longer than broad, in contrast to some others), not a feature of pseudophyllidean biology, where discrete gravid segments do not occur in the first place.

## title
Organism passing undigested through the intestine

## question
Foreign organism that passes through the human intestine without causing any disease, is a/an ...... parasite.

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Coprozoic

## explanation_a
Correct. A coprozoic organism is a free-living species that passes through the alimentary tract undigested, appearing in a stool sample as an artefact of examination rather than as evidence of true infection. Exactly the scenario described, a foreign organism transiting the intestine without causing disease.

## answer_b
Opportunistic

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An opportunistic pathogen is normally harmless or low-virulence but causes disease specifically when host defences are impaired (for example in immunosuppression), which is the opposite of an organism that passes through causing no disease under any circumstance being described here.

## answer_c
Nonspecific

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 'Nonspecific' is not a recognised parasitological term for this scenario; it does not describe a defined category of organism found in stool examination the way coprozoic, opportunistic or facultative do.

## answer_d
Endoparasite

## explanation_d
Incorrect. An endoparasite lives inside its host and, by definition as a parasite, causes some degree of harm or draws nutrition at the host's expense; the organism described here causes no disease at all and is not actually infecting the host, so it does not qualify as an endoparasite.

## answer_e
Facultative

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A facultative parasite is one capable of both a free-living and a parasitic existence, actually establishing infection when it does behave parasitically. The organism in this stem never infects the host at all, which is a coprozoic (transient, non-infecting) finding rather than facultative parasitism.

## title
Host harbouring larvae without further development

## question
A host which harbours the larval stage of the parasite without further development is a/an ...... host.

## subject
inf

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Intermediate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The intermediate host is where the parasite's larval stages actively develop further, progressing toward the next infective stage — the opposite of the arrested-development scenario the stem describes.

## answer_b
Definitive

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The definitive (final) host harbours the parasite's adult or sexually reproducing stage, not an arrested larval stage being merely carried onward.

## answer_c
Vector

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A vector is an arthropod that actively transmits a parasite or pathogen between hosts, often supporting some part of its development or multiplication; the stem instead describes a host in which no further development occurs at all.

## answer_d
Reservoir

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A reservoir host is a non-human animal harbouring the same parasite species that infects humans, serving as an additional source of human infection — a role defined by being an alternative source of infection, not by arrested larval development.

## answer_e
Paratenic

## explanation_e
Correct. A paratenic (transfer) host harbours the parasite in an arrested state of development. No further maturation happens there, but the host carries the organism onward until it reaches a host in which development can resume or the definitive host is reached, exactly the scenario in the stem.

Incorrect. A mouth surrounded by an oral sucker is a trematode (fluke) feature, not a nematode one; the nematode mouth instead bears lips, teeth or plates as described in the correct option.

Continuing the same pregnant patient's case (Trichomonas vaginalis infection): What is the mode of infection of this parasite?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Ingestion of the mature cyst

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Trichomonas vaginalis has no cyst stage at all, so ingestion of a 'mature cyst' cannot be its mode of infection.

## answer_b
Ingestion of the mature trophozoite

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Trichomonas vaginalis is not acquired by ingesting a trophozoite; the fragile trophozoite instead spreads by direct genital contact or brief environmental contact via a moist fomite, not through the gastrointestinal route.

## answer_c
Ingestion of the mature oocyst

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An oocyst is the environmentally resistant stage of organisms such as Toxoplasma gondii, not a stage in the Trichomonas vaginalis life cycle, which has no oocyst.

## answer_d
Using of contaminated towels

## explanation_d
Correct. While its principal route is sexual contact, Trichomonas vaginalis trophozoites can survive briefly outside the body in a moist, warm environment. This allows non-sexual transmission by fomites such as shared damp or contaminated towels. The organism has no resistant cyst stage, so this fomite spread depends on the fragile trophozoite surviving briefly in a favourable environment rather than on ingesting any resistant form.

## answer_e
Ingestion of pseudocyst

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A pseudocyst is a tissue-cyst-like stage seen in Toxoplasma gondii bradyzoites, not a stage in the Trichomonas vaginalis life cycle.

## title
Cause of dyspnea via inhalant allergy

## question
Which of the following can be a cause of dyspnea through an allergic (inhalant) mechanism rather than tissue invasion?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Strongyloidiasis.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Strongyloides larvae migrating through the lungs (Loeffler-type pneumonitis) can cause dyspnea, but through direct larval tissue migration and eosinophilic inflammation, not through an inhalant allergic mechanism.

## answer_b
Schistosomiasis.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Schistosomiasis can cause pulmonary symptoms through acute (Katayama) hypersensitivity to migrating schistosomula or through chronic egg-induced pulmonary hypertension, both tissue-based mechanisms rather than inhalant allergy.

## answer_c
Enterobiasis.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis (pinworm) causes perianal itching and does not have a recognised pulmonary migration phase or mechanism causing dyspnea.

## answer_d
Paragonemiasis.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Paragonimus (lung fluke) causes dyspnea and haemoptysis through direct parenchymal and pleural tissue invasion and cyst formation in the lung, not through an inhalant allergic mechanism.

## answer_e
Dust mites.

## explanation_e
Correct. House dust mites are a major perennial inhalant allergen. Their faecal particles and body fragments trigger IgE-mediated allergic reactions in sensitised individuals, presenting as allergic rhinitis and allergic asthma with dyspnea and wheeze. This allergic mechanism is entirely distinct from the tissue-invasive or migratory mechanisms by which helminths and flukes can cause respiratory symptoms.
