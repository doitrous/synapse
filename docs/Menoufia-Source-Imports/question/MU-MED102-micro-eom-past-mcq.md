## title
Structure absent from the bacterial cell

## question
One of the following is NOT present in the bacterial cell:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ribosome.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bacteria are prokaryotes and do have ribosomes, though a smaller 70S type rather than the eukaryotic 80S ribosome. The ribosome is where bacterial protein synthesis happens and is essential to the cell, and it is also the target of several antibiotic classes such as aminoglycosides and macrolides. Because it is present, it cannot be the answer to a NOT-present question.

## answer_b
Plasmid.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Many bacteria carry one or more plasmids, small circular extrachromosomal DNA molecules that are not essential for survival but often carry accessory genes such as antibiotic resistance. Plasmids are a normal, well-documented bacterial structure, even though not every bacterium has one. Since plasmids can and do occur in bacteria, this option is present rather than absent.

## answer_c
Mitochondria.

## explanation_c
Correct. Mitochondria are membrane-bound organelles found only in eukaryotic cells; bacteria are prokaryotes and lack any membrane-bound organelles altogether, generating their ATP instead across the cytoplasmic membrane itself. This absence of internal membrane-bound organelles is one of the defining structural differences between prokaryotic bacteria and eukaryotic cells. No bacterial species possesses mitochondria.

## answer_d
Cell membrane.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Every bacterial cell is bounded by a cytoplasmic (cell) membrane, a phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves the cell and is where oxidative phosphorylation occurs in the absence of mitochondria. This membrane is one of the few structures common to essentially all bacteria. It is clearly present, not absent.

## answer_e
Pili.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Pili (fimbriae) are hair-like surface appendages found on many bacteria, used for adherence to surfaces or, in the case of the sex pilus, for conjugative DNA transfer. Not every bacterium has pili, but they are a recognised and well-described bacterial structure. Their occasional presence means they cannot be the structure that is never found in bacteria.

## title
Composition of the bacterial flagellum

## question
Bacterial flagellum is:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Necessarily used for adherence.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adherence is chiefly the job of fimbriae (pili) and adhesins, not the flagellum, whose primary role is motility. A flagellum can occasionally contribute to attachment in some organisms, but this is not its defining or necessary function. The question asks what the flagellum fundamentally is, and that is a motility organelle, not an adherence structure.

## answer_b
Found in staphylococci.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Staphylococci are non-motile cocci and do not possess flagella; flagella are found on many motile bacilli, both Gram-positive (such as Listeria) and Gram-negative (such as E. coli and Salmonella). Attributing flagella to staphylococci confuses them with a different structure, such as pili, which some staphylococci do carry. This option misassigns the structure to the wrong genus.

## answer_c
Found only in Gram-negative bacteria.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Flagella occur in both Gram-positive bacteria (for example Listeria, Clostridium and Bacillus species) and Gram-negative bacteria (for example E. coli, Salmonella, Vibrio); they are not restricted to one Gram-stain group. Motility by flagella is a trait scattered across many bacterial families rather than defined by Gram reaction. Restricting flagella to Gram-negative organisms alone is therefore incorrect.

## answer_d
Made up of protein.

## explanation_d
Correct. The bacterial flagellum is a helical filament built from repeating subunits of the protein flagellin, assembled into a long filament that is rotated by a basal-body motor embedded in the cell envelope to drive the cell through liquid. This protein composition, and the rotary motor mechanism it depends on, is the structural basis of bacterial motility. Flagellin is also the antigen recognised in H-antigen serotyping schemes.

## answer_e
Important for conjugation.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Conjugation, the transfer of DNA between bacteria, depends on the sex pilus (F pilus), not the flagellum; the two are separate appendages with separate jobs, adherence/DNA-transfer for pili and motility for flagella. Confusing the flagellum with the conjugative pilus is a common distractor pattern in bacterial-structure questions. The flagellum itself plays no role in conjugative gene transfer.

## title
Microscopic diagnosis of Candida albicans

## question
Candida albicans is diagnosed by examination of infected tissues for:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Spherules containing endospores.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Spherules containing endospores are the tissue form of Coccidioides immitis, a dimorphic fungus that causes coccidioidomycosis, not Candida. Confusing the two dimorphic fungi is a classic distractor in mycology questions. Candida does not form spherules in infected tissue.

## answer_b
Metachromatic granules.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Metachromatic granules (volutin granules) are a bacterial storage structure, classically described in Corynebacterium diphtheriae stained with special stains, and have nothing to do with fungal diagnosis. This option mixes a bacteriology finding into a mycology question as a distractor. Candida infected tissue does not show metachromatic granules.

## answer_c
Yeasts and pseudohyphae.

## explanation_c
Correct. Candida albicans is a dimorphic yeast that, on microscopic examination of infected tissue, is recognised by the simultaneous presence of budding yeast cells and pseudohyphae, elongated budding cells that remain attached end to end, mimicking true hyphae without their continuous branching structure. This combined yeast-plus-pseudohyphae picture is the classic diagnostic finding that distinguishes Candida from fungi that show only one form. It is the standard teaching point for tissue diagnosis of candidiasis.

## answer_d
Asci containing 2-8 ascospores.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Asci containing 2-8 ascospores describe the sexual reproductive structure of ascomycete fungi in culture, not a tissue-invasion finding used to diagnose candidiasis at the bedside. This is a mycology culture/taxonomy detail rather than the tissue diagnostic picture asked about here. It is not how Candida is recognised in infected tissue.

## answer_e
Abundance of septate rhizoids.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Broad, non-septate (or sparsely septate) hyphae with right-angle branching are the classic tissue finding for the Mucorales (mucormycosis), not for Candida, which shows pseudohyphae rather than true septate rhizoids. This option borrows a finding from a different, more aggressive fungal infection as a distractor. Candida tissue invasion is not characterised by septate rhizoids.

## title
Pyoderma isolate: beta-haemolytic, catalase-negative

## question
A culture of skin lesions from a patient with pyoderma (impetigo) showed numerous colonies surrounded by zone of beta-haemolysis on a blood agar plate. A Gram-stained smear showed Gram-positive cocci which was catalase test-negative. Name the organism that you MOST probably isolated:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Streptococcus pyogenes.

## explanation_a
Correct. The catalase test is the key discriminator here: all staphylococci are catalase-positive and all streptococci are catalase-negative, so a catalase-negative Gram-positive coccus must be a streptococcus, not a staphylococcus. Combined with the beta-haemolysis on blood agar and the classic impetigo (pyoderma) presentation, this picture is Streptococcus pyogenes (group A strep), the organism most associated with impetigo and beta-haemolytic skin infection. Staphylococcus aureus is the other common cause of impetigo but is catalase-positive, which this culture is not.

## answer_b
Staphylococcus aureus.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Staphylococcus aureus is a very common cause of impetigo and is also typically beta-haemolytic on blood agar, but it is catalase-positive. Since this isolate is explicitly catalase-negative, it cannot be S. aureus, however well the clinical picture otherwise fits. The catalase result is what rules this organism out.

## answer_c
Staphylococcus epidermidis.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Staphylococcus epidermidis is catalase-positive, like all staphylococci, so it is excluded by the catalase-negative result for the same reason as S. aureus. It is also typically non-haemolytic and a skin commensal rather than a classic cause of impetigo. Both the catalase result and the clinical picture argue against it.

## answer_d
Streptococcus pneumoniae.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Streptococcus pneumoniae is catalase-negative like other streptococci, but it is alpha-haemolytic (not beta-haemolytic) and is a cause of pneumonia, otitis media and meningitis, not impetigo or other pyoderma. The haemolysis pattern and the clinical context both point away from this organism.

## answer_e
Enterococcus faecalis.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Enterococcus faecalis is catalase-negative but is typically non-haemolytic (gamma-haemolytic) rather than beta-haemolytic, and it is a cause of urinary tract infection, endocarditis and biliary infection, not a classic skin/impetigo pathogen. The haemolysis pattern and clinical setting do not fit this organism.

## title
Mechanism of naked DNA uptake

## question
Bacteria can take up fragments of naked DNA by:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Conjugation.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Conjugation is the transfer of DNA (typically a plasmid) between two bacterial cells through direct contact via a sex pilus; it requires a living donor cell and cell-to-cell contact, not the uptake of free, naked DNA fragments from the environment. This is a mechanism-of-transfer distractor, since conjugation is one of the three classic gene-transfer routes but is not the one matching "naked DNA fragments".

## answer_b
Transformation.

## explanation_b
Correct. Transformation is the uptake by a recipient bacterium of free, soluble (naked) DNA released into the environment from a donor cell (often after its lysis); it requires the recipient to be in a physiologically competent state and generally needs sequence homology with the recipient genome for stable incorporation. This is precisely the process of taking up naked DNA fragments described in the question, and it does not involve a bacteriophage, a sex pilus, or a restriction endonuclease as the uptake mechanism.

## answer_c
Generalized transduction.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Generalized transduction is the transfer of bacterial DNA between cells via a bacteriophage that mistakenly packages host chromosomal DNA instead of its own genome during lysis; the DNA is delivered inside a phage particle, not as free naked DNA taken up directly from solution. This distractor swaps the vector (phage) for the true naked-DNA mechanism.

## answer_d
Specialized transduction.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Specialized transduction is a related phage-mediated process in which a lysogenic (temperate) phage excises imprecisely and carries adjacent bacterial genes packaged in its own capsid to a new host; like generalized transduction, the DNA moves inside a phage particle rather than as free naked DNA. It is not the mechanism described in the question.

## answer_e
Transcription.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Transcription is the intracellular process of copying DNA into RNA and has nothing to do with the movement of DNA between bacterial cells; it is not a gene-transfer mechanism at all. Including it here tests whether the student can distinguish basic molecular-biology processes from the three classic bacterial gene-transfer routes.

## title
Component unique to the Gram-positive cell wall

## question
One of the following is ONLY found in the cell walls of Gram positive bacteria:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Cytoplasmic membrane.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cytoplasmic membrane is present in both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria; it is a basic component of essentially every bacterial cell envelope, not something restricted to Gram-positive organisms. Since it occurs in both groups, it cannot be the wall component unique to Gram-positive bacteria.

## answer_b
Lipopolysaccharide.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is a component of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria specifically; it is absent from Gram-positive organisms, which lack an outer membrane altogether. This option describes the reciprocal, Gram-negative-only structure, the opposite of what the question asks for.

## answer_c
Outer membrane.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An outer membrane, studded with LPS and porins, is a defining feature of Gram-negative bacteria and is absent in Gram-positive organisms, which have only a single cytoplasmic membrane surrounded by a thick peptidoglycan wall. Like LPS, this is a Gram-negative-specific structure, not a Gram-positive one.

## answer_d
Peptidoglycan.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peptidoglycan is present in both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, though in very different amounts, a thick multilayer in Gram-positive walls versus a thin single layer in Gram-negative walls. Because it occurs, even if thinner, in both groups, it is not unique to Gram-positive organisms.

## answer_e
Teichoic acid.

## explanation_e
Correct. Teichoic acid (and its membrane-anchored form, lipoteichoic acid) is threaded through the thick peptidoglycan wall of Gram-positive bacteria and contributes to wall charge, adhesion and immune activation; Gram-negative bacteria lack it entirely, having an outer membrane and LPS instead. This makes teichoic acid the component genuinely restricted to Gram-positive cell walls among the options given.

## title
Structure that is NOT a Staphylococcus aureus virulence factor

## question
One of the following is NOT a virulence factor in Staphylococcus aureus:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
B-lactamases

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Beta-lactamases are enzymes many Staphylococcus aureus strains produce that hydrolyse the beta-lactam ring of penicillins, allowing the organism to resist these antibiotics; this resistance mechanism is a genuine, well-documented S. aureus virulence/resistance factor. Since it is a real staphylococcal factor, it is not the answer to a NOT-a-virulence-factor question.

## answer_b
Coagulase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Coagulase is a classic S. aureus virulence factor and identification marker; it clots plasma, helping the organism wall itself off from host defences within a fibrin clot, and coagulase-positivity is what distinguishes S. aureus from the coagulase-negative staphylococci. It is a genuine staphylococcal virulence factor and laboratory marker.

## answer_c
Enterotoxins

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Enterotoxins are heat-stable exotoxins produced by many S. aureus strains that act as superantigens and cause staphylococcal food poisoning; they are a well-recognised staphylococcal virulence factor. Their presence in this list is a true positive, not the exception the question is looking for.

## answer_d
M protein

## explanation_d
Correct. M protein is a Streptococcus pyogenes surface virulence factor, an antiphagocytic, antigenically variable protein used for streptococcal serotyping; it is not produced by Staphylococcus aureus at all. Because M protein belongs to a different genus's virulence arsenal, it is the one option on this list that is NOT a staphylococcal virulence factor.

## answer_e
Protein A

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Protein A is a well-known S. aureus surface virulence factor that binds the Fc portion of IgG antibodies, preventing opsonisation and phagocytosis; it is one of the organism's classic immune-evasion mechanisms. It is a genuine staphylococcal factor, not the exception being tested.

## title
Essential structure of the bacterial cell

## question
One of the following is an essential structure of the bacterial cell......

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Capsule.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A capsule is an accessory virulence structure that helps some, but far from all, bacteria evade phagocytosis; many bacterial species are entirely unencapsulated and survive perfectly well. Because it is optional rather than universal, the capsule is not an essential bacterial structure.

## answer_b
Fimbriae (pili).

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fimbriae (pili) assist adherence to surfaces in some bacteria but are not present in, or required by, every bacterial cell; many bacteria lack them entirely. As an accessory rather than a universal structure, fimbriae are not essential to bacterial survival.

## answer_c
Flagella.

## explanation_c

## subject

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Teichoic acid.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Teichoic acid contributes to wall charge, adhesion and immune activation in Gram-positive bacteria, but it is not the load-bearing structural component that gives the wall its mechanical strength against osmotic pressure. That mechanical role belongs to peptidoglycan.

## answer_b
Lipopolysaccharide.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lipopolysaccharide is the immunologically active outer-membrane component of Gram-negative bacteria (its lipid A causes septic shock); it contributes to the outer permeability barrier but is not the rigid, cross-linked layer that mechanically resists osmotic lysis. Peptidoglycan.

## explanation_c
Correct. Phospholipids.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phospholipids are not the structural component that resists osmotic rupture.

## answer_e
Endospores.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Endospores are dormant survival structures formed under adverse conditions such as nutrient depletion, heat or desiccation; they are not a routine structural feature of the actively growing (vegetative) cell and are unrelated to protecting an ordinary vegetative bacterial cell from osmotic lysis. Only spore-forming genera such as Bacillus and Clostridium make them at all.

## title
Classification of botulinum toxin

## question
Botulinum toxin is an example of which ONE of the following:

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Endotoxin.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Endotoxin refers specifically to the lipopolysaccharide of the Gram-negative outer membrane, a structural cell-wall component released mainly on bacterial lysis; botulinum toxin is instead a protein actively secreted by a living Gram-positive organism (Clostridium botulinum), the opposite category from endotoxin.

## answer_b
Lipopolysaccharide.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lipopolysaccharide is the specific chemical structure of Gram-negative endotoxin; botulinum toxin is a secreted polypeptide neurotoxin with no lipopolysaccharide component at all. This option misclassifies the toxin's chemical nature.

## answer_c
Carbohydrate.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Botulinum toxin is a protein (polypeptide), not a carbohydrate; this option misstates its basic chemical class entirely.

## answer_d
Hemolysin.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A hemolysin is a toxin that lyses red blood cells; botulinum toxin's mechanism is instead blocking acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction to cause flaccid paralysis, an entirely different toxic action from haemolysis.

## answer_e
Exotoxin.

## explanation_e
Correct. Botulinum toxin is a polypeptide/protein actively secreted by living Clostridium botulinum bacteria, which by definition makes it an exotoxin, distinguished from the lipid-based, structural endotoxin of Gram-negative bacteria. It is, in fact, one of the most potent exotoxins known, acting as a neurotoxin at the neuromuscular junction.
