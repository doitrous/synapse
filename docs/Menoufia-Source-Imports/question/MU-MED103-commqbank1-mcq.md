## title
Which is NOT a benefit of good communication

## question
The following are benefits of good communication except:

## subject
psy

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Making an accurate diagnosis.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this IS a true benefit). Clear communication helps the physician gather accurate history and findings, which supports an accurate diagnosis — so this is a genuine benefit, not the exception the question asks for.

## answer_b
Improving patient compliance.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this IS a true benefit). Patients who understand and trust their physician are more likely to follow the agreed treatment plan, so improved compliance is a recognised benefit of good communication.

## answer_c
Decrease questions asked by the patients.

## explanation_c
Correct. Good communication does not reduce the number of questions patients ask — if anything, a patient who feels heard and is given space to talk tends to ask more questions, not fewer, because they are more engaged and comfortable raising concerns. This is the one listed option that is not an actual benefit of good communication, which is why it is the answer to an "except" question about benefits: accurate diagnosis, compliance, patient forgiveness of imperfect outcomes, fewer complaints, and faster recovery are all real benefits, but "fewer questions" is not.

## answer_d
Allowing for rapid recovery.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. this IS a true benefit). Patients who communicate well with their physician, understand their condition and follow instructions correctly tend to recover more quickly, so rapid recovery is a genuine benefit.

This is why the source lists "all of the above" as the correct, complete answer rather than any single component.
