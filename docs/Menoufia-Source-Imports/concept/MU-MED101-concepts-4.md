## definition
Peptides are classified by how many amino acid residues they contain. Glutathione, built from three amino acids -- glutamic acid, cysteine and glycine, linked by peptide bonds (an unusual gamma-linkage joins the glutamate and cysteine residues) -- is the standard worked example of a tripeptide. This is distinct from a dipeptide such as anserine (two residues), a larger oligopeptide hormone such as the nonapeptide oxytocin (nine residues) or the decapeptide kinin kallidin (ten residues), and from a single free amino acid such as glycine, which is not a peptide at all.

## explicit_objective
Identify glutathione, by name, as the tripeptide (three-residue) example among peptides and free amino acids of other chain lengths.

## pitfalls
Confusing glutathione's three-residue tripeptide structure with a larger peptide hormone such as oxytocin (nonapeptide) or kallidin (decapeptide) -- chain length, not biological role, is what the classification tests. Also confusing it with glutathione's separate biosynthetic role as a downstream product of glycine, cysteine and glutamate metabolism, which is a different (catabolic-pathway) fact from its own three-residue composition.
