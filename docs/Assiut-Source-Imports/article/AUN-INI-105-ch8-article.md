## summary
AUN-INI-105's eighth chapter opens the bank's parasitology half with the vocabulary every later parasite fact depends on: how a parasite's dependency on its host is classified (obligate, facultative, opportunistic), the several distinct host roles a life cycle can pass through (definitive, intermediate, paratenic/transfer, reservoir), and the non-infecting artefacts (pseudoparasites, coprozoic organisms, spurious findings) that can produce a false-positive stool examination if not recognized. It closes with the classic three-way distinction between parasitism, commensalism and mutualism.

## sections
### Definition
Parasites are classified by how dependent they are on a host. An obligate parasite cannot live outside a host at all. A facultative parasite can live either freely in the environment or parasitically, switching between the two. An opportunistic parasite is normally held in check by an intact immune system and causes disease specifically in immunocompromised hosts. Two further terms distinguish a parasite in the wrong place from one in the wrong host: an incidental (accidental) parasite establishes itself in a host species other than its ordinary one, while an erratic (aberrant) parasite occurs in the correct host but wanders to an abnormal body site within it.

### Mechanism
A parasite's life cycle can distribute across several distinct host roles. The definitive host harbours the adult or sexual reproductive stage. The intermediate host is where all larval developmental stages take place. The paratenic (transfer) host carries the organism in an arrested state of development, with no further maturation, propagating it onward until it reaches a host in which development can resume. A reservoir host is a non-human animal harbouring the same parasite species that infects humans, serving as an additional source of human infection. A vector, typically an arthropod, shares actively in the parasite's life cycle by transmitting it between hosts.

### Key determinants
Not every organism found in a stool sample is a true infection. A pseudoparasite is any artefact or non-parasitic object mistaken for a genuine parasite on examination. A coprozoic organism is a free-living species that passes through the human alimentary tract undigested, without infecting the host. A spurious finding is a genuine parasite stage of another host species -- ingested, for example, in undercooked meat -- that survives gut transit and appears unchanged in the stool without the human ever having been truly infected. Host-microbe relationships more broadly fall into three categories: in parasitism the organism benefits at the host's expense; in commensalism the organism benefits while the host is neither helped nor harmed; and in mutualism (symbiosis in the strict sense) both partners benefit.

### Clinical significance
Recognizing which category a given organism-host relationship falls into shapes both diagnosis and clinical concern: an opportunistic parasite is a red flag for underlying immunocompromise, an incidental infection raises the question of an unusual host exposure, and a spurious or coprozoic finding on stool examination should not trigger antiparasitic treatment, since no true infection occurred. Vectors are a central target of parasite control programmes precisely because they actively transmit infection between hosts.

### Common misconceptions
Treating "spurious" and "coprozoic" as fully interchangeable, when a coprozoic organism is free-living by nature while a spurious finding is a genuine parasite of a different host species that was ingested. Confusing "wrong host" (incidental) with "wrong site within the right host" (erratic/aberrant). Confusing the paratenic (transfer) host, where development is arrested, with the intermediate host, where active larval development occurs.

## hold_these
Obligate parasites cannot live outside a host; facultative parasites can live either freely or parasitically; opportunistic parasites cause disease specifically in immunocompromised hosts.
Incidental parasites infect the wrong host species; erratic (aberrant) parasites occur in the right host but the wrong body site.
Host roles in a life cycle: definitive (adult/sexual stage), intermediate (larval development), paratenic/transfer (arrested development, carries onward), reservoir (non-human, additional infection source).
Pseudoparasites are mistaken-identity artefacts; coprozoic organisms are free-living species passing through undigested; spurious findings are another species' parasite stage passed unchanged after ingestion.
Parasitism (host harmed), commensalism (host unaffected) and mutualism (both benefit) are the three host-microbe relationship categories.

## lose_the_mark
Confusing facultative with opportunistic parasitism.
Swapping incidental (wrong host) and erratic/aberrant (wrong site, right host).
Confusing the paratenic/transfer host with the intermediate host.
Treating a spurious or coprozoic stool finding as a true infection requiring treatment.
