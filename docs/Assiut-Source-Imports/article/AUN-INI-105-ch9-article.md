## sections
### Definition
Parasitic infection is acquired through several distinct modes: ingestion of an infective stage in contaminated food or water; injection, as by the bite of a blood-feeding vector; and autoinfection, in which the parasite re-infects the same host without ever leaving the body. The number of intermediate hosts a platyhelminth requires is species-specific: Fasciola hepatica, Schistosoma haematobium and Fasciolopsis buski each need only one, Diphyllobothrium latum needs two (a copepod, then a fresh-water fish), and among the cestodes Taenia solium is the exception needing only one (the pig) against the two-host pattern of most other cestodes tested alongside it.

### Mechanism
When a Schistosoma cercaria penetrates human skin, it sheds its forked tail and becomes the schistosomulum -- a cercaria without its tail, the stage found only after skin penetration. Fasciolopsis buski, by contrast, infects humans by ingestion of encysted metacercariae, typically found on aquatic vegetation such as water chestnuts, not by skin penetration. Heterophyes heterophyes uses a brackish/coastal-water fish (mullet) as its second intermediate host, distinguishing it from the fresh-water-fish hosts of Diphyllobothrium latum and Metagonimus yokogawai.

### Key determinants
Most trematode eggs are operculate, with a lid through which the miracidium emerges -- Schistosoma mansoni is the classic exception, its eggs non-operculate and instead bearing a spine. Trematodes, as a group, require a snail as the intermediate host, though not every species shares Schistosoma's non-operculate eggs or fork-tailed cercaria, and trematodes are acoelomate flatworms with parenchyma filling the body rather than a true body cavity. In schistosomiasis, the eggs -- not the living or dead adult worms -- are the main agent of pathology: trapped eggs provoke a granulomatous inflammatory reaction that drives the disease's fibrosis and organ damage, while the adult worms themselves are comparatively well tolerated by the host immune system.

### Clinical significance
Recognizing that schistosomiasis pathology is egg-driven, not worm-driven, shapes how the disease's chronic complications (periportal fibrosis, bladder wall changes) are understood and monitored. Knowing which fish or plant vehicle carries a given trematode's infective stage -- mullet for Heterophyes heterophyes, aquatic vegetation for Fasciolopsis buski -- underlies dietary counselling and outbreak investigation in endemic areas.

### Common misconceptions
Assuming every trematode/cestode needs the same number of intermediate hosts, when Diphyllobothrium latum (two) and Taenia solium (one, against the usual two-host cestode pattern) are the tested exceptions. Assuming all trematode eggs are operculate, when Schistosoma's are not. Assuming the schistosomulum is a separate organism from the cercaria, rather than the same organism after tail loss. Assuming adult schistosome worms, not eggs, are the main driver of schistosomiasis pathology.

### Snail intermediate hosts by species
Biomphalaria alexandrina is the intermediate host of Schistosoma mansoni. Bulinus truncatus is the intermediate host of Schistosoma haematobium. Lymnaea is the intermediate host of Fasciola. Pirenella conica, a brackish-water snail, is the first intermediate host of Heterophyes heterophyes.

### Habitat, transmission and infective stage by species
Fasciola lives as an adult in the large bile ducts of the liver. Fasciola is transmitted by ingesting metacercariae encysted on aquatic vegetation. The encysted metacercaria is Fasciola's infective stage for humans. Heterophyes heterophyes lives in the small intestine. Heterophyes heterophyes is the smallest trematode parasitizing humans. Fish-eating mammals such as dogs and cats serve as reservoir hosts for Heterophyes heterophyes. Schistosoma is transmitted by direct skin penetration of the furcocercous cercaria, which is also its infective stage. Adult Schistosoma worms reside in the portal venous system before migrating against portal blood flow to their species-specific final site. Schistosoma haematobium adults reside in, and deposit their eggs in, the venules of the urinary bladder wall. The diagnostic stage of Schistosoma haematobium, recovered from urine, is the egg. Schistosoma mansoni deposits its lateral-spined eggs in the small venules of the large intestinal wall. Schistosoma mansoni eggs bear a lateral spine, while Schistosoma haematobium eggs bear a terminal spine. Unlike Schistosoma species with important animal reservoir hosts, Schistosoma haematobium's definitive host is essentially humans alone. It is specifically the mature schistosome egg, not an immature one, that provokes the granulomatous reaction driving schistosomiasis's pathology.

### Morphology, classification and general characteristics
Most trematodes are hermaphroditic; Schistosoma is the exception with separate sexes. Trematodes are characteristically leaf-shaped, dorsoventrally flattened worms. Trematodes' organs of attachment are suckers -- typically an oral sucker and a ventral sucker. Heterophyes heterophyes possesses a genital sucker (gonotyl), a third sucker in addition to the usual oral and ventral suckers of a trematode. Among the major helminth classes, Trematoda specifically requires a mollusc intermediate host to complete its life cycle.

### Host-number classification and cercaria types
A heteroxenous parasite is one that requires more than one host to complete its life cycle. A monoxenous parasite, by contrast, completes its entire life cycle within a single host. Leptocercous names a cercaria with a simple, unforked tail, the type produced by Fasciola-group flukes. The miracidium is the stage infective for the snail intermediate host in a trematode's life cycle.

## lose_the_mark
Treating every platyhelminth as needing the same number of intermediate hosts.
Describing Schistosoma eggs as operculate.
Attributing schistosomiasis pathology mainly to the adult worms rather than the eggs.
Confusing the schistosomulum with the (still-tailed) cercaria that produces it.
Mixing up which snail genus goes with which trematode species (Biomphalaria/Bulinus/Lymnaea/Pirenella).
Assuming Fasciola's cercaria is furcocercous (forked-tail) like Schistosoma's, rather than leptocercous (simple-tailed).
Confusing S. mansoni's lateral-spined, large-intestinal-venule pattern with S. haematobium's terminal-spined, bladder-venule pattern.
Treating "eggs cause schistosomiasis pathology" as undifferentiated, missing that it is specifically mature, antigen-secreting eggs.
