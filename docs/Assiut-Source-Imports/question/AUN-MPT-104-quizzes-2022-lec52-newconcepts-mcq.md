## title
Cyclosporine inhibits calcineurin

## question
The mechanism of action of cyclosporine is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Inhibition of WBC division.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cyclosporine does not act by directly inhibiting white blood cell division (that is closer to the mechanism of antimetabolite immunosuppressants such as azathioprine or methotrexate).

## answer_b
Inhibition of calcineurin.

## explanation_b
Correct. Cyclosporine binds cyclophilin, and the resulting complex inhibits calcineurin, a calcium/calmodulin-dependent phosphatase. Since calcineurin normally dephosphorylates the transcription factor NFAT, allowing it to enter the nucleus and switch on interleukin-2 (IL-2) gene transcription, inhibiting calcineurin blocks IL-2 production and suppresses T-lymphocyte activation.

## answer_c
Blocking of IL receptors.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Blocking interleukin receptors directly is the mechanism of drugs such as basiliximab (an IL-2 receptor antagonist), not cyclosporine, which acts upstream by blocking IL-2 production itself.

## answer_d
Calcineurin activation.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cyclosporine inhibits, rather than activates, calcineurin; activating calcineurin would promote, not suppress, T-cell activation.

Gum hyperplasia is a cyclosporine side effect

## question
Which side effect is related to cyclosporine?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Gum hyperplasia

## explanation_a
Correct. Gingival, or gum, hyperplasia is a well-recognised side effect of cyclosporine. It is seen in a substantial proportion of patients on long-term therapy. It is managed with meticulous oral hygiene or, if severe, a dose change.

## answer_b
Hypotension

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cyclosporine characteristically causes hypertension, not hypotension, as one of its cardiovascular side effects.

## answer_c
Muscular paralysis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Muscular paralysis is not a recognised cyclosporine side effect; cyclosporine's neurological side effects more typically include tremor.

## answer_d
Arthritis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Arthritis is not a recognised cyclosporine side effect; cyclosporine is, if anything, used to treat certain autoimmune arthritic conditions rather than cause joint inflammation.

Cyclosporine toxicity does not include hypokalemia

## question
Toxic effects of cyclosporine DON'T include:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hyperuricemia

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. cyclosporine DOES cause this). Cyclosporine can cause hyperuricemia, sometimes precipitating gout.

## answer_b
Hypokalemia

## explanation_b
Correct (i.e. cyclosporine does NOT typically cause this, the exception). Cyclosporine's renal toxicity characteristically causes hyperkalemia, from reduced potassium excretion due to impaired renal tubular function, not hypokalemia -- the reverse electrolyte disturbance from what the other toxicities in this list would suggest.

## answer_c
Nephrotoxicity

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. cyclosporine DOES cause this). Nephrotoxicity is a major, dose-limiting toxic effect of cyclosporine, from direct renal vasoconstriction and tubular injury.

## answer_d
Hypertension

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. cyclosporine DOES cause this). Hypertension is a well-recognised toxic effect of cyclosporine, related to its renal vasoconstrictive action.

## title
Cyclosporine is metabolized mainly by CYP3A4

## question
Cyclosporine is metabolized mainly by:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
CYP2D6

## explanation_a
Incorrect. CYP2D6 metabolises many drugs (e.g. codeine, several beta-blockers), but not cyclosporine as its main pathway.

## answer_b
CYP3A4

## explanation_b
Correct. Cyclosporine is metabolised mainly by the cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) enzyme, chiefly in the liver and intestinal wall. This makes cyclosporine highly susceptible to drug interactions with CYP3A4 inhibitors (which raise cyclosporine levels, risking toxicity) and CYP3A4 inducers (which lower cyclosporine levels, risking rejection), a clinically important prescribing consideration.

## answer_c
CYP2C9

## explanation_c
Incorrect. CYP2C9 metabolises drugs such as warfarin and phenytoin, not cyclosporine's main pathway.

## answer_d
CYP2C19

## explanation_d
Incorrect. CYP2C19 metabolises drugs such as clopidogrel and omeprazole, not cyclosporine's main pathway.

## title
Cyclosporine is the drug of choice for preventing post-transplant rejection

## question
The drug of choice for prevention of post-transplant rejection is

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Cyclosporine

## explanation_a
Correct. Cyclosporine is a calcineurin inhibitor that blocks T-lymphocyte activation and IL-2 production. This mechanism, the same one tested earlier in this quiz, is what makes cyclosporine the drug of choice for preventing post-transplant organ rejection. It typically forms the backbone of a combination immunosuppressive regimen alongside a glucocorticoid and an antimetabolite.

## answer_b
Glucocorticoid

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glucocorticoids are used as adjuncts in transplant immunosuppression (and for treating acute rejection episodes), but are not the drug of choice specifically for rejection prevention the way cyclosporine is.

## answer_c
Alacizumab

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Alacizumab is not a standard drug of choice for transplant rejection prevention in the department's teaching.

## answer_d
Anti-D immunoglobulins

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Anti-D immunoglobulin is used to prevent Rh(D) alloimmunisation in Rh-negative mothers, an entirely different clinical use unrelated to solid-organ transplant rejection.
