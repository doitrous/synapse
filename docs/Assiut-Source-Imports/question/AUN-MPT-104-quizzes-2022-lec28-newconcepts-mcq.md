## title
Dopamine may be used in cardiogenic shock

## question
One of the following statements is TRUE regarding sympathomimetics:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ritodrine is used in cases of delayed labor

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ritodrine, a beta2 agonist, is used to delay premature labor (a tocolytic), not to induce delayed labor -- this statement reverses its actual clinical use.

## answer_b
α-methyldopa is ineffective in cases of hypertension during pregnancy

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Alpha-methyldopa is an established, effective antihypertensive used specifically in pregnancy, not an ineffective one.

## answer_c
Dopamine may be used in cardiogenic shock

## explanation_c
Correct. Dopamine activates dopaminergic D1/D2 receptors together with adrenergic alpha and beta receptors, including a beta1-mediated inotropic effect on the heart. This multi-receptor profile is why dopamine may be used in cardiogenic shock, supporting cardiac output when it is failing.

## answer_d
Amphetamine is contraindicated in cases of narcolepsy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amphetamine is used to treat, not contraindicated in, narcolepsy -- its CNS-stimulant action helps counter the excessive daytime sleepiness of that condition.

## title
Phenylephrine causes mydriasis without cycloplegia

## question
Phenylephrine instilled in the eye produces:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Mydriasis but no cycloplegia

## explanation_a
Correct. Phenylephrine, a selective alpha1 agonist, contracts the iris dilator muscle to produce mydriasis. It has no action on the ciliary muscle, which is muscarinic-, not adrenergic-, controlled, so accommodation for near vision is unaffected -- mydriasis without cycloplegia.

## answer_b
Neither mydriasis nor cycloplegia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Phenylephrine does produce mydriasis, through its alpha1 agonist action on the iris dilator muscle -- this option denies a real effect.

## answer_c
Cycloplegia but no mydriasis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cycloplegia requires paralysing the ciliary muscle, which is muscarinic-controlled -- phenylephrine's alpha1 action has no effect there, so it does not produce cycloplegia at all, and it does produce mydriasis.

## answer_d
Both mydriasis and cycloplegia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Both mydriasis and cycloplegia together describes an antimuscarinic's ocular effect (like scopolamine or atropine), not phenylephrine's alpha1-selective, muscarinic-sparing action.

## title
Dopamine activates D1, D2, alpha and beta receptors

## question
Dopaminergic D1 and D2 as well as adrenergic alpha and beta receptors are activated by:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Phenylephrine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Phenylephrine is a selective alpha1 agonist with no dopaminergic or beta activity, far narrower than the profile described here.

## answer_b
Dopamine

## explanation_b
Correct. Dopamine activates dopaminergic D1 and D2 receptors together with adrenergic alpha and beta receptors. This broad, multi-receptor profile is unique among the sympathomimetics discussed in this course. It is why dopamine's clinical effect shifts with dose, moving from dopaminergic toward beta1- and then alpha1-dominant as the dose rises.

## answer_c
Norepinephrine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Norepinephrine acts on alpha1, alpha2 and beta1 adrenoceptors, but not dopaminergic receptors and not beta2, a narrower profile than dopamine's.

## answer_d
Dobutamine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dobutamine is a relatively beta1-selective adrenergic agonist, with no meaningful dopaminergic receptor activity.

## title
Tachycardia is a selective beta2-agonist side effect

## question
Select the side-effect characteristic for selective beta2-adrenomimics:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Tachycardia

## explanation_a
Correct. Selective beta2-adrenomimetics can still produce tachycardia as a side effect. This can come from some cross-reactivity at beta1 receptors at higher doses, reflex responses to beta2-mediated vasodilation, or direct beta2 effects on the atria. "Selective" therefore does not mean entirely free of cardiac effect.

## answer_b
Dry mouth

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dry mouth is an antimuscarinic effect, unrelated to beta2-adrenoceptor stimulation.

## answer_c
Peripheral vasoconstriction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Beta2 agonists cause vasodilation, not vasoconstriction, in vascular smooth muscle -- the opposite of this option.

## answer_d
Depression of the breathing centre

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Beta2 agonists relax bronchial smooth muscle (bronchodilation) and do not depress the breathing centre; they are stimulants, not depressants, of relevant respiratory and cardiovascular tissue.
