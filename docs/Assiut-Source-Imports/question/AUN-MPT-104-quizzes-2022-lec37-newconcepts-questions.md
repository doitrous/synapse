## question
A benign tumour usually shows:

## subject
fnd

## status

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
blood spread

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Blood-borne spread is a feature of malignant, not benign, tumours.

## answer_b
mature cell population

## explanation_b
Correct. A benign tumour is usually composed of a mature, well-differentiated cell population that closely resembles its tissue of origin, without blood or lymphatic spread, extensive necrosis, or numerous mitotic figures. These other four features instead characterise a malignant tumour, whose growth outpaces its blood supply and whose cells divide rapidly and abnormally.

## answer_c
lymphatic spread

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Lymphatic spread is a feature of malignant, not benign, tumours.

## answer_d
extensive necrosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Extensive necrosis, from outgrowing its blood supply, is a feature of malignant, not benign, tumours.

## answer_e
many mitotic figures

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Many mitotic figures reflect the rapid, abnormal division of malignant, not benign, cells.

Neoplasia is autonomous, parasitic growth

## question
Neoplasia is an autonomous growth characterized by:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
parasitic

## explanation_a
Correct. Neoplasia is defined as an autonomous, uncontrolled new growth that, once established, persists independently of the stimulus that initiated it, has no useful function to the host, and escapes normal biological growth control -- behaving parasitically by drawing nutrition from the host without contributing anything useful in return. This is what "parasitic" means in the classic definition of neoplasia, distinguishing it from a normal, controlled, functionally useful, stimulus-dependent tissue response.

## answer_b
has stimulus,

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Neoplastic growth is autonomous of a continuing stimulus, not dependent on one persisting.

## answer_c
has useful function,

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Neoplastic growth serves no useful function to the host, unlike a normal, physiological tissue response.

## answer_d
has biological control,

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Neoplastic growth escapes normal biological growth control, rather than remaining subject to it.

Incorrect. Malignant nuclei are typically enlarged, not normal in size.

Abnormal mitosis

Correct. The nuclei of malignant cells show abnormal mitotic figures, atypical, sometimes tripolar spindle patterns, reflecting the disordered, excessive cell division that drives malignant growth. normochromatism

Incorrect. Malignant nuclei are typically hyperchromatic, not normochromatic.

## answer_e
similar in shape

Incorrect. Malignant nuclei typically show pleomorphism (variation in shape), not uniform shape.
