## title
Onchocerca volvulus microfilariae are subcutaneous

## question
Microfilaria of Onchocerca volvulus Present in ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Blood

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Blood is where the microfilariae of Wuchereria bancrofti, Brugia malayi and Loa loa circulate, not Onchocerca volvulus, whose own microfilariae remain in the skin.

## answer_b
Lymphatics

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lymphatics are the habitat of adult Wuchereria bancrofti and Brugia malayi, not the site where Onchocerca volvulus microfilariae are found.

## answer_c
Subcutaneous

## explanation_c
Correct. Microfilariae of Onchocerca volvulus are found in the skin and subcutaneous tissue, not circulating in peripheral blood like most other human filarial worms. They migrate through the dermis after being released from adult worms coiled within a fixed subcutaneous nodule, and it is this skin-dwelling location that makes a skin snip, not a blood film, the standard diagnostic sample for onchocerciasis. This same skin-dwelling behaviour is also why Onchocerca volvulus microfilariae show no defined blood periodicity, unlike Wuchereria bancrofti, Brugia malayi or Loa loa, whose microfilariae circulate in blood on a timed schedule.

## answer_d
Serous Cavity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A serous cavity is not a recognised site for Onchocerca volvulus microfilariae, which are instead found in the skin and subcutaneous tissue.

## title
Adult Loa loa inhabits subcutaneous tissue

## question
The habitat of adult Loa loa is ?

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Lymphatics are the habitat of adult Wuchereria bancrofti and Brugia malayi, not Loa loa.

## answer_b
Blood

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Blood carries the circulating microfilariae released by the adult worm, not the adult Loa loa itself.

## answer_c
Serous cavity

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A serous cavity is not the habitat of adult Loa loa, which instead migrates through subcutaneous and connective tissue.

## answer_d
Subcutaneous tissues

## explanation_d
Correct. The adult Loa loa worm lives in subcutaneous and connective tissue, migrating slowly through these planes over months to years rather than settling in one fixed site. This is unlike Wuchereria bancrofti and Brugia malayi, whose adults live fixed within the lymphatics, and unlike Onchocerca volvulus, whose adult becomes sedentary within a fixed subcutaneous nodule rather than continuing to migrate.

## title
Baermann's technique detects Ancylostoma larvae in soil

## question
A technique used to detect larvae of Ancylostoma in the soil is ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Knott's technique

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Knott's technique is used to concentrate microfilariae from a blood sample, not to detect larvae in soil.

## answer_b
Stoll's technique

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Stoll's technique is a dilution egg-counting method applied to a faecal sample, not a soil-larvae detection method.

## answer_c
Baermann's technique

## explanation_c
Correct. Baermann's technique is used to detect and recover Ancylostoma larvae from soil samples: a soil sample is suspended over warm water, and the larvae, drawn by their own active motility and thermotropism, migrate out of the soil and sediment in the water below, where they can be collected and examined. This technique specifically exploits the larva's living behaviour, which is why it works only for a viable, motile larval stage rather than for eggs. ## title
Taenia saginata eggs are not infective to man

## question
The eggs of the following parasites are infective to man when passed in the faeces EXCEPT ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Taenia saginata

## explanation_a
Correct. Taenia saginata eggs are the exception: man cannot act as its intermediate host at all, so its eggs, even if swallowed, cannot establish any infection in man, and are instead infective only to cattle, the species's true intermediate host. Taenia solium eggs and Echinococcus granulosus eggs, by contrast, are both directly infective to man, since man can act as an accidental intermediate host for each, developing cysticercosis or hydatid disease respectively. Enterobius vermicularis eggs are likewise directly infective to man, since man is its normal, only host, and swallowing or inhaling its mature egg establishes ordinary enterobiasis.

## answer_b
Taenia solium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Taenia solium eggs are directly infective to man, since man can act as an accidental intermediate host, developing cysticercosis, so it is not the exception.

## answer_c
Echinococcus granulosus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Echinococcus granulosus eggs are directly infective to man, since man can act as an accidental intermediate host, developing hydatid disease, so it is not the exception.

## answer_d
Enterobius vermicularis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis eggs are directly infective to man, who is its normal host, so it is not the exception.

## title
Cyclops is intermediate host for Dracunculus and both Diphyllobothrium species

## question
Cyclops acts as intermediate host of ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Dracunculus medinensis

## explanation_a
Incorrect alone. It is true that Cyclops is an intermediate host of Dracunculus medinensis, but this is only one of three jointly true statements offered.

## answer_b
Diphyllobothrium latum

## explanation_b
Incorrect alone. It is true that Cyclops is an intermediate host of Diphyllobothrium latum, but this is only one of three jointly true statements offered.

## answer_c
Diphyllobthrium mansoni

## explanation_c
Incorrect alone. It is true that Cyclops is an intermediate host of Diphyllobothrium mansoni, but this is only one of three jointly true statements offered.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. Cyclops, a small freshwater crustacean, acts as an intermediate host for all three of Dracunculus medinensis, Diphyllobothrium latum and Diphyllobothrium mansoni: each species' larva must first develop within an ingested Cyclops before it can go on to infect man, whether directly (Dracunculus, via drinking water) or after a second intermediate host (the two Diphyllobothrium species, via fish). Because each of these three individual host relationships is true, 'all of the above' is the intended combined answer. Trichinella spiralis, tested elsewhere in this chapter, is the clear contrast, since it has no Cyclops or other aquatic intermediate host stage at all, being acquired directly by ingesting infected meat.
