Chromosome content of primordial germ cells

## question
The primordial germ cells contain:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
23 double strand chromosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 23 double-strand chromosomes describes a haploid cell after DNA replication (e.g. a secondary spermatocyte/oocyte), not the diploid primordial germ cell.

## answer_b
23 single strand chromosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 23 single-strand chromosomes would be a haploid, unreplicated cell -- primordial germ cells are diploid, not haploid.

## answer_c
46 single chromosomes

## explanation_c
Correct. Primordial germ cells (PGCs) are ordinary diploid cells, carrying the same somatic chromosome number as any other body cell: 46 single-strand (unreplicated) chromosomes. They arise in the wall of the yolk sac and migrate to the gonadal ridges, proliferating by ordinary mitosis before meiosis begins. Only after meiotic S-phase and the first meiotic division do their descendants become haploid, and only within meiotic prophase do their chromosomes carry duplicated (double-strand) chromatids.

None of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 46 single chromosomes is a real, correct description (option C), so "none of the above" is wrong.

## title
Meiotic stage of synapsis and crossing over

## question
Synapsis and crossing over occur in:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Prophase of the second meiotic division

## explanation_a
Incorrect. By the time meiosis II begins, homologous chromosomes are already segregated into haploid sets, so there are no longer homologous pairs available to synapse.

## answer_b
None of the above

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Prophase of the first meiotic division is a real, correct answer (option C), so "none of the above" is wrong.

## answer_c
Prophase of the first meiotic division

## explanation_c
Correct. Synapsis is the pairing of homologous chromosomes, and crossing over is the reciprocal exchange of chromosomal segments between them; both take place during prophase of the first meiotic division (meiosis I), specifically within its pachytene sub-stage. Neither event occurs during prophase of meiosis II, since homologous pairs no longer exist by then. Mitotic prophase likewise involves no pairing of homologues, since mitosis produces genetically identical daughter cells rather than the genetically varied gametes crossing over is for.

## answer_d
Prophase of mitosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mitotic prophase involves no pairing of homologous chromosomes at all, since mitosis produces genetically identical daughter cells.

## title
Definition of ploidy

## question
Ploidy refers to:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Number of ribosomes in each cell

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ribosome number is a measure of a cell's protein-synthetic machinery, unrelated to its chromosome number.

## answer_b
Amount of DNA in each cell

## explanation_b
Incorrect. DNA amount changes with replication even when chromosome number does not -- a cell in G2 has double the DNA of a G1 cell at the same ploidy -- so ploidy and DNA content are not the same measurement.

## answer_c
Number of chromosomes in each cell

## explanation_c
Correct. Ploidy is the term for the number of complete chromosome sets, and by extension the number of chromosomes, present in a cell -- for example diploid (46, two sets) in somatic cells or haploid (23, one set) in mature gametes. It is not a measure of DNA content, ribosome number, or nuclear number, each of which is a separate cellular property. An abnormal ploidy state, such as triploidy or aneuploidy, is defined specifically by a change in chromosome number.

## answer_d
Number of nuclei in each cell

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nuclear number (e.g. multinucleated cells such as osteoclasts or skeletal muscle fibres) is a separate cellular property from chromosome number.

## subject
dev

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A haploid gamete carries a full haploid set -- 22 autosomes plus one sex chromosome -- not merely a sex chromosome with no autosomes.

None of the above

Incorrect. "Haploid number of chromosomes" (option A) is the correct answer, so "none of the above" is wrong.

## title
Stage of development at which implantation occurs

## question
At which stage of development does a fertilized ovum undergo implantation?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
4-cell stage

## explanation_a
Incorrect. At the 4-cell stage the conceptus is still an early cleavage-stage embryo, well before it has differentiated into the structures implantation requires.

## answer_b
Blastocyst

## explanation_b
Correct. Implantation, the embedding of the conceptus into the endometrium, occurs once the conceptus has reached the blastocyst stage, roughly six days after fertilisation. The blastocyst is the first stage at which the conceptus has differentiated into an inner cell mass (the future embryo) and an outer trophoblast layer, whose outer syncytiotrophoblast is what actually invades the endometrial stroma. Neither the earlier cleavage stages nor the later gastrula stage (a post-implantation, second-to-third-week event) is when implantation itself begins.

## answer_c
2-cell stage

## explanation_c
Incorrect. At the 2-cell stage the zygote has only just begun cleaving, long before the trophoblast and inner cell mass needed for implantation exist.

## answer_d
Gastrula

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gastrulation is a later, post-implantation event of the second-to-third week, not the stage at which implantation begins.

## answer_e
Morula

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The morula is a solid ball of cells that has not yet formed the fluid-filled cavity and differentiated inner cell mass/trophoblast that define the blastocyst.

## title
Requirements for blastocyst implantation

## question
All of the following structures are necessary for blastocyst implantation EXCEPT?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Endometrium in progestational phase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A progestational (secretory-phase) endometrium is genuinely required -- it is the receptive state implantation depends on.

## answer_b
Functional layer of endometrium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An intact functional layer of the endometrium is genuinely required, since this is the tissue the blastocyst invades.

## answer_c
Syncytiotrophoblast

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Syncytiotrophoblast is genuinely required, since it is the invasive layer that actually erodes into the endometrial stroma.

## answer_d
Zona pellucida

## explanation_d
Correct. The zona pellucida is required earlier in development -- to prevent polyspermy at fertilisation and to keep the dividing blastomeres from sticking prematurely to the tubal wall -- but it is shed ("hatching") before the blastocyst contacts the endometrium, so it plays no role in implantation itself. Implantation instead depends on a progestational, functionally active endometrium and a differentiated trophoblast providing both syncytiotrophoblast and cytotrophoblast. Listing the zona pellucida as a requirement for implantation reverses its actual developmental timing.

## answer_e
Cytotrophoblast

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cytotrophoblast is genuinely required, since it underlies and continues to supply the invasive syncytiotrophoblast.

Vignette: right-sided abdominal pain and bleeding after a missed period

## question
A 20-year old woman presents at the ER with severe abdominal pain on the right side with signs of abdominal bleeding. ## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Appendicitis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Appendicitis can cause right-sided abdominal pain but does not explain the missed period or the signs of abdominal (rather than gastrointestinal) bleeding described here.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A normal intrauterine pregnancy does not by itself cause severe abdominal pain and signs of abdominal bleeding; those findings point to a complication, not a normal course.

Toxemia of pregnancy

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Toxemia of pregnancy (pre-eclampsia) is a later-pregnancy hypertensive disorder, not an acute cause of abdominal pain and bleeding around the time of a missed period.

Endoderm

Incorrect. Endoderm, like ectoderm, does not yet exist at the blastocyst stage -- it differentiates later from the inner cell mass.

## title
Total number of somites

## question
The whole number of somites is

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
42 to 44

## explanation_a
Correct. Somites, the paired segmental blocks of paraxial mesoderm, appear in craniocaudal sequence through the fourth and fifth weeks and total approximately 42 to 44 pairs by the end of somite formation. This is considerably more than the roughly 30 to 35 vertebrae that persist in the adult axial skeleton. Several of the most caudal somites subsequently regress, which is why the final vertebral count is smaller than the total number of somites ever formed.

## answer_b
46 to 48

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 46 to 48 overstates the total somite count; the correct total is approximately 42 to 44 pairs.

## answer_c
30 to 35

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 30 to 35 is closer to the final adult vertebral count, not the total number of somites ever formed, several of which regress.

## answer_d
36 to 40

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 36 to 40 understates the total somite count; the correct total is approximately 42 to 44 pairs.

Germ layer derivative of the vertebral column

## question
The vertebral column is derived from

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Lateral mesoderm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Lateral plate mesoderm forms the body wall, limbs and serous cavity linings, not the vertebral column.

## answer_b
Dermomyotomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The dermomyotome, the other half of a differentiated somite, forms the dermis and skeletal muscle, not the vertebrae.

## answer_c
Sclerotomes

## explanation_c
Correct. Each somite splits into a ventromedial sclerotome and a dorsolateral dermomyotome. The sclerotome cells migrate medially to surround the notochord and neural tube and form the vertebrae and intervertebral discs, which is why the vertebral column is described as derived from the sclerotomes.

## answer_d
Intermediate mesoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Intermediate mesoderm forms the kidneys and gonads, not the vertebral column.

## title
Timing of embryonic folding

## question
Folding of the embryo occurs during:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The fourth week

## explanation_a
Correct. Cephalocaudal and lateral folding converts the flat trilaminar embryonic disc into a cylindrical, tube-within-a-tube body, with the amniotic cavity surrounding it and the yolk sac partly incorporated as the primitive gut. This folding takes place during the fourth week of development. It is the same week in which somites, the neural tube and the first pharyngeal arches are also forming.

## answer_b
The third week

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The third week is when gastrulation forms the trilaminar disc that folding subsequently acts on, not when folding itself occurs.

## answer_c
The sixth week

## explanation_c
Incorrect. By the sixth week, folding is long complete and the embryo already has its basic body form; folding itself is a fourth-week event.

## answer_d
The second week

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The second week is the implantation/bilaminar-disc period, well before the trilaminar disc that folding requires even exists.

## title
Midgut communication with the yolk sac

## question
The midgut communicates with the yolk sac by

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Allantois

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The allantois runs through the connecting stalk toward the hindgut/cloacal region and contributes to bladder development, not the midgut-yolk sac connection.

## answer_b
Cloacal membrane

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cloacal membrane is a bilaminar site at the caudal end of the disc, not a communicating duct between midgut and yolk sac.

## answer_c
Oropharyngeal membrane

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The oropharyngeal (buccopharyngeal) membrane is a bilaminar site at the cranial end of the disc, not a communicating duct between midgut and yolk sac.

## answer_d
Vitelline duct

## explanation_d
Correct. Once the embryo folds and the dorsal part of the yolk sac is incorporated into the embryo as the primitive gut tube, the midgut remains in open communication with the remaining, extraembryonic part of the yolk sac through a narrow connecting channel: the vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct, which runs through the umbilical cord. The duct normally obliterates later in development; its persistence is the embryological basis of Meckel's diverticulum.
