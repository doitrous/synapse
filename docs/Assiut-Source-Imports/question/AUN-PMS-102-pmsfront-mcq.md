## title
Sections used for electron microscopic examination

## question
Which of the following is used for E.M examination?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Frozen sections

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Frozen sections preserve labile enzymes and fat for light-microscopic histochemistry, not electron microscopy.

## answer_b
Paraffin sections

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Paraffin sections are cut too thick and lack the resolution needed for the electron beam used in EM.

## answer_c
Plastic sections

## explanation_c
Correct. Plastic (resin) sections are cut ultrathin, on the order of 60 to 90 nanometres, which is thin enough for electrons to pass through them. Tissue destined for plastic embedding is fixed in glutaraldehyde and post-fixed in osmium tetroxide, which gives the strong contrast electron micrographs need. This combination of ultrathin sectioning and heavy-metal contrast is what makes plastic embedding the standard preparation for electron microscopy.

## answer_d
Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) sections

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hematoxylin and eosin is a light-microscopic stain applied to paraffin sections and has no role in electron microscopy.

## title
Principle of the electron microscope

## question
Which of the following is based on the passage of electron beams through the tissue?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Electron microscope

## explanation_a
Correct. The electron microscope forms its image by passing a beam of electrons through an ultrathin section and focusing the transmitted and scattered electrons with electromagnetic lenses onto a viewing screen or detector. Because electrons have a much shorter wavelength than visible light, this gives a resolving power thousands of times greater than the light microscope, which is why it can resolve organelle ultrastructure such as membranes and cristae. The specimen must therefore be cut ultrathin and heavy-metal stained, since a thick or unstained section would scatter or absorb the beam instead of transmitting it.

## answer_b
Cell culture

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cell culture is a technique for growing living cells outside the body and involves no electron beam at all.

## answer_c
Acidic stains

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Acidic stains bind basic tissue components by chemical charge and are a light-microscopic histochemical method, not an imaging principle.

## answer_d
Light microscope

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The light microscope forms its image from visible light rays, not electron beams, which is exactly why its resolution is so much lower than the electron microscope's.

## title
Bulk movement of macromolecules into the cell

## question
Bulk movement of macromolecules into the cell by formation of vesicles is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Exocytosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Exocytosis moves material out of the cell by fusing a cytoplasmic vesicle with the plasma membrane, the reverse direction of the process described.

## answer_b
Channel

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A channel is an integral membrane protein that lets small molecules or ions cross the membrane directly, not a vesicle-forming process.

## answer_c
Endocytosis

## explanation_c
Correct. Endocytosis is the bulk movement of material into the cell by the plasma membrane forming a vesicle around it and pinching it off into the cytoplasm. It takes three forms: phagocytosis engulfs solid particles with pseudopodia, pinocytosis takes in extracellular fluid through small invaginations, and receptor-mediated endocytosis clusters ligand-bound receptors into a clathrin-coated pit that pinches off as a coated vesicle. All three share the same defining feature tested here, namely that material enters the cell packaged inside a membrane-bound vesicle rather than crossing the membrane directly.

## answer_d
Fluid mosaic model

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The fluid mosaic model describes the general structural organisation of the membrane's lipids and proteins, not a transport process.

## question
In plasma membrane, ____ are organized into a double layer (bilayer):

## subject

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cholesterol molecules are interspersed among the phospholipid tails to stabilise the membrane, but it is the phospholipid, not cholesterol, that forms the bilayer's basic architecture.

## answer_c
Phospholipids

## explanation_c
Correct. Cholesterol, integral and peripheral proteins, and carbohydrate are all additional components carried within or upon this phospholipid bilayer rather than components that themselves form it.

Incorrect. Carbohydrate is attached to lipid and protein on the membrane's outer face only and does not form the bilayer.

What is directly incorporated within the phospholipid bilayer?

## subject

Integral protein

Cell coat

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The cell coat (glycocalyx) is the carbohydrate-rich layer coating the outer surface of the membrane, not a structure embedded within the bilayer itself.

## explanation_a
Correct. Skeletal muscles are classified by the arrangement of their fibres, and pennate muscles have fibres set obliquely on a tendon like the barbs of a feather. A bipennate muscle, such as rectus femoris, has its tendon running down the middle with two rows of fibres attaching obliquely on both sides of it, exactly the feather-like arrangement described. unipennate

Incorrect. A unipennate muscle, such as flexor pollicis longus, has its tendon running along only one side with a single row of oblique fibres, not two rows on either side of a central tendon.
