## title
Mild-to-moderate intestinal amebiasis is treated with metronidazole plus diloxanide furoate

## question
A male patient presents with lower abdominal discomfort, flatulence, and occasional diarrhea. A diagnosis of intestinal amebiasis is made, and E histolytica is identified in his diarrheal stools. The preferred treatment that he should have received for the initial symptoms (which were indicative of mild-to moderate disease) is

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Diloxanide furoate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diloxanide furoate alone is a luminal agent used to eradicate residual cysts after tissue treatment, or for asymptomatic cyst passers; it is not sufficient alone for symptomatic mild-to-moderate intestinal disease.

## answer_b
Metronidazole plus diloxanide furoate

## explanation_b
Correct. For mild-to-moderate symptomatic intestinal amebiasis, the preferred treatment combines metronidazole (a tissue amebicide that treats the invasive trophozoite infection causing symptoms) with diloxanide furoate (a luminal agent that eradicates any remaining intestinal cysts after the tissue infection is treated). This combination approach ensures both the active tissue infection and the luminal cyst reservoir are cleared, unlike either drug given alone.

## answer_c
Metronidazole

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Metronidazole alone treats the tissue infection but does not reliably eradicate luminal cysts, risking relapse or continued transmission.

## answer_d
Iodoquinol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Iodoquinol is a luminal agent, similar in role to diloxanide furoate, but the printed key specifically pairs metronidazole with diloxanide furoate for this scenario.

## title
Hydatid disease does not respond to praziquantel

## question
Which helminthic infection does not respond to treatment with praziquantel?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Hydatid disease

## explanation_a
Correct. Hydatid disease, caused by the larval (cyst) stage of Echinococcus granulosus, does not respond reliably to praziquantel; albendazole (often with surgical or percutaneous cyst management) is the standard treatment instead. This is an important exception, since praziquantel is broadly effective against most other trematode and cestode infections, including paragonimiasis, pork tapeworm (Taenia solium) infection and schistosomiasis.

## answer_b
Paragonimiasis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Paragonimiasis (lung fluke infection) responds to praziquantel.

## answer_c
Pork tapeworm infection

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pork tapeworm (Taenia solium) infection responds to praziquantel.

## answer_d
Schistosoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Schistosomiasis responds to praziquantel, which is in fact the drug of choice for all Schistosoma species.

## question
After the acute infection, which of the following medications is given to treat the asymptomatic colonization state of E. histolytica?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Metronidazole is a tissue amebicide for active invasive infection; it does not reliably eradicate the luminal cyst carriage state.

## answer_b
Chloroquine.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Chloroquine is an antimalarial, not an amebicide used for luminal cyst eradication.

## answer_c
Diloxanide furoate

## explanation_c
Correct. Diloxanide furoate is a luminal amebicide used to treat the asymptomatic colonization (cyst-passer) state of Entamoeba histolytica after the acute tissue infection has resolved. It clears the intestinal lumen of cysts, preventing relapse or transmission to others. This luminal role is distinct from metronidazole's tissue-amebicide role, so the two drugs are often paired rather than used interchangeably.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Primaquine is an antimalarial that targets liver hypnozoites, not an amebicide for luminal cyst eradication.

## title
Chloroquine treats acute P vivax attacks but does not eradicate exoerythrocytic forms

## question
Which drug should be used for oral treatment of the acute attack of P vivax malaria but does not eradicate exoerythrocytic forms of the parasite?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Mefloquine also acts on blood-stage parasites without eradicating exoerythrocytic (liver hypnozoite) forms, but the printed key specifically names chloroquine as the standard oral treatment for an acute P. vivax attack.

## answer_b
Primaquine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Primaquine is the drug that specifically DOES eradicate exoerythrocytic (liver hypnozoite) forms, the opposite of what this question asks for; it is not the standard treatment for the acute blood-stage attack itself.

## answer_c
Pyrimethamine-sulfadoxine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pyrimethamine-sulfadoxine is not the standard oral treatment for an acute P. vivax attack.

## answer_d
Chloroquine

## explanation_d
Correct. Chloroquine is the standard oral treatment for the acute attack of P. vivax malaria, acting on blood-stage (erythrocytic) parasites to resolve the acute illness, but it does not eradicate exoerythrocytic (dormant liver hypnozoite) forms. Because of this, chloroquine treatment for P. vivax must be followed by primaquine to eradicate the hypnozoites and prevent relapse.

## question
The drug regimen most likely to be effective in treating severe extraintestinal disease in this patient is:

## subject
pharm

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Emetine is an older, more toxic amebicide, not the printed key's preferred regimen for severe extraintestinal disease here.

## answer_b
Diloxanide furoate plus iodoquinol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Diloxanide furoate plus iodoquinol combines two luminal agents without a tissue amebicide, insufficient for severe extraintestinal (tissue-invasive) disease.

## answer_c

## explanation_d
Correct. Severe extraintestinal amebiasis (such as amebic liver abscess) is effectively treated with tinidazole, a tissue amebicide related to metronidazole, combined with diloxanide furoate to eradicate any residual luminal cysts once the tissue infection is controlled. This mirrors the same tissue-agent-plus-luminal-agent pairing principle used for intestinal disease, adapted for tinidazole's efficacy against extraintestinal (tissue) infection.

Malarone manages multidrug-resistant P falciparum in endemic areas

## question
Which ONE of the following drugs is efficiently used in management of multidrug-resistant plasmodium falciparum in endemic area?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Chloroquine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Chloroquine is ineffective against multidrug-resistant P. falciparum, precisely the setting this question describes.

## answer_b
Doxycycline

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Doxycycline is used as an adjunct or for prophylaxis, but the printed key specifically names Malarone as efficiently managing multidrug-resistant P. falciparum on its own.

## answer_c
Malarone (Atovaquone-Proguanil)

## explanation_c
Correct. Malarone (atovaquone-proguanil) is efficiently used in the management of multidrug-resistant P. falciparum malaria in endemic areas. It combines atovaquone's action on the parasite's mitochondrial electron transport chain with proguanil's antifolate activity. Together these two mechanisms give a synergistic effect against strains resistant to older agents such as chloroquine.

Primaquine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Primaquine targets liver hypnozoites and gametocytes; it is not the standard choice for managing multidrug-resistant P. falciparum blood-stage infection.

## question
A traveler in a geographical region where chloroquine-resistant P falciparum is endemic used a drug for prophylaxis but nevertheless developed a severe attack of P vivax malaria. The drug used for prophylaxis was probably:

## subject

## explanation_b

Incorrect. Iodoquinol is an amebicide, not an antimalarial prophylactic agent.
