## title
Trichinella spiralis is larviparous

## question
Which of the following parasite is larviparous ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ancylostoma duodenale

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ancylostoma duodenale is oviparous, releasing eggs that pass out in the faeces, not live larvae.

## answer_b
Fasciola gigantica

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fasciola gigantica is oviparous, releasing eggs that pass out in the bile and faeces, not live larvae.

## answer_c
Trichinella spiralis

## explanation_c
Correct. Trichinella spiralis is larviparous: the fertilized adult female releases live first-stage larvae directly into the intestinal mucosa, rather than laying eggs, and these larvae then migrate via the bloodstream to encyst in skeletal muscle. This viviparous (larviparous) reproductive strategy sets Trichinella apart from most other intestinal nematodes tested in this bank, which are oviparous and instead pass eggs. Ancylostoma duodenale, Fasciola gigantica and Taenia solium are all oviparous, releasing eggs rather than live larvae.

## answer_d
Taenia solium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Taenia solium is oviparous (its gravid proglottids release eggs), not larviparous.

## title
Enterobius vermicularis is best diagnosed with the perianal cellophane tape technique

## question
The Enterobius vermicularis is best detected by ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Applying the sticky surface of cellophane tape to the peri anal area. Applying the tape as microscopic slide, and viewing

## explanation_a
Correct. Enterobius vermicularis is best diagnosed with the perianal cellophane (Scotch) tape technique: the sticky surface of a strip of tape is pressed against the perianal skin early in the morning, before the patient bathes or defecates, then applied to a microscope slide and examined directly for the characteristic flattened, asymmetric eggs. This works because the gravid female Enterobius migrates out through the anus at night to lay her eggs on the perianal skin rather than releasing them into the intestinal lumen, so almost no eggs reach the stool. A standard stool examination therefore has a low sensitivity for pinworm and is not the preferred diagnostic method.

## answer_b
Collection of stool in the morning and preparing a wet mount for examination

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A morning stool wet mount is the standard diagnostic method for most other intestinal helminths, whose eggs are passed in the faeces, but it has a low yield for Enterobius vermicularis because so few of its eggs are found in stool.

## answer_c
Microscopic examination of the sediment from centrifuged urine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Centrifuged urine sediment is used to look for eggs of Schistosoma haematobium, not Enterobius vermicularis, which does not shed eggs into urine.

## answer_d
Examination of skin biopsy from mucosal lesion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Skin biopsy is not used to diagnose Enterobius vermicularis; muscle biopsy, not skin biopsy, is instead the diagnostic method for Trichinella spiralis.

## answer_e
None is correct

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A correct method (the cellophane tape technique) does exist and is listed as option A, so 'none is correct' is wrong.

## title
Diagnostic stages of ascariasis are found in faeces and sputum

## question
Diagnostie stages of ascariasis is present in ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Faeces and sputum

## explanation_a
Correct. Ascariasis can be diagnosed from two different specimens at two different points in the life cycle: the characteristic fertilized, bile-stained, mamillated egg is recovered from the faeces of a patient harbouring adult worms, while during the obligatory pulmonary migratory phase the larvae passing through the alveoli and airways can be coughed up and recovered in the sputum, sometimes together with eosinophils in Loeffler's syndrome. No blood or urine stage of Ascaris lumbricoides exists, since the parasite never enters the bloodstream as a diagnosable free stage nor is excreted in urine. Faeces and sputum are therefore the two correct specimens to examine.

Blood and faeces

Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides eggs are not diagnosed from blood; blood films are used for blood-dwelling parasites such as microfilariae, not for Ascaris.

## answer_c
Blood and urine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Neither blood nor urine carries a diagnostic stage of Ascaris lumbricoides.

## answer_d
Faeces and urine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ascaris is not excreted in urine, so urine is not a correct diagnostic specimen for ascariasis.

## title
Ancylostoma duodenale's infective stage is the ensheathed filariform larva

## question
The infective stage of Ancylostoma duodenale is ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Ensheathed filariform larvae

## explanation_a
Correct. The infective stage of Ancylostoma duodenale is the ensheathed, third-stage filariform larva, which develops from the egg after it hatches in warm, moist soil and moults twice from the rhabditiform larval stage. This filariform larva is actively motile and exhibits positive skin-penetrating (histotropic) behaviour, allowing it to invade intact human skin, usually of the bare foot, and enter the bloodstream to begin its migratory cycle. Unlike the mature (embryonated) egg that is infective for Ascaris and Trichuris, or the microfilaria that is transmitted by mosquitoes for filarial worms, hookworm is transmitted by this skin-penetrating larval stage.

## answer_b
Microfilaria

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Microfilaria is the transmissible blood stage of filarial worms such as Wuchereria bancrofti, not of Ancylostoma duodenale.

## answer_c
Mature eggs

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A mature (embryonated) egg is the infective stage for Ascaris lumbricoides and Trichuris trichiura, which are infective by ingestion, not for the skin-penetrating hookworm.

## answer_d
Forked cercaria

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A forked (furcocercous) cercaria is the infective stage of blood flukes such as Schistosoma, not of Ancylostoma duodenale.

## title
The diagnostic egg of Ancylostoma is immature and translucent

## question
Diagnostic stage of Ancylostoma is ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Immature translucent egg

## explanation_a
Correct. The diagnostic stage of Ancylostoma duodenale recovered in stool is the immature (unembryonated) translucent egg: the thin-shelled, oval, colourless egg is typically passed while still in an early cleavage stage, containing only a few blastomeres rather than a fully formed larva. If a stool sample is left standing at ambient temperature for more than about 24 hours, the egg can continue developing and hatch into a rhabditiform larva before the sample is examined, which is why fresh stool examination is preferred. This immature-egg appearance is the standard diagnostic feature used to identify hookworm infection on stool microscopy.

## answer_b
Mature translucent egg

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The egg of Ancylostoma duodenale is passed immature (in an early cleavage stage), not fully mature, at the time of defecation.

## answer_c
Larvae

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Larvae are not the diagnostic stage found on routine stool microscopy for Ancylostoma; the egg is, unless the stool sample has been left standing long enough for larvae to hatch.

## answer_d
Microfilaria

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Microfilaria is the diagnostic blood stage of filarial worms such as Wuchereria bancrofti, not of the intestinal nematode Ancylostoma duodenale.

## question
Enterobius vermicularis infective stage is ?

## subject

## status

## vignette

## answer_a
Larva

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Larva is the infective stage for skin-penetrating nematodes such as hookworm and Strongyloides, not for Enterobius vermicularis, which infects by ingestion of the egg.

## answer_b
Sporozoites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sporozoites are the infective stage of sporozoan protozoa such as Plasmodium and Toxoplasma, not of a nematode like Enterobius vermicularis.

## answer_c
Oocyst

This short maturation time, with no obligatory period of development in soil, is what makes Enterobius vermicularis unusually easy to transmit within households and institutions.

## title
Ascaris lumbricoides inhabits the small intestine

## question
The habitat of Ascaris lumbercoide is ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Small Intestine

## explanation_a
Correct. The adult Ascaris lumbricoides worm inhabits the lumen of the small intestine, where it lives free (not attached to the mucosa) and feeds on the partly digested contents of the gut. Large numbers of adult worms in the small intestine can cause intestinal obstruction, particularly in children, and adult worms are occasionally passed spontaneously in the stool or vomited. The small intestine is therefore both the site where the adult worm matures and the site from which its complications, such as obstruction or migration into the bile or pancreatic ducts, arise.

Illocaecal region

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The ileocaecal region is more characteristically associated with Trichuris trichiura and Enterobius vermicularis, which favour the caecum and adjacent large-bowel segments, not with adult Ascaris lumbricoides.

## answer_c
Sigmoid colon

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The sigmoid colon is not the habitat of Ascaris lumbricoides.

## answer_d
Liver

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The liver is not the adult habitat of Ascaris lumbricoides, although migrating larvae do pass transiently through the liver during their obligatory hepato-pulmonary migratory phase.

## question
The Nematodes are ?

## subject

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Leaf-shaped, flattened worms describes the trematodes (flukes), such as Fasciola hepatica and Schistosoma, not the nematodes.

## answer_b
Segmented worms

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Segmented worms describes the cestodes (tapeworms), whose body is divided into repeating proglottid segments, not the nematodes, which are unsegmented.

## answer_c
Round worms

## explanation_c
Correct. Nematodes (roundworms) are elongated, cylindrical, unsegmented worms possessing a complete tubular digestive tract running from mouth to anus, and, unlike the cestodes, have separate sexes (males and females are distinct individuals). This cylindrical, round-in-cross-section body shape is the feature that gives the group its common name and distinguishes it at a glance from the other major helminth groups. Ascaris lumbricoides, Enterobius vermicularis, Trichuris trichiura, hookworms and the filarial worms are all classic examples of nematodes.

## answer_d
Tape worms

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tapeworms are the cestodes, a wholly separate helminth class from the nematodes.

## title
Enterobius vermicularis has no lung migratory phase

## question
Which one of the following has no migratory phase to the lung ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ascaris lumbricoides

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides larvae undergo an obligatory hepato-pulmonary migratory phase (gut wall to liver to lungs to trachea) before returning to the small intestine.

## answer_b
Enterobius vermicularis

## explanation_b
Correct. Enterobius vermicularis has an unusually short and direct life cycle: after an infective egg is swallowed, the larva hatches in the duodenum and travels directly to the caecum to mature, with no obligatory migration through the liver and lungs. This is in clear contrast to Ascaris lumbricoides, Ancylostoma duodenale and Strongyloides stercoralis, whose larvae must all pass through the bloodstream, liver and lungs, be coughed up and swallowed again, before completing development in the small intestine. Enterobius's lack of a pulmonary phase is also why it does not cause the eosinophilic pneumonitis (Loeffler's syndrome) associated with the other three.

## answer_c
Ancylostoma duodenale

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ancylostoma duodenale larvae, after penetrating the skin, migrate through the bloodstream to the lungs before being coughed up and swallowed to reach the small intestine.

## answer_d
Strongyloid stercoralis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Strongyloides stercoralis larvae also migrate through the lungs on their way to the small intestine, whether the initial infection was by skin penetration or by internal autoinfection.

## title
Strongyloides stercoralis can live both a parasitic and free-living life

## question
Which one of the following can live both parasitic or free living life ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Ascaris lumbricoides

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides is an obligate parasite with no free-living adult stage; its eggs mature in soil, but there is no free-living adult generation.

## answer_b
Enterobius vermicularis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis is an obligate parasite with a direct life cycle and has no free-living stage.

## answer_c
Strongyloid stercoralis

## explanation_c
Correct. Strongyloides stercoralis is unusual among human intestinal nematodes in possessing both a parasitic cycle, within the human host, and a free-living cycle, in which rhabditiform larvae passed in the stool can develop into free-living adult males and females that mate and reproduce in the soil for one or more generations. Under favourable soil conditions this free-living cycle can continue indefinitely, while under unfavourable conditions the rhabditiform larvae instead moult directly into infective filariform larvae. Ascaris lumbricoides, Enterobius vermicularis and Trichinella spiralis all lack this facultative free-living option and are obligate parasites throughout their life cycle.

## answer_d
Trichinella spiralis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Trichinella spiralis is an obligate parasite that completes its entire life cycle within a mammalian host and has no free-living stage.

## title
Trichinella spiralis is diagnosed by muscle biopsy

## question
Muscle biopsy is used to diagnose ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Trichinella spiralis

## explanation_a
Correct. Trichinella spiralis is diagnosed by muscle biopsy, most often of a heavily used muscle such as the deltoid or gastrocnemius, which demonstrates the coiled larva encysted within a nurse cell inside a striated muscle fibre. This is possible because the larvae released by the female worm in the small intestine migrate through the bloodstream specifically to skeletal muscle, where they encyst and can persist for years. Muscle biopsy is a definitive, though invasive, confirmatory test that is usually reserved for cases where serology and the clinical picture (myalgia, periorbital oedema, eosinophilia) leave the diagnosis in doubt.

## answer_b
Enterobius vermicularis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis is diagnosed by the perianal cellophane tape technique, not by muscle biopsy, since it does not have a muscle-encysting larval stage.

## answer_c
Ascaris lumbricoides

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides is diagnosed by finding eggs (or occasionally larvae in sputum), not by muscle biopsy, since it has no muscle-encysting stage.

## answer_d
Strongyloid stercoralis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Strongyloides stercoralis is diagnosed by finding rhabditiform larvae in the stool (or duodenal aspirate), not by muscle biopsy.

## title
Enterobius vermicularis causes perianal itching

## question
Which one of the following nematodes can cause perianal itchin ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Trichinella spiralis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Trichinella spiralis causes myalgia, periorbital oedema and fever from muscle invasion, not perianal itching.

## answer_b
Enterobius vermicularis

## explanation_b
Correct. Enterobius vermicularis is the classic cause of perianal itching (pruritus ani). The gravid female worm migrates out of the anus at night to deposit her eggs on the perianal skin, and the mechanical and possibly irritant/allergic effect of this migration produces intense itching that is characteristically worse at night and can disturb sleep. Scratching the itchy area contaminates the fingers and nails with infective eggs, which promotes both autoinfection (re-ingestion by the same patient) and spread to other household members.

## answer_c
Ascaris lumbricoides

Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides does not characteristically cause perianal itching; its manifestations relate instead to pulmonary migration and intestinal bulk effects.

## answer_d
Strongyloid stercoralis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Strongyloides stercoralis can cause a migratory, serpiginous cutaneous rash (larva currens), but perianal pruritus specifically is the classic feature of Enterobius, not Strongyloides.

Trichinella spiralis infects man by ingestion of infected food

## question
Which one of the following parasites enter the host through ingestion of infected food ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Strongyloides stercoralis classically infects by skin penetration of the filariform larva, not by ingestion of infected food.

## answer_b
Schistosoma mansoni

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Schistosoma mansoni infects by skin penetration of the cercaria released from an infected freshwater snail, not by ingestion of infected food.

## answer_c
Necator americans

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Necator americanus, like other hookworms, infects by skin penetration of the filariform larva, not by ingestion.

Once ingested, the larvae are released from their cysts by gastric digestion, mature into adults in the small intestine, and the resulting new generation of larvae then migrates to skeletal muscle to encyst again. This oral, food-borne route sharply distinguishes Trichinella from the other three organisms listed, all of which infect by skin penetration rather than ingestion.

## title
Embryonated egg is the infective stage of both Ascaris and Trichuris

## question
Embryonated egg is the infective stage for the following nematode ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Enterobius vermicularis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis eggs embryonate rapidly on the perianal skin, not after an obligatory period of maturation in soil like Ascaris and Trichuris.

## answer_b
Trichocephalus trichuris

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Trichuris trichiura alone is only half of the correct pairing; Ascaris lumbricoides shares the same embryonated-egg infective stage and must be included.

## answer_c
Ascaris lumbricoides

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides alone is only half of the correct pairing; Trichuris trichiura shares the same embryonated-egg infective stage and must be included.

## answer_d
Hookworms

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hookworms are infective as a skin-penetrating filariform larva, not as an ingested egg at all.

## answer_e
Both b&c

## explanation_e
Correct. The embryonated (mature) egg is the infective stage shared by both Trichuris trichiura (Trichocephalus trichuris) and Ascaris lumbricoides. Both species pass an unembryonated egg in the faeces that must then mature in warm, moist soil for roughly two to four weeks before becoming infective, at which point ingestion of the embryonated egg (via contaminated soil, food or hands) establishes infection. Enterobius vermicularis eggs, by contrast, embryonate within hours directly on the perianal skin rather than requiring a soil phase, and hookworms are not infective as an egg at all, since man is instead infected by a skin-penetrating filariform larva, so 'both b&c' is the only answer that groups the two true soil-maturing, egg-ingestion nematodes correctly.
