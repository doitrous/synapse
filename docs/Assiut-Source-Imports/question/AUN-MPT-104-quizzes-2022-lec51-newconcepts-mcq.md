## title
A congenital, unchanging, reddish chest nodule in a teenager is a hemangioma

## question
14-year-old healthy girl has a 0.3 cm reddish, slightly raised nodule on the skin of the upper part of her chest found on a routine physical examination. She states that this lesion has been present since birth and has not appreciably changed in size or color. Which of the following neoplasms is this nodule most likely to be?

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 14-year-old healthy girl has a 0.3 cm reddish, slightly raised chest-skin nodule present since birth, unchanged in size or colour.

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Melanoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Melanoma is a malignant tumour of melanocytes that would be expected to grow, change colour or shape over time, not remain stable and unchanged since birth.

## answer_b
Haemangioma

## explanation_b
Correct. A small, reddish, raised skin nodule present since birth and unchanged in size or colour describes a haemangioma, a benign tumour of blood vessels (a vascular birthmark). Its congenital onset and long-term stability are the key features distinguishing it from a malignant, acquired or progressively changing lesion.

## answer_c
Carcinoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Carcinoma is a malignant epithelial tumour, typically acquired later in life and progressively enlarging, not a stable congenital vascular nodule.

## answer_d
Lymphoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lymphoma is a malignant tumour of lymphoid tissue, not a congenital vascular skin birthmark.

A soft, painless, stable forearm mass is a lipoma

## question
A 27-year-old woman in excellent health has a routine health maintenance examination. A 2 cm soft, rounded mass is palpable beneath the skin of the left forearm. She has no difficulty using the arm and there is no associated pain with the mass, either in movement or on palpation. The overlying skin appears normal. ## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Rhabdomyosarcoma is a malignant skeletal-muscle tumour that would be expected to grow and cause symptoms, not remain a stable, painless, soft mass over a year.

Lipoma

## explanation_b
Correct. A soft, rounded, painless, slow-growing subcutaneous mass with normal overlying skin is the classic presentation of a lipoma. ## explanation_c
Incorrect. Melanoma is a malignant pigmented skin lesion, not a soft, non-pigmented, subcutaneous mass.

Metastatic carcinoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Metastatic carcinoma would present as a growing, often firm and irregular mass reflecting an underlying malignancy elsewhere, not a stable benign-feeling soft mass in an otherwise healthy young woman.

## title
A hair- and cartilage-containing cystic ovarian mass is a teratoma

## question
A 32-year-old woman has noted dull pelvic pain for the last two months. On physical examination there is a mass palpated in the right lower quadrant. An abdominal ultrasound reveals an 8 cm mass involving the right ovary. The mass is surgically excised. On gross inspection, the surface of the mass is smooth and is not adherent to surrounding pelvic structure. On sectioning it is cystic and filled with hair. On microscopic examination there is squamous epithelium, tall columnar glandular epithelium, cartilage, and fibrous connective tissue. Which of the following neoplasms is she most likely to have?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 32-year-old woman has an 8 cm right ovarian mass, smooth and non-adherent, cystic and hair-filled on sectioning, with squamous epithelium, glandular epithelium, cartilage and fibrous connective tissue on microscopy.

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Choristoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A choristoma is normal tissue found in an abnormal (ectopic) location, not a neoplasm composed of tissues from all three germ layers.

## answer_b
Myxoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A myxoma is a benign tumour of primitive mesenchymal (connective) tissue with abundant myxoid stroma, not one composed of squamous epithelium, glandular epithelium and cartilage together.

## answer_c
Hamartoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A hamartoma is a disorganised overgrowth of mature tissue elements normal to the site, not a tumour derived from all three germ layers.

## answer_d
Teratoma

## explanation_d
Correct. A cystic ovarian mass containing hair, along with squamous epithelium (ectoderm), glandular epithelium (endoderm) and cartilage/fibrous connective tissue (mesoderm), demonstrates tissue derived from all three germ layers. This mixture of three-germ-layer tissue is the defining feature of a teratoma. Here it presents as a mature cystic teratoma, also called a dermoid cyst, of the ovary.

## title
A slow-growing, solid, circumscribed uterine nodule is a leiomyoma

## question
A 35-year-old woman had a firm nodule palpable on the dome of the uterus six years ago recorded on routine physical examination. The nodule has slowly increased in size and now appears to be about twice the size it was when first discovered. By ultrasound scan it is solid and circumscribed. She remains asymptomatic. Which of the following neoplasms is she most likely to have?

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 35-year-old woman has a firm uterine-dome nodule, present for 6 years, slowly doubled in size, solid and circumscribed on ultrasound, asymptomatic.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Leiomyosarcoma is the malignant counterpart, which typically grows faster and more aggressively, unlike this slow (6-year) doubling course.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hemangioma is a benign vascular tumour, not the typical benign smooth-muscle tumour that produces a firm, solid, circumscribed uterine nodule of this kind.

Leiomyoma

## explanation_c
Correct. A firm, solid, well-circumscribed, slow-growing uterine nodule is the classic presentation of a leiomyoma, also called a uterine fibroid. Leiomyoma is the benign tumour of uterine smooth muscle, extremely common, typically asymptomatic, and growing very slowly over years. This matches the 6-year, slowly-doubling course described here exactly.

## answer_d
Adenocarcinoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adenocarcinoma is a malignant glandular epithelial tumour, not the smooth-muscle-derived firm nodule described here, and would not typically remain circumscribed and slow-growing over 6 years.

## title
Hamartoma is a mass of disorganised but mature native tissue

## question
Hamartoma refers to:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Mass of disorganised but mature cells indigenous to the part

## explanation_a
Correct. A hamartoma is a mass of disorganised, but otherwise mature and histologically normal, cells and tissue elements. Those elements are indigenous, or native, to the part of the body in which it occurs. It is a developmental malformation of local tissue architecture rather than a true, clonal neoplasm.

## answer_b
Tumour arising from totipotent cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A tumour arising from totipotent cells (able to give rise to any tissue type, including extra-embryonic tissue) describes a germ-cell tumour, not a hamartoma.

## answer_c
Tumour differentiating towards more than one cell line

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A tumour differentiating towards more than one cell line derived from all three germ layers describes a teratoma, not a hamartoma.

## answer_d
Mass of ectopic rests of normal tissue

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A mass of ectopic rests of normal tissue -- normal tissue in an abnormal location -- describes a choristoma, the entity hamartoma is classically distinguished from (hamartoma: native tissue, disorganised; choristoma: normal tissue, wrong location).

## title
Sarcomas are not made of masses and sheets of malignant cells

## question
Sarcomas are NOT characterized by:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Areas of heamorrhage and necrosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. sarcomas ARE characterized by this). Sarcomas commonly show areas of haemorrhage and necrosis, especially in rapidly growing, poorly vascularised regions.

## answer_b
common local recurrence

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. sarcomas ARE characterized by this). Sarcomas are known for common local recurrence after excision, reflecting their infiltrative growth pattern.

Correct (i.e. sarcomas are NOT characterized by this, the exception). Usually attain large sizes

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. sarcomas ARE characterized by this). Sarcomas usually attain large sizes before detection, since they often arise in deep soft tissue without early symptoms.

## question
What is a benign tumor called that is derived from all 3 germ cell layer

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Teratoma

## explanation_a
Correct. Teratoma is the tumour derived from all three germ cell layers -- ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm -- the same defining fact tested with a clinical vignette earlier in this lecture set (the hair- and cartilage-containing ovarian mass). A mature teratoma is benign; an immature teratoma, containing embryonic-appearing tissue, is malignant.

## answer_b
Lymphoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lymphoma is a malignant tumour of lymphoid tissue, a single lineage, not a tumour of all three germ layers.

## answer_c
Fibroma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fibroma is a benign tumour of fibrous connective tissue, a single mesenchymal lineage, not all three germ layers.

## answer_d
Myoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Myoma is a benign tumour of muscle tissue, a single mesenchymal lineage, not all three germ layers.

Haematoma is not a benign tumour

## question
Which is Not a benign tumour:

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Papilloma

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. papilloma IS a benign tumour). Papilloma is a genuine benign neoplasm of non-glandular epithelium.

## answer_b
Haematoma

## explanation_b
Correct (i.e. haematoma is NOT a benign tumour, the exception). A haematoma is a localised collection of extravasated blood outside blood vessels, caused by bleeding into tissue -- a reactive, non-neoplastic process, not a true clonal neoplasm at all, despite the name's superficial resemblance to a tumour suffix.

## answer_c
Adenoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. adenoma IS a benign tumour). Adenoma is a genuine benign neoplasm of glandular epithelium.
