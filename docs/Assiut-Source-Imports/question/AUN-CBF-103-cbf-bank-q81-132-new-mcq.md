## explanation_a
Incorrect as the single best answer -- this statement is true, but it is only one of several coordinated cold responses listed, so it is not the most complete answer.

## answer_b
There is excessive secretion of catecholamines.

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the single best answer -- this statement is true (sympathetic activation raises circulating catecholamines), but it too is only one part of the full response.

The sympathetic N.S. is activated causing cutaneous V.C. and piloerection.

## explanation_c

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the single best answer -- this statement is true (protein's high specific dynamic action, ~30%, generates more diet-induced heat than carbohydrate or fat), but it is one contributing factor among several.

All of the above.

Correct. Exposure to cold triggers a coordinated sympathetic response, not any single isolated change: muscle tone rises toward shivering, catecholamine secretion increases, cutaneous vasoconstriction and piloerection reduce heat loss, and protein food is physiologically preferred for its high specific dynamic action. All four of the preceding statements are true simultaneously, which is exactly why 'all of the above' is the best answer rather than any one of them alone.

## title
Evaporation: the only heat loss mechanism at all environmental temperatures

## question
The only heat loss mechanism that can occur in all environmental temperatures:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Radiation.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Radiation only moves heat from the body to the environment when the environment is cooler than the body; above body temperature it stops contributing, or reverses.

## answer_b
Evaporation.

## explanation_b
Correct. Evaporation depends on a humidity gradient rather than a temperature gradient, so it keeps working even when the environment is at or above body temperature, unlike radiation, conduction and convection, which all require a negative body-to-environment temperature gradient. This is why evaporation is the one mechanism that remains available at every ambient temperature, including hot environments where the other three cannot contribute to heat loss.

## answer_c
Conduction.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Conduction, like radiation, requires the environment to be cooler than the body; it cannot move heat out of the body once that gradient disappears or reverses.

## answer_d
Convection.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Convection likewise depends on a temperature gradient between the body surface and the surrounding air or fluid, and stops contributing to heat loss once the environment is as warm as, or warmer than, the body.

## title
Hot humid atmosphere: vasodilatation in skin blood vessels

## question
On exposure to a hot humid atmosphere:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Sweat secretion is inhibited.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sweat secretion is stimulated, not inhibited, by heat exposure -- the problem in a hot humid environment is that evaporation of that sweat is impaired, not that secretion itself is blocked.

## answer_b
V.D. occurs in the skin blood vessels.

## explanation_b
Correct. In a hot atmosphere, cutaneous vasodilation increases blood flow to the skin. This brings core heat to the body surface so it can be lost by whatever gradient remains and supports evaporative loss, even though the high humidity itself blunts how effectively that sweat can actually evaporate. Vasodilation is therefore the circulatory half of the heat-loss response, working alongside sweating rather than in place of it.

## answer_c
Sweat evaporation increases

## explanation_c
Incorrect. High humidity impairs, rather than increases, sweat evaporation, since the surrounding air is already close to saturated with water vapour and cannot take up much more.

## answer_d
Na+ is retained in the body.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Heat exposure does not characteristically produce sodium retention as a direct thermoregulatory response; sweat itself contains sodium loss, if anything, rather than net retention being the defining response here.

## answer_e
The muscle tone and secretion of thyroxine tend to increase.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Heat exposure reduces, rather than increases, muscle tone and thyroxine-driven metabolic heat production, since both would add unwanted heat -- this is the opposite direction from the cold-exposure response.

Heat stroke primarily caused by inability to evaporate sweat in high humidity

## question
Heat stroke (hyperthermia) is primarily caused by:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Excessive heat gain by conduction.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Conduction alone is not the primary mechanism behind heat stroke; heat stroke's defining failure is evaporative, not conductive.

## answer_b
Excessive heat production in the body.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Heat stroke is not primarily a matter of excessive heat production -- it is the failure of the body's main heat-loss route (evaporation) that lets body temperature climb dangerously, even without unusually high heat production.

## answer_c
Inability to evaporate sweat due to excessive humidity.

## explanation_c
Correct. Heat stroke is caused primarily by the inability to evaporate sweat effectively when ambient humidity is excessive. Since evaporation depends on a dry-air gradient, high humidity blocks the body's one heat-loss mechanism that would otherwise still work even at high ambient temperatures, leaving the body with no effective way to dissipate the heat it continues to produce and gain.

## answer_d
Inability to lose heat by radiation and other non-evaporative mechanisms.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. In a hot environment, radiation and other non-evaporative mechanisms are already unavailable (no favourable temperature gradient); it is specifically the failure of the remaining evaporative route, not those already-unavailable mechanisms, that defines heat stroke.

## answer_e
Too much clothing

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Excess clothing can contribute to heat retention but is not the primary physiological cause of heat stroke; the defining mechanism is evaporative failure in high humidity.

Hyperpyrexia mainly due to irregular central thermoregulatory mechanisms

## question
Elevation of the body temperature to dangerous levels (hyperpyrexia):

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Is often produced as a result of excessive heat production.

## explanation_a

Is mainly due to irregularity of the central thermoregulatory mechanisms and development of vicious circles.

## explanation_b
Correct. Leads to death mainly due to renal failure and liver damage.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Renal failure and liver damage are not identified as the main mechanism of death in hyperpyrexia in standard teaching; cellular/enzyme damage and CNS effects are the more commonly cited dangers.

An increase in the body fat content causes reduction of the lean body mass.

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. ICF (intracellular fluid) is the fluid inside cells; the internal environment classically refers to the fluid surrounding cells, the extracellular fluid, not the fluid within them.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. ECG (electrocardiogram) is a recording of the heart's electrical activity, unrelated to the internal-environment concept this question tests.

## answer_c
ECF

## explanation_c
Correct. The internal body environment, in the classical (Claude Bernard) sense, refers to the extracellular fluid (ECF). This is the fluid that directly bathes the body's cells, distinct from the fluid inside them. The body's homeostatic mechanisms work continuously to keep the composition of this ECF constant, which is exactly what the term "internal environment" is meant to capture.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Organs are structures, not a fluid compartment, and are not what the term "internal environment" refers to.
