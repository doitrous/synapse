## title
Zileuton inhibits 5-lipoxygenase

## question
Zileuton is effective in relieving asthma because it produces one of the following:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inhibits 5-lipoxygenase

## explanation_a
Correct. Zileuton relieves asthma by inhibiting 5-lipoxygenase, the enzyme that converts arachidonic acid into leukotrienes, the bronchoconstricting, pro-inflammatory mediators central to asthma pathophysiology. This upstream enzyme-inhibition mechanism is distinct from inhibiting mast cell degranulation, antagonizing leukotriene receptors at their target, or inhibiting cyclooxygenase.

## answer_b
Inhibits mast cell degranulation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Inhibiting mast cell degranulation is the mechanism of a drug such as cromolyn, not zileuton.

## answer_c
Antagonizes leukotriene receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Antagonizing leukotriene receptors at their target is the mechanism of a drug such as montelukast, not zileuton, which instead blocks leukotriene synthesis upstream.

## answer_d
Inhibits cyclooxygenase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Inhibiting cyclooxygenase is the mechanism of an NSAID, a different enzyme in the arachidonic acid pathway from the 5-lipoxygenase that zileuton targets.

## title
Alprostadil maintains a patent ductus arteriosus

## question
For temporary maintenance of a patent ductus arteriosus, the drug of choice is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Zileuton is a 5-lipoxygenase inhibitor used for asthma, not for ductal patency.

## answer_b
alprostadil

## explanation_b
Correct. Alprostadil (prostaglandin E1) keeps the ductus arteriosus open by maintaining the smooth-muscle-relaxing prostaglandin tone that closes physiologically after birth, making it the drug of choice for temporary maintenance of ductal patency in a neonate with ductus-dependent congenital heart disease. This is the opposite therapeutic goal from indomethacin, a prostaglandin-synthesis inhibitor used instead to close a patent ductus arteriosus.

## answer_c
epoprostenol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Epoprostenol (PGI2) is used for pulmonary arterial hypertension, not for maintaining ductal patency.

## answer_d
indomethacin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Indomethacin, a prostaglandin-synthesis inhibitor, is used to close a patent ductus arteriosus, the opposite of maintaining its patency.

## title
Glucocorticoids decrease phospholipase A2 activity

## question
A 34-year-old man complains of nasal congestion and a "runny nose". He was advised to take glucocorticoids, the mechanism of action of them is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Leukotriene-receptor antagonists

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Acting as leukotriene-receptor antagonists is the mechanism of a drug such as montelukast, not a glucocorticoid.

## answer_b
Inhibit lipoxygenase pathway

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glucocorticoids act further upstream than the lipoxygenase pathway alone; their mechanism blocks the shared first step feeding both the lipoxygenase and cyclooxygenase pathways.

## answer_c
Antagonizes H2 receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Antagonizing H2 receptors is the mechanism of a drug such as ranitidine, not a glucocorticoid.

## answer_d
Decrease phospholipase A2 activity

## explanation_d
Correct. Glucocorticoids relieve allergic conditions such as nasal congestion and rhinorrhea by inducing lipocortin (annexin-1), which inhibits phospholipase A2, the enzyme that liberates arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids, the shared first step feeding both the cyclooxygenase (prostaglandin) and lipoxygenase (leukotriene) pathways. This single upstream mechanism, not direct leukotriene-receptor antagonism, lipoxygenase-pathway inhibition, or H2-receptor antagonism, is how a glucocorticoid blocks eicosanoid-driven inflammation broadly.

## title
A PGE1 analog prevents NSAID-induced gastritis

## question
A 38-year-old woman is taking ibuprofen for severe dysmenorrhea but develops epigastric pain. The physician prescribes a medication to prevent gastritis. Which of the following best describes the medication?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
PGE 1 antagonist

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A PGE1 antagonist would block, not restore, the gastroprotective prostaglandin tone the patient needs.

## answer_b
PGE 2 analog

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Gastric mucosal protection against NSAID-induced injury is specifically restored by a PGE1 analog (misoprostol), not a PGE2 analog.

## answer_c
PGE 1 analog

## explanation_c
Correct. NSAIDs such as ibuprofen inhibit cyclooxygenase, reducing the gastroprotective prostaglandin PGE1 that normally maintains gastric mucus and bicarbonate secretion. A PGE1 analog such as misoprostol restores this protective effect, preventing NSAID-induced gastritis. A PGE1-receptor antagonist, a PGE2 analog or a PGI2 analog do not describe this specific replacement-therapy mechanism.

## answer_d
PGI 2 analog

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A PGI2 (prostacyclin) analog such as epoprostenol is used for pulmonary arterial hypertension, not for preventing NSAID-induced gastritis.

Dinoprostone ripens the cervix near term

## question
Which one of the following is approved for "ripening" of an unfavorable cervix at or near term in a pregnant patient?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ergonovine, an ergot alkaloid, is used post-partum to prevent haemorrhage by contracting the uterus, not for cervical ripening.

## answer_b
Terbutaline

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Terbutaline, a beta2 agonist tocolytic, is used to delay labour, the opposite of cervical ripening for induction.

## answer_c
Dinoprostone

## explanation_c
Correct. Dinoprostone (a PGE2 analog) is approved specifically for ripening an unfavourable cervix at or near term, softening and preparing it for labour induction. This distinguishes it from ergonovine (post-partum haemorrhage prevention), terbutaline (a tocolytic that delays labour) and alprostadil (PGE1, used for ductal patency, not cervical ripening).

## answer_d
Alprostadil

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Alprostadil (PGE1) is used for maintaining ductal patency, not for cervical ripening; that obstetric indication belongs to dinoprostone (PGE2).

## title
Leukotriene biosynthesis requires 5-lipoxygenase

## question
Which of the following enzymes do leukotrienes require for their biosynthesis?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
5-Lipoxygenase

## explanation_a
Correct. Leukotrienes are synthesised from arachidonic acid via the 5-lipoxygenase pathway, not the 8-lipoxygenase, COX-1 or COX-2 pathways, which instead generate prostaglandins and thromboxane. 5-Lipoxygenase is therefore the required enzyme for leukotriene biosynthesis, and the target of a drug such as zileuton.

## answer_b
8-Lipoxygenase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 8-Lipoxygenase is not the human enzyme pathway responsible for leukotriene biosynthesis.

## answer_c
COX-1

## explanation_c
Incorrect. COX-1 generates prostaglandins and thromboxane via the cyclooxygenase pathway, a separate branch from leukotriene synthesis.

## answer_d
COX-2

## explanation_d
Incorrect. COX-2 generates prostaglandins via the cyclooxygenase pathway, a separate branch from leukotriene synthesis.
