## title
Endoderm derivative among a mixed list

## question
Which of the following structures is derived from endoderm?

## subject

## status

C

## answer_a
Endothelial lining of blood vessels

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The endothelial lining of blood vessels is a mesodermal derivative, not endodermal.

## answer_b
Endoneurial fibroblasts and Schwann cells of peripheral nerves

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Endoneurial fibroblasts and the Schwann cells of peripheral nerves arise from neural crest and mesenchyme, not endoderm.

## answer_c
Epithelial lining of the respiratory tract

## explanation_c
Correct. Endoderm lines the primitive gut tube, and the respiratory tract epithelium buds from the foregut (as the laryngotracheal groove) and keeps its endodermal epithelial lining throughout development. This is the same rule that gives the thyroid, liver and pancreas their endodermal parenchyma. The mesenchyme and cartilage supporting that same respiratory epithelium, by contrast, come from surrounding mesoderm, not endoderm.

## answer_d
Cytotrophoblast placental cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cytotrophoblast cells are extraembryonic trophoblast, a lineage that separates from the embryo proper before gastrulation and is not assigned to any of the three intraembryonic germ layers.

## answer_e
Cells lining the amniotic membrane

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The amnion's lining arises from amnioblasts closely related to ectoderm, not endoderm.

Prochordal plate

Incorrect (this structure DOES turn ventral). The prochordal plate/oropharyngeal membrane is carried ventrally and caudally by the same folding process.

## answer_e
Pericardial cavity

## explanation_e
Incorrect (this structure DOES turn ventral). The pericardial cavity, initially cranial, ends up in its definitive ventral thoracic position after folding.

## title
Dating method used in the first month

## question
The age of the embryo is therefore usually expressed in somites during ……………

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The first month

## explanation_a
Correct. During the first month, before the embryo is large enough for crown-rump length to be a practical or precise measure, embryonic age is instead expressed by counting somites -- the paired blocks of paraxial mesoderm that appear in a predictable, near-daily sequence. This gives a more precise age estimate than external form alone at this very early stage. From the second month onward, once the embryo has grown, crown-rump length becomes the standard dating measurement instead.

## answer_b
The third month

## explanation_b
Incorrect. By the third month somites are no longer discrete countable structures, and fetal size is expressed by crown-rump length instead.

## answer_c
The fourth month

## explanation_c
Incorrect. By the fourth month, well into the fetal period, dating relies on crown-rump length, not somite count.

## answer_d

Placenta accreta is:

## subject

## answer_a
Membranous placenta

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A membranous placenta is a structural variant in which villi persist over the whole chorionic sac, an entirely different abnormality from accreta.

## answer_b
Has a peripheral cord attachment

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A peripheral cord attachment describes where the umbilical cord inserts on the placental disc, not the placenta's depth of attachment to the uterine wall.

## answer_c
Placenta deeply penetrating into the uterine wall

## explanation_c
Correct. Placenta accreta is an abnormality of placental attachment in which chorionic villi attach directly to, or invade into, the myometrium because the decidua basalis is deficient or absent, rather than remaining confined to a normal decidual plane. The placenta is, in effect, deeply and abnormally penetrating into the uterine wall. This is a distinct abnormality of depth of invasion, separate from placenta previa (an abnormality of implantation site) and from structural variants such as a membranous or fenestrated placenta.

## answer_d
Has a central hole

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A placenta with a central hole (placenta fenestrata) is a structural variant of the placental disc's shape, not an abnormality of invasion depth.
