## title
Correctly describing mammalian mRNA synthesis

## question
Which one of the following statements correctly describes the synthesis of mammalian (eukaryotic) messenger RNA?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
It is produced from the same genes that produce rRNA and tRNA

## explanation_a
Incorrect. mRNA is synthesised by RNA polymerase II, while rRNA and tRNA are made mainly by RNA polymerase I and III respectively -- different genes, different polymerases, different products.

## answer_b
Mammalian mRNA undergoes minimal modification during its maturation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Mammalian mRNA undergoes extensive, not minimal, modification during maturation: a 5' cap is added, introns are removed by splicing, and a 3' poly-A tail is added.

## answer_c
Each mRNA often encodes several different proteins

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Each mature mRNA typically encodes one protein in eukaryotes (monocistronic), not several different proteins.

## answer_d
The RNA sequence transcribed from a gene is virtually identical to the mRNA that exits from nucleus to cytoplasm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The primary transcript is not virtually identical to the mature, exported mRNA -- splicing, capping and polyadenylation change it substantially before export.

## answer_e
Colinearity of the mature mRNA sequence and the amino acid sequence of its encoded protein

## explanation_e
Correct. The mature mRNA sequence is colinear with the amino acid sequence of the protein it encodes: the codon order in the transcript matches the amino acid order in the product, even though the primary transcript itself was extensively processed to reach that mature, colinear form. This colinearity is what lets ribosomes translate an mRNA codon-by-codon into a protein with the correct, corresponding sequence, and is a distinct fact from how heavily the transcript was modified along the way.

## title
What binds upstream of the transcription start site

## question
Which of the following binds to specific nucleotide sequences that are upstream of the start site of transcription?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Helicase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Helicase unwinds the DNA double helix at the replication fork; it does not recognise a specific upstream promoter sequence to begin transcription.

## answer_b
Primase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Primase lays down the RNA primer for DNA replication, an entirely separate process from transcription initiation.

## answer_c
RNA polymerase

## explanation_c
Correct. RNA polymerase, together with its associated transcription factors, recognises and binds specific nucleotide sequences upstream of the transcription start site -- the promoter -- and this binding is what positions the polymerase to begin synthesising RNA at the correct site and in the correct direction. Different promoter elements (such as the TATA box) are recognised at characteristic distances upstream of the start site, and this upstream recognition step is what distinguishes transcription initiation from the downstream elongation that follows.

## answer_d
Restriction endonuclease

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Restriction endonucleases cut DNA at specific palindromic recognition sites; they play no role in normal cellular transcription initiation.

## answer_e
Histone protein

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Histone proteins package DNA into nucleosomes and can influence chromatin accessibility, but they do not themselves bind a specific upstream promoter sequence to initiate transcription.

## title
Erythromycin's mechanism of protein synthesis inhibition

## question
Erythromycin is the antibiotic of choice when treating respiratory tract infections in Legionnaires' disease, whooping cough, and mycoplasma-based pneumonia because of its ability to inhibit protein synthesis in certain bacteria by doing which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Causing premature chain termination

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Causing premature chain termination is not erythromycin's mechanism; that describes a different class of translation inhibitor.

## answer_b
Inhibiting initiation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Erythromycin does not act at the initiation step of protein synthesis; its target is a later, elongation-phase step.

## answer_c
Mimicking mRNA binding

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Erythromycin does not mimic mRNA binding; it is not an mRNA-competing inhibitor.

## answer_d
Inhibiting translocation by binding to 50S ribosomal subunits

## explanation_d
Correct. Erythromycin, a macrolide antibiotic, binds the large (50S) ribosomal subunit of susceptible bacteria and specifically blocks translocation -- the step in which the ribosome moves forward by one codon along the mRNA after a peptide bond has formed. Blocking translocation halts the elongation cycle and stops bacterial protein synthesis, which is why erythromycin is effective against Legionella, Bordetella pertussis and Mycoplasma, organisms for which it is a first-line or alternative agent.

## answer_e
Acting as an analogue of mRNA

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Erythromycin does not act as an mRNA analogue; it targets the ribosome directly, not the message being read.

## title
Correct order of protein synthesis steps

## question
Which of the following is the correct order of the following steps in protein synthesis?
1. A peptide bond is formed
2. The small ribosomal subunit is loaded with initiation factors, messenger RNA, and initiation aminoacyl-tRNA
3. The intact ribosome slides forward three bases to read a new codon
4. The primed small ribosomal subunit binds with the large ribosomal subunit
5. Elongation factors deliver aminoacyl-tRNA to bind to the A site

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
1, 2, 4, 5, 1, 3

## explanation_a
Correct. Protein synthesis proceeds: (2) the small ribosomal subunit is loaded with initiation factors, mRNA and the initiator aminoacyl-tRNA; (4) the primed small subunit binds the large ribosomal subunit to complete the ribosome. Elongation then begins: (5) elongation factors deliver an aminoacyl-tRNA to the A site; (1) a peptide bond is formed between the new residue and the growing chain; then (3) the intact ribosome slides forward three bases (one codon) to read the next codon. This is printed as the order 2, 4, 5, 1, 3, and the elongation steps 5, 1 and 3 (A-site delivery, bond formation, translocation) then repeat for each subsequent amino acid.

## answer_b
1, 2, 5, 4, 3

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This order places the peptide bond (1) before the large subunit even joins (4), which is not possible -- an intact ribosome (both subunits) is required before a peptide bond can form.

## answer_c
3, 2, 4, 5, 1

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Starting with translocation (3) before the ribosome has even been assembled (2, 4) reverses cause and effect.

## answer_d
2, 3, 4, 5, 1

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This order omits ever loading the small subunit with initiation factors and mRNA as its own first step, running straight into subunit joining before initiation is complete.

## answer_e
4, 5, 1, 2, 3

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Beginning with large-subunit joining (4) before the small subunit has been loaded with initiation factors, mRNA and initiator tRNA (2) reverses the correct initiation order.

Phosphate-bond cost of amino acid activation

## question
How many high-energy phosphate-bond equivalents are utilized in the process of activation of amino acids for protein synthesis?

## subject
fnd

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Four phosphate bonds is not the cost of activation alone; that figure would overstate what a single aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase reaction consumes.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Activation is not free; ATP is consumed to charge the amino acid onto its tRNA.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Correct. Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase activates an amino acid by using ATP to form an aminoacyl-adenylate intermediate, releasing pyrophosphate (PPi); the PPi is then hydrolysed to two inorganic phosphates by pyrophosphatase, which pulls the reaction forward. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. Counting only the first bond broken (ATP to AMP + PPi) and forgetting the second high-energy bond spent hydrolysing PPi undercounts the true cost.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Three phosphate bonds overstates the cost of activation alone; only two are consumed in this specific step.

## title
Which deamination is mutagenic if unrepaired

## question
Spontaneous deamination of certain bases in DNA occurs at a constant rate under all conditions. Such deamination can lead to mutations if not repaired. Which deamination indicated below would lead to a mutation in a resulting protein if not repaired?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
G to A

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This is not a recognised spontaneous deamination product that introduces a base foreign to DNA in the way that drives this classic mutation.

## answer_b
T to U

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Thymine already carries a methyl group where cytosine does not, and thymine is not the base that deaminates to uracil in this classic mechanism.

## answer_c
U to C

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Uracil does not normally arise in DNA to then convert to cytosine; the mutagenic direction runs the other way, from cytosine to uracil.

## answer_d
C to U

## explanation_d
Correct. Cytosine spontaneously deaminates to uracil, a base that does not normally occur in DNA. If base excision repair (via uracil-DNA glycosylase) does not remove this uracil before the next round of replication, DNA polymerase reads it as thymine's partner and pairs it with adenine, converting the original C-G base pair into a T-A pair -- a permanent mutation. This is the classic example of spontaneous base damage causing a point mutation, and it is precisely because uracil is foreign to DNA that repair enzymes can specifically recognise and excise it before it does harm.

## answer_e
A to G

## explanation_e
Incorrect. This is not the deamination pathway responsible for the classic C-G to T-A mutation described here.
