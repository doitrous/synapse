## title
Acyclovir is the drug used to inhibit herpes viruses

## question
Which ONE of the following drugs is used to inhibit herpes viruses?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Azidothymidine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Azidothymidine (zidovudine) is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor used against HIV, not herpesviruses.

## answer_b
Oseltamivir

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Oseltamivir is a neuraminidase inhibitor used against influenza virus, not herpesviruses.

## answer_c
Acyclovir

## explanation_c
Correct. Acyclovir is a guanosine nucleoside analogue that, once selectively phosphorylated by the herpesvirus-encoded thymidine kinase, is converted to its triphosphate form and incorporated into viral DNA, inhibiting the viral DNA polymerase and terminating the growing DNA chain. This selective activation only in herpesvirus-infected cells makes it the prototypical and most widely used drug to inhibit herpes simplex and varicella-zoster viruses.

## answer_d
Amantadine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amantadine blocks the M2 ion channel of influenza A virus, unrelated to herpesvirus replication.

## title
Valacyclovir is the oral drug for ongoing genital herpes management

## question
A 27-year-old nursing mother is diagnosed as suffering from genital herpes. She has a history of this viral infection. Previously, she responded to a drug used topically. Apart from her current problem, she is in good health. Which drug to be used orally is most likely to be prescribed at this time?

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A 27-year-old nursing mother with a history of genital herpes, previously responsive to a topical drug, otherwise in good health, presents with a recurrence.

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Foscarnet

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Foscarnet is an intravenous drug reserved for acyclovir-resistant herpesvirus or cytomegalovirus infections, not first-line oral therapy for an otherwise healthy patient with recurrent genital herpes.

## answer_b
Ritonavir

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ritonavir is a protease inhibitor used against HIV, not herpes simplex virus.

## answer_c
Valacyclovir

## explanation_c
Correct. Valacyclovir is the oral prodrug of acyclovir, rapidly converted to acyclovir after absorption with much greater oral bioavailability than acyclovir itself. It is the standard oral drug for episodic or suppressive treatment of recurrent genital herpes in an otherwise healthy patient, and is compatible with breastfeeding, making it the drug most likely prescribed here.

## answer_d
Amantadine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amantadine is an anti-influenza drug, not active against herpes simplex virus.

## title
Ganciclovir suppresses herpetic infections and provides CMV retinitis prophylaxis

## question
The drug most likely to suppress herpetic infections and provide prophylaxis against CMV retinitis in this patient is

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
Continuing the same immunocompromised-risk clinical scenario as the preceding quiz item, the patient now needs cover against both herpetic infection and cytomegalovirus retinitis.

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Indinavir

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Indinavir is an HIV protease inhibitor with no activity against herpesviruses or cytomegalovirus.

## answer_b
Fluconazole

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fluconazole is an antifungal (triazole) drug, not active against viruses.

## answer_c
Rifabutin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Rifabutin is an antimycobacterial drug used for Mycobacterium avium complex prophylaxis, not an antiviral.

## answer_d
Gancyclovir

## explanation_d
Correct. Ganciclovir is an acyclovir analogue with added activity against cytomegalovirus (CMV). After phosphorylation, first by a CMV-encoded kinase and then by host kinases, it inhibits viral DNA polymerase in both herpesviruses and CMV. This dual activity makes it the drug of choice for both suppressing herpetic infections and providing prophylaxis against CMV retinitis, a common opportunistic infection in immunocompromised patients.

## question
Oseltamivir can eradicate H1N1 influenza virus through:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Inhibition of viral RNA replication

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Oseltamivir does not directly inhibit viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase; that is the mechanism of drugs such as favipiravir, not oseltamivir.

## answer_b
Inhibition of viral DNA replication

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Influenza is an RNA virus with no DNA replication step for a drug to inhibit.

## answer_c
Inhibition of viral uncoating

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Inhibition of viral uncoating is the mechanism of the M2-channel blockers amantadine and rimantadine, not oseltamivir.

## answer_d
Inhibition of the viral neuraminidase enzyme

## explanation_d
Correct. Oseltamivir is a neuraminidase inhibitor. By blocking the viral neuraminidase enzyme, it prevents newly formed influenza virions (including H1N1) from being released from the surface of infected cells, halting the spread of infection to new cells.
