## title
Trichinella spiralis is acquired from undercooked pork

## question
Man gets infected with Trichinella spiralis on eating?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Undercooked beef

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Undercooked beef is the source of Taenia saginata (beef tapeworm), not Trichinella spiralis.

## answer_b
Undercooked pork

## explanation_b
Correct. Man is infected with Trichinella spiralis by eating undercooked pork containing encysted larvae in the animal's striated muscle. In the human small intestine the larva matures to an adult, and the female releases newborn larvae directly that are carried via the bloodstream to encyst in the host's own striated muscle -- the classic pork-borne transmission route (trichinosis) tested throughout this section.

## answer_c
Undercooked fish

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Undercooked fish is the source of Capillaria philippinensis and several fish-borne trematodes (e.g. Heterophyes heterophyes), not Trichinella spiralis.

## answer_d
Infected fleas

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Infected fleas transmit Dipylidium caninum by accidental ingestion, not Trichinella spiralis.

## subject

## answer_a
Encysted metacercaria

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An encysted metacercaria is the infective stage of trematodes such as Fasciola or Heterophyes, not of the nematode Trichinella spiralis.

## answer_b
Mature egg

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Trichinella spiralis is viviparous and passes no egg stage at all, so a mature egg cannot be its infective stage.

Correct. The infective stage of Trichinella spiralis is the encysted larva, present in the striated muscle of an infected animal (classically pork) and swallowed when that muscle is eaten undercooked. Filariform larva

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A filariform larva is the infective stage for hookworms, Strongyloides and Trichostrongylus, not for Trichinella spiralis, which instead has an encysted muscle-larva infective stage.

## title
Trichinella larvae are carried to striated muscle

## question
Larva of Trichinella spiralis are carried to?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Liver

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Newborn Trichinella larvae are carried through, not lodged in, the liver on their way to their final site.

## answer_b
Lung

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Unlike Ascaris, hookworm or Strongyloides, Trichinella's newborn larvae do not undergo a pulmonary migratory phase as their final destination.

## answer_c
Lumen of small intestine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The small-intestinal lumen is where the ingested encysted larva first matures into an adult, not where the newborn larvae are ultimately carried to encyst.

## answer_d
Active striated muscles

## explanation_d
Correct. Newborn Trichinella spiralis larvae, released by the adult female in the small intestine, penetrate the gut wall, enter the bloodstream, and are carried throughout the body, finally encysting selectively in active striated (skeletal) muscle -- not liver, lung or the intestinal lumen -- where they can persist and eventually calcify. Muscles with a rich blood supply and continuous activity (diaphragm, tongue, extraocular, masseter) are classically most heavily parasitized.

Incorrect. Like the small intestine, the large intestine is not the habitat of Dracunculus medinensis's adult worm.

Lymph nodes

## title
W. bancrofti microfilaria differs from Loa loa's

## question
Microfilaria of Wuchereria bancrofti differs from microfileria of Loa loa in?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Loose sheath

## explanation_a
Incorrect alone. It is true W. bancrofti's sheath is loose compared with Loa loa's tightly fitting sheath, but this is only one of three jointly true differences offered.

## answer_b
Gentle curves

## explanation_b
Incorrect alone. It is true W. bancrofti's body shows gentle, smooth curves compared with Loa loa's kinky, irregular curves, but this is only one of three jointly true differences offered.

## answer_c
No nuclei in posterior end

## explanation_c
Incorrect alone. It is true W. bancrofti's posterior end is free of nuclei compared with Loa loa's nuclei extending to the tail tip, but this is only one of three jointly true differences offered.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. The microfilaria of Wuchereria bancrofti differs from that of Loa loa in three linked morphological features, combined here into one 'all of the above' item: W. bancrofti's sheath is loose (versus Loa loa's tightly fitting sheath); its body shows gentle, smooth curves (versus Loa loa's kinky, irregular curves); and its posterior end is free of nuclei (versus Loa loa's nuclei extending to the very tip of the tail). This three-point comparison is the standard way to differentiate the two filarial species on a blood film.

## title
Onchocerca volvulus microfilariae show no periodicity

## question
Periodicity is a characteristic feature for the following microfilariae EXCEPT?

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Wuchereria bancrofti

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Wuchereria bancrofti microfilariae circulate in blood with nocturnal periodicity, so it is not the exception.

## answer_b
Brugia malayi

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Brugia malayi microfilariae circulate in blood with (nocturnally subperiodic) periodicity, so it is not the exception.

## answer_c
Onchocerca volvulus

## explanation_c
Correct. Onchocerca volvulus microfilariae are the exception among filariae in not showing blood periodicity, since they reside in the skin and subcutaneous tissue rather than in the bloodstream. Wuchereria bancrofti, Brugia malayi and Loa loa microfilariae all circulate in peripheral blood with a defined periodicity timed to their respective biting-vector activity, but Onchocerca's skin-dwelling microfilariae show no periodicity at all -- a fact that also explains why Onchocerca is diagnosed by skin snip, not by a timed blood film.

## answer_d
Loa loa

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Loa loa microfilariae circulate in blood with diurnal periodicity, so it is not the exception.
