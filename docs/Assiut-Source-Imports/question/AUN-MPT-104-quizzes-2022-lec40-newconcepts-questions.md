## title
Benzathine penicillin prophylaxes against Strep haemolyticus

## question
The best effective prophylactic drug in streptococcus haemolyticus is:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Benzathine penicillin

## explanation_a
Correct. Benzathine penicillin, a long-acting depot form of penicillin G, is the best prophylactic drug against Streptococcus haemolyticus (group A beta-haemolytic streptococcus), maintaining protective drug levels for weeks with a single injection -- the basis for long-term rheumatic fever prophylaxis. Doxycycline, flucloxacillin and ceftriaxone are not the standard prophylactic choice for this organism.

## answer_b
Doxycycline

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Doxycycline is not the standard long-term prophylactic choice against group A streptococcus.

## answer_c
Flucloxacillin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Flucloxacillin, a short-acting oral penicillinase-resistant penicillin, is not suited to long-term depot prophylaxis the way benzathine penicillin is.

## answer_d
Ceftriaxone

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ceftriaxone is not the standard long-term prophylactic choice against group A streptococcus.

## answer_d
Moxalactam

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Moxalactam is a cephalosporin-class antibiotic itself, not a beta-lactamase inhibitor.

## title
Beta-lactam resistance arises from multiple mechanisms

## question
Resistance to Penicillin and other β-lactams is due to:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Inactivation by β lactamase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Beta-lactamase inactivation is a genuine mechanism, but not the only one; it is one of several that together give the complete answer.

## answer_b
All of the above

## explanation_b
Correct. Bacteria can resist penicillin and other beta-lactam antibiotics through several distinct, non-mutually-exclusive mechanisms: inactivation by beta-lactamase enzymes, altered porin structure reducing drug entry into gram-negative organisms, and altered penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) reducing the drug's target affinity. Because all three mechanisms genuinely contribute to beta-lactam resistance, "all of the above" is the correct, most complete answer.

## answer_c
Change of porin structure

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Altered porin structure is a genuine mechanism, but not the only one; it is one of several that together give the complete answer.

## answer_d
Alteration of PBPs

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Altered PBPs is a genuine mechanism, but not the only one; it is one of several that together give the complete answer.

Concerning vancomycin, the following is correct EXCEPT:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is used intravenously in the treatment of pseudomembranous colitis

## explanation_a
Correct (as the EXCEPT). Vancomycin inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis, causes histamine release (red man syndrome) as an adverse effect, and is a first-choice drug for MRSA -- but for pseudomembranous colitis, typically Clostridioides difficile, it must be given orally, not intravenously, since intravenous vancomycin does not reach an effective concentration within the gut lumen where the infection occurs. Oral vancomycin acts locally within the colon instead, so the statement that it is used intravenously for pseudomembranous colitis is the incorrect one, the EXCEPT this question asks for.

It inhibits synthesis of bacterial cell wall.

Incorrect (as the EXCEPT) -- this statement is true. Vancomycin does inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis.

## answer_c
Histamine release is one of its adverse effects

## explanation_c
Incorrect (as the EXCEPT) -- this statement is true. Histamine release (red man syndrome) is a recognised vancomycin adverse effect.

## answer_d
It is effective as a first choice drug in the treatment of MRSA

## explanation_d
Incorrect (as the EXCEPT) -- this statement is true. Vancomycin is a first-choice drug for MRSA.

Incorrect. Acute haemolytic anemia is not ampicillin's characteristic adverse effect; it is more associated with drugs such as certain sulfonamides in G6PD deficiency.

## answer_b
Maculpapular rash

## explanation_b
Correct. A maculopapular (morbilliform) skin rash is a characteristic adverse effect of ampicillin, occurring in a substantial minority of patients and especially common, and non-allergic in mechanism, when ampicillin is given during infectious mononucleosis (EBV infection). This is distinct from acute haemolytic anemia, agranulocytosis or phototoxicity, none of which is ampicillin's characteristic reaction.

Phototoxicity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phototoxicity is not ampicillin's characteristic adverse effect; it is more associated with drugs such as tetracyclines or fluoroquinolones.

## title
Erythromycin for penicillin-allergic Strep pneumoniae infection

## question
A patient with hypersensitivity to pencillin V acquired an infection with streptococcus pneumonia, which of the following antimicrobials would be the best choice for that patient?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Amoxicillin/clavulanate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Amoxicillin/clavulanate is itself a penicillin, which a patient with true penicillin hypersensitivity should avoid.

## answer_b
Ampicillin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ampicillin is itself a penicillin, which a patient with true penicillin hypersensitivity should avoid.

## answer_c
Cefaclor

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cefaclor, a cephalosporin, carries some cross-reactivity risk with penicillin allergy and is not the safest choice here.

## answer_d
Erythromycin

## explanation_d
Correct. In a patient with true hypersensitivity to penicillin V who develops a Streptococcus pneumoniae infection, erythromycin, a macrolide, is the appropriate alternative, since it belongs to a structurally unrelated antibiotic class with no cross-reactivity risk. This is unlike amoxicillin/clavulanate and ampicillin, both penicillins, or cefaclor, a cephalosporin, which carries some cross-reactivity risk with penicillin allergy.

## subject
pharm
