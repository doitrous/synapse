## title
Ligands that interact with ligand-gated ion channels

## question
One of the following interact with ligand-gated ion channels:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Glutamate

## explanation_a
Correct. Ligand-gated ion channels (ionotropic receptors) are directly linked to an ion channel, so ligand binding opens the channel within milliseconds -- the fastest of the four receptor superfamilies. Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors are the best-known example, and glutamate, GABA, glycine and 5-HT3 receptors are this same structural type, all producing their effect by directly gating an ion channel rather than through an intermediate signalling step.

## answer_b
Insulin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Insulin acts at an enzyme (tyrosine-kinase)-linked receptor -- its extracellular binding stimulates an intrinsic cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase, a response unfolding over minutes, not the millisecond ion-channel-gating mechanism this question asks about.

## answer_c
Cortisol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cortisol is a lipid-soluble steroid hormone acting at an intracellular (nuclear) receptor, chaperoned by hsp90 in its resting state and requiring translocation to the nucleus and new gene transcription -- the slowest of the four superfamilies (hours to days), the opposite end of the time scale from a ligand-gated ion channel.

## answer_d
Adrenaline

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adrenaline (epinephrine) acts at G-protein coupled (metabotropic) adrenergic receptors, which regulate an effector enzyme and a second messenger over seconds -- an indirect signalling mechanism, unlike the direct channel-gating of an ionotropic receptor.
