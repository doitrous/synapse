## title
Consequence of FDA pregnancy category D

## question
A drug has been classified under "D" in FDA pregnancy categories. What would be the consequences of using that drug?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Animal studies have demonstrated adverse effects

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adverse effects only in animal studies, with no human data, describes category C, a less certain level of risk than category D, which is based on demonstrated risk from human data or experience, not animal studies alone.

## answer_b
Fetal risk has been demonstrated, in certain circumstances benefits could outweighs the risk

## explanation_b
Correct. Category D means fetal risk has been demonstrated, from animal studies, human studies, or post-marketing experience. The drug may still be justified in certain circumstances, typically a serious maternal illness with no safer alternative, because its potential benefit could outweigh that known risk.

## answer_c
No risk to the fetus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "No risk to the fetus" would describe category A, the safest category, not category D, which specifically means risk has been demonstrated.

## answer_d
Fetal risk has been demonstrated, the risk outweighs any possible benefit to the mother

## explanation_d
Incorrect. "Risk always outweighs benefit" describes category X, an absolute contraindication -- category D instead leaves room for the drug to be used if the benefit outweighs the risk in a given case.

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. "Benefits could outweigh the risk in certain circumstances" describes category D, not category X -- category X instead means the risk always outweighs any possible benefit, with no justified-use exception.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. "No risk to the fetus" would describe category A, the safest category, the opposite of category X, which carries demonstrated risk that outweighs any benefit.

## answer_c
Animal studies have demonstrated no adverse effects

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "No adverse effects in animal studies" would describe category A or B, not category X, which is defined by demonstrated fetal risk, not an absence of it.

## answer_d
Fetal risk has been demonstrated, the risk outweighs any possible benefit to the mother

## explanation_d
Correct. Category X means fetal risk has been demonstrated, and that risk outweighs any possible benefit to the mother under any circumstance. Category X drugs are therefore contraindicated in pregnancy, with no risk-benefit exception the way category D allows.
