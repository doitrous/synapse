## title
Prostatic carcinoma produces osteosclerotic bone metastases

## question
Which one of these tumours metastasizing in bone is osteosclerotic:

## subject

## status

## answer_a
breast carcinoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Breast carcinoma's bone metastases are classically mixed (both lytic and sclerotic), not the purely osteosclerotic pattern this question asks for.

## answer_b
prostatic carcinoma

## explanation_b
Correct. Prostatic carcinoma characteristically produces osteosclerotic (osteoblastic) bone metastases, in which new bone formation dominates over resorption, giving a dense, radio-opaque appearance on imaging. This is a distinctive teaching point that helps distinguish prostatic metastases from the classically osteolytic pattern of renal cell carcinoma and thyroid carcinoma, and from bronchogenic carcinoma's typically lytic metastases.

## answer_c
renal cell carcinoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Renal cell carcinoma's bone metastases are classically osteolytic, the opposite pattern from the osteosclerotic one this question asks for.

## answer_d
bronchogenic carcinoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Bronchogenic (lung) carcinoma's bone metastases are typically osteolytic, not osteosclerotic.

## answer_e
thyroid gland carcinoma

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Thyroid carcinoma's bone metastases are classically osteolytic, not osteosclerotic.
