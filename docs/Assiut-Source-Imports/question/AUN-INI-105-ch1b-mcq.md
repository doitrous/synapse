## title
Bacteria have no mitochondria

## question
Bacterial cell does not contain?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Cell wall

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bacteria (with rare exceptions such as Mycoplasma) do have a cell wall, the structure giving the cell its shape and rigidity.

## answer_b
Mitochondria

## explanation_b
Correct. Bacteria are prokaryotes and lack membrane-bound organelles entirely, including mitochondria. Where a eukaryotic cell houses its respiratory-chain enzymes in mitochondria, a bacterial cell instead carries out respiration at its cytoplasmic membrane, classically at membrane invaginations called mesosomes. This absence of mitochondria is a defining structural difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

## answer_c
70s ribosomes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bacteria do contain 70S ribosomes, the smaller ribosome size characteristic of prokaryotes, distinct from the 80S ribosomes of eukaryotic cells.

## answer_d
Mesosomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mesosomes are cytoplasmic membrane invaginations found in bacteria, classically described as housing respiratory enzymes and coordinating chromosome segregation.

## question

## subject

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Spores are characteristically dehydrated, with a markedly lower water content than a vegetative cell, not a higher one -- this dehydration itself is part of what confers resistance, the reverse of this option's claim.

## answer_b
Presence of cortex and coat

## explanation_b
Correct. The spore's dense, multilayered coat and underlying cortex form a highly impermeable barrier around the dehydrated core, physically restricting the penetration of water, heat and chemical disinfectants. Highly metabolic activity

Incorrect. A spore is metabolically dormant, not highly active -- this near-total metabolic shutdown is itself part of the survival strategy, not a source of heat resistance.

Incorrect. A spore does not multiply at all; it is a dormant, non-replicating survival stage that must first germinate back into a vegetative cell before any multiplication can resume.

## answer_e
Low content of calcium dipicolinate

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Spore resistance is associated with a high, not low, content of calcium dipicolinate, which complexes with core DNA and helps stabilise it against heat damage -- this option reverses the actual relationship.

## question
Plasmids are?

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cytoplasmic storage (inclusion) granules are reserve-nutrient structures such as volutin or glycogen granules, a wholly different structure from a plasmid.

## answer_b
RNA molecules

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A plasmid is a DNA molecule, not RNA -- it is a small, circular, double-stranded DNA element separate from the chromosome.

## answer_c
Chromosomal DNA

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A plasmid is extrachromosomal DNA by definition, distinct from the bacterium's own chromosomal DNA.

Incorrect. It is specifically the chromosome that carries the genetic material essential for the bacterium's core life processes -- a plasmid instead carries accessory, non-essential genes.

## answer_e
Dispensable

## explanation_e
Correct. A plasmid typically carries accessory genes, such as antibiotic-resistance or virulence genes, that are advantageous but not required for the cell's basic survival. Because these genes are not essential, plasmids are properly described as dispensable genetic elements: a bacterium can lose a plasmid and still survive, unlike loss of its chromosome.

## question
Bacterial nucleoid differs from eukaryotic nucleus in being?

## subject

## status

## answer_a
Contains histones

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Bacterial DNA is not organised around histone proteins the way eukaryotic chromatin is -- the nucleoid lacks true histones, so this is not a feature distinguishing it from a eukaryotic nucleus in the direction implied here.

## answer_b
Contains ribosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ribosomes are found free in the cytoplasm surrounding the nucleoid, not inside it, in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells' respective compartments; this is not the defining structural difference asked for.

## answer_c
Contains multiple chromosome

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The bacterial nucleoid typically holds a single circular chromosome, not multiple ones -- multiple linear chromosomes are the eukaryotic pattern, the reverse of this option's claim.

## answer_d
Lack nuclear membrane

## explanation_d
Correct. The bacterial nucleoid is an unbound region of cytoplasm holding the cell's DNA, with no membrane enclosing it -- unlike the eukaryotic nucleus, whose DNA is enclosed within a double nuclear membrane. This absence of a nuclear membrane is the defining structural distinction between the two.
