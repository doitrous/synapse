## title
Suture as a fibrous joint

## question
Suture is example of ----------- joint

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
primary cartilaginous

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A primary cartilaginous joint (synchondrosis) is a temporary union by hyaline cartilage, such as an epiphyseal plate, not a skull suture.

## answer_b
Fibrous

## explanation_b
Correct. A fibrous joint is an immobile union between bones by fibrous tissue and comes in three named types: syndesmosis, held by an interosseous ligament; gomphosis, a tooth root held in its socket by the periodontal ligament; and suture, where skull bones are held together by a thin sutural ligament that is obliterated in old age. A suture is therefore the fibrous joint found specifically between the flat bones of the skull vault. It is this fibrous connective-tissue union, not a cartilage plug or a synovial cavity, that both permits the skull to grow in childhood and fixes the bones rigidly once the ligament ossifies with age.

## answer_c
Synovial

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A synovial joint has a joint cavity and is freely mobile, the opposite of a suture's rigid, immobile union.

## answer_d
Secondry cartilaginous

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A secondary cartilaginous joint (symphysis) is a permanent union by fibrocartilage, such as the pubic symphysis, not a skull suture.

## answer_b
Plane synovial joint

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A plane joint allows only gliding between flat surfaces, unlike the elbow's hinge-like flexion and extension.

## answer_c
Hinge synovial joint

## explanation_c
Correct. The elbow (humero-ulnar) joint is a uniaxial hinge (ginglymus) synovial joint, permitting flexion and extension only, around a single transverse axis through the trochlea. Hinge joints in general, including the elbow, the knee and the interphalangeal joints, are confined to one plane of movement around a transverse axis, which distinguishes them from a pivot joint's rotation about a longitudinal axis. Forearm rotation, pronation and supination, happen at the separate radioulnar joints, not at this humero-ulnar hinge itself.

## answer_d
Ball and socket synovial joint

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A ball and socket joint, such as the hip or shoulder, is multi-axial, unlike the elbow's single-plane hinge movement.

It keeps the defining every-plane movement of the ball and socket class even though it is less freely mobile than the shoulder.

Incorrect. "Both A and B" is incoherent as an answer here since option A is itself a self-reference; the site of active blood-cell formation is red marrow alone, not red and yellow marrow together.

## answer_b
Red bone marrow

## explanation_b
Correct. Red bone marrow is the site of active haemopoiesis: it has a stroma of fixed cells (reticular cells and fibres, fibroblasts, osteogenic cells and fat cells) and wide, irregular blood sinusoids lined by a discontinuous basement membrane, through which finished blood cells leave into the circulation. Between the stroma and the sinusoids sit the free cells, the developing stages of every blood-cell line and the haemopoietic stem cells that give rise to them. This combination of supporting stroma, permeable sinusoids and actively dividing free cells is what makes red marrow, specifically, the tissue that manufactures blood cells.

## title
The epidermis as the outer layer of skin

## question
The epidermis is the ----------layer of the skin

## subject
derm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Intermediate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The skin has only two layers, so there is no intermediate layer between them.

## answer_b
Outer

## explanation_b
Correct. The skin has two layers: the epidermis, the outer layer, a stratified keratinised epithelium with no blood vessels of its own, and the dermis, the inner layer, a vascular layer of dense irregular connective tissue that nourishes the avascular epidermis sitting on top of it by diffusion. Because the epidermis has no blood supply of its own, it depends entirely on the dermis beneath it, which is exactly why the two layers must be told apart correctly by position, outer versus inner. Naming the epidermis as outer, not inner, is the whole of what this fact tests.

## answer_c
Inner

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The dermis, not the epidermis, is the inner layer of the skin.

## answer_d
