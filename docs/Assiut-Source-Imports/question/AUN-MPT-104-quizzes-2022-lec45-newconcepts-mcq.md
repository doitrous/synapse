## title
Chronic UV exposure is the most important risk factor for this fair-skinned man's skin cancer

## question
A 42 years old fair skin man has a skin nodule on the right hand. It becomes larger and dark in color. The lesion was excised and proved to be skin cancer. Which of the following risk factor is most important for development of this tumor?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Inheritance of Rb gene mutation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Inherited Rb gene mutation predisposes to retinoblastoma and certain sarcomas, not the sporadic skin cancer this fair-skinned patient's sun-exposed hand lesion suggests.

## answer_b
Asbestos exposure

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Asbestos exposure is linked to mesothelioma and lung cancer, not skin cancer.

## answer_c
Chronic exposure to UV rays

## explanation_c
Correct. Chronic exposure to ultraviolet (UV) rays is the most important risk factor for skin cancer, especially in fair-skinned individuals whose lower melanin content gives less natural photoprotection. A darkening, enlarging pigmented nodule on a sun-exposed area such as the hand in a fair-skinned man is a classic presentation pointing to UV-driven skin malignancy, distinct from the risk factors named for the other options.

## answer_d
Cigarette smoking

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cigarette smoking is a major risk factor for lung, bladder and several other cancers, but it is not the most important risk factor for this fair-skinned patient's sun-exposed skin lesion.

## question

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Gastric lymphoma

## explanation_a
Correct. Chronic Helicobacter pylori infection is implicated in the development of gastric lymphoma, particularly MALT (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue) lymphoma, through sustained antigenic stimulation of gastric lymphoid tissue, as well as gastric adenocarcinoma via chronic gastritis. This is a distinctive bacterial-carcinogenesis association, unlike brain tumours, metastasis in general or colon carcinoma, none of which H. pylori is characteristically linked to.

## answer_b
Brain tumors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. H. pylori infection is not characteristically linked to brain tumour development.

## answer_c
Metastasis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Metastasis is a general process of tumour spread, not a specific disease H. pylori is implicated in causing.

## answer_d
Colon carcinoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. H. pylori's carcinogenic association is with the stomach, not colon carcinoma specifically.

## subject

## status

Hepatitis C virus

Correct. Hepatitis C virus (HCV) is taught as the most important carcinogenic virus in Egypt, reflecting the country's historically very high HCV prevalence and its strong association with chronic liver disease progressing to hepatocellular carcinoma. This local-epidemiology teaching point distinguishes HCV's importance in Egypt specifically from HPV, HIV or EBV, each carcinogenic in its own right but not the department's named most important carcinogenic virus for this population.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. HIV itself is not directly oncogenic in the way the printed key describes, though it predisposes to certain cancers through immunosuppression; it is not the answer this Egypt-specific question asks for.

## answer_d
Epstein-Barr virus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Epstein-Barr virus is an important carcinogenic virus generally, but the printed key names hepatitis C virus specifically as most important in Egypt.
