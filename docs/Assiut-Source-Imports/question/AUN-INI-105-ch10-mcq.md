## title
Platyhelminths needing two intermediate hosts, except

## question
The following platyhelminths require two intermediate hosts EXCEPT ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Diphyllobothrium latum

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the EXCEPT answer. Diphyllobothrium latum does need two intermediate hosts -- Cyclops, then a fresh-water fish -- so it belongs with the other three-host parasites, not with the exception.

## answer_b
Heterophyes heterophyes

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the EXCEPT answer. Heterophyes heterophyes, a fish-borne intestinal fluke, also needs two intermediate hosts (a snail, then a fish), so it too belongs with the two-host group.

## answer_c
Metagonimus yokogawai

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the EXCEPT answer. Metagonimus yokogawai likewise needs two intermediate hosts (a snail, then a fish), so it stays with the two-host group rather than being the exception.

## answer_d
Taenia Solium

## explanation_d
Correct. Taenia solium is the exception: it needs only a single intermediate host, the pig, in which its egg develops into the infective cysticercus cellulosae -- unlike Diphyllobothrium latum, Heterophyes heterophyes and Metagonimus yokogawai, which all require two intermediate hosts in succession before becoming infective. This single-intermediate-host pattern is one of the ways exam questions test host-count differences among the platyhelminths.

## answer_b
Echinococcus granulosus

## explanation_b
Incorrect alone (true but partial). The dog is indeed the definitive host of Echinococcus granulosus, whose adult tapeworm lives in the dog intestine, but this too is only one of three jointly true statements.

## explanation_c
Incorrect alone (true but partial). The dog is indeed the definitive host of Multiceps multiceps, but this is again only one of three jointly true statements, not the complete answer alone.

Correct. This grouping of three unrelated parasites under one definitive host is a common EXCEPT/All-of-the-above exam pattern.

## question

## subject
inf

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Man, not the dog, is Taenia saginata's definitive host -- the dog is definitive host for other cestodes, such as Echinococcus granulosus, not for T. saginata.

## answer_b
Pig is the intermediate host

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cattle, not the pig, is Taenia saginata's intermediate host; the pig is the intermediate host of the related species Taenia solium instead.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Taenia saginata inhabits the small intestine, not the large intestine, like the other human intestinal cestodes.

Correct. Taenia saginata belongs to the order Cyclophyllidea, the order that includes most of the medically important tapeworms (Taenia, Hymenolepis, Echinococcus, Dipylidium), distinguished from the pseudophyllidean order that Diphyllobothrium latum belongs to. Recognising the correct order distinguishes T. saginata from cyclophyllidean look-alikes that share some, but not all, of these features.

T. solium

Incorrect. Man is a genuine (not dead-end) intermediate host for Taenia solium in the sense that his own tissue cysts can, in principle, be a source of further transmission if eaten; "blind" intermediate host specifically describes E. granulosus's dead-end relationship with man.

Cysticercus cellulosae

Incorrect. Ingesting the Taenia egg causes cysticercosis (tissue disease), not taeniasis (the adult intestinal worm).

None of the above

Incorrect. Option B, cysticercus cellulosae, is correct, so "none of the above" does not apply.
