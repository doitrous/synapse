## title
Endarteritis obliterans means thick-walled arteries with narrow lumen

## question
Endarteritis obliterans means:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Thin-walled arteries with wide lumen

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This is the opposite finding -- endarteritis obliterans thickens the vessel wall and narrows the lumen, it does not thin the wall or widen the lumen.

## answer_b
Arteries are end arteries

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Being an end artery is an unrelated anatomical fact about a vessel's supply pattern, not a description of the chronic intimal thickening that defines endarteritis obliterans.

## answer_c
Obliteration of the arterial lumen

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Endarteritis obliterans narrows the lumen through wall thickening; describing it as complete obliteration overstates the process, which is progressive narrowing, not necessarily total closure.

## answer_d
Thick-walled arteries with narrow lumen

## explanation_d
Correct. Endarteritis obliterans is chronic thickening of the arterial intima. The vessel wall becomes thicker while its lumen narrows, producing the classic thick-walled, narrow-lumen picture. It is seen accompanying chronic granulomatous and chronic infective processes such as tuberculosis.

## title
Macrophage is the most important cell in granuloma development

## question
The most important cell in the development of a granuloma is a:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Neutrophil

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Neutrophils are the hallmark cell of acute, not granulomatous, inflammation -- they are not the driving cell of a granuloma.

## answer_b
Macrophage

## explanation_b
Correct. The macrophage, transformed by persistent antigenic stimulation into an epithelioid cell, is the central, key cell of every granuloma. Activated macrophages present antigen, respond to T-cell lymphokines, and can fuse into giant cells, making them the cell type that both forms and defines the granuloma.

## answer_c
Mast cell

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mast cells drive immediate (type I) hypersensitivity reactions, not the type IV, macrophage-driven process that builds a granuloma.

## answer_d
Fibroblast

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fibroblasts contribute to the later fibrotic/healing response around a chronic lesion, not to the granuloma's defining epithelioid-macrophage architecture.

## title
Silicosis is a cause of non-infective granuloma

## question
Which of the following is a cause of non infective granuloma:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Silicosis

## explanation_a
Correct. Silicosis, from chronic inhalation of crystalline silica particles that macrophages cannot digest, is a standard non-infective cause of granuloma -- the trigger is an inert particle, not an organism. The particles resist macrophage digestion the same way an indigestible foreign material does, sustaining the same persistent-antigen-style stimulus that drives epithelioid-macrophage transformation. This inert-particle mechanism is what separates silicosis from every infective cause on this list.

## answer_b
Bilharziasis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Bilharziasis is caused by Schistosoma ova and is an infective, not non-infective, cause of granulomatous inflammation.

## answer_c
Tuberculosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis and is a classic infective cause of granuloma, not a non-infective one.

## answer_d
Leprosy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Leprosy is caused by Mycobacterium leprae and, like tuberculosis, is an infective granuloma-forming disease.

Epithelioid cells are modified macrophages

## question
Epithelioid cells are modiﬁed:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Lymphocytes are present in a granuloma and drive macrophage activation through lymphokines, but they are not themselves what transforms into an epithelioid cell.

## answer_b
Epithelial cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Despite the name, epithelioid cells are not derived from epithelial cells -- "epithelioid" describes their epithelium-like microscopic appearance, not their cell of origin.

## answer_c
Macrophages

## explanation_c
Correct. Epithelioid cells are macrophages that have been transformed by persistent antigenic stimulation. They enlarge, flatten their nucleus and gain abundant pink cytoplasm that resembles epithelium, which is where the name "epithelioid" comes from. Despite that epithelium-like appearance, they remain macrophages in origin and function, and are the key cell of granuloma formation.

## answer_d
Plasma cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Plasma cells are antibody-secreting B-lymphocyte derivatives found in chronic inflammation, not the cell that transforms into an epithelioid cell.

Tuberculous reaction

Bilharzial reaction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A bilharzial reaction is caused by Schistosoma ova, an entirely different infective process from the Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection that produces the Ghon focus.

## title
Lymphokines mediate the chronic inflammation of delayed hypersensitivity

## question
The chronic inﬂammation of a delayed hypersensitivity reaction is mediated by

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Complement

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Complement acts chiefly in acute inflammation and antibody-mediated (type II/III) hypersensitivity, not in the T-cell-driven chronic inflammation of a delayed (type IV) reaction.

## answer_b
Bradykinin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Bradykinin is a mediator of acute inflammation's vascular changes and pain, not of the lymphocyte-driven chronic inflammation of delayed hypersensitivity.

## answer_c
Lymphokines

## explanation_c
Correct. Lymphokines are cytokines released by activated T lymphocytes. They recruit and activate macrophages at the reaction site, sustaining the mononuclear infiltrate. This lymphokine-driven activation is what mediates the chronic inflammation of a delayed (type IV) hypersensitivity reaction, including granuloma formation.

## answer_d
Histamine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Histamine is the principal mediator of immediate (type I) hypersensitivity and early acute-inflammatory vasodilation, not of delayed hypersensitivity's chronic inflammation.

## answer_e
Polymorphs

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Polymorphs (neutrophils) are a cell type, not a chemical mediator, and they characterise acute rather than chronic inflammation.
