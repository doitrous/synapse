## title
Sulfasalazine treats inflammatory bowel disease

## question
A patient admitted to the gastroenterology service is being treated with sulfasalazine. What is the most likely purpose for this drug to be given?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inflammatory bowel disease

## explanation_a
Correct. Sulfasalazine is a standard treatment for inflammatory bowel disease, particularly ulcerative colitis, since gut bacteria cleave it into 5-aminosalicylic acid, which acts locally in the colon to reduce mucosal inflammation. It is not a standard treatment for pseudomembranous colitis, H. pylori infection or E. coli diarrhea, so a gastroenterology patient on sulfasalazine is most likely being treated for inflammatory bowel disease.

## answer_b
Antibiotic associated pseudomembranous colitis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pseudomembranous colitis, typically from Clostridioides difficile, is standardly treated with oral vancomycin or metronidazole, not sulfasalazine.

## answer_c
Gastric H. pylori infections

## explanation_c
Incorrect. H. pylori infection is standardly treated with a combination regimen (a proton pump inhibitor plus antibiotics such as clarithromycin and amoxicillin or metronidazole), not sulfasalazine.

## answer_d
Echerichia coli induced diarrhea

## explanation_d
Incorrect. E. coli-induced diarrhea is not a standard indication for sulfasalazine.

## title
Sulfamethoxazole can cause crystalluria

## question
Which antibiotic produces crystalluria?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Sulfamethoxazole

## explanation_a
Correct. Sulfamethoxazole and other sulfonamides can precipitate out in acidic urine to form crystals in the renal tubules, a recognised adverse effect called crystalluria, which can be reduced by adequate hydration and urine alkalinisation. Penicillin, doxycycline and gentamicin do not characteristically cause this specific adverse effect.

## answer_b
Penicillin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Penicillin is not characteristically associated with crystalluria.

## answer_c
Doxycycline

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Doxycycline is not characteristically associated with crystalluria; its own recognised issues are photosensitivity and teeth/bone effects.

## answer_d
Gentamycin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gentamicin's recognised toxicities are nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity, not crystalluria specifically.

Pseudomembranous colitis may be treated with either vancomycin or metronidazole

## question
Pseudomembranous colitis may be treated with:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect (as the single answer). Metronidazole alone is a genuine treatment option, but the printed key names both agents together as the more complete answer.

## answer_b
Clindamycin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Clindamycin is not a treatment for pseudomembranous colitis; it is instead one of the antibiotics classically implicated in CAUSING it by disrupting normal gut flora.

## answer_c
Vancomycin

## explanation_c
Incorrect (as the single answer). Vancomycin (given orally) alone is a genuine treatment option, but the printed key names both agents together as the more complete answer.

## answer_d
Both Vancomycin and Metronidazole

## explanation_d
Correct. Pseudomembranous colitis, typically caused by Clostridioides difficile overgrowth after gut flora disruption, may be treated with either oral vancomycin or metronidazole, both standard options depending on severity and local practice. Since both metronidazole and vancomycin are genuinely effective choices for this condition, the combined option is the most complete and correct answer among those given.

## title
Tendonitis is a side effect of ciprofloxacin

## question
Which of the following is a side effect of ciprofloxacin?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pseudomembranous toxicity

## explanation_a
Incorrect. "Pseudomembranous toxicity" is not a recognised term for a ciprofloxacin side effect; pseudomembranous colitis can follow broad-spectrum antibiotic use generally, but it is not ciprofloxacin's characteristic, defining adverse effect.

## answer_b
Teeth discolouration

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Teeth discolouration is characteristic of tetracyclines, not fluoroquinolones such as ciprofloxacin.

## answer_c
Ototoxicity

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ototoxicity is characteristic of aminoglycosides, not ciprofloxacin's characteristic adverse effect.

## answer_d
Tendonitis

## explanation_d
Correct. Tendonitis, and in more severe cases tendon rupture (especially of the Achilles tendon), is a recognised, characteristic adverse effect of ciprofloxacin and other fluoroquinolones, thought to relate to the drug class's effect on collagen and connective tissue. This risk is heightened in older patients and those on concurrent corticosteroids, and is a distinctive teaching point that distinguishes fluoroquinolone toxicity from teeth discolouration (tetracyclines) or ototoxicity (aminoglycosides).
