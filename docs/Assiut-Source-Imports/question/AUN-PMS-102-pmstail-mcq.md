Regions of the mouth

## question
Regarding the mouth, the correct statement is:

## subject
gi

## status

## owner

## vignette

C

## answer_a
The mouth proper is the all mouth cavity

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The mouth proper is only the inner of the mouth's two regions, not the whole cavity.

## answer_b
The vestibule is the slit-like space separating the lips from the cheeks

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The vestibule is the slit-like space between the lips/cheeks on the outside and the teeth/gums on the inside, not a space separating the lips from the cheeks themselves.

## answer_c
The mouth is divided into vestibule and mouth proper

## explanation_c
Correct. The mouth (oral cavity) is divided into two regions: the vestibule, the slit-like space outside the teeth and gums and inside the lips and cheeks, and the mouth proper, the space inside the teeth and gums that extends back to the oropharyngeal isthmus. This two-part division is the standard description of the mouth's regions taught in gross anatomy. Recognising both named regions, and not collapsing them into one cavity or splitting them into three, is what the question is testing.

## answer_d
The mouth is divided into three parts

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The mouth is divided into two regions, the vestibule and the mouth proper, not three.

Getting this pairing right, greater with left/convex/longer and lesser with right/concave/shorter, is the essential distinction being tested.

## title
Cell death as irreversible cessation of vital activity

## question
The irreversible cessation of vital activities inside the cell:

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Cell cycle

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cell cycle is the recurring sequence of growth and division stages a living cell passes through, not the ending of its vital activity.

## answer_b
Cell differentiation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cell differentiation is the process by which a cell acquires a specialised, mature form and function, not the ending of its vital activity.

## answer_c
Cell growth

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cell growth is an increase in cell size or number as part of normal living activity, not its ending.

## answer_d
Cell death

## explanation_d
Correct. Cell death is defined as the irreversible cessation of a cell's vital activities, the point past which the cell can no longer be revived or continue any of its normal functions. It can occur by different mechanisms, such as necrosis from injury or the programmed process of apoptosis, but in every case the defining feature is that the loss of function is permanent, not simply a slowed or paused metabolic state. This irreversibility is what separates true cell death from a reversible interruption of cell activity.

## title
Stem cells and asymmetric division

## question
What is the role of stem cells with regard to the function of adult tissues and organs?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Stem cells are undifferentiated cells that divide asymmetrically, giving rise to one daughter that remains a stem cell and one daughter that will differentiate to replace damaged and worn out cells in the adult tissue or organ.

## explanation_a
Correct. A stem cell is an undifferentiated cell that divides asymmetrically: one of its two daughter cells remains an undifferentiated stem cell, preserving the stem-cell population, while the other daughter goes on to differentiate and replace the damaged or worn-out cells of the adult tissue or organ. This self-renewing, asymmetric pattern of division is what lets a small, stable pool of stem cells continuously supply replacement cells throughout adult life without depleting itself. It is this combination, undifferentiated state plus asymmetric self-renewing division, that defines a stem cell functionally.

## answer_b
Stem cells are differentiated cells that reside under the surface of epithelia, in position to take over the function of the tissue when the overlying cells become damaged or worn out.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A stem cell is by definition undifferentiated, not a differentiated cell waiting beneath the epithelium.

## answer_c
Stem cells are differentiated cells and can, when needed, divide to supply new cells for growth of the tissue.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A stem cell is undifferentiated; a differentiated cell that can still divide when needed instead describes a progenitor or a stable/labile differentiated cell type, not a stem cell.

## answer_d
Stem cells are postmitotic cells which specialize and differentiate.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A stem cell is not postmitotic; its defining feature is that it keeps dividing throughout life to renew itself and to supply differentiating progeny.

## title
Progenitor cells divide infrequently

## question
Except one all the following are characteristics of progenitor cells:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
are daughter cells of stem cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Progenitor cells genuinely do arise as daughter cells of stem cells, so this is a true characteristic, not the exception.

## answer_b
have reduced potentiality (committed to a single-cell lineage)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Progenitor cells genuinely do have reduced potentiality, being already committed to a single lineage rather than able to give rise to multiple different cell types, so this is a true characteristic, not the exception.

## answer_c
divide infrequently

## explanation_c
Correct. Unlike stem cells, which divide relatively rarely so as to preserve the long-term stem-cell pool, progenitor cells divide frequently to rapidly amplify their numbers before their progeny go on to terminally differentiate. "Divide infrequently" is therefore the false statement among the four, since it describes the stem cell's own division pattern rather than the progenitor cell's characteristically rapid, transit-amplifying division. This frequent-division behaviour is exactly what lets a small stem-cell pool ultimately supply large numbers of mature, differentiated cells.

## answer_d
Cells formed by progenitor cells are terminally differentiated

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cells progenitor cells give rise to genuinely are terminally differentiated, so this is a true characteristic, not the exception.
