## title
Definition of bacterial growth

## question
The orderly increase in all components of protoplasm of a cell is called?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Reproduction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Reproduction describes the production of new daughter cells (in bacteria, via binary fission); it names the outcome event, not the orderly, proportional increase of a cell's own protoplasmic components that precedes it.

## answer_b
Cell division

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cell division is the physical splitting of one cell into two; it is the mechanism that converts individual-cell growth into population increase, not the definition of growth itself.

## answer_c
Growth

## explanation_c
Correct. "Growth" specifically names the orderly increase in all of a cell's protoplasmic components -- its cytoplasm, membranes, ribosomes and other constituents increasing together in proportion -- rather than any single event such as division. This proportional, whole-cell increase is what must happen before a bacterium is large enough to divide, making growth logically prior to, and distinct from, reproduction and cell division.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Reproduction and cell division are each a specific event distinct from the protoplasmic increase that defines growth, so "all of the above" incorrectly collapses growth into its own downstream consequences.

## title
Bacterial cell multiplication: usual mechanism

## question
The bacterial cell multiplication is usually by?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Mitosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Mitosis is a eukaryotic process using a mitotic spindle to segregate paired chromosomes; bacteria have no nucleus or spindle apparatus and do not divide by mitosis.

## answer_b
Meiosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Meiosis is the eukaryotic reductive division that halves chromosome number to form gametes; bacteria are haploid with a single chromosome and have no meiotic cycle.

## answer_c
Conjugation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Conjugation is a mechanism of horizontal gene transfer between two bacterial cells via a sex pilus; it moves genetic material between existing cells but is not itself how a bacterial cell multiplies.

## answer_d
Binary-fission

## explanation_d
Correct. Bacterial cells usually multiply by binary fission: a single cell replicates its chromosome and splits into two genetically identical daughter cells. This simple, asexual mechanism -- not mitosis, meiosis or conjugation -- is the ordinary route of bacterial multiplication, and it is what the bacterial growth curve actually tracks over time.

## title
Bacteria multiply by simple binary fission

## question
Bacteria multiply by?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Spore formation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Spore formation (sporulation) is a survival strategy certain Gram-positive genera use under nutrient-poor or hostile conditions, producing a dormant endospore -- it is not the ordinary route by which a bacterial population multiplies.

## answer_b
Simple binary fission

## explanation_b
Correct. Bacteria multiply by simple binary fission: one cell duplicates its single chromosome and divides into two identical daughter cells, without any spore, sexual, or gamete-based step. This asexual mechanism is what produces the exponential increase in cell number seen during the log phase of the growth curve.

## answer_c
Conjugation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Conjugation transfers genetic material (often a plasmid) from a donor to a recipient bacterial cell across a pilus; it changes a recipient's genetic content but does not itself produce a new daughter cell.

## answer_d
Gametes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gametes are haploid sex cells produced by eukaryotic meiosis for sexual reproduction; bacteria have no gametes and do not reproduce sexually.

## title
Convalescence corresponds to the decline phase

## question
Convalescence stage of the disease is corresponding to?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Lag phase of growth curve

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The lag phase, when bacteria are adapting to a new environment with no net division, corresponds instead to the incubation period of disease -- before symptoms appear, not to recovery.

## answer_b
Logarithmic phase of growth curve

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The logarithmic (exponential) phase, when division is at its maximum rate, corresponds to the invasive period of disease, as the organism spreads and symptoms escalate -- the opposite direction from convalescence.

## answer_c
Stationary phase of growth curve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The stationary phase, when new cells balance dying cells and the population plateaus, corresponds to the period of overt symptoms and signs -- the clinical peak of illness, not its resolution.

## answer_d
Decline phase of growth curve

## explanation_d
Correct. Convalescence is the recovery stage of an infectious disease, when the pathogen population is falling as host defences and/or treatment take effect. This maps onto the decline (death) phase of the bacterial growth curve, in which dying cells outnumber newly formed ones and the population shrinks -- the direct in-vitro counterpart of a patient's clinical recovery.

## title
Lag phase correlates with the incubation period

## question
The lag phase of bacterial growth curve correlate in vivo with which of the following?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Symptoms & Signs of diseases

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Overt symptoms and signs correspond instead to the stationary phase, the clinical peak of illness, once the organism's population has already expanded -- not to the lag phase, before division has even begun.

## answer_b
Incubation period

## explanation_b
Correct. The lag phase is the period during which bacteria adapt to a new environment and accumulate the enzymes needed for multiplication, with no net cell division yet occurring. This silent adaptation period is the direct in-vitro counterpart of the incubation period in vivo -- the interval after infection during which the pathogen establishes itself before any symptoms appear.

Incorrect. The invasive period, when the organism is actively spreading and symptoms are escalating, corresponds to the exponential (log) phase of maximal division, not the lag phase.

Incorrect. Convalescence (recovery) corresponds to the decline phase, when the pathogen population is falling -- the opposite end of the disease course from the lag phase's silent establishment.

## answer_e
Marker of chronic phase of the disease

## explanation_e
Incorrect. "Marker of chronic phase of the disease" is not a recognised correlate in this four-phase growth-curve-to-disease-stage scheme; the lag phase's specific correlate is the incubation period.

## title
Stationary phase correlates with symptoms and signs

## question
Which one of the following is correct for stationary phase of bacterial growth?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Correlates with symptoms & signs in vivo

## explanation_a
Correct. The stationary phase, when the number of newly formed cells balances the number of dying cells and the population plateaus at its peak, corresponds in vivo to the period of overt symptoms and signs -- the clinical peak of an infection, once the organism has already reached its maximum burden. This is the point at which a patient's illness is typically most apparent, since the pathogen load driving those symptoms is at its highest and steadiest.

## answer_b
The division occurs at maximum & constant rate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Division at its maximum and constant rate describes the exponential (log) phase, not the stationary phase, in which net division has instead fallen to zero as births and deaths balance.

## answer_c
The rate of division depends on the nature of the organism

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The nature of the organism is a determinant more specifically associated with the length of the lag phase, not a defining feature of the stationary phase itself.

## answer_d
It continues until nutrients in the medium become exhausted

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nutrient exhaustion is what drives the population INTO the stationary phase (and later the decline phase) rather than something the stationary phase itself continues until; by the time stationary phase is established, nutrients are already limiting.

## answer_e
Correlates with invasive period in vivo

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The invasive period corresponds instead to the exponential (log) phase, when the organism is actively multiplying and spreading -- not to the stationary phase's clinical-peak correlate.

## title
Binary fission relies on a single chromosome

## question
Reproduction in bacterin takes place by binary fission as they possess?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Multiple chromosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Multiple chromosomes would require a more complex segregation mechanism (as eukaryotic mitosis has); a bacterium's simple binary fission relies instead on a single genome copy.

## answer_b
Spores

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Spores are dormant survival structures formed by certain Gram-positive genera under stress, not the genetic basis that enables ordinary binary fission.

## answer_c
Linear chromosome

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A bacterial chromosome is typically circular, not linear; a linear chromosome is not the feature that enables simple binary fission here.

## answer_d
Single chromosome

## explanation_d
Correct. Bacteria typically carry a single, usually circular, chromosome. Having only one chromosome to replicate and segregate -- rather than multiple paired chromosomes needing a mitotic spindle -- is exactly what makes simple binary fission possible: one replication event, one segregation, one division into two identical daughters.

## answer_e
No nucleolus

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Bacteria lack a nucleolus entirely (they lack a true nucleus altogether), but the absence of a nucleolus is not itself the feature that enables binary fission; the enabling feature is the single chromosome.

## id
QST-AUNINI105-INI-MCQ-CH2-INI-MCQ-CH2-Q036

## title
Aerotolerant anaerobes are not killed by oxygen exposure

## question
Anaerobes that are not Killed by exposure to oxygen are?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Obligate anaerobes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Obligate anaerobes are, by definition, the group that IS killed or fails to grow on exposure to oxygen, because they lack the enzymes (catalase, superoxide dismutase) needed to detoxify the reactive oxygen species oxygen exposure generates -- the opposite of the organisms this question describes.

## answer_b
Microaerophilic

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Microaerophilic bacteria grow best with only small (trace) amounts of oxygen and are, like obligate anaerobes, harmed by full atmospheric oxygen concentrations; that is a preference/tolerance issue distinct from the aerotolerant anaerobe's outright survival of oxygen exposure.

## answer_c
Aerotolerant anaerobes

## explanation_c
Correct. Aerotolerant anaerobes do not use oxygen in their metabolism at all -- like obligate anaerobes, they rely on fermentative, anaerobic pathways -- but unlike obligate anaerobes they are not killed by oxygen's presence. This tolerance is generally attributed to their retaining enough superoxide dismutase and/or peroxidase to detoxify incidental reactive oxygen species, even without using oxygen for energy production.

## answer_d
Thermophilic

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Thermophilic describes temperature preference (bacteria that grow best at high temperatures), an entirely different environmental axis from oxygen tolerance.

Bacterial cell multiplies by?

## answer_a
Binary fission

## explanation_a
Correct. A bacterial cell multiplies by binary fission -- replicating its single chromosome and splitting into two identical daughter cells. This simple asexual mechanism, not budding, sexual multiplication or mitosis, is the ordinary route of bacterial reproduction.

## answer_b
Budding

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Budding is a reproductive strategy seen in some yeasts and a small number of bacteria (e.g. Hyphomicrobium) in which a smaller daughter cell forms as an outgrowth of the parent; it is not the usual bacterial reproductive mechanism being tested here.

## answer_c
Sexual multiplication

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sexual multiplication implies gamete fusion and genetic recombination between two parents producing offspring; bacteria reproduce asexually by binary fission, though they can separately exchange genes by conjugation, transformation or transduction, which are not reproduction.

## answer_d
Mitosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mitosis is a eukaryotic process requiring a mitotic spindle to segregate paired chromosomes; bacteria, lacking a nucleus and spindle apparatus, do not divide by mitosis.

## title
Growth of bacteria: increase in size and number

## question
Growth of bacteria refers to?

## subject
inf

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Increase in size

## explanation_a
Incorrect alone (true but partial). An individual bacterial cell does increase in size as it grows, but this is only one facet of what "growth of bacteria" as a population-level term refers to, not the complete answer.

## answer_b
Increase in number

## explanation_b
Incorrect alone (true but partial). A bacterial population does increase in cell number through repeated binary fission, but this too is only one facet, not the complete answer by itself.

## answer_c
Deposition of fat

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Deposition of fat (as an intracellular storage inclusion) is not what defines bacterial growth; it is an unrelated metabolic storage phenomenon, not a growth criterion.

## answer_d
A,B

## explanation_d
Correct. "Growth of bacteria" refers jointly to an increase in cell size and an increase in cell number -- at the individual-cell level a cell grows in size before dividing, and at the population level growth is observed as an increase in the number of cells. Since both A and B are genuine facets of bacterial growth and C is not, "A,B" is the accurate combined answer rather than either alone or "all of the above." This combined size-and-number framing is why the growth curve, which plots cell number, is only one lens on bacterial growth -- individual cells also enlarge before each division.

## answer_e
All of the above

## explanation_e
Incorrect. "All of the above" would wrongly include deposition of fat (C) as a defining feature of growth, when fat deposition is an unrelated storage phenomenon, not a growth criterion.
