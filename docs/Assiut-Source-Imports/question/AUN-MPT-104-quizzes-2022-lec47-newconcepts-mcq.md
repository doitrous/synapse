## title
Papilloma is the benign epithelial tumour of non-glandular surfaces

## question
A benign epithelial cell neoplasm derived from non-glandular surfaces is referred to as:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Hamartoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hamartoma is a disorganised overgrowth of tissue elements normal to the site, not the term for a benign neoplasm of non-glandular epithelium.

## answer_b
squamous cell carcinoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Squamous cell carcinoma is a malignant, not benign, epithelial tumour.

## answer_c
Adenoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Adenoma is the benign epithelial tumour of glandular, not non-glandular, surfaces.

## answer_d
Sarcoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sarcoma is a malignant mesenchymal tumour, not a benign epithelial one.

## answer_e
Papilloma

## explanation_e
Correct. Papilloma is the benign epithelial cell neoplasm derived from non-glandular surfaces (such as skin or mucosal squamous epithelium), characteristically growing as multiple finger-like (papillary) projections. This distinguishes it from adenoma, the benign tumour of glandular epithelium, and from the malignant options (squamous cell carcinoma, sarcoma) and hamartoma, a developmental malformation rather than a true neoplasm.

## question
If a tumor is benign and glandular in origin, what is it called?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Adenoma

## explanation_a
Correct. Adenoma is the term for a benign tumour of glandular epithelial origin, forming gland-like or duct-like structures resembling the tissue it arises from. This distinguishes it from papilloma (the benign tumour of non-glandular surfaces), carcinoma (a malignant epithelial tumour), a Krukenberg tumour (a specific metastatic, not primary benign, ovarian tumour) and the general descriptor "malignant."

## answer_b
Krukenburg

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A Krukenberg tumour is a metastatic (typically gastric-signet-ring-cell-derived) tumour of the ovary, not the general term for a benign glandular tumour.

## answer_c
Papilloma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Papilloma is the benign epithelial tumour of non-glandular, not glandular, surfaces.

## answer_d
Carcinoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Carcinoma denotes a malignant epithelial tumour, not a benign one.

## answer_e
Malignant

## explanation_e
Incorrect. "Malignant" is a behavioural descriptor, not the specific name for a benign glandular tumour.

## title
Papilloma characteristically shows finger-like projections

## question
Which of the following is a benign tumor of the epithelium characterized by multiple finger like projections?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Fibroma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fibroma is a benign mesenchymal (connective tissue) tumour, not an epithelial one, and does not characteristically show finger-like projections.

## answer_b
Papilloma

## explanation_b
Correct. Papilloma is the benign epithelial tumour characterised grossly by multiple finger-like (papillary) projections growing outward from the epithelial surface. This distinctive gross morphology, alongside its non-glandular epithelial origin, is what distinguishes papilloma from fibroma, adenoma or lymphoma among the options.

## answer_c
Adenoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Adenoma, the benign tumour of glandular epithelium, typically forms gland-like structures rather than the finger-like papillary projections this stem describes.

## answer_d
Lymphoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lymphoma is a malignant tumour of lymphoid tissue, not a benign epithelial tumour with a papillary morphology.
