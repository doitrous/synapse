## title
Prejunctional alpha2 activation inhibits noradrenaline release

## question
Activation of prejunctional α2 receptors on sympathetic nerve endings is associated with:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Inhibition of NEP release

## explanation_a
Correct. Noradrenaline released at the terminal acts back on its own prejunctional alpha2 autoreceptors. This Gi-coupled activation inhibits further noradrenaline (NEP) release, a negative-feedback loop that limits how much transmitter a single nerve impulse releases.

## answer_b
Increase in IP3 and DAG

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Increased IP3 and DAG is a Gq-coupled signalling pattern, such as that of postsynaptic alpha1 receptors, not the Gi-coupled presynaptic alpha2 autoreceptor.

## answer_c
Stimulation of NEP synthesis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Alpha2 autoreceptor activation inhibits, rather than stimulates, further noradrenaline release -- it does not stimulate its synthesis either.

## answer_d
Inhibition of 5-HT release only

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The prejunctional alpha2 autoreceptor's feedback effect is on noradrenaline release at the sympathetic terminal itself, not specifically or exclusively on serotonin (5-HT) release.

## title
Heart failure is not a standard epinephrine indication

## question
Epinephrine is used in the treatment of all the following conditions EXCEPT:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cardiac arrest

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cardiac arrest is a genuine epinephrine indication -- its alpha1-mediated vasoconstriction improves coronary and cerebral perfusion pressure during resuscitation.

## answer_b
Anaphylactic shock

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Anaphylactic shock is a core epinephrine indication, reversing bronchospasm and hypotension through its combined alpha/beta effects.

## answer_c
Heart failure

## explanation_c
Correct. Heart failure is not a standard epinephrine indication. Despite epinephrine's inotropic and chronotropic effects, its arrhythmogenic potential and alpha-mediated increase in afterload outweigh any short-term inotropic benefit in this chronic setting, so it is the one condition on this list epinephrine is not used to treat.

## answer_d
Complete heart block

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Complete heart block is a genuine epinephrine indication, used to increase heart rate when the normal conduction pathway has failed.

## title
Norepinephrine is given by IV infusion only

## question
Norepinephrine is used by:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
S.C. route

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Norepinephrine's severe local vasoconstrictive potency makes subcutaneous injection a tissue-necrosis risk, ruling out this route.

## answer_b
Oral route

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Norepinephrine is inactivated by first-pass gut and liver metabolism, so it is not effective by the oral route.

## answer_c
I.M. route

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Like the SC route, intramuscular injection risks severe local vasoconstriction and tissue damage, ruling it out for norepinephrine.

## answer_d
I.V. infusion route

## explanation_d
Correct. Norepinephrine is used only by IV infusion. This route avoids the local tissue-necrosis risk of SC or IM injection and the first-pass inactivation of oral dosing, while allowing the careful, continuous titration a potent vasopressor needs.

## title
Noradrenaline is agonist at alpha1, alpha2, beta1 but not beta2

## question
The following sympathomimetic amine has agonistic action on alpha1 + alpha 2+ beta 1 + beta 3 adrenoceptors, but not on beta 2 receptors:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Isoprenaline

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Isoprenaline is essentially beta-selective (beta1 and beta2), with negligible alpha activity -- the opposite emphasis from the profile described here.

## answer_b
Noradrenaline

## explanation_b
Correct. Noradrenaline is an agonist at alpha1, alpha2, beta1 and beta3 adrenoceptors, but has negligible activity at beta2. This selective sparing of beta2 is what distinguishes its receptor profile from adrenaline's, which activates all four classic subtypes including beta2.

## answer_c
Adrenaline

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Adrenaline is an agonist at all four classic adrenoceptor subtypes -- alpha1, alpha2, beta1 and beta2 -- so it does not spare beta2 the way this question describes.

## answer_d
Phenylephrine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phenylephrine is a selective alpha1 agonist with essentially no beta activity at all, not the alpha1/alpha2/beta1/beta3-without-beta2 profile described here.

## title
Axonal reuptake terminates noradrenaline's action

## question
The principal process which terminates the action of noradrenaline released from adrenergic nerve ending is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Extraneuronal uptake

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Extraneuronal uptake (Uptake-2) contributes to noradrenaline clearance but is a secondary route, not the principal termination mechanism.

## answer_b
Axonal uptake

## explanation_b
Correct. Axonal (neuronal) uptake back into the presynaptic terminal -- often called Uptake-1 -- is the principal process terminating noradrenaline's synaptic action. It is quantitatively dominant over extraneuronal uptake, COMT methylation and MAO degradation, all of which play only secondary roles.

## answer_c
Methylation by COMT

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Methylation by COMT is a real but secondary metabolic route for noradrenaline, largely extraneuronal, not the principal termination mechanism.

## answer_d
Degradation by MAO

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Degradation by MAO occurs mainly intraneuronally on noradrenaline that has escaped storage vesicles, a secondary rather than the principal clearance route.

## title
Ephedrine is a noncatecholamine sympathomimetic

## question
Which of the following is a noncatecholamine sympathomimetic:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Adrenaline

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adrenaline is a true catecholamine, carrying the catechol ring structure -- it is not the noncatecholamine option here.

## answer_b
Ephedrine

## explanation_b
Correct. Ephedrine lacks the catechol (dihydroxybenzene) ring structure that defines a true catecholamine, making it a noncatecholamine sympathomimetic. This structural difference is part of why ephedrine, unlike the catecholamines, survives oral administration.

## answer_c
Dopamine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Dopamine is a catecholamine (the direct biosynthetic precursor of noradrenaline), not the noncatecholamine sympathomimetic described here.

## answer_d
Isoprenaline

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Isoprenaline is a synthetic catecholamine derivative, carrying the catechol ring, not a noncatecholamine sympathomimetic.

## title
Sympathetic postganglionic fibres are adrenergic

## question
Which one of the following sites is characterized by adrenergic transmission?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Synaptic fibers in the eccrine gland

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sympathetic fibres to eccrine (thermoregulatory sweat) glands are an anatomical exception -- although sympathetic, they release acetylcholine (cholinergic), not noradrenaline.

## answer_b
Parasympathetic preganglionic fibers

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Parasympathetic preganglionic fibres are cholinergic, acting on nicotinic receptors at the parasympathetic ganglion, not adrenergic.

## answer_c
Sympathetic fibers in the adrenal medulla

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The sympathetic fibres innervating the adrenal medulla are themselves preganglionic and cholinergic, even though the medulla they stimulate secretes catecholamines into the blood as a hormone rather than releasing them as a synaptic neurotransmitter.

## answer_d
Sympathetic postganglionic fibers

## explanation_d
Correct. Adrenergic transmission -- release of noradrenaline as the neurotransmitter -- is the property of sympathetic postganglionic fibres. This is the site classically described as adrenergic, distinguishing it from the cholinergic exceptions among the other options.
