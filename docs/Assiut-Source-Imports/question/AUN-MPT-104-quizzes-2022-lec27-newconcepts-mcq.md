## title
Organization means replacement by fibrous tissue

## question
Organization means replacement of the damaged tissue by:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Calcified tissue

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Calcified tissue is not a standard tissue-repair outcome -- dystrophic calcification can occur in dead tissue in some settings, but it does not define organization.

## answer_b
New tissue of the same type

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Replacement by new tissue of the same type describes regeneration, not organization -- these are the two distinct outcomes this lecture pairs against each other.

## answer_c
Neural tissue

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Neural tissue is not a repair outcome at all; damaged neural tissue itself typically cannot regenerate (nerve cells are permanent cells) and instead undergoes organization like other permanent-cell tissue.

## answer_d
Fibrous tissue

## explanation_d
Correct. Organization means replacement of the damaged tissue by fibrous tissue. This scar fills the defect without restoring the original tissue type or function. It is the outcome that occurs when a tissue's own cells cannot regenerate it -- for example, permanent-cell tissue like nerve, cardiac or skeletal muscle.

## title
Post-appendectomy nodule with giant cells is granuloma formation

## question
A 25-year-old has an appendectomy, one month later she palpates a small nodule beneath the skin at the site of the healed sutured incision. The nodule is excised, and microscopic examination shows macrophages, collagen deposition, small lymphocytes, and multinucleated giant cells. A foreign refractile material is seen in the nodule. Which of the following complications of the surgery best accounts for these findings?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Exuberant granulation tissue

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Exuberant granulation tissue is excess vascular and fibroblastic proliferation, without the giant cells and foreign material seen here.

## answer_b
Granuloma formation

## explanation_b
Correct. Macrophages, collagen, lymphocytes and multinucleated giant cells surrounding a foreign refractile material describe a foreign-body granuloma. The retained suture material (the refractile particle) is the indigestible antigen provoking this macrophage-driven granulomatous reaction.

## answer_c
Hyaline degeneration

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hyaline degeneration is extracellular protein deposition producing a glassy, homogeneous appearance, not a cellular reaction with giant cells and foreign material.

## answer_d
Abscess formation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. An abscess is a neutrophilic, pus-forming collection, not the macrophage-and-giant-cell picture with visible foreign material described here.

## title
A month-old myocardial infarction shows fibrous scar

## question
A 58-year-old man had chest pain persisting for 4 hours. A radiographic imaging procedure showed area of necrosis involving a 4-cm area of the posterior left ventricular free wall. Which of the following pathologic findings would most likely be seen in the left ventricular lesion 1 month later?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Liquefactive necrosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Liquefactive necrosis does not describe myocardial infarction healing -- it is characteristic of tissues like the brain, not the heart.

## answer_b
Fibrous scar

## explanation_b
Correct. By one month after a myocardial infarction, the acute necrotic phase has already been replaced by mature fibrous scar tissue. Cardiac myocytes are permanent cells, so organization (fibrous scarring) is the only possible repair outcome for the necrotic myocardium. This is why a healed infarct is read as scar, not regenerated muscle.

## answer_c
Coagulative necrosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Coagulative necrosis is the acute-phase finding, present in the first days after infarction, not still present a month later once organization has completed.

## answer_d
Regeneration

## explanation_d
Incorrect. True regeneration -- replacement by new cardiac muscle of the same type -- is not possible here, because cardiac myocytes are permanent cells that cannot regenerate.

## subject
fnd

## status

Incorrect. Stable cells multiply when stimulated -- they retain regenerative capacity, just not the continuous, unstimulated kind labile cells have.

Regeneration means replacement by the same tissue type

## question
Regeneration means:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Replacement of the damaged tissue by new one of the same type

## explanation_a
Correct. Regeneration means replacement of the damaged tissue by new tissue of the same type. It restores normal structure and function, unlike organization's fibrous scar. This outcome is possible only for labile and stable-cell tissue, not for permanent-cell tissue.

## answer_b
Replacement of the damaged tissue by new one of the different type

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Replacement by a different tissue type is not regeneration -- true regeneration specifically restores the original tissue type.

## answer_c
Replacement of the damaged tissue by fibrosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Replacement by fibrosis describes organization, the alternative outcome to regeneration, not regeneration itself.

## answer_d
Replacement of the damaged tissue by granulation tissue

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Granulation tissue is the vascular, fibroblast-rich tissue that forms during organization/healing, an intermediate step toward a fibrous scar, not the definition of regeneration.
