## title
5-Fluorouracil's cellular target in colorectal cancer chemotherapy

## question
A 42-year-old man with colorectal cancer undergoes therapy with 5-Fluorouracil. This medication works through inhibition of which of the following?

## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
DNA synthase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. "DNA synthase" is not a real single enzyme name for the DNA-replication machinery (DNA polymerase is), and 5-Fluorouracil does not act on DNA polymerase directly — its cytotoxic action is upstream, at nucleotide synthesis.

## answer_b
RNA polymerase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. RNA polymerase transcribes RNA from a DNA template; it is not 5-Fluorouracil's target, although 5-FU can be mis-incorporated into RNA as a secondary effect — the drug's defining, dose-limiting action is on the enzyme below, not on transcription itself.

## answer_c
RNA transferase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "RNA transferase" is not the mechanism tested here and is not how 5-Fluorouracil is taught to act; this option names no real step in 5-FU's pathway.

## answer_d
Thymidylate synthase

## explanation_d
Correct. 5-Fluorouracil is converted intracellularly to 5-FdUMP (5-fluorodeoxyuridine monophosphate), which forms a stable complex with thymidylate synthase and its folate cofactor, inhibiting the enzyme. Thymidylate synthase methylates dUMP to dTMP, the only de novo source of thymidine for DNA synthesis; blocking it starves rapidly dividing cells (like a colorectal tumour) of thymidine, halting DNA replication — "thymineless death."
