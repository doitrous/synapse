## title
Vitamin B12 absorption requirement

## question
Absorption of vitamin B12 requires the presence of:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Intrinsic factor

## explanation_a
Correct. Intrinsic factor, secreted by the gastric parietal cells, binds vitamin B12 (cobalamin) in the gut lumen and carries it to the terminal ileum, where the intrinsic-factor-B12 complex is recognised by a specific receptor and taken up by endocytosis. Without intrinsic factor, dietary B12 cannot be absorbed in adequate amounts regardless of how much is ingested, which is why autoimmune destruction of parietal cells (as in pernicious anaemia) causes B12 deficiency even on a normal diet. This absorption pathway is unique to B12 among the vitamins and is the reason B12 deficiency, unlike most other vitamin deficiencies, can arise from a gastric rather than a dietary problem.

## answer_b
Pepsin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pepsin is a gastric protease that digests dietary protein; it plays no specific role in carrying B12 to its ileal absorption site.

## answer_c
Thiamin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Thiamin is vitamin B1, an unrelated water-soluble vitamin with its own separate absorption pathway; it is not required for B12 absorption.

## answer_d
Both (B) and (C)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Since neither pepsin nor thiamin is the factor B12 absorption depends on, combining them is also wrong.

## title
Hypertonic solution for red blood cells

## question
Which of the following solutions is hypertonic for red blood cells?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
1.0% NaCl

## explanation_a
Correct. Normal saline, 0.9% NaCl, is isotonic to red blood cells and is the reference point against which every other concentration is judged. Since 1.0% NaCl carries a higher non-penetrating solute concentration than this 0.9% isotonic reference, it is hypertonic to the red blood cell: water leaves the cell by osmosis down its own concentration gradient, and the cell shrinks (crenates). Both 0.10% and 0.5% NaCl, by contrast, are more dilute than the 0.9% reference and so are hypotonic, not hypertonic, drawing water into the cell instead.

## answer_b
0.9% NaCl

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 0.9% NaCl is the isotonic reference itself, producing no net water movement or volume change, not the hypertonic solution being asked for.

## answer_c
0.10% NaCl

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 0.10% NaCl is far more dilute than the isotonic 0.9% reference, making it markedly hypotonic, not hypertonic; it would swell, not shrink, the cell.

## answer_d
0.5% NaCl

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 0.5% NaCl is still more dilute than the isotonic 0.9% reference, making it hypotonic, not hypertonic.

## title
Which is not an oxygen-derived free radical

## question
Which of the following is not an oxygen derived free radical (ROS)?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Superoxide anion

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Superoxide anion (O2 with an unpaired electron) is one of the primary oxygen-derived free radicals (ROS), generated for example as a byproduct of the mitochondrial respiratory chain.

## answer_b
Hydroxyl radical

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The hydroxyl radical is an oxygen-derived free radical, and the most reactive one of the group, typically generated from hydrogen peroxide via the Fenton reaction.

## answer_c
Hydrogen peroxide

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hydrogen peroxide is grouped with the oxygen-derived free radicals (ROS) because, although not itself a radical in the strict sense, it readily generates the hydroxyl radical.

## answer_d
Nitric oxide

## explanation_d
Correct. Nitric oxide is a genuine free radical, since it carries an unpaired electron, but it is nitrogen-centred rather than oxygen-derived, and is classed separately as a reactive nitrogen species (RNS) rather than one of the oxygen-derived ROS (superoxide, hydroxyl radical, hydrogen peroxide). This distinction between the oxygen-derived ROS family and the separately classed nitrogen-centred radicals such as nitric oxide is exactly what this question is testing. Nitric oxide has its own distinct physiological roles, including vasodilation and neurotransmission, that are unrelated to the oxidative-injury role of the ROS family.

## title
Substrate class that cannot form free radicals

## question
Which of the following can't form free radicals?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Xenobiotics

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Xenobiotics (foreign chemicals, including many drugs and toxins) can generate free radicals during their metabolism, for example through cytochrome P450-mediated reactions.

## answer_b
Oxygen

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Molecular oxygen is the direct source of the oxygen-derived free radicals (superoxide, hydroxyl radical, hydrogen peroxide).

## answer_c
Lipids

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Lipids can propagate free-radical chain reactions through lipid peroxidation, particularly the polyunsaturated fatty acids.

## answer_d
Carbohydrates

## explanation_d
Correct. Carbohydrates, unlike oxygen, lipids and xenobiotics, are not a recognised source of free radical formation in this bank's teaching. Their oxidation and metabolism proceed through pathways (such as glycolysis and the citric acid cycle) that do not themselves generate radical intermediates in the way lipid peroxidation or xenobiotic metabolism do. This makes carbohydrates the correct exception among the four listed biomolecule classes.

## title
Which is not an effect of free radicals

## question
Which of the following is not an effect of free radicals?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Damage of cell membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Free radicals damage cell membranes through lipid peroxidation of membrane phospholipids, which is one of their recognised effects.

## answer_b
Mutations & cancer

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Free radicals damage DNA and cause mutations, predisposing to cancer, which is also a recognised effect.

## answer_c
Loss of biological activity of some enzymes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Free radicals oxidise functional groups on enzymes and other proteins, causing loss of their biological activity, which is likewise a recognised effect.

## answer_d
Protein glycosylation

## explanation_d
Correct. Protein glycosylation is the non-enzymatic attachment of a sugar molecule to a protein, as seen in glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c); it proceeds by a separate, non-oxidative chemical mechanism and is not itself caused by free radicals. This makes it the correct exception among the four listed options, each of which describes damage attributed to free-radical activity except this one. Recognising that glycosylation and oxidative (free-radical) damage are mechanistically distinct processes is the specific distinction this question is testing.

## title
Lipid most vulnerable to free-radical damage

## question
Which of the following lipid is most affected by free radicals?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Polyunsaturated fatty acids

## explanation_a
Correct. Polyunsaturated fatty acids carry multiple carbon-carbon double bonds, and the carbon atoms between those double bonds (the allylic positions) hold hydrogen atoms that are especially easy for a free radical to abstract, initiating a self-propagating chain reaction called lipid peroxidation. This makes polyunsaturated fatty acids the lipid class most vulnerable to free-radical damage, since more double bonds mean more of these easily abstracted hydrogens along the chain. This vulnerability is why polyunsaturated membrane phospholipids are a principal target of oxidative injury in cells.

## answer_b
Saturated fatty acids

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Saturated fatty acids have no carbon-carbon double bonds and lack the easily abstracted allylic hydrogens, making them far more resistant to free-radical (peroxidative) damage.

## answer_c
Monounsaturated fatty acids

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Monounsaturated fatty acids have only one double bond, giving them intermediate but still much lower vulnerability than the multiple double bonds of polyunsaturated fatty acids.

## answer_d
Long chain fatty acids

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chain length alone (long chain fatty acids) is not what determines vulnerability to free-radical attack; the degree of unsaturation (number of double bonds) is the determining factor, and that is captured by option A, not this one.
