## title
Brugia malayi's habitat is the lymphatics

## question
Lymphatics are the habitat of ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Onchocerca volvulus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Onchocerca volvulus adults live in subcutaneous fibrous nodules, not in the lymphatics.

## answer_b
Brugia malayi

## explanation_b
Correct. Brugia malayi, like its close relative Wuchereria bancrofti, is a lymphatic-dwelling filarial nematode: its adult worms live coiled within the lymphatics and lymph nodes, where the resulting lymphatic inflammation and obstruction produce lymphoedema and, in chronic cases, elephantiasis of the limbs. This lymphatic habitat is the defining feature that groups Brugia malayi with Wuchereria bancrofti as the two classic causes of lymphatic filariasis. Its microfilariae, released from these lymphatic adults, then circulate periodically in the peripheral blood, from where they are taken up by a mosquito vector.

## answer_c
Strongyloid stercoralis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Strongyloides stercoralis adults live in the mucosa of the small intestine, not in the lymphatics.

## answer_d
Loa loa

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Loa loa adults migrate through subcutaneous and connective tissues (giving rise to Calabar swellings and the classic subconjunctival passage of the worm), not through the lymphatics.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Brugia malayi is transmitted by Mansonia and Anopheles mosquitoes, not by Simulium.

Blinding worm

## explanation_a
Correct. Onchocerca volvulus is known as the 'blinding worm' because chronic onchocerciasis is one of the leading infectious causes of blindness worldwide. Microfilariae released by adult worms in subcutaneous nodules migrate through the skin and into the eye, where the resulting inflammatory reaction to dying microfilariae progressively damages the cornea, iris and retina, eventually causing irreversible blindness (river blindness). This ocular damage, rather than the adult worm itself being visible, is what earns Onchocerca its name.

## answer_d
Guinea worm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 'Guinea worm' is the common name for Dracunculus medinensis, not for Onchocerca volvulus.

## title
Creeping eruption is caused by non-human hookworm larvae

## question
Creeping eruption is caused by skin penetration of ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Filariform larvae of Hookworms

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The filariform larvae of the human hookworms, Ancylostoma duodenale and Necator americanus, penetrate the skin but then migrate through the bloodstream to the lungs and small intestine rather than remaining trapped in the skin, so they do not typically cause creeping eruption.

## answer_b
Non human schistosomes cercaria

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Non-human schistosome cercariae cause a different condition, cercarial dermatitis (swimmer's itch), a brief itchy rash at the site of penetration, not the migrating, snake-like track of creeping eruption.

## answer_c
Non human hookworms larvae

## explanation_c
Correct. Creeping eruption (cutaneous larva migrans) results from skin penetration by the filariform larvae of non-human (animal) hookworms, classically Ancylostoma caninum and Ancylostoma braziliense, which normally infect dogs and cats. Because these larvae are adapted to a non-human host, they cannot complete their normal migratory cycle in man and instead wander aimlessly within the epidermis, producing an intensely itchy, serpiginous (snake-like) track that advances slowly over days to weeks. This is distinct from human hookworm species, whose filariform larvae, once they penetrate human skin, do complete the normal migratory route to the lungs and small intestine rather than remaining confined to the skin.

## answer_d
Strongyloid stercoralis larvae

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Strongyloides stercoralis larvae can cause a related but distinct migratory skin condition called larva currens, which moves much faster (centimetres per hour) than the slower creeping eruption caused by non-human hookworm larvae.

Wearing shoes is protective against all three soil-transmitted skin-penetrating nematodes but does not protect against Schistosoma, which requires avoiding contact with infested water instead.

## explanation_a
Correct. Lymphatic filariasis (elephantiasis) due to Wuchereria bancrofti is diagnosed by finding microfilariae circulating in the peripheral blood. Because Wuchereria bancrofti microfilariae characteristically show nocturnal periodicity, appearing in greatest numbers in the peripheral blood at night, a blood sample for microscopy is best taken between roughly 10 p.m. and 2 a.m. to maximise the chance of detection.
