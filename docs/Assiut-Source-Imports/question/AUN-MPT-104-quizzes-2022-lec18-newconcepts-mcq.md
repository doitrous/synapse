## title
Correct statement on sympathetic/parasympathetic transmitters

## question
Which of the following statements is correct regarding the sympathetic and parasympathetic systems?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Acetylcholine activates muscarinic receptors.

## explanation_a
Correct. Acetylcholine is the cholinergic system's transmitter, and muscarinic receptors are one of the two receptor families it activates (nicotinic being the other). This receptor-transmitter pairing holds throughout the parasympathetic postganglionic synapse and the few cholinergic sympathetic exceptions.

## answer_b
Activation of the sympathetic system causes a drop in blood pressure.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sympathetic activation raises, not lowers, blood pressure -- alpha-1-mediated vasoconstriction and beta-1-mediated cardiac stimulation together increase peripheral resistance and cardiac output.

## answer_c
Norepinephrine activates muscarinic receptors.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Norepinephrine is the adrenergic system's transmitter and activates adrenergic (alpha/beta) receptors, not muscarinic receptors -- the two transmitter-receptor systems do not cross over.

## answer_d
Acetylcholine activates adrenergic receptors.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Acetylcholine activates muscarinic and nicotinic receptors, not adrenergic receptors -- adrenergic receptors are activated by norepinephrine and epinephrine instead.

## title
Parasympathetic system is craniosacral

## question
Which of the following statements concerning the parasympathetic nervous system is Correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The parasympathetic nervous system uses epinephrine as a neurotransmitter.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The parasympathetic system uses acetylcholine, not epinephrine, as its transmitter at every synapse (ganglionic and postganglionic alike).

## answer_b
Is considered cranio-sacral.

## explanation_b
Correct. The parasympathetic division is described as craniosacral because its outflow leaves the CNS from cranial nerve nuclei (III, VII, IX, X) and the sacral spinal cord (S2-S4). This contrasts directly with the sympathetic system's thoracolumbar outflow (T1 to L2/L3). The two labels name opposite anatomical levels for each division, which is exactly what this quiz item tests.

## answer_c
The postganglionic fibers of the parasympathetic division are long, compared to those of the sympathetic system.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. It is the parasympathetic preganglionic fibres that are relatively long (travelling near to the target organ before synapsing) and the postganglionic fibres that are short -- the reverse of the sympathetic pattern, not what this option states.

## answer_d
The preganglionic fibers of the parasympathetic division are short, compared to those of the sympathetic system.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Parasympathetic preganglionic fibres are relatively long, not short, since the ganglion lies near or within the target organ rather than close to the spinal cord.

## title
Acetylcholine acts on muscarinic and nicotinic receptors

## question
The correct statement about acetylcholine:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Its released from nerve terminals is stimulated by botulinum toxin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Botulinum toxin blocks, rather than stimulates, acetylcholine release from nerve terminals -- it cleaves the SNARE proteins vesicles need to fuse with the presynaptic membrane, producing flaccid paralysis, the opposite of stimulation.

## answer_b
Is synthesized from an amino acid precursor

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Acetylcholine is synthesised from choline and acetyl-CoA by choline acetyltransferase, not from an amino acid precursor.

## answer_c
It acts on both muscarinic and nicotinic receptors

## explanation_c
Correct. Acetylcholine acts on both muscarinic receptors (G-protein coupled, at postganglionic parasympathetic effector junctions) and nicotinic receptors (ligand-gated ion channels, at every autonomic ganglion and the skeletal neuromuscular junction). No other autonomic transmitter has this dual receptor-family activation. This is exactly what sets acetylcholine apart from noradrenaline, which activates only adrenergic receptors.

## answer_d
Has a half-life of 8 to10 hours in plasma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Acetylcholine's half-life in the synapse is a matter of milliseconds, not hours -- it is hydrolysed almost instantly by acetylcholinesterase, which is exactly why acetylcholine itself is unsuitable as a clinical drug.

## title
Neurotransmitters released from presynaptic terminals

## question
Which of the following is correct regarding neurotransmitters and neurotransmission?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Neurotransmitter release is triggered by the arrival of action potentials in the postsynaptic cell.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Release is triggered by an action potential arriving at the presynaptic terminal, not the postsynaptic cell -- the postsynaptic cell is where the released transmitter then acts, not where release originates.

## answer_b
Neurotransmitters are released from the presynaptic nerve terminals.

## explanation_b
Correct. Neurotransmitters, including acetylcholine, are synthesised and stored in the presynaptic nerve terminal. They are released from that presynaptic terminal into the synaptic cleft when an arriving action potential triggers calcium influx. The postsynaptic cell is where the transmitter then acts, not where it is released from.

## answer_c
Serotonin and dopamine are the primary neurotransmitters in the ANS.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Acetylcholine and noradrenaline, not serotonin and dopamine, are the primary neurotransmitters of the autonomic nervous system.

## answer_d
Intracellular calcium levels drop in the neuron before the neurotransmitter is released.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Intracellular calcium rises, not falls, in the presynaptic terminal before release -- calcium influx through voltage-gated channels is the proximate trigger for vesicle fusion and transmitter release.

## title
M3 receptor blockade decreases DAG in salivary gland

## question
Which of the following is an expected effect of a therapeutic dose of a drug that blocks muscarinic-3 receptors?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Increased IP3 in intestinal smooth muscle

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A muscarinic-3 blocker would decrease, not increase, IP3 in intestinal smooth muscle -- IP3 generation there depends on M3 receptor stimulation, which the antagonist prevents.

## answer_b
Increased sodium influx into the skeletal muscle end plate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sodium influx into the skeletal muscle end plate is a nicotinic (not muscarinic) neuromuscular junction event; an M3 blocker at glandular/smooth-muscle muscarinic receptors would not affect it.

## answer_c
Decreased cAMP in cardiac muscle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cardiac cAMP is governed by the Gi-coupled M2 receptor, not M3 -- a selective M3 blocker would not be expected to change cardiac cAMP.

## answer_d
Decreased DAG in salivary gland tissue

## explanation_d
Correct. M3 muscarinic receptors are Gq-coupled, signalling through phospholipase C to generate IP3 and DAG. Blocking M3 receptors in salivary gland tissue therefore decreases this Gq/IP3-DAG signalling. Because DAG normally drives the secretory response, blocking the receptor that generates it reduces salivary secretion, which is exactly the expected effect of a therapeutic antimuscarinic dose.

## title
Calcium influx triggers ACh release

## question
Influx of what ion causes ACh release into the synaptic cleft, prior to ACh being terminated by acetylcholinesterase (AChE)?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cl-

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Chloride influx hyperpolarises a cell and is not the trigger for vesicular acetylcholine release at the presynaptic terminal.

## answer_b
Na+

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sodium influx underlies the action potential's depolarisation phase as it travels along the axon, but it is calcium influx at the terminal itself that directly triggers vesicle fusion and transmitter release.

## answer_c
K+

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Potassium efflux, not influx, repolarises the membrane after an action potential -- it is not the trigger for transmitter release.

## answer_d
Ca2+

## explanation_d
Correct. The arrival of an action potential at the presynaptic terminal opens voltage-gated calcium channels. The resulting Ca2+ influx is the proximate trigger causing acetylcholine-containing vesicles to fuse with the presynaptic membrane. This releases their contents into the synaptic cleft, where acetylcholinesterase then terminates acetylcholine's action.

## title
Parasympathetic role: accommodation, GI motility, urination

## question
Which of the following statements concerning the parasympathetic nervous system is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The parasympathetic system often discharges as a single, functional system.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Unlike the sympathetic system, which tends to discharge diffusely as a single unit ('fight-or-flight'), the parasympathetic system typically activates discretely, organ by organ, rather than as one unified discharge.

## answer_b
The postganglionic fibers of the parasympathetic division are long compared to those of the sympathetic nervous system.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Parasympathetic postganglionic fibres are short (the ganglion lies near or within the target organ), while it is the preganglionic fibres that are relatively long -- the reverse of what this option states.

## answer_c
The parasympathetic system uses norepinephrine as a neurotransmitter.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The parasympathetic system uses acetylcholine, not norepinephrine, as its transmitter throughout.

## answer_d
The parasympathetic division is involved in accommodation of near vision, movement of food, and urination.

## explanation_d
Correct. The parasympathetic ('rest-and-digest') division governs near-vision accommodation via ciliary muscle contraction. It also governs GI motility and secretion, moving food through the gut. It equally governs bladder emptying (urination) via detrusor contraction, the opposite of the sympathetic system's storage-favouring role.

M3 activation in bronchiolar smooth muscle increases IP3/DAG

## question
The activation of muscarinic receptors in bronchiolar smooth muscle is associated with

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
decrease in cAMP formation mediated by G-proteins

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A decrease in cAMP is the M2 receptor's Gi-coupled signalling pattern in the heart, not the M3 receptor's Gq-coupled pattern in bronchiolar smooth muscle.

## answer_b
inhibition of protein kinase C

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Muscarinic activation of bronchiolar smooth muscle activates, rather than inhibits, protein kinase C downstream of the DAG generated by Gq/phospholipase C signalling.

## answer_c
opening of Na+/K+ cation channels

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Opening of Na+/K+ cation channels describes fast nicotinic (ligand-gated ion channel) signalling, not the Gq-coupled M3 muscarinic pathway in smooth muscle.

## answer_d
activation of adenylyl cyclase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Activation of adenylyl cyclase would raise, not the pathway involved here -- that is the Gs-coupled pattern of a different receptor family, not the Gq-coupled M3 muscarinic receptor.

## answer_e
increase in IP3 and DAG

## explanation_e
Correct. M3 muscarinic receptors on bronchiolar smooth muscle are Gq-coupled. Their activation stimulates phospholipase C, generating IP3 and DAG. These second messengers raise intracellular calcium and drive smooth-muscle contraction, producing bronchoconstriction rather than bronchodilation.
