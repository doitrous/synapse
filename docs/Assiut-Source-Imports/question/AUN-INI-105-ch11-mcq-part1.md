## title
Heterophyes heterophyes is not cosmopolitan

## question
The geographical distribution of the following parasites is cosmopolitan, EXCEPT?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Heterophyes heterophyes

## explanation_a
Correct. Heterophyes heterophyes, a small intestinal fluke acquired by eating undercooked brackish-water fish, has a geographically restricted distribution -- the Far East, the Middle East (including Egypt) and parts of the Balkans -- wherever its snail and fish intermediate hosts and local raw-fish-eating habits coincide. This is unlike Ascaris lumbricoides, Hymenolepis nana and Fasciola hepatica, which are found worldwide because their transmission does not depend on a specific brackish-water snail-fish complex. Its restricted distribution is the standard reason it is picked as the exception in a cosmopolitan-distribution list.

## answer_b
Fasciola hepatica

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fasciola hepatica, transmitted through lymnaeid snails and aquatic vegetation available in many temperate and tropical regions, has a cosmopolitan distribution, not a restricted one.

## answer_c
Hymenolepis nana

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hymenolepis nana, transmitted directly faecal-orally with no obligatory intermediate host, is one of the most widespread cestodes worldwide, so it is cosmopolitan.

## answer_d
Ascaris lumbricoides

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides, transmitted by simple faecal-oral ingestion of soil-matured eggs, is among the most cosmopolitan of all human helminths.

## title
Hydatid disease is not a common childhood parasite

## question
The following parasites affect commonly children, EXCEPT?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Hydatid disease

## explanation_a
Correct. Hydatid disease (Echinococcus granulosus cystic hydatidosis) is acquired by ingesting eggs shed by an infected dog, and because the resulting cyst grows slowly over years before becoming clinically apparent, it is not characteristically a disease of childhood. This is unlike Schistosoma mansoni, Enterobius vermicularis and Hymenolepis nana, which commonly and characteristically affect children through play-related soil/water contact, close-contact autoinfection and poor hand hygiene respectively. The distinction here is epidemiological -- who is typically affected -- not transmission route.

## answer_b
Schistosoma mansoni

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Schistosoma mansoni commonly affects children in endemic areas, who are heavily exposed through swimming and wading in infested fresh water.

## answer_c
Enterobius vermicularis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis is one of the most common childhood parasitic infections, spread easily among children in close contact (school, household) through autoinfection and airborne/fingernail eggs.

## answer_d
Hymenolepis nana

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hymenolepis nana is the commonest cestode infection of children, favoured by its direct life cycle and poor childhood hand hygiene.
