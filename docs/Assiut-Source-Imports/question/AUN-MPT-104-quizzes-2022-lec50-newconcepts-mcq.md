## title
An iron-oxygen-complexing chemotherapy drug causing DNA strand breaks works in G2 phase

## question
A drug used in a chemotherapy regimen works by complexing with iron and oxygen to promote DNA strand breaks. While on this drug the patient must be monitored closely due to pulmonary side effects. In what phase of the cell cycle does this drug work?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette
A chemotherapy drug that complexes with iron and oxygen to promote DNA strand breaks, requiring monitoring for pulmonary toxicity (a description matching bleomycin).

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
G1 phase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. G1 phase is the pre-synthetic growth phase; this drug's iron-oxygen-complex DNA-strand-breaking mechanism is not maximal here.

## answer_b
M phase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. M phase is mitosis; this drug (matching bleomycin's iron-oxygen-free-radical mechanism and pulmonary-fibrosis toxicity) is not classified as acting in M phase.

## answer_c
S phase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. S phase is DNA synthesis; while DNA is a target, this specific iron-oxygen-complex drug is classified to act in G2, not S, phase.

## answer_d
G2 phase

## explanation_d
Correct. This description matches bleomycin: it complexes with iron and oxygen to generate free radicals that cause DNA strand breaks, and carries dose-limiting pulmonary toxicity/fibrosis requiring close monitoring. Bleomycin is a cell-cycle-specific antitumour antibiotic classified as acting in the G2 phase of the cell cycle, where it blocks cells from progressing into mitosis.

## title
Paclitaxel targets microtubules

## question
The cellular target of paclitaxel is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Deoxyribonucleotide

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Deoxyribonucleotides are the substrate targeted by antimetabolite drugs (e.g. methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil), not paclitaxel's mechanism.

## answer_b
Microtubules

## explanation_b
Correct. Paclitaxel is a taxane that binds to and stabilises microtubules, preventing their normal depolymerisation. This locks the mitotic spindle in place and arrests dividing cells in mitosis, making microtubules its cellular target.

## answer_c
Topoisomerase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Topoisomerase is the target of drugs such as etoposide (topoisomerase II) and irinotecan (topoisomerase I), not paclitaxel.

## answer_d
Tyrosine kinase enzyme

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tyrosine kinase enzymes are the target of targeted small-molecule inhibitors (e.g. imatinib), not the cytotoxic mechanism of paclitaxel.

## title
Cisplatin's major toxicity is nephrotoxicity rather than bone marrow suppression

## question
Which of the following medications has the major side effect of nephrotoxicity rather than bone marrow suppression?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Methotrexate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Methotrexate's dose-limiting toxicity is bone marrow suppression (and mucositis), not nephrotoxicity as its major effect.

## answer_b
Doxorubicin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Doxorubicin's dose-limiting toxicities are bone marrow suppression and cardiotoxicity, not primarily nephrotoxicity.

## answer_c
Vinblastine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Vinblastine's dose-limiting toxicity is bone marrow suppression, not nephrotoxicity.

## answer_d
Cisplatin

## explanation_d
Correct. Cisplatin is distinctive among common cytotoxic chemotherapy drugs for causing significant nephrotoxicity (renal tubular damage) as its major dose-limiting side effect. This contrasts with most other cytotoxic agents, such as methotrexate, doxorubicin and vinblastine, whose dose-limiting toxicity is instead bone marrow suppression. Adequate hydration is used clinically to reduce cisplatin's renal toxicity.

Methotrexate is a folic acid analog

## question
Which of the following anticancer drugs is a folic acid analog?

## subject
fnd

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Fluorouracil

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fluorouracil is a pyrimidine (uracil) analogue, not a folic acid analogue.

## answer_b
Methotrexate

## explanation_b
Correct. Methotrexate is a folic acid (folate) analogue. It competitively inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, blocking the regeneration of tetrahydrofolate needed for purine and thymidylate synthesis, and so halting DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells.

## answer_c
Irinotecan

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Irinotecan is a topoisomerase I inhibitor, structurally and mechanistically unrelated to folic acid.

## answer_d
Cyclophosphamide

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cyclophosphamide is an alkylating agent, not a folic acid analogue.

## title
Cyclophosphamide is a cell-cycle non-specific anticancer agent

## question
Which of the following anticancer drugs is a cell cycle non specific agent?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Vinblastine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Vinblastine is cell-cycle specific, acting in M phase by binding tubulin and blocking mitotic spindle formation.

## answer_b
Methotrexate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Methotrexate is cell-cycle specific, acting in S phase by blocking the folate pathway needed for DNA synthesis.

## answer_c
Vincristine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Vincristine, like vinblastine, is cell-cycle specific, acting in M phase.

## answer_d
Cyclophosphamide

## explanation_d
Correct. Cyclophosphamide is an alkylating agent. Alkylating agents are cell-cycle non-specific: they can damage DNA, by cross-linking DNA strands, in cells at any phase of the cell cycle, including non-dividing cells at rest. This is unlike the phase-restricted antimetabolites and mitotic-spindle poisons among the other options, which only act on actively cycling cells.
