## title
5HT2 blockers counteract carcinoid syndrome

## question
Which one of the following statements regarding drug effects on serotonin receptor systems is accurate?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Buspirone is a partial agonist at 5HT2a receptors used for treatment of psychosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Buspirone is a partial agonist at 5HT1A receptors, used for anxiety, not psychosis, and not at 5HT2a receptors.

## answer_b
5HT2 receptor blockers counteract bronchoconstriction and diarrhea of carcinoid.

## explanation_b
Correct. Carcinoid tumours secrete excess serotonin, producing bronchoconstriction and diarrhea through serotonin's action at 5HT2 receptors. A 5HT2 receptor blocker counteracts these specific symptoms by antagonising serotonin at that receptor. This is the accurate statement among the four, since each of the other three misattributes a drug's receptor action or indication.

## answer_c
Sumatriptan is an antiemetic because it blocks 5HT3 receptors.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sumatriptan is a 5HT1 receptor agonist used for migraine, not an antiemetic acting by blocking 5HT3 receptors.

## answer_d
Ondansetron is used in migraine because it activates 5HT3 receptors.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ondansetron is a 5HT3 receptor antagonist (blocker, not activator) used as an antiemetic, not for migraine.

## title
Cimetidine raises phenytoin's half-life

## question
The widely used anticonvulsant phenytoin is often implicated in drug interactions. If phenytoin is used by a patient taking cimetidine for a GI ulcer, which one of the following is likely to occur?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
increase clearance of phenytoin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cimetidine inhibits, rather than increases, the CYP450-mediated clearance of phenytoin.

## answer_b
increase half-life of phenytoin

## explanation_b
Correct. Cimetidine is a CYP450 enzyme inhibitor. Co-administered with phenytoin, an anticonvulsant metabolised by CYP450, it inhibits that metabolism, so phenytoin is cleared more slowly and its half-life increases. This raises the risk of phenytoin toxicity if the phenytoin dose is not adjusted accordingly.

## answer_c
increase half-life of cimetidine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The interaction described affects phenytoin's metabolism (via cimetidine's inhibitory action), not cimetidine's own half-life.

## answer_d
decrease half-life of phenytoin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This reverses the actual effect -- phenytoin's half-life increases, not decreases, with cimetidine co-administration.

## title
Ondansetron for cisplatin-induced nausea

## question
A patient undergoing chemotherapy with cisplatin has severe nausea. Which of the following would be the drug to use in this patient?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cimetidine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cimetidine, an H2 blocker used for acid-related disease, has no targeted role against the serotonin-mediated mechanism of chemotherapy-induced nausea.

## answer_b
Scopolamine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Scopolamine, an anticholinergic used mainly for motion sickness, is not the targeted agent for highly emetogenic chemotherapy.

## answer_c
Cyclizine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cyclizine, an antihistamine antiemetic, is not the targeted, standard agent for cisplatin-induced nausea.

## answer_d
Ondansetron

## explanation_d
Correct. Ondansetron is a selective 5HT3 receptor antagonist, the preferred drug for severe nausea from highly emetogenic chemotherapy such as cisplatin. Cisplatin triggers nausea largely through serotonin release acting on 5HT3 receptors in the gut and chemoreceptor trigger zone. Blocking this receptor with ondansetron is therefore the targeted, standard approach for this specific clinical scenario.

## title
Ranitidine blocks histamine at parietal cells

## question
Ranitidine inhibits which of the following:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Histamine binding to parietal cells

## explanation_a
Correct. Ranitidine is a histamine H2-receptor antagonist. It works by blocking histamine binding to H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells, reducing the histamine-driven stimulation of acid secretion. This mechanism -- blocking histamine at its receptor -- is what distinguishes ranitidine from a proton pump inhibitor.

## answer_b
Gastrin binding to parietal cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ranitidine does not block gastrin receptors; its mechanism is specific to the histamine H2 receptor.

## answer_c
Parietal cell prostaglandin receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ranitidine does not act on prostaglandin receptors; that pathway is targeted by a different class of gastroprotective agents (e.g. misoprostol).

## answer_d
H+, K+ ATPase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Direct inhibition of the H+/K+-ATPase pump is the mechanism of a proton pump inhibitor, not an H2 blocker like ranitidine, which acts one step upstream at the histamine receptor.

## title
Diphenhydramine impairs a driver's ability

## question
You see a 43-year-old man long-distance truck driver in the clinic who complains of serious allergic rhinitis. Which one of the following drugs could significantly impair his ability to drive?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Loratadine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Loratadine is a second-generation antihistamine, designed to minimise CNS penetration and sedation, unlike a first-generation agent.

## answer_b
Diphenhydramine.

## explanation_b
Correct. Diphenhydramine is a first-generation antihistamine that readily crosses the blood-brain barrier and produces significant CNS sedation. In a patient who needs to remain alert, such as a long-distance truck driver, this sedating property could significantly impair the ability to drive. This is why diphenhydramine, unlike a second-generation agent, is the option that carries this specific risk.

## answer_c
Sumatriptan

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sumatriptan is a migraine drug (a 5HT1 agonist), not an antihistamine, and is not the drug being tested for sedation-related driving impairment here.

## answer_d
Fexofenadine.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fexofenadine is a second-generation antihistamine, specifically designed to avoid the CNS sedation that would impair driving.
