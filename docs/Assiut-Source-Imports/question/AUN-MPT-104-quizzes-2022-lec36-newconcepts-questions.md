## title
Losartan's advantage over enalapril: less angioedema

## question
Which of the following is an advantage of losartan over enalapril?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Better efficacy in lower blood pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Losartan (an ARB) and enalapril (an ACE inhibitor) are comparably effective at lowering blood pressure; superior antihypertensive efficacy is not losartan's distinguishing advantage.

## answer_b
Better prevention of secondary myocardial events

## explanation_b
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors such as enalapril have well-established evidence for preventing secondary myocardial events; this is not an advantage that favours losartan over enalapril.

## answer_c
Less incidence of angioedema

## explanation_c
Correct. Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) such as losartan do not affect bradykinin metabolism, since they act downstream at the angiotensin II receptor rather than inhibiting the ACE enzyme that also degrades bradykinin. This gives losartan a lower incidence of angioedema than an ACE inhibitor such as enalapril, even though the two classes are otherwise comparable in efficacy for lowering blood pressure. Losartan has not been shown to be superior at preventing secondary myocardial events or to cost less.

## answer_d
Less cost

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Losartan is not generally the lower-cost option compared with enalapril; cost is not the basis of its advantage.

## title
Lisinopril enhances bradykinin's effects

## question
Enhancement of the effects of bradykinin is most with one of the following drugs

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Losartan

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Losartan is an ARB, acting downstream at the angiotensin II receptor; it does not inhibit ACE and so does not block bradykinin degradation.

## answer_b
Propranolol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Propranolol is a beta-blocker with no effect on ACE-mediated bradykinin degradation.

## answer_c
Lisinopril

## explanation_c
Correct. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) normally degrades bradykinin in addition to converting angiotensin I to angiotensin II. ACE inhibitors such as lisinopril block this degradation, so bradykinin accumulates and its effects are enhanced. Losartan, propranolol and clonidine do not inhibit ACE and so do not share this bradykinin-enhancing effect.

## answer_d
Clonidine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Clonidine is a central alpha2 agonist with no effect on ACE-mediated bradykinin degradation.

## title
Captopril for a woman with diabetic nephropathy

## question
Which one of the following is the most appropriate drug to use for the patient described in parentheses?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Propranolol (a patient with peripheral vascular disease)

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Propranolol, a nonselective beta-blocker, can worsen peripheral vascular disease by unopposed alpha-mediated vasoconstriction; it is not the appropriate choice for this patient.

## answer_b
Captopril (man with hyperkalemia)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Captopril, an ACE inhibitor, reduces aldosterone secretion and raises serum potassium; it is not appropriate for a patient who already has hyperkalemia.

## answer_c
Enalapril (pregnant women)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Any ACE inhibitor, including enalapril, is contraindicated in pregnancy owing to teratogenic effects on the fetal kidneys.

## answer_d
Captopril (woman with diabetic nephropathy)

## explanation_d
Correct. ACE inhibitors such as captopril reduce intraglomerular pressure by preferentially dilating the efferent glomerular arteriole, slowing the progression of diabetic nephropathy. This renoprotective effect makes captopril the appropriate choice for a woman with diabetic nephropathy among these four drug-patient pairings. The other three pairings are each inappropriate or contraindicated for the specific patient factor named.

## title
Aliskiren is a direct renin inhibitor

## question
A woman with high blood pressure is given a drug that is considered a direct renin inhibitor. Which one of these drugs?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Aliskiren

## explanation_a
Correct. Aliskiren directly inhibits renin, the enzyme that initiates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone cascade by cleaving angiotensinogen to angiotensin I. This acts one step upstream of an ACE inhibitor, which blocks angiotensin I to angiotensin II conversion, and an ARB, which blocks the angiotensin II receptor. This distinguishes aliskiren as the direct renin inhibitor among these four antihypertensive options.

## answer_b
Losartan

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Losartan is an ARB, blocking the angiotensin II receptor downstream of renin, not a direct renin inhibitor.

## answer_c
Valsartan

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Valsartan is an ARB, blocking the angiotensin II receptor downstream of renin, not a direct renin inhibitor.

## answer_d
Lisinopril

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lisinopril is an ACE inhibitor, blocking angiotensin I to angiotensin II conversion downstream of renin, not a direct renin inhibitor.
