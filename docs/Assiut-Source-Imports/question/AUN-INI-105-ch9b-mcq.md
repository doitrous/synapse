Which one of the following trematodes has separate sex?

## subject

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hetrophys hetrophys

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Heterophyes heterophyes, like most trematodes, is hermaphroditic, with both male and female reproductive organs in the same worm.

## answer_b
Schistosoma

## explanation_b
Correct. Schistosoma is the classic exception among trematodes: it has separate sexes (is dioecious), with the male and female worms pairing together in copula for their entire adult lives, the female residing in the male's gynaecophoric canal. This dioecious pattern distinguishes Schistosoma from essentially all other medically important trematodes.

Fasciola

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fasciola, like most trematodes, is hermaphroditic.

## answer_d
Paragonimus westermani

Which of the following trematodes inhabit the large bile ducts?

## question
Mode of transmission of Fasciola is by?

## subject
inf

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ingestion of undercooked crabs harboring metacercaria

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ingesting undercooked crabs or crayfish harbouring metacercariae is the transmission route for Paragonimus westermani (lung fluke), not Fasciola.

## answer_b
Ingestion of metacercaria found in plants

## explanation_b
Correct. Fasciola is transmitted by ingesting metacercariae encysted on aquatic vegetation, such as watercress or other water plants eaten raw. Ingestion of metacercaria found in undercooked fish

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ingesting metacercariae in undercooked fish is the transmission route for fish-borne flukes such as Heterophyes heterophyes and Clonorchis sinensis, not Fasciola.

## answer_d
Penetration of the skin by furcocercus cercaria

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Skin penetration by a furcocercous cercaria is the transmission route for Schistosoma species, not Fasciola, whose cercariae instead encyst on vegetation before ingestion.

Trematodes are leaf-shaped worms

## question
Trematodes are?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Leaf-shaped worms

## explanation_a
Correct. Trematodes (flukes) are characteristically leaf-shaped -- dorsoventrally flattened, oval to elongate bodies that give the group its common name, "flukes." This flattened, leaf-like body plan is a basic morphological feature used to recognise trematodes at a glance. It holds across the whole trematode group covered in this chapter, from the small Heterophyes heterophyes to the much larger Fasciola.

## answer_b
Rounded worms

## explanation_b
Incorrect. "Rounded worms" describes the nematodes (roundworms), a separate helminth group with a cylindrical, unsegmented body, not the trematodes.

## answer_c
Tape like worms

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "Tape-like worms" describes the cestodes (tapeworms), whose flattened but ribbon-like, segmented body differs from the trematodes' unsegmented, leaf-shaped one.

## answer_d
Segmented worms

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Trematodes are unsegmented; a segmented body plan (proglottids) is instead characteristic of the cestodes.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Small venules of the small intestinal wall are not where Schistosoma mansoni deposits its eggs.

## answer_b
Small venules in large intestinal wall

## explanation_b
Correct. Schistosoma mansoni deposits its lateral-spined eggs in the small venules of the large intestinal (colonic) wall. The eggs then work their way through the bowel wall to be excreted in stool, and eggs that instead embolise to the liver drive the periportal fibrosis of hepatosplenic schistosomiasis.

## answer_c
Small venules in bladder wall

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Small venules of the bladder wall are where Schistosoma haematobium deposits its eggs, not Schistosoma mansoni.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Small venules of the biliary tracts are not where Schistosoma mansoni deposits its eggs; the biliary tree is instead Fasciola's habitat.
