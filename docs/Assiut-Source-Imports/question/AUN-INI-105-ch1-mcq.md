## title
Function of a bacterial plasmid

## question
The functions of plasmid are?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
DNA replication

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A plasmid is a small, circular, extrachromosomal DNA molecule that can replicate itself, but "DNA replication" as a generic cellular process is not what the plasmid does for the cell -- it is not the bacterial chromosome's own replication machinery.

## answer_b
Protein synthesis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Protein synthesis is carried out by ribosomes reading messenger RNA; a plasmid is DNA, not a protein-synthesis apparatus, though it may carry genes that are later transcribed and translated.

## answer_c
Cell wall synthesis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cell wall synthesis is directed by chromosomally encoded genes and enzymes (e.g. those building peptidoglycan); it is not a function carried out by a plasmid.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. A plasmid is a small circular extrachromosomal DNA molecule that typically carries accessory genes -- most classically antibiotic-resistance or virulence genes -- rather than performing any of the three listed cellular processes itself. None of DNA replication (of the chromosome), protein synthesis, or cell wall synthesis describes what a plasmid actually is or does; it is best understood as an accessory genetic element the cell carries, not a functional apparatus for these core processes.

## title
Clone: a population from a single cell

## question
A population of cells derived from a single cell are called?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Monoclonal cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. "Monoclonal" is the adjective describing something derived from a single clone (e.g. a monoclonal antibody); it is not the standard noun used for the population itself.

## answer_b
Clones

## explanation_b
Correct. A clone is the population of genetically identical cells produced when a single parent cell divides repeatedly -- ordinary cell division (binary fission in bacteria) does not introduce new genetic variation, so every descendant remains identical to the original cell. "Clone" names this population directly, which is why it is the term tested here rather than the adjective "monoclonal." This vocabulary underlies later concepts such as monoclonal antibodies, which are themselves the product of one such clone.

## answer_c
Protoplasts

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A protoplast is a bacterial cell that has had its cell wall removed (e.g. by lysozyme), leaving only the membrane-bound cell -- an unrelated concept to a population derived from one ancestor cell.

## answer_d
Subculture

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A subculture is the transfer of cells from one growth medium to fresh medium to maintain or expand a culture; it describes a laboratory technique, not the genetic relationship among the cells produced from a single ancestor.

## question

## subject

## status

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Correct. Gram-negative bacteria have a thin peptidoglycan layer and an outer lipid membrane; the decolourising step (alcohol/acetone) disrupts this thin wall and washes out the crystal violet-iodine complex, so the cell instead takes up the safranin counterstain and appears pink/red under the microscope. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gram-negative bacteria do reliably produce a colour on Gram stain (pink/red); "none of these" is only correct if the true answer is absent from the list, which it is not.

## title
Eukaryotic vs prokaryotic cells: the false statement

## question
Differences between eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells include all of the following EXCEPT?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Eukaryotic cells have mitochondria

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this statement is true, so it is not the EXCEPT answer). Eukaryotic cells do have membrane-bound mitochondria; prokaryotic cells lack this organelle entirely -- a genuine difference between the two cell types.

## answer_b
Prokaryotic cells division by binary fission

## explanation_b
Incorrect (true statement). Prokaryotic cells do divide by binary fission, a simple splitting of one cell into two identical daughters, unlike the mitotic division of eukaryotic cells.

## answer_c
Eukaryotic cells have cilia and flagella with complex structure

## explanation_c
Incorrect (true statement). Where present, eukaryotic flagella and cilia have a complex internal "9+2" microtubule structure, unlike the simpler flagellin-filament flagella of bacteria -- a genuine structural difference.

## answer_d
Prokaryotic cells have no genetic material

## explanation_d
Correct. This is the false statement, so it is the EXCEPT answer. A prokaryotic cell does have genetic material -- a single circular chromosome of DNA -- it simply lacks a membrane-bound nucleus to enclose it, holding the DNA instead in an unbound cytoplasmic region called the nucleoid. "No genetic material" would mean no DNA at all, which is not true of any living bacterial cell.

## title
A true statement about prokaryotes

## question
Which statement is true about prokaryotes?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
They contain a nucleus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Prokaryotes specifically lack a true, membrane-bound nucleus -- their DNA sits in an unbound cytoplasmic nucleoid region instead.

## answer_b
They have mitochondria

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Mitochondria are a eukaryotic membrane-bound organelle; prokaryotic cells have no mitochondria at all.

## answer_c
They usually lack a cell wall

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Most prokaryotes (bacteria) typically do have a cell wall (peptidoglycan-based); only exceptions such as Mycoplasma naturally lack one.

## answer_d
They divide by mitosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Prokaryotes divide by binary fission, a simple splitting process, not by mitosis, which is a eukaryotic process involving a mitotic spindle and chromosome segregation.

## answer_e
They contain a single circular DNA molecule as the genetic material

## explanation_e
Correct. The defining genetic feature of a prokaryotic cell is that its entire genome is typically carried on a single circular DNA chromosome, held free in the cytoplasm (the nucleoid) rather than enclosed by a nuclear membrane. This single-circular-chromosome arrangement, together with the absence of a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, is what makes a cell prokaryotic rather than eukaryotic.

## title
Plasma membrane as a selective barrier

## question
The plasma membrane is considered a barrier to the environment because?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Only water and small molecules may penetrate the membrane

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Restricting passage to only water and small molecules overstates the membrane's selectivity -- many larger molecules and ions cross it too, via specific transport proteins and channels, not just small solutes by simple diffusion.

## answer_b
The membrane is the outermost structure of the cell

## explanation_b
Incorrect. In a bacterium the plasma membrane sits internal to the cell wall (and capsule, where present); it is not the cell's outermost structure.

## answer_c
It controls the passage of molecules into the cell

## explanation_c
Correct. The plasma membrane is termed a barrier because it selectively controls which molecules pass into and out of the cell, rather than letting everything through freely or blocking everything entirely. This selective-permeability function -- not rigidity, not being the outermost layer, and not total exclusion -- is the defining reason the membrane is described as a barrier to the environment. It achieves this control through the lipid bilayer itself plus embedded transport proteins and channels.

## answer_d
It is a rigid structure

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The plasma membrane is a fluid phospholipid bilayer, not a rigid structure; rigidity and shape are instead provided by the cell wall.

## answer_e
No molecules may pass through it

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The membrane does allow selective passage of molecules (via diffusion, channels and transporters); it does not block all molecules completely, which would make the cell unable to obtain nutrients or excrete waste.

## title
Selective barrier regulating cellular traffic

## question
What structure acts as a selective barrier, regulating the traffic of materials into and out of the cell?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cytoskeleton

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The cytoskeleton provides internal structural support and enables shape and movement; it does not regulate what crosses into or out of the cell.

## answer_b
Nuclear envelope

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The nuclear envelope regulates traffic between the nucleus and cytoplasm specifically (via nuclear pores), not traffic between the whole cell and its external environment.

## answer_c
Extracellular matrix

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The extracellular matrix is the network of material surrounding cells in a tissue, providing structural and biochemical support; it is not itself a selective barrier regulating what enters or leaves an individual cell.

## answer_d
Plasma membrane

## explanation_d
Correct. The plasma membrane is the structure that regulates the traffic of materials into and out of the cell as a whole -- it is a selectively permeable barrier, controlling passage through the lipid bilayer itself and through embedded channel and transport proteins. This whole-cell gatekeeping role is distinct from the nuclear envelope's more limited nucleus-cytoplasm traffic, the cytoskeleton's structural role, and the extracellular matrix's role outside the cell entirely.

## explanation_a

Capsule

This makes inclusion granules the one listed structure that is not a virulence factor.

## title
Bacterial spores: the false statement

## question
All of the following statement regarding bacterial spores are true EXCEPT?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Are metabolically active

## explanation_a
Correct. This is the false statement, so it is the EXCEPT answer. Bacterial endospores (formed by genera such as Bacillus and Clostridium) are dormant, non-replicating structures with essentially no active metabolism -- their entire biological purpose is to survive harsh conditions in a metabolically inactive state, not to remain metabolically active.

## answer_b
Are more resistant than vegetative cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect (true statement). Endospores are highly resistant to heat, desiccation, radiation and chemicals, far more so than the vegetative (actively growing) bacterial cell that formed them.

## answer_c
Are formed within certain Gram +ve cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect (true statement). Endospore formation (sporulation) occurs within certain Gram-positive rods, classically Bacillus and Clostridium species, typically triggered by nutrient limitation.

## answer_d
Don't take gram stain

## explanation_d
Incorrect (true statement). The dense, resistant spore coat does not take up the Gram stain, so spores appear as unstained, clear/refractile areas within or beside the parent cell on a Gram-stained smear.
