## title
Hypoproteinemia in chronic liver disease's oedema

## question
A 55 years old man had chronic liver disease. On examination, there is bilateral pitting edema of the leg and ascites. Which of the following findings is most likely to be expected?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Hypoproteinemia

## explanation_a
Correct. In chronic liver disease, impaired hepatic synthetic function reduces plasma albumin production, producing hypoproteinemia. The resulting fall in plasma oncotic pressure allows fluid to leak from the vasculature into the interstitium and peritoneal cavity, producing bilateral pitting leg oedema and ascites. Leukocytosis, hypertension and hypercalcaemia are not the expected accompanying findings of this hypoalbuminemic mechanism.

## answer_b
Leukocytosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Leukocytosis reflects an inflammatory or infective process, not the hypoalbuminemic mechanism behind hepatic oedema and ascites.

## answer_c
Hypertension

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Chronic liver disease more typically produces hypotension, from reduced oncotic pressure and vasodilation, than hypertension.

## answer_d
Hypercalceamia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hypercalcaemia is not a recognised feature of chronic liver disease's oedema and ascites; the expected biochemical finding is a low, not high, plasma protein level.

## title
Subdural fluid collection is a hematoma

## question
A 78-year-old woman falls in the bathtub and strikes the back of her head. Over the next 24 hours, she becomes increasingly somnolent. A head CT scan shows an accumulation of fluid beneath the dura, compressing the left cerebral hemisphere. Which of the following terms best describes this collection of fluid?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Congestion

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Congestion describes passive venous engorgement of blood within vessels, not a discrete extravasated collection outside them.

## answer_b
Petechia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Petechiae are pinpoint haemorrhagic spots in skin or mucosa, not a large fluid collection compressing the brain.

## answer_c
Ecchymosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ecchymosis is a flat subcutaneous bruise, not a discrete, space-occupying fluid collection.

## answer_d
Hematoma

## explanation_d
Correct. A localized collection of extravasated blood outside the vessel wall, such as the fluid accumulating beneath the dura after head trauma, compressing the underlying brain, is termed a hematoma. This is distinct from congestion, a passive venous engorgement within vessels, and from petechiae and ecchymosis, neither of which describes a discrete space-occupying collection. The elderly patient's delayed somnolence after a fall fits the classic evolving picture of such a hematoma.
