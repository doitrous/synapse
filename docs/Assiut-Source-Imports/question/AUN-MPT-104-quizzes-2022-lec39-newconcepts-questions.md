## question
Malignat tumours characterized by:

## subject

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Malignant tumours characteristically show a rapid, not slow, rate of growth.

## answer_b
Being localized

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Malignant tumours are not localized; they invade and can spread beyond their site of origin.

## answer_c
Does not recur after removal

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Malignant tumours characteristically do recur after removal, unlike a benign tumour.

## answer_d
Grow by infiltration

## explanation_d
Correct. Malignant tumours grow by infiltration: they invade and destroy surrounding tissue rather than merely pushing it aside. They characteristically show a rapid, not slow, rate of growth, are not localized, and do recur after removal -- the opposite growth pattern from a benign tumour's expansile growth.

Tumour stage is the strongest prognostic factor

## question
The most important factor in prognosis of malignant tumours is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Tumour grade

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Tumour grade (differentiation) informs prognosis but is not the single most important factor; it ranks below stage.

## answer_b
The size of the tumour

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Raw tumour size alone, without regard to nodal or metastatic spread, is not the single most important prognostic factor.

## answer_c
Tumour stage

## explanation_c
Correct. Among the factors that inform a malignant tumour's prognosis -- grade, size, stage and cell shape -- tumour stage, its anatomic extent of spread, captured by the TNM system's tumour size, nodal involvement and distant metastasis axes, is the single most important. A widely spread cancer carries a substantially worse prognosis regardless of how differentiated its cells appear.

## answer_d
The shape of the cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cell shape alone, without regard to the extent of spread, is not the single most important prognostic factor.

## title
Invasion confirms malignancy

## question
A study is performed to analyze characteristics of malignant neoplasms in biopsy specimens. The biopsies were performed on patients who had rectal carcinoma. Of the following microscopic findings, which is most important feature to indicate that the neoplasm is malignant?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pleomorphism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pleomorphism can also be seen in severe dysplasia without invasion, which by definition is not yet malignant, so it is not the most important confirmatory feature.

## answer_b
Necrosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Necrosis can occur in a benign or non-invasive lesion outgrowing its blood supply, so it is not specific enough to confirm malignancy on its own.

## answer_c
Increased nuclear/cytoplasmic ratio

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An increased nuclear/cytoplasmic ratio can also be seen in severe dysplasia without invasion, so it is not the most important confirmatory feature.

## answer_d
Invasion

## explanation_d
Correct. Among pleomorphism, necrosis, an increased nuclear/cytoplasmic ratio, invasion and atypia, invasion -- histological evidence that tumour cells have breached the basement membrane and infiltrated surrounding tissue -- is the most important feature confirming malignancy. The other four can each also be seen in severe dysplasia without invasion, which by definition is not yet malignant.

## answer_e
Atypia

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Atypia can also be seen in severe dysplasia without invasion, so it is not the most important confirmatory feature.
