## answer_a
42 L

Correct. Total body water (TBW) is about 60% of body weight in a normal adult male. For a 70 kg reference adult, 60% of 70 kg is 42 L -- the standard worked figure this whole compartment scheme is built from, since the ICF, ECF, interstitial and plasma volumes are all fractions of this same 42 L.

## answer_d
10 L

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 10 L is the interstitial fluid volume, a subcompartment within the ECF -- far short of the 42 L total body water figure.

## title
Protein concentration: plasma vs interstitial fluid

## question
Which of the following is in higher concentration in plasma than interstitial space:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
sodium

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sodium is the dominant ECF cation in both plasma and interstitial fluid at nearly identical concentration -- the capillary wall is freely permeable to small ions, so sodium does not build up preferentially on either side.

## answer_b
K

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Potassium, like sodium, crosses the capillary wall freely and sits at nearly the same low concentration in plasma and interstitial fluid; it is not the substance this question isolates.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Glucose also crosses the capillary wall freely and is present at essentially the same concentration in plasma and interstitial fluid, not preferentially retained in plasma.

proteins

## explanation_d
Correct. Plasma proteins (chiefly albumin) are too large to cross the capillary wall freely, so plasma carries a much higher protein concentration than interstitial fluid, even though the two fluids are otherwise nearly identical extracellular fluid. This protein difference is exactly what generates the plasma colloidal osmotic pressure.

Barrier between ICF and ECF

## question

## subject
fnd

not separated from each other

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ICF and ECF are physically separated by the cell membrane; they are not a single, undivided compartment.

## title
Reabsorbing force from interstitial fluid to plasma

## question
The reabsorbing force of fluids from interstitial fluid to the plasma is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
capillary hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Capillary hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid out of the capillary into the interstitial space -- the filtering force, the opposite direction from the reabsorbing force this question asks about.

## answer_b
plasma colloidal osmotic pressure

## explanation_b
Correct. Plasma colloidal osmotic pressure (oncotic pressure), generated by plasma's higher protein concentration relative to interstitial fluid, pulls water back into the capillary from the interstitial space. It is the reabsorbing force in Starling's balance across the capillary wall. It opposes capillary hydrostatic pressure, which pushes fluid the opposite way, out of the capillary.

## answer_c
interstitial fluid hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Interstitial fluid hydrostatic pressure, where present, also opposes filtration out of the capillary, but it is a hydrostatic, not the colloidal osmotic, force, and is not the reabsorbing force named here.

## answer_d
interstitial fluid colloidal osmotic pressure

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Interstitial fluid colloidal osmotic pressure would pull fluid out of the capillary (favouring filtration, not reabsorption), since interstitial fluid has a lower, not higher, protein concentration than plasma.

## id
QST-AUNCBF103-CBFBANKNEW-Q018

## title
Cause of non-pitting oedema

## question
Which of the following causes non-pitting oedema:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
heart failure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Heart failure raises capillary hydrostatic pressure, producing ordinary pitting oedema (free interstitial fluid that can be displaced), not non-pitting oedema.

## answer_b
venous obstruction by thrombosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Venous obstruction by thrombosis also raises hydrostatic pressure upstream of the block, producing pitting, not non-pitting, oedema.

## answer_c
liver failure

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Liver failure lowers plasma colloidal osmotic pressure (via reduced albumin synthesis), producing pitting oedema, the same mechanism family as heart failure and venous obstruction, not the non-pitting pattern.

## answer_d
lymphatic obstruction

## explanation_d
Correct. Lymphatic obstruction prevents the lymphatics from draining protein-rich interstitial fluid back to the circulation. That fluid accumulates and organises with the tissue rather than flowing freely. The result is oedema that is characteristically non-pitting, unlike the hydrostatic- or oncotic-pressure-driven causes above.

Mechanism of the absolute refractory period

## question
During absolute refractory period :

## subject
neuro

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
K+ channels are inactivated

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Voltage-gated K+ channels do not have the same fast inactivation gate mechanism that Na+ channels do, and it is Na+ channel inactivation, not K+ channel inactivation, that defines the absolute refractory period.

## answer_b
Na+ channels are inactivated by inner gates

## explanation_b
Correct. Voltage-gated Na+ channels have a separate inactivation gate that closes shortly after the activation gate opens, regardless of continued depolarization. ## answer_c
stronger stimulus higher than threshold can excite the nerve fiber

## explanation_c
Incorrect. By definition, no stimulus of any strength -- however far above the normal threshold -- can excite the fibre during the absolute refractory period, because the Na+ channels required are inactivated, not merely harder to trigger.

## answer_d
coincides with whole duration of the action potential

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The absolute refractory period spans roughly from the start of depolarization until repolarization is well underway, not the whole duration of the action potential -- the relative refractory period, during which excitability is reduced but not abolished, covers the remaining, later part.
