## title
Bacterial spores: the one accurate statement

## question
Several bacteria that form spores are important human pathogens. Which one of the following is the most accurate statement about bacterial spores?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
They are killed by boiling for 15 minutes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Boiling for 15 minutes does not reliably kill bacterial endospores -- their heat resistance is exactly what makes them a sterilisation challenge; reliable destruction needs autoclaving (moist heat under pressure, roughly 121C) for an adequate hold time, not simple boiling.

## answer_b
They are produced primarily by gram-negative cocci

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Endospores are formed by certain Gram-positive rods (classically Bacillus and Clostridium species), not by Gram-negative cocci.

## answer_c
They are formed primarily when the bacterium is exposed to antibiotics

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sporulation is triggered by nutrient limitation and other unfavourable environmental conditions, not by exposure to antibiotics.

## answer_d
They are produced by anaerobes only in the presence of oxygen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sporulation is not restricted to anaerobes, nor is it specifically dependent on the presence of oxygen -- both aerobic (Bacillus) and anaerobic (Clostridium) spore-formers exist, and sporulation is driven by nutrient depletion, not an oxygen requirement.

## answer_e
They are metabolically inactive yet can survive for years in that inactive state

## explanation_e
Correct. The single most accurate statement here is that bacterial endospores are metabolically inactive (dormant) yet can survive for years in that inactive state -- this dormancy is exactly what gives spores their extraordinary resistance to heat, desiccation, radiation and chemicals, and it is the biological purpose of sporulation: to let the organism outlast harsh conditions until the environment improves and the spore can germinate back into an active vegetative cell. None of the other listed claims (boiling sensitivity, gram-negative-cocci origin, antibiotic trigger, obligatory aerobic formation) is correct.

## title
Plasmids play no direct role in bacterial cell division

## question
The following bacterial structures play a role in cell division EXCEPT?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cell wall

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this structure IS involved in cell division). The cell wall, via septum formation (new wall material laid down at the division site), is directly involved in splitting one bacterial cell into two.

## answer_b
Mesosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect (involved in division). Mesosomes have classically been described as anchoring the bacterial chromosome and coordinating its segregation during cell division.

## answer_c
Chromosomes

## explanation_c
Incorrect (involved in division). The chromosome must replicate and segregate for cell division to succeed; it is central to the process, not peripheral to it.

## answer_d
Plasmids

## explanation_d
Correct. Plasmids are accessory extrachromosomal DNA elements -- while they do get replicated and partitioned into daughter cells alongside the chromosome, they are not themselves structures that carry out or organise the mechanical process of cell division, unlike the cell wall's septum formation, the mesosome's classical chromosome-anchoring role, the chromosome's own segregation, or the cytoplasmic membrane's role in septum ingrowth. A plasmid's role is to carry accessory genes (e.g. antibiotic resistance), not to drive division itself.

## answer_e
Cytoplasmic membrane

## explanation_e
Incorrect (involved in division). The cytoplasmic membrane grows inward at the division site (septum ingrowth) as part of splitting the cell in two.

Incorrect (true statement). Protoplasts are indeed produced after treating a bacterium with lysozyme, which digests the peptidoglycan wall.

Storage granules are present in the cytoplasm of?

## subject

## status

## explanation_a

## answer_c
Corynebacterium diphtheriae

Incorrect. Klebsiella is notable for its prominent polysaccharide capsule, not for cytoplasmic storage granules.

## title
Membrane-active antimicrobials alter selective permeability

## question
Antimicrobials that affect bacterial cytoplasmic membrane are going to alter?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Selective permeability of the cell

## explanation_a
Correct. The cytoplasmic membrane's defining function is to act as a selectively permeable barrier, controlling what enters and leaves the cell -- so an antimicrobial that disrupts this membrane directly alters that selective-permeability function, typically by disrupting the lipid bilayer and letting essential ions and metabolites leak out uncontrollably. This is the mechanism of action for membrane-active agents such as polymyxins, distinct from cell-wall-active agents (like beta-lactams, which alter rigidity/shape instead) or ribosome-active agents (which alter protein synthesis, not permeability).

## answer_b
Rigidity of the cell

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Rigidity is provided by the peptidoglycan cell wall, not the cytoplasmic membrane; an agent targeting the membrane does not primarily alter rigidity.

## answer_c
Staining affinity of the cell

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Staining affinity depends on the cell wall's peptidoglycan content, not the cytoplasmic membrane.

## answer_d
Shape of the cell

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Shape is maintained by the rigid peptidoglycan wall, not the fluid cytoplasmic membrane; membrane-active agents do not primarily alter cell shape.

## answer_e
Antigenicity of the cell

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Antigenicity is largely determined by surface molecules such as LPS, flagella (H antigen) and capsule, not by the cytoplasmic membrane's permeability function.

## answer_b

## answer_c
Bacteria

## explanation_c
Correct. Bacteria are the classic prokaryotic organisms -- cells with no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles, a single circular chromosome held in the nucleoid, and 70S ribosomes. Among the options given, only bacteria fit the prokaryote category; fungi and human cells are eukaryotic, and a virus is not a cell (prokaryotic or otherwise) at all.

## answer_d
Virus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A virus is acellular -- not a prokaryote, not a eukaryote, not any kind of true cell.
