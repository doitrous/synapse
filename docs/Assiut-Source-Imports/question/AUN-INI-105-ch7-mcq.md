## title
Yeasts reproduce by budding

## question
Yeasts reproduce by?

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
None of the above

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Budding is a specific, well-defined mechanism (see D), so "none of the above" is wrong.

## answer_b
Spore formation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Spore formation is a fungal reproductive/dispersal structure, but it is not the routine way an individual yeast cell multiplies -- that is budding.

## answer_c
Binary fission

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Binary fission is the bacterial (prokaryotic) reproductive mechanism -- a single cell splitting symmetrically into two -- not a fungal one.

## answer_d
Budding

## explanation_d
Correct. Yeasts, being unicellular fungi, multiply asexually by budding: a small outgrowth forms on the parent cell, enlarges, and eventually separates as a new, genetically identical daughter cell. This is distinct from mold propagation, which instead occurs by fragmentation of the mycelium.

## title
Fungi are eukaryotes with nucleus and chromosomes

## question
Which statement regarding fungi is correct?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
All fungi are able to grow as yeast and mold

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Only dimorphic fungi grow as both yeast and mold depending on conditions; many fungi are fixed as either yeasts or molds throughout their life cycle.

## answer_b
Although fungi are eukaryotes, they lack mitochondria

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Fungi are eukaryotes and, like other eukaryotic cells, possess mitochondria for aerobic respiration -- this is part of what makes them eukaryotic in the first place.

## answer_c
Fungi are photosynthetic

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Fungi are not photosynthetic; they lack chloroplasts and obtain nutrition heterotrophically, by absorption of organic material from their environment.

## answer_d
Fungi have nucleus and chromosomes

## explanation_d
Correct. Fungi are eukaryotic organisms: each cell has a true, membrane-bound nucleus containing chromosomes. This is unlike prokaryotic bacteria, whose single circular chromosome lies free in the cytoplasm with no nuclear membrane at all. The nucleus, together with membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria, is what places fungi in the eukaryotic domain alongside animal and plant cells rather than with bacteria.

## answer_e
Few fungi possess cell membranes

## explanation_e
Incorrect. All fungal cells, like all living cells, possess a cytoplasmic (cell) membrane -- this is not a rare or optional feature.

## title
Mold hyphae may or may not be septate

## question
Which statement regarding fungal growth and morphology is correct?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Pseudohyphae are produced by all yeasts

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pseudohyphae (chains of elongated, budding cells) are characteristic of some yeasts, such as Candida when it invades tissue, but not a universal feature of every yeast.

## answer_b
Molds produce hyphae that may or may not be partitioned with cross wall or septa

## explanation_b
Correct. Mold hyphae may be septate, cross-walled into individual compartments by regularly spaced septa. Or they may be non-septate/coenocytic, a continuous, multinucleate cytoplasmic mass with no cross-walls at all, as in the lower fungi such as Mucor. This variability is itself the medically testable distinction, not a fixed property of "molds" as a whole.

## answer_c
Conidia are produced by sexual reproduction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Conidia are asexual spores, produced by asexual reproduction, not sexual reproduction.

## answer_d
Most yeasts reproduce by budding and lack cell wall

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Yeasts do reproduce mainly by budding, but they do have a cell wall -- lacking a cell wall is not a feature of ordinary yeast cells.

## answer_e
Most pathogenic dimorphic include produce hyphae in the host and yeasts at 30 C

## explanation_e
Incorrect. This reverses the standard dimorphism pattern: pathogenic dimorphic fungi typically produce hyphae (mold form) in the environment at room temperature and convert to yeasts in the host at body temperature (37 C), not the other way round.

## question
Selective toxicity of antifungal drugs is based on presence of?

## subject

## status

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The 30S ribosomal subunit is a bacterial target (for example, of aminoglycosides and tetracyclines) -- fungi are eukaryotes with 80S ribosomes, so this is not the fungal-selectivity target.

## answer_b
Dihydrofolate reductase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dihydrofolate reductase is targeted by antifolate drugs (such as trimethoprim, or pyrimethamine against protozoa) -- it is not the basis of antifungal selective toxicity.

## answer_c
DNA gyrase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. DNA gyrase is the bacterial target of fluoroquinolone antibiotics, not an antifungal target.

Ergosterol

## explanation_d
Correct. The fungal cytoplasmic membrane contains ergosterol as its characteristic sterol, in place of the cholesterol used by human cell membranes. Because ergosterol is a fungus-specific target absent from human cells, most major antifungal drug classes are built around it, giving them selective toxicity against fungi while sparing the human host.

## answer_e
Mycolic acid

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Mycolic acid is a component of the mycobacterial cell wall (targeted by isoniazid), not a fungal structure.

Flucytosine inhibits fungal DNA synthesis

## question
Which of the following is an antifungal drug that acts by inhibiting fungal DNA synthesis?

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ketoconazole is an azole that inhibits ergosterol synthesis, acting on the fungal cell membrane, not DNA synthesis.

Flucytosine

## explanation_b
Correct. Flucytosine (5-FC) is taken up by fungal cells and converted to 5-fluorouracil. The active metabolites of 5-fluorouracil then inhibit fungal DNA and RNA synthesis, halting replication. ## answer_e
Amphotericin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Amphotericin B is a polyene that binds ergosterol and forms membrane pores, not a DNA-synthesis inhibitor.

## answer_e
Dermatophytes
