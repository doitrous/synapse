## title
Definition of agenesis

## question
Complete absence of an organ is called

## answer_a
Dysplasia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Dysplasia is disordered growth with loss of uniformity of individual cells and their architectural orientation, a pattern seen within a tissue that still exists -- not the complete failure of an organ to form.

## answer_b
Anaplasia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Anaplasia is loss of cellular differentiation, a feature of malignant tumour cells reverting to a more primitive state -- it describes a cell's degree of differentiation, not the complete absence of an organ.

## answer_c
Agenesis

## explanation_c
Correct. Agenesis is the complete congenital failure of an organ to form during development, so the organ is entirely absent at birth. It is a developmental anomaly, distinct from the four adaptive cellular responses (hypertrophy, hyperplasia, atrophy, metaplasia) that an already-formed tissue can undergo.

## answer_d
Hyperplasia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hyperplasia is an increase in the number of cells of an organ that has already formed -- the opposite situation from an organ that never formed at all.
