# Item

## id
CON-INF-81BF7086EC5B07

## label
Antigenic drift in viruses is caused by point mutation, not gene reassortment

## canonical_key
virus.antigenic-variation.drift-by-mutation

## aliases
Antigenic drift
Antigenic drift vs antigenic shift

## arabic_label
الانحراف المستضدي الفيروسي

## arabic_aliases
الطفرة النقطية والتغير المستضدي

## definition
Antigenic drift is a gradual change in a virus's surface antigens caused by the accumulation of point mutations in the genes encoding those surface proteins, most classically described for influenza's hemagglutinin and neuraminidase. It is distinct from antigenic shift, which is a sudden, major change produced instead by reassortment -- the exchange of whole genome segments between two different viral strains co-infecting the same cell -- and which requires a segmented genome, unlike drift.

## explicit_objective
State that antigenic drift is produced by the gradual accumulation of point mutations, and distinguish it from antigenic shift, which is produced instead by segment reassortment.

## pitfalls
Confusing antigenic drift (mutation, gradual, can occur in any virus) with antigenic shift (reassortment, abrupt, requires a segmented genome); assuming both terms describe the same mechanism just at different speeds.

## label
Naked (non-enveloped) viruses consist of the nucleocapsid only, with no surrounding envelope

## canonical_key
virus.classification.naked-nonenveloped-nucleocapsid-only

## aliases
Naked virus
Non-enveloped virus

## arabic_label
الفيروسات العارية

## arabic_aliases
فيروسات بدون غلاف

## definition
A naked (non-enveloped) virus is one whose complete particle consists of the nucleocapsid alone -- genome plus capsid -- with no lipid envelope surrounding it, in contrast to an enveloped virus, whose nucleocapsid is wrapped in a host-membrane-derived lipoprotein envelope acquired during budding. "Naked" and "non-enveloped" are synonyms for this same structural fact, and naked viruses are consequently more resistant than enveloped viruses to lipid solvents, detergents and drying.

## explicit_objective
State that a naked (non-enveloped) virus consists of the nucleocapsid only, with no envelope, and that this is what "naked" and "non-enveloped" both mean.

## pitfalls
Confusing "naked virus" with "incomplete virus" (a defective particle missing genes) or "simple virus" (a loosely used, non-standard term); forgetting that naked viruses are more environmentally resistant precisely because they lack the fragile lipid envelope.

## label
The fungal cell membrane contains ergosterol, not cholesterol

## canonical_key
fungus.cell-membrane.ergosterol-vs-cholesterol

## arabic_label

## definition
The fungal cell membrane's characteristic sterol is ergosterol, the fungal counterpart of the cholesterol found in animal cell membranes -- not cholesterol itself, and not the lipopolysaccharide that instead belongs to the Gram-negative bacterial outer membrane. Chitin, another commonly confused fungal component, is a cell-wall polysaccharide, not a membrane constituent, so it does not answer a membrane-composition question.

## explicit_objective
State that ergosterol, not cholesterol, is the fungal cell membrane's characteristic sterol, distinguishing it from both the bacterial outer-membrane LPS and the fungal cell-wall's chitin.

## pitfalls
Assuming fungal membranes use cholesterol like animal cells (ergosterol is the fungal-specific sterol, and this difference is the basis of azole/polyene antifungal selectivity); confusing the membrane sterol (ergosterol) with the cell-wall polysaccharide (chitin).

Including anaerobic wound conditions among fungal-infection risk factors by confusing it with anaerobic bacterial infection risk; forgetting that broad-spectrum antibiotics predispose to fungal overgrowth precisely by removing bacterial competition.

## label
A prion is an infectious protein particle with no detectable nucleic acid, and unlike a virus it does not replicate via nucleic-acid-based multiplication

## canonical_key
virus.prion.definition-infectious-protein-no-nucleic-acid

## aliases
Prion definition
Infectious protein particle

## arabic_label
تعريف البريون

## arabic_aliases
جسيم بروتيني معدٍ بلا حمض نووي

## definition
A prion is an infectious protein particle that contains no detectable nucleic acid of any kind (no DNA, no RNA, no plasmid) -- it is protein-only, distinguishing it from a virus (which always has DNA or RNA), a viroid (uncoated circular RNA, no protein) and a defective virus (which does have nucleic acid, just an incomplete genome). Because a prion lacks nucleic acid, it cannot "multiply" in the sense a virus does through nucleic acid replication and protein synthesis; instead it propagates by inducing a conformational (misfolding) change in the host's own normal cellular protein, a mechanism responsible for transmissible spongiform encephalopathies.

## explicit_objective
Define a prion as an infectious protein particle without detectable nucleic acid, and state that it propagates by inducing host-protein misfolding rather than by nucleic-acid-based multiplication.

## pitfalls
Attributing a nucleic acid (even a small one) to prions, which by definition have none; describing prion propagation as "multiplication" or "replication" in the viral sense rather than as protein-misfolding conversion; confusing a prion with a viroid (RNA, no protein) or a defective virus (has nucleic acid).
