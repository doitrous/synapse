# Item

## label
Colinearity: the sequence of a mature eukaryotic mRNA corresponds directly to the amino acid sequence of the protein it encodes

## canonical_key
transcription.mrna.colinearity-with-protein-sequence

## aliases
Colinearity of mRNA and protein
mRNA-protein sequence correspondence

## arabic_label
التوازي الخطي بين تتابع الحمض النووي الريبي المرسال والبروتين

## arabic_aliases
التوازي الخطي

## definition
Colinearity is the principle that the linear sequence of codons in a mature messenger RNA corresponds, in the same order, to the linear sequence of amino acids in the protein it is translated into -- the reading frame is not scrambled or rearranged between transcript and product. This is distinct from other statements sometimes confused with it: mRNA is not produced from the same genes as rRNA/tRNA (different polymerases, different products), mature mammalian mRNA does undergo extensive modification (capping, splicing, polyadenylation) rather than minimal modification, and the primary transcript is not virtually identical to the mature mRNA that exits the nucleus (introns are removed).

## explicit_objective
State that the mature mRNA sequence and the encoded protein's amino acid sequence are colinear, and reject the distractor claims about mRNA being minimally modified or unchanged from the primary transcript.

## pitfalls
Confusing "colinearity" with "the primary transcript is unchanged" -- splicing, capping and polyadenylation all occur between the primary transcript and the mature, colinear mRNA that is actually translated.

## label
RNA polymerase and its associated transcription factors bind specific nucleotide sequences upstream of the transcription start site (the promoter) to initiate transcription

## definition
Transcription begins when RNA polymerase, together with its associated transcription (sigma/general) factors, recognises and binds specific nucleotide sequences located upstream of (before) the transcription start site -- the promoter region -- rather than binding downstream sequences, restriction sites, or histone proteins. ## pitfalls
Confusing promoter (upstream) binding with the replication-associated enzymes helicase and primase, which act at the replication fork rather than at a transcriptional start site.

Protein synthesis proceeds: small ribosomal subunit loaded with initiation factors, mRNA and initiator aminoacyl-tRNA; large subunit joins; elongation factors deliver aminoacyl-tRNA to the A site; a peptide bond forms; the ribosome translocates one codon

Getting this order right distinguishes initiation-complex assembly from the repeating elongation cycle that follows it.

## pitfalls
Placing peptide bond formation before aminoacyl-tRNA delivery to the A site, or placing translocation before the peptide bond that triggers it.

## label
Activating an amino acid for protein synthesis (charging it onto its tRNA) consumes two high-energy phosphate-bond equivalents

## canonical_key
translation.amino-acid-activation.two-phosphate-bonds

## aliases

## definition
Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase activates an amino acid by using ATP to form an aminoacyl-adenylate intermediate, releasing pyrophosphate (PPi); the PPi is then hydrolysed to two inorganic phosphates by pyrophosphatase, pulling the reaction forward. Because ATP is converted to AMP (losing two phosphate groups rather than one), amino acid activation is said to consume two high-energy phosphate-bond equivalents per amino acid, not one, zero, three or four.

## explicit_objective
State that charging an amino acid onto its tRNA (activation) costs two high-energy phosphate bonds (ATP to AMP + 2 Pi), not one.

## pitfalls
Counting only the first bond broken (ATP to AMP + PPi) and forgetting that PPi hydrolysis is a second high-energy bond spent to drive the reaction forward.

## label
Spontaneous deamination of cytosine to uracil in DNA is mutagenic if not repaired before replication

## canonical_key
mutation.spontaneous-damage.cytosine-deamination-to-uracil

## aliases
Cytosine deamination
C to U mutation

## arabic_label
نزع الأمين التلقائي من السيتوزين إلى يوراسيل في الحمض النووي

## arabic_aliases
تحول السيتوزين إلى يوراسيل

## definition
Certain DNA bases undergo spontaneous deamination at a constant background rate regardless of conditions. Deamination of cytosine produces uracil, a base that does not normally occur in DNA; if base excision repair does not remove this uracil before the next round of replication, DNA polymerase reads it as thymine's partner and pairs it with adenine, converting the original C-G pair into a T-A pair -- a mutation. Other deaminations (e.g. adenine to hypoxanthine, guanine to xanthine) are also mutagenic but by different base-pairing consequences; cytosine-to-uracil is the classic exam example because uracil in DNA is itself the signal that base excision repair (uracil-DNA glycosylase) specifically recognises and removes.

## explicit_objective
State that cytosine deaminates to uracil and that this specific deamination causes a mutation (C-G to T-A) if left unrepaired before replication.

## pitfalls
Picking a deamination that does not by itself produce a base absent from normal DNA (e.g. G to A rather than C to U), or forgetting that the mutagenic consequence depends on the change going unrepaired before replication.

## definition
Maple syrup urine disease results from a deficiency of branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase, the enzyme that continues the catabolism of the three branched-chain amino acids -- leucine, isoleucine and valine -- after their initial transamination. It is not a disorder of acidic amino acids, of phenylalanine/tyrosine, or of serine/threonine.

## explicit_objective
Identify leucine, isoleucine and valine as the amino acid group whose metabolism is impaired in maple syrup urine disease.

## pitfalls
Confusing maple syrup urine disease's branched-chain amino acids with phenylketonuria's aromatic amino acids -- both are inborn errors of amino acid metabolism but affect different substrates and enzymes.

## label
Phenylketonuria is caused by a lack of phenylalanine hydroxylase, the enzyme that converts phenylalanine to tyrosine

## canonical_key
metabolism.aminoacidopathy.pku-phenylalanine-hydroxylase-deficiency

## aliases
Phenylketonuria cause

## arabic_label
بيلة الفينيل كيتون ناتجة عن نقص إنزيم فينيل ألانين هيدروكسيلاز

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Phenylketonuria (PKU) is caused by a deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase, the hepatic enzyme that normally converts phenylalanine to tyrosine (using tetrahydrobiopterin as cofactor). Without this enzyme, phenylalanine and its transamination product phenylpyruvate (a "phenylketone") accumulate, and downstream pathways that depend on phenylalanine hydroxylase's product tyrosine -- melanin, thyroid hormone and catecholamine synthesis -- are also starved of substrate unless dietary tyrosine is supplemented. Cystathionine synthetase, DOPA decarboxylase and homogentisate oxidase are enzymes in other pathways (homocysteine metabolism, catecholamine synthesis, and tyrosine catabolism respectively) and are not the cause of PKU.

## explicit_objective
Identify phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency as the cause of phenylketonuria, and state that it converts phenylalanine to tyrosine.

## pitfalls
Confusing phenylalanine hydroxylase (PKU) with homogentisate oxidase (alkaptonuria) -- both are enzymes in the phenylalanine/tyrosine pathway but their deficiencies cause different diseases.

GABA is synthesised from glutamate by glutamate decarboxylase

## definition
Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter, is synthesised from glutamate by a single decarboxylation reaction catalysed by glutamate decarboxylase (GAD), which requires pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B6) as cofactor. This is distinct from glutamate transaminase (which interconverts glutamate with alpha-ketoacids) and glutamate synthase (which makes glutamate from glutamine and alpha-ketoglutarate, the reverse direction).

## explicit_objective
Identify glutamate decarboxylase as the enzyme that synthesises GABA from glutamate.

## pitfalls
Confusing glutamate decarboxylase (GABA synthesis) with glutamate transaminase or glutamate synthase, which are different reactions in glutamate metabolism.

## label
Phenylketonuria impairs melanin, thyroid hormone and catecholamine synthesis, but not melatonin, since melatonin derives from tryptophan rather than phenylalanine/tyrosine

## canonical_key
metabolism.aminoacidopathy.pku-spares-melatonin

## aliases

PKU-deficient pathways

## arabic_label
بيلة الفينيل كيتون لا تؤثر على تخليق الميلاتونين

## arabic_aliases

## definition
Because phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency starves the tyrosine-dependent pathways of substrate, PKU impairs synthesis of melanin, thyroid hormone and the catecholamines -- all of which are built from tyrosine. Melatonin, by contrast, is synthesised from tryptophan (via serotonin), a completely separate amino acid pathway unaffected by a phenylalanine hydroxylase defect, so melatonin synthesis is the exception that is NOT deficient in PKU.

## explicit_objective
Identify melatonin as the compound whose synthesis is spared in PKU, because it derives from tryptophan rather than the tyrosine pathway PKU disrupts.

## pitfalls
Assuming every "amine hormone" is affected by PKU; melatonin's tryptophan origin puts it outside the tyrosine-dependent pathways that PKU actually blocks.

## label
Arginine is the amino acid precursor of nitric oxide, an endogenous vasodilator used clinically as a hypotensive, anti-anginal agent

## canonical_key
metabolism.aminoacid.arginine-nitric-oxide-precursor

## aliases
Arginine nitric oxide precursor
NO synthesis from arginine

## arabic_label
الأرجينين هو سلف أكسيد النيتريك الموسع للأوعية الدموية

## arabic_aliases
سلف أكسيد النيتريك

## definition
Nitric oxide synthase converts the amino acid arginine (releasing citrulline as a by-product) to nitric oxide (NO), a potent endogenous vasodilator. Because NO relaxes vascular smooth muscle, arginine-derived NO (and NO-donor drugs) are of interest as a hypotensive and anti-anginal agent, lowering blood pressure and dilating coronary vessels. Tryptophan, histidine and phenylalanine are precursors of other biomolecules (serotonin/niacin, histamine, and tyrosine/catecholamines respectively) and are not the NO precursor.

## explicit_objective
Identify arginine as the amino acid precursor of the vasodilator nitric oxide.

## pitfalls
Confusing arginine's nitric-oxide role with histidine's role as the histamine precursor, or with tryptophan's role as the serotonin/niacin precursor.
