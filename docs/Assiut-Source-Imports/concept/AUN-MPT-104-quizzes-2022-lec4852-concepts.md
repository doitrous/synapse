## label
Acyclovir is used to inhibit herpes viruses

## canonical_key

## arabic_label

## definition
Acyclovir is a guanosine nucleoside analogue used to inhibit herpesviruses, principally herpes simplex virus (HSV) and varicella-zoster virus (VZV). It is selectively activated by the viral thymidine kinase and, once triphosphorylated, is incorporated by the viral DNA polymerase to terminate the growing viral DNA chain. Among azidothymidine (an anti-HIV drug), oseltamivir (anti-influenza) and amantadine (anti-influenza), acyclovir is the agent specific for herpesviruses.

## explicit_objective
Identify acyclovir as the drug used to inhibit herpes viruses, distinguishing it from anti-HIV and anti-influenza agents.

## pitfalls
Confusing anti-influenza (oseltamivir, amantadine) or anti-retroviral (azidothymidine) drugs with the herpesvirus agent. Acyclovir's selectivity comes from requiring the viral thymidine kinase for activation.

## definition
Valacyclovir is the L-valyl ester prodrug of acyclovir, giving markedly better oral bioavailability. For a nursing mother with recurrent genital herpes previously controlled by a topical drug, valacyclovir is the oral agent most likely to be prescribed, in preference to foscarnet, ritonavir or amantadine, which are not first-line oral herpes simplex therapy.

## explicit_objective
Select valacyclovir as the oral drug for genital herpes simplex in a patient previously managed topically.

## pitfalls
Choosing foscarnet (reserved for resistant CMV/HSV), ritonavir (anti-HIV protease inhibitor) or amantadine (anti-influenza). Valacyclovir is chosen for its oral prodrug pharmacokinetics.

## definition
Oseltamivir eradicates H1N1 (and other) influenza virus by inhibiting the viral neuraminidase enzyme. Blocking neuraminidase prevents newly assembled virions from being cleaved free of the host-cell surface, halting viral release and spread. It does not act by inhibiting viral RNA replication, DNA replication or uncoating.

## explicit_objective
State that oseltamivir works by inhibiting the viral neuraminidase enzyme.

## pitfalls
Choosing "inhibition of viral RNA/DNA replication" or "inhibition of uncoating" (the amantadine mechanism). Oseltamivir's target is neuraminidase.
