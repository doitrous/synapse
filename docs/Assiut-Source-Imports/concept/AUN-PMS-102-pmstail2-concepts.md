# Item

## id
CON-MSK-8F0BC0538673CD

## label
The clavicle is subcutaneous throughout its whole extent

## canonical_key
anatomy.clavicle.subcutaneous-throughout

## aliases
Clavicle surface anatomy
Collar bone

## arabic_label
الترقوة تقع تحت الجلد مباشرة طوال امتدادها

## arabic_aliases
الترقوة

## definition
The clavicle is the one long bone of the body that is subcutaneous throughout its whole extent, lying immediately deep to the skin with no intervening muscle along its entire S-shaped length, from the sternal end medially to the acromial end laterally. This subcutaneous position is exactly why the clavicle is both easily palpated along its whole length in the living subject and one of the most commonly fractured bones, since it has no overlying muscle to cushion a fall onto the shoulder or an outstretched hand. Its lateral end is flattened, not quadrangular, and its superior surface is smooth, not rough, distinguishing this fact from other, incorrect statements sometimes printed about the bone.

## explicit_objective
State that the clavicle is subcutaneous throughout its whole extent, with no overlying muscle.

## pitfalls
Assuming a bone described as commonly fractured must be deeply placed and protected. The clavicle fractures easily precisely because it lies unprotected just under the skin along its whole length.

The lateral end of the scapular spine forms the acromion, which articulates with the clavicle

## definition
The scapula (shoulder blade) carries a prominent spine on its posterior surface, dividing it into a supraspinous fossa above and an infraspinous fossa below. The spine's lateral end projects forward and flattens out into the acromion, a subcutaneous process that overhangs the shoulder joint and articulates with the lateral end of the clavicle at the acromioclavicular joint. The coracoid process, by contrast, projects forward (not backward) from the scapula's superior border, and the lateral angle, not the medial angle, carries the pear-shaped glenoid cavity that receives the head of the humerus.

## explicit_objective
State that the lateral end of the scapular spine forms the acromion, which articulates with the clavicle at the acromioclavicular joint.

## pitfalls
Assigning the glenoid cavity to the scapula's medial angle. It is the lateral angle that carries the glenoid cavity; the medial (superior) angle is where the levator scapulae attaches.

## label
Below the head of the humerus lies the surgical neck, and below that the greater and lesser tuberosities separated by the bicipital groove

## canonical_key

## aliases

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases
العنق الجراحي لعظم العضد

## definition
Immediately below the rounded head of the humerus is the anatomical neck, and immediately below that is the surgical neck, a common fracture site, named because it is the neck a surgeon most often has to treat. Below the surgical neck lie the greater tuberosity laterally and the lesser tuberosity anteriorly, separated from each other by the intertubercular (bicipital) groove, which carries the tendon of the long head of biceps brachii. The humerus articulates with more than only the scapula at the shoulder, since it also articulates with the radius and ulna at the elbow, and its lower end carries more than only the two epicondyles, since it also carries the capitulum and trochlea that form the elbow joint's own articular surfaces.

## explicit_objective
Sequence the humerus's proximal landmarks below its head: the surgical neck, then the greater and lesser tuberosities separated by the bicipital groove.

## pitfalls
Confusing the anatomical neck with the surgical neck. The anatomical neck is the narrow groove immediately at the base of the head; the surgical neck, the common fracture site, lies below the tuberosities, at the junction of the head-and-tuberosity region with the shaft.

## canonical_key

## arabic_label
عظم الفخذ هو أطول وأثقل عظم في الجسم

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The femur, the single long bone of the thigh, is the longest and heaviest bone in the human body, reflecting its role in transmitting the full weight of the trunk from the hip joint to the knee. Most of its shaft is smoothly rounded except for a prominent posterior ridge, the linea aspera, not the medial aspect described in some incorrect option lists. Its lower end carries two femoral condyles, a medial and a lateral one, that articulate at the knee, while its upper end, not its lower end, carries the head, neck and two trochanters that articulate at the hip.

## explicit_objective
State that the femur is the longest and heaviest bone in the body.

## pitfalls
Placing the head, neck and trochanters at the femur's lower end. Those landmarks belong to the upper end, where the femur articulates with the hip bone; the lower end carries the two femoral condyles that articulate at the knee.

## arabic_label
لا يشارك عظم الشظية في مفصل الركبة

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The fibula is the slender lateral (not medial) bone of the leg, and unlike the tibia it takes no part in the articulation at the knee joint; the knee joint is formed entirely between the femur and the tibia. The fibula's own lateral interosseous border gives attachment to the interosseous membrane joining it to the tibia, and although it bears very little of the body's weight, it does provide attachment for muscles and contributes to the ankle joint distally, where its lower end forms the lateral malleolus.

## explicit_objective
State that the fibula takes no part in the knee joint, which is formed by the femur and tibia alone.

## pitfalls
Assuming every long bone of a limb segment shares in every joint at that segment's ends. The fibula's own articular contribution is at the ankle, not the knee; the knee is a two-bone joint, femur with tibia only.

Spermatogenesis includes both meiosis and spermiogenesis

## canonical_key
andrology.spermatogenesis.process-includes-meiosis-and-spermiogenesis

## definition
Spermatogenesis, the whole process turning a spermatogonium into a mature spermatozoon, includes two component processes taught separately elsewhere: meiosis, the two reduction divisions that turn a diploid primary spermatocyte into haploid spermatids, and spermiogenesis, the subsequent transformation of a round spermatid into a streamlined, motile spermatozoon without any further cell division. Because both of these processes -- one dividing the cell, one remodelling it without dividing it further -- are genuine parts of spermatogenesis as a whole, a question asking what the process "includes" is answered by naming both together, not by choosing one at the expense of the other.

## explicit_objective
State that spermatogenesis as a whole includes both meiosis (the reduction divisions) and spermiogenesis (the subsequent non-dividing remodelling into a spermatozoon).

## pitfalls
Treating meiosis and spermiogenesis as alternative, competing answers. They are sequential, complementary parts of the same overall process, which is exactly why both is the correct choice when both are offered alongside a nonsense distractor.

Spermatogenesis is under combined pituitary hormonal control: follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinising hormone (LH) together regulate the process, LH acting mainly on Leydig cells to drive testosterone production and FSH acting mainly on Sertoli cells. ## explicit_objective

## pitfalls
Assuming LH alone regulates spermatogenesis because it drives testosterone production. FSH's own separate actions on the Sertoli cell, testicular fluid production and testosterone-receptor synthesis, are equally part of the hormonal regulation of the process.
