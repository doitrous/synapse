## label
Enterobius vermicularis is best diagnosed with the perianal cellophane tape technique

## canonical_key
enterobius-vermicularis.diagnosis.cellophane-tape-technique

## aliases


## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Enterobius vermicularis is best detected by applying the sticky surface of cellophane tape to the perianal area early in the morning before washing or defecation, then examining the tape as a microscopic slide, since the gravid female migrates out at night to deposit eggs on the perianal skin rather than releasing them into the gut lumen; a standard stool wet mount, by contrast, is a poor method for Enterobius because so few eggs are actually present in the faeces.

## explicit_objective
Identify the perianal cellophane tape technique, not a stool wet mount, as the preferred diagnostic method for Enterobius vermicularis.

## pitfalls
Assuming a routine morning stool wet mount (the standard method for most other intestinal helminths) is also the best method for pinworm; it is not, because pinworm eggs are deposited on perianal skin, not into the gut lumen.

## label
Diagnostic stages of ascariasis are recovered from both faeces and sputum

## canonical_key
ascaris-lumbricoides.diagnostic-stage.faeces-and-sputum

## aliases


## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
The diagnostic stages of ascariasis can be recovered from both faeces and sputum: the fertilized, unembryonated egg is the diagnostic stage found in the stool of the adult-worm-bearing patient, while migrating Ascaris lumbricoides larvae can be coughed up and recovered in the sputum during the obligatory pulmonary migratory phase of the life cycle (Loeffler's syndrome), before the larvae are swallowed back into the small intestine to mature.

## explicit_objective
Identify faeces and sputum, not blood or urine, as the two specimens carrying a diagnostic stage of ascariasis.

## pitfalls
Forgetting that Ascaris has two separate diagnostic windows -- the migratory larva in sputum during pulmonary migration, and the egg in stool once the adult worm is established -- and assuming only stool applies.

Nematodes are round worms: elongated, cylindrical, unsegmented, with separate sexes

## canonical_key
nematoda.classification.round-worms

## aliases


## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
Nematodes are round worms: elongated, cylindrical, unsegmented worms with a complete (tubular) digestive tract and separate sexes, distinguishing them from the flat, leaf-shaped trematodes (flukes) and from the flat, segmented, hermaphroditic cestodes (tapeworms).

## explicit_objective
Define nematodes as round worms, distinguishing the class from the leaf-shaped trematodes and the segmented cestodes.

## pitfalls
Confusing the three helminth classes: nematodes (round, unsegmented), trematodes (flat, leaf-shaped) and cestodes (flat, segmented).

## definition
Enterobius vermicularis is the classic cause of perianal itching (pruritus ani), produced by the gravid female's nocturnal migration onto the perianal skin to lay her eggs, an irritation that is often worse at night and can lead to scratching, autoinfection and secondary bacterial infection of the excoriated skin.

## explicit_objective
Identify Enterobius vermicularis as the classic cause of perianal itching (pruritus ani), and link it to the mechanism of nocturnal egg-laying.

## pitfalls
Attributing perianal itching to Trichinella, Ascaris or Strongyloides; only Enterobius's nocturnal perianal egg-laying produces this specific symptom.

Trichinella spiralis infects man through ingestion of infected meat

## definition
Trichinella spiralis is unique among these four parasites in infecting man through ingestion of infected food, specifically undercooked meat (classically pork) containing encysted larvae, whereas Strongyloides stercoralis and Necator americanus infect by skin penetration and Schistosoma mansoni infects by cercarial skin penetration during contact with contaminated water.

## explicit_objective
Identify Trichinella spiralis as transmitted by ingestion of infected meat, unlike Strongyloides, Necator and Schistosoma, which all infect by skin penetration.

## pitfalls
Assuming every parasite in a mixed list shares the same skin-penetration route; Trichinella is the food-borne, ingestion-route exception.

## label
The embryonated egg is the infective stage shared by Ascaris lumbricoides and Trichuris trichiura

## canonical_key
ascaris-and-trichuris.infective-stage.embryonated-egg

## aliases


## arabic_label


## arabic_aliases


## definition
The embryonated (mature) egg is the infective stage shared by both Trichuris trichiura (Trichocephalus trichuris) and Ascaris lumbricoides, both of which require a period of maturation in soil before their eggs become infective by ingestion; this differs from Enterobius vermicularis, whose egg embryonates within hours on the perianal skin rather than in soil, and from hookworms, which infect instead through a skin-penetrating filariform larva rather than through an ingested egg at all.

## explicit_objective
Identify Ascaris lumbricoides and Trichuris trichiura together as sharing the embryonated-egg infective stage, unlike Enterobius (rapid perianal embryonation) or hookworm (skin-penetrating larva).

## pitfalls
Picking only one of Ascaris/Trichuris rather than recognising both share this soil-maturing, embryonated-egg infective stage; or wrongly including Enterobius or hookworm in the same group.
