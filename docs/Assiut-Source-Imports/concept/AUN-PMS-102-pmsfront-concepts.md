# Item

## id
CON-FND-57D59518A84D98

## label
Plastic (resin) sections, ultrathin and heavy-metal contrasted, are used for electron microscopy

## canonical_key
histology.specimen-prep.plastic-sections-for-em

## aliases
Plastic embedding for EM
Resin sections electron microscopy

## arabic_label
تستخدم المقاطع البلاستيكية للفحص بالمجهر الإلكتروني

## arabic_aliases
مقاطع بلاستيكية

## definition
Tissue destined for electron microscopy is embedded in plastic (epoxy or acrylic resin) rather than paraffin, because the resin can be cut into ultrathin sections roughly 60 to 90 nanometres thick, thin enough to let an electron beam pass through. The tissue is first fixed in glutaraldehyde and then post-fixed in osmium tetroxide, which binds heavy metal to membranes and lipid so the section scatters electrons unevenly and produces contrast on the detector. Paraffin sections, by contrast, are cut at 4 to 8 micrometres, an order of magnitude too thick for the electron beam to penetrate usefully.

## explicit_objective
State that plastic (resin) embedding, not paraffin, is the specimen preparation required for electron microscopy, and explain why.

## pitfalls
Assuming any well-fixed tissue block will do for EM regardless of the embedding medium. It is specifically the plastic embedding's ability to be cut ultrathin, combined with heavy-metal post-fixation for contrast, that makes a preparation usable under the electron beam.

# Item

## id
CON-FND-168FA6F0EC6DE9

## label
The electron microscope forms its image from a beam of electrons transmitted through the tissue

## canonical_key
microscopy.electron-microscope.electron-beam-principle

## aliases
Electron microscope principle
Transmission electron microscopy

## arabic_label
يعتمد المجهر الإلكتروني على مرور حزمة إلكترونات خلال النسيج

## arabic_aliases
المجهر الإلكتروني

## definition
The electron microscope forms its image by passing a focused beam of electrons through an ultrathin, heavy-metal-stained section and using electromagnetic lenses to focus the transmitted and scattered electrons onto a viewing screen or digital detector. Because electrons have a far shorter wavelength than visible light, this beam-based principle gives a resolving power thousands of times greater than the light microscope, letting it resolve organelle ultrastructure such as membranes and cristae. The specimen's ultrathin plastic section and heavy-metal contrast exist specifically to let this electron beam pass through and scatter usefully.

## explicit_objective
State that the electron microscope's image is formed by a beam of electrons transmitted through the tissue, and relate this to its higher resolving power than the light microscope.

## pitfalls
Confusing the electron microscope's electron-beam principle with the light microscope's visible-light principle, or assuming resolution differences are simply a matter of better lenses rather than the shorter wavelength of the electron beam itself.

CON-FND-7AE930E2CF1EEC

## label
Smooth endoplasmic reticulum synthesises lipids and detoxifies harmful substances

## canonical_key
cytology.smooth-er.lipid-synthesis-and-detoxification

## aliases
SER function
Smooth ER detoxification

## arabic_label
تقوم الشبكة الإندوبلازمية الملساء بتصنيع الدهون وإزالة السموم

## arabic_aliases
الشبكة الإندوبلازمية الملساء

## definition
Smooth endoplasmic reticulum is a ribosome-free system of tubules, continuous with the rough ER, that carries out lipid and steroid synthesis and the enzymatic detoxification of drugs and other harmful substances -- work that is especially prominent in liver cells. Because it carries no attached ribosomes it is identified on electron micrographs as an acidophilic, tubular network distinct from the ribosome-studded rough ER, and told apart from it by that ribosome presence or absence rather than by shape alone. These two roles, lipid synthesis and detoxification, are the organelle's defining functions, in contrast to the rough ER's role in protein synthesis.

## explicit_objective
State that smooth endoplasmic reticulum synthesises lipids and steroids and detoxifies harmful substances, and distinguish this from the rough ER's protein-synthesis role.

## pitfalls
Assuming any endoplasmic reticulum synthesises protein. Only the ribosome-studded rough ER does; the ribosome-free smooth ER's role is lipid synthesis and detoxification instead.

## label
Secretory transport runs from the rough ER through the Golgi to the lysosome

## canonical_key
cytology.secretory-pathway.rer-golgi-lysosome

## aliases
Rough ER Golgi lysosome pathway
Secretory pathway sequence

## arabic_label
يبدأ المسار الإفرازي من الشبكة الإندوبلازمية الخشنة إلى جهاز جولجي ثم الليسوسوم

## arabic_aliases
المسار الإفرازي

## definition
Newly synthesised protein begins on ribosomes attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum, which packages it into transfer vesicles that travel to the Golgi apparatus's entry (cis) face. The Golgi concentrates, chemically modifies and sorts the protein as it moves toward the trans (exit) face, from which it buds off as a primary lysosome or another vesicle type. This rough-ER-to-Golgi-to-lysosome sequence is the classic secretory/lysosomal transport pathway linking protein synthesis to its final packaged destination, and it runs in that direction only, not in reverse.

## explicit_objective
Sequence the secretory transport pathway from the rough ER through the Golgi to the lysosome.

## pitfalls
Reversing the pathway's direction, or starting it from the Golgi or lysosome rather than from the rough ER where protein synthesis begins.

## label
The three fundamental anatomical planes are sagittal, coronal and transverse

## canonical_key
anatomy.body-planes.coronal-transverse-sagittal

## aliases
Anatomical planes
Coronal plane
Transverse plane

## arabic_label
المستويات التشريحية الأساسية الثلاثة هي السهمي والإكليلي والعرضي

## arabic_aliases
المستويات التشريحية

## definition
Anatomical description uses three fundamental planes. A sagittal plane runs vertically front-to-back and divides the body into right and left parts, with the mid-sagittal plane splitting it exactly into equal halves along the midline. A coronal (frontal) plane runs vertically at a right angle to the sagittal plane, dividing the body into anterior (front) and posterior (back) parts. A transverse (horizontal, axial) plane runs at a right angle to the body's long axis, dividing it into upper (superior) and lower (inferior) parts, and is the plane most familiar from cross-sectional imaging such as CT.

## explicit_objective
Name the three fundamental anatomical planes and state which pair of body parts each one divides.

## pitfalls
Mixing up the coronal plane's anterior/posterior division with the transverse plane's upper/lower division, or assuming any vertical plane other than the true mid-sagittal one splits the body into exactly equal halves.

## label
Medial describes a structure nearer the median plane than another

## canonical_key
anatomy.terms-of-relation.medial-lateral

## aliases

## arabic_label
الإنسي هو ما كان أقرب إلى المستوى المتوسط من غيره

## arabic_aliases
إنسي، وحشي

## definition
Anatomical terms of relation describe one structure's position relative to another rather than an absolute location. A structure is medial when it lies nearer to the body's median (mid-sagittal) plane than the structure it is being compared with, and lateral when it lies farther from that plane. These paired terms are used constantly in gross anatomy to describe the relative side-to-side position of two structures, independent of any absolute measurement.

## explicit_objective
Define medial as nearer to the median plane, and distinguish it from lateral, superior and distal as terms of anatomical relation.

## pitfalls
Treating medial and lateral as absolute positions rather than relative comparisons between two named structures, or confusing them with the vertical (superior/inferior) or limb-length (proximal/distal) axes.

## label
The patella, the largest sesamoid bone, is the knee cap

## canonical_key
skeleton.patella.sesamoid-bone-of-knee

## aliases
Knee cap
Patella sesamoid bone

## arabic_label
الرضفة هي أكبر عظمة سمسمانية وتُعرف بغطاء الركبة

## arabic_aliases
الرضفة، غطاء الركبة

## definition
The patella, commonly called the knee cap, is a sesamoid bone -- a bone that develops within a tendon rather than articulating directly between two long bones -- lying within the tendon of the quadriceps femoris muscle. It sits anterior to the knee joint and glides within the femoral trochlear groove as the knee flexes and extends, protecting the joint and improving the mechanical leverage of the quadriceps tendon. It is the largest sesamoid bone in the human body, which is why it is the standard teaching example of that bone category.

## explicit_objective
Identify the patella as the sesamoid bone commonly known as the knee cap, and state its location within the quadriceps tendon.

## pitfalls
Confusing the patella (a sesamoid bone) with a long bone of the lower limb, or forgetting that a sesamoid bone by definition develops within a tendon rather than forming a joint between two other bones directly.
