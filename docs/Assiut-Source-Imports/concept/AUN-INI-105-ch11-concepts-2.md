## label
Trichostrongylus colubriformis infects by ingestion of its infective (filariform) larva, unlike the skin-penetrating nematodes

## canonical_key
trichostrongylus-colubriformis.mode-of-infection.ingestion-of-filariform-larva

## aliases
Trichostrongylus infection route
Ingestion vs skin-penetration nematodes

## arabic_label
عدوى الترايكوسترونجيلس بابتلاع اليرقة الخيطية

## arabic_aliases


## definition
Trichostrongylus colubriformis infects the human host when the infective filariform (third-stage) larva, present on contaminated vegetation, is ingested -- unlike Ancylostoma duodenale, Strongyloides stercoralis and Schistosoma mansoni, whose portal of entry is instead the skin, penetrated directly by their own infective larvae. This oral route is the standard basis for identifying Trichostrongylus as the exception whenever a list of nematodes is tested for skin portal of entry.

## explicit_objective
State that Trichostrongylus colubriformis infects man by ingestion of the filariform larva, unlike the skin-penetrating Ancylostoma duodenale, Strongyloides stercoralis and Schistosoma mansoni.

## pitfalls
Assuming every soil-transmitted helminth infects by skin penetration; Trichostrongylus is the recurring exception, infecting orally like Ascaris and Trichuris rather than percutaneously like hookworm and Strongyloides.

State that Ancylostoma duodenale inhabits the small intestine, not the gall bladder or large intestine.

Reading the species name "duodenale" as meaning the worm is confined to the duodenum only, rather than the small intestine broadly.

Ancylostoma duodenale and Necator americanus differ chiefly in geographical distribution

## arabic_label

## definition
Ancylostoma duodenale (the "Old World" hookworm) and Necator americanus (the "New World" hookworm) are both hookworms causing the same disease (ancylostomiasis/necatoriasis) by the same general mechanism, but they differ chiefly in geographical distribution: A. duodenale predominates in the Mediterranean, Middle East, North Africa and parts of Asia, while N. americanus predominates in the Americas, sub-Saharan Africa and parts of Asia -- their habitat (small intestine), general life cycle and treatment are essentially the same, so distribution is the standard distinguishing point tested between the two species.

## explicit_objective
State that Ancylostoma duodenale and Necator americanus differ chiefly in geographical distribution, not habitat, life cycle or treatment.

## pitfalls
Assuming the two hookworm species differ in habitat or treatment; the standard distinguishing teaching point between them is geography (Old World vs New World), plus minor morphological differences not tested here.

Ancylostoma duodenale infects man by skin penetration of its filariform larva, not by ingestion of eggs, and its adult female lays eggs that pass out with faeces in an immature (unsegmented, unembryonated) state -- not already infective. State that Ancylostoma duodenale infects by skin penetration and passes a thin-shelled, translucent, immature egg in faeces.

## pitfalls
Confusing hookworm's thin-shelled, translucent, immature egg with the thicker, mamillated, bile-stained Ascaris egg; assuming hookworm eggs are already infective or embryonated when passed.

## pitfalls
Assuming every helminth listed alongside pulmonary-migrating nematodes shares the same lung-passage life cycle; Hymenolepis nana's cycle is entirely intestinal.

## label
Visceral larva migrans follows ingestion of Toxocara (dog/cat ascarid) eggs, not human Ascaris or other nematode eggs

## canonical_key
visceral-larva-migrans.transmission.toxocara-dog-cat-ascarid-eggs

Toxocara egg ingestion

## arabic_label
انتقال الهجرة اليرقية الحشوية بابتلاع بيض التوكسوكارا

## arabic_aliases


## definition
Visceral larva migrans (VLM) follows ingestion of embryonated eggs of Toxocara canis or Toxocara cati -- the ascarid roundworms of dogs and cats -- not eggs of the human-specific Ascaris lumbricoides, Trichuris trichiura or Enterobius vermicularis. Because man is an accidental, aberrant (paratenic) host for Toxocara, the hatched larvae cannot complete their normal cycle and instead wander through the viscera (liver, lung, eye, CNS), producing eosinophilic granulomatous inflammation wherever they lodge.

## explicit_objective
State that visceral larva migrans is caused by ingestion of Toxocara (dog/cat ascarid) eggs, not human Ascaris, Trichuris or Enterobius eggs.

## pitfalls
Confusing visceral larva migrans (Toxocara, a zoonotic dog/cat parasite) with ordinary human ascariasis (Ascaris lumbricoides); the two share a superficial "roundworm egg ingestion" pattern but differ in host specificity and clinical picture.

## explicit_objective
State that Strongyloides stercoralis, unlike Ancylostoma duodenale, is capable of autoinfection, sheds rhabditiform larvae in fresh stool, and has a free-living generation.

## pitfalls
Assuming hookworm (Ancylostoma duodenale) shares any of these three features; all three are specific, testable distinguishing points unique to Strongyloides among the intestinal nematodes in this chapter.

## definition
The egg of Trichostrongylus colubriformis is larger than, but otherwise resembles, the egg of Ancylostoma duodenale (both are oval, thin-shelled and immature when passed) -- the key distinguishing feature tested is that the Trichostrongylus egg already contains a morula (an early cluster of several blastomeres/dividing cells) at the time it is passed, whereas the Ancylostoma egg is still at an earlier, less-developed cleavage stage.

## explicit_objective
State that the Trichostrongylus colubriformis egg is distinguished from the Ancylostoma egg by already showing a morula stage when passed.

## pitfalls
Assuming the oval, thin-shelled, immature features shared by both eggs are themselves the distinguishing point; the morula stage is the specific feature the bank tests as the difference.

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases


## definition
The filariform (infective, third-stage) larva of Strongyloides stercoralis differs from that of Ancylostoma duodenale in three linked morphological features, often tested together as an "all of the above" item: it is unsheathed (has shed the second-stage cuticle, unlike Ancylostoma's retained sheath); it has a distinctively notched (forked) tail tip; and it is shorter overall, at roughly 500-600 micrometres, compared with Ancylostoma's longer filariform larva.

## explicit_objective
State that the Strongyloides stercoralis filariform larva differs from Ancylostoma's by being unsheathed, notch-tailed and shorter.

## pitfalls
Reversing the sheathed/unsheathed assignment between Strongyloides and Ancylostoma filariform larvae, a frequently tested distinguishing pair.
