## title
M2 receptor activation on the heart decreases cAMP

## question
Activation of postsynaptic M2 receptors on the heart is associated with

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
decrease in cAMP formation

## explanation_a
Correct. M2 muscarinic receptors on the heart are Gi-protein coupled. Their activation inhibits adenylate cyclase, decreasing intracellular cAMP. This produces the receptor's negative chronotropic (heart-rate-slowing) effect.

## answer_b
opening of Na+/K+ cation channels

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Opening of Na+/K+ cation channels describes nicotinic (ligand-gated) receptor signalling, not the G-protein-coupled M2 muscarinic receptor.

## answer_c
activation of adenylyl cyclase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. M2 receptor activation inhibits, rather than activates, adenylyl cyclase -- activation of adenylyl cyclase is instead the pattern of a Gs-coupled receptor such as cardiac beta-1.

## answer_d
increase in IP3 and DAG

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Increased IP3 and DAG is the Gq-coupled M3 receptor's signalling pattern, not the Gi-coupled M2 receptor found in the heart.

## answer_e
inhibition of protein kinase C

## explanation_e
Incorrect. M2 receptor signalling does not work through protein kinase C at all -- that pathway belongs to Gq-coupled receptors like M3, not the Gi-coupled M2.

## question
Which of the following is correct regarding the autonomic nervous system (ANS)?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sympathetic postganglionic neurons release noradrenaline (NE), not ACh, at most effector organs -- ACh is used only at the sweat-gland and skeletal-muscle-vasodilator exceptions.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Correct. Every preganglionic autonomic fibre, sympathetic and parasympathetic alike, is cholinergic. So the neurotransmitter at the sympathetic ganglion (the preganglionic-to-postganglionic synapse) is acetylcholine. It acts there on nicotinic receptors.

The neurotransmitter at the parasympathetic ganglion is norepinephrine (NE).

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The neurotransmitter at the parasympathetic ganglion is acetylcholine, not norepinephrine -- all preganglionic autonomic fibres, including parasympathetic ones, are cholinergic.

## title
Why cellulitis spreads diffusely

## question
Which of the following is the cause of the diffuse nature of cellulitis?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Poor patient resistance

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Poor patient resistance is a general risk factor for infection severity, not the specific mechanism that makes cellulitis spread diffusely rather than stay localized.

## answer_b
Large doses of infective organisms

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Inoculum size affects whether infection takes hold, but it is not what determines the diffuse, unwalled-off spreading pattern specifically.

## answer_c
Production of fibrinolysin and hyaluronidase by the organism

## explanation_c
Correct. Streptococcus pyogenes, the organism typically causing cellulitis, secretes fibrinolysin, which breaks down the fibrin that would otherwise wall off the infection. It also secretes hyaluronidase, a spreading factor that breaks down connective-tissue ground substance. Together these let the infection spread diffusely instead of being localized.

## answer_d
Occurrence in loose tissues

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Loose tissue is where cellulitis characteristically occurs, but the tissue location is a site, not the causal mechanism of diffuse spread -- the organism's own enzymes are the cause.

## answer_e
Excess fibrin in the inflammatory exudate

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cellulitis is characterized by a lack of walling-off fibrin (because fibrinolysin breaks it down), not by excess fibrin -- excess fibrin would instead favour localization, as in an abscess.
