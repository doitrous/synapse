## title
Facultative anaerobe: standard definition

## question
A facultative anaerobic is?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Only grow anaerobically

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Growing only anaerobically describes an obligate anaerobe, which is harmed by oxygen -- the opposite of a facultative anaerobe, which tolerates and can use oxygen when present.

## answer_b
Only grow in the presence of O2

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Growing only in the presence of oxygen describes an obligate aerobe, which cannot grow at all without oxygen -- not a facultative organism, which can also grow without it.

## answer_c
Ordinarily an anaerobe but can grow with O2

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This reverses the standard definition's emphasis: a facultative anaerobe is ordinarily described as preferring aerobic respiration (for more efficient energy yield) while remaining ABLE to grow anaerobically, not the other way round.

## answer_d
Ordinarily an aerobe but can grow in absence of O2

## explanation_d
Correct. A facultative anaerobe is standardly defined as an organism that is ordinarily an aerobe -- preferring to use oxygen for more efficient aerobic respiration -- but that can also grow via fermentation in the absence of oxygen. This metabolic flexibility (Escherichia coli is a classic example) is what distinguishes it from both obligate aerobes (cannot grow without O2) and obligate anaerobes (cannot grow with O2).

## question

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The lag phase is a period of adaptation with no net division; metabolic activity is directed at accumulating enzymes for multiplication rather than being at its peak.

## explanation_b
Correct. The logarithmic (exponential) phase is when bacterial division occurs at its maximal rate, and this rapid, sustained multiplication requires the cell's metabolic machinery -- DNA replication, protein synthesis, energy production -- to be running at its highest, most active level. ## explanation_c
Incorrect. The stationary phase is a plateau, with new-cell formation balanced by cell death; overall population metabolic activity has fallen from its exponential-phase peak.

## answer_d

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The decline phase is marked by cell death exceeding formation; metabolic activity is winding down, not at a peak.

## title
New cells balance declining cells in the stationary phase

## question
During which phase of growth does the number of new cells balance the number of declining cells ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. In the lag phase there is no net cell division at all, so there are no new cells being formed to balance against dying ones; this is a distinct state from a true dividing-versus-dying balance.

## answer_b
Logarithmic

## explanation_b
Incorrect. In the logarithmic phase new-cell formation vastly outpaces any cell death, driving population number sharply upward -- not a balance, but a rapid net increase.

## answer_c
Stationary

## explanation_c
Correct. The stationary phase is defined by the number of newly formed cells equaling the number of cells dying, so the total population plateaus even though both division and death continue to occur. This balance -- not an absence of activity -- is what distinguishes the stationary phase's horizontal line on the growth curve from the lag phase's own horizontal line, where no division occurs at all.

## answer_d
Decline

## explanation_d
Incorrect. In the decline phase, dying cells outnumber newly formed ones, so the population falls rather than balances.

## title
Obligate anaerobe: best definition

## question
What’s the best definition of obligatory anaerobic bacteria ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Bacteria that can grow & multiply in complete absence of oxygen

## explanation_a
Correct. Obligate anaerobic bacteria are best defined as organisms that can grow and multiply in the complete absence of oxygen -- and, more specifically, that are typically harmed or killed by oxygen's presence because they lack the enzymes (catalase, superoxide dismutase) needed to detoxify the reactive oxygen species it generates. Obligate anaerobes typically achieve this by lacking catalase and superoxide dismutase, the enzymes an aerobic organism needs to detoxify oxygen exposure.

## answer_b
Bacteria that can’t grow or multiply in complete absence of oxygen

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This is the reverse of the correct definition: an organism that CANNOT grow without oxygen is an obligate AEROBE, not an obligate anaerobe.

## answer_c
Bacteria that have a lot of peroxidase enzyme

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Having abundant peroxidase would help detoxify reactive oxygen species, which is the opposite enzymatic profile from an obligate anaerobe, which characteristically lacks these ROS-defence enzymes.

## answer_d
Bacteria that lack coagulase enzyme

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Coagulase is a virulence-related enzyme (classically distinguishing Staphylococcus aureus from coagulase-negative staphylococci) unrelated to oxygen-requirement classification.

## answer_e
Bacteria that need minimal amount of oxygen for growth

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Needing only a minimal amount of oxygen for growth describes a microaerophile, not an obligate anaerobe, which needs none at all and is harmed by oxygen's presence.

## question
Only one of the following is true about bacterial growth curve ?

## subject
inf

## status

## vignette

## answer_a
Can be obtained by growing bacteria on solid media

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A growth curve is classically obtained by following a liquid (broth) culture's viable count or optical density over time, not by growing bacteria on solid media, which is used for isolating colonies rather than tracking a continuous population curve.

## answer_b
Decline phase is absent is some bacteria

Incorrect. The logarithmic phase correlates with the invasive period of disease, not the convalescent phase, which corresponds instead to the decline phase.

Correct. Although the stationary phase's population count is flat, this reflects a dynamic balance, not an absence of activity: cells are continuously dying at the same rate new cells are being formed, so the stationary phase genuinely is accompanied by ongoing bacterial death, just death that is exactly offset by division. The other four options each misstate a standard growth-curve fact, making E the sole correct statement.

Facultative anaerobes are characterized by one of the following ?

## question
Log phase is due to ?

## subject
inf

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Exhaustion of nutrients

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Exhaustion of nutrients is what drives a population OUT of the log phase and into the stationary/decline phases, not what causes the log phase itself.

## answer_b
Oxygen starvation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Oxygen starvation is not the defining cause of the log phase; the log phase reflects a period of ample resources allowing maximal, unrestricted division, not a resource limitation.

## answer_c
Accumulation of toxic substances

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Accumulation of toxic substances is associated with the transition out of exponential growth toward stationary/decline, not with the log phase's own defining cause.

## answer_d
Regular cell division at a maximal rate

## explanation_d
Correct. The log (exponential) phase is due to regular cell division proceeding at a maximal, constant rate, as long as nutrients and space remain abundant -- each cell divides at close to the same interval, producing the characteristic exponential rise in cell number that gives this phase its name. This is also the phase during which generation (doubling) time is calculated, since it is the only phase with a stable, measurable division rate.
