## title
The brain removes ammonia mainly by forming glutamine

## question
In the brain, the major mechanism for removal of ammonia is the formation of:

## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Asparagine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Asparagine is not the brain's ammonia-disposal product; that role belongs to glutamine, formed from glutamate and ammonia by glutamine synthetase.

## answer_b
Glutamine

## explanation_b
Correct. Ammonia travels out of the brain as glutamine (formed by glutamine synthetase condensing ammonia with glutamate) rather than as free ammonia itself, which would be directly neurotoxic; this is the major mechanism by which the brain removes ammonia, paralleling how muscle instead exports its nitrogen as alanine via the glucose-alanine cycle. When this mechanism is overwhelmed, as in liver failure with impaired ammonia clearance, glutamine accumulates in astrocytes and contributes to the osmotic brain oedema of hepatic encephalopathy. Glutaminase in the liver and kidney then hydrolyses this glutamine back to glutamate and ammonia for final disposal.

## answer_c
Glutamate

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Glutamate is the substrate glutamine synthetase acts on, not the final disposal form; it is glutamine, not glutamate, that carries the ammonia out of the brain.

## answer_d
Aspartate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Aspartate is not the brain's ammonia-carrying molecule; it instead donates a nitrogen atom directly into the hepatic urea cycle, a separate role from the brain's glutamine-based ammonia disposal.
