## title
Opportunistic parasites: self-limited in healthy hosts, severe in immunocompromised

## question
Opportunistic parasites?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Cause a self-limited infestation in healthy individuals but cause severe diseases to immunocompromised patients

## explanation_a
Correct. Opportunistic parasites typically cause only a mild, self-limited infestation in an immunocompetent host, but can cause severe, even life-threatening disease once the host becomes immunocompromised. This immune-status-dependent severity is the defining feature of an opportunistic parasite, distinguishing it from parasites that cause similar disease regardless of host immune status.

## answer_b
Live in the body of its host in another place than its normal one

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Living in an abnormal body site within the normal host describes an erratic parasite, not an opportunistic one.

## answer_c
Are artifacts which could be mistaken as parasite during examination,like some pollen grains or yeasts

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Being an artifact mistaken for a parasite (such as a pollen grain or free-living yeast) describes a pseudoparasite, not a true opportunistic parasite.

## answer_d
Establish themselves in a host which is not its ordinary one

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Establishing itself in a host species other than its ordinary one describes an incidental (accidental) parasite, not an opportunistic one.

## answer_e
Are none of the above

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Since option A correctly captures the defining, immune-status-dependent feature of an opportunistic parasite, "none of the above" is wrong.
