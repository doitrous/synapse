## title
Carcinoma's distant spread occurs early through lymph vessels

## question
In carcinoma distant spread occurs early through:

## subject

## answer_a
veins

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Veins carry blood-borne (haematogenous) spread, the route the department book attributes early and predominantly to sarcoma rather than to carcinoma.

## answer_b
lymph vessels

## explanation_b
Correct. Carcinoma, a malignant tumour of epithelium, characteristically undergoes distant spread early through lymph vessels, reaching regional lymph nodes before other routes -- the reverse of sarcoma, a malignant tumour of mesenchyme, which spreads early and predominantly by the bloodstream instead. This lymphatic-first pattern is one of the general behavioural features that distinguishes carcinoma from sarcoma in the department's own comparison teaching, alongside carcinoma's greater frequency and later age of onset.

## answer_c
arteries

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Arteries are rarely invaded by malignant cells at all, since their thick, elastic-rich wall resists tumour penetration, so they are not a route of early spread for either carcinoma or sarcoma.

## answer_d
capillaries

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Capillaries participate in haematogenous spread generally, but the printed key specifically identifies lymph vessels, not capillaries, as carcinoma's early distant-spread route.
