## title
Transduction: phage carrying host cell DNA

## question
Bacteriophage containing host cell DNA is involved in?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Mutation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Mutation is a permanent change in DNA sequence, unrelated to a phage particle physically carrying host DNA.

## answer_b
Conjugation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Conjugation transfers DNA through direct cell-to-cell contact via a sex pilus; it does not involve a bacteriophage carrying host DNA at all.

## answer_c
Transformation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Transformation transfers free (naked) DNA taken up directly by a competent recipient cell; it does not involve a bacteriophage as the carrier.

## answer_d
Transduction

## explanation_d
Correct. A bacteriophage that has come to contain host cell DNA -- accidentally or specifically packaged into a new phage particle during that phage's replication in a previous host -- is, by definition, a transducing phage, and delivering that host DNA into the next bacterium it infects is transduction. This is exactly the phage-mediated gene-transfer mechanism that distinguishes transduction from transformation's naked-DNA uptake and conjugation's direct cell contact.

## answer_e
Recombination

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Recombination is the general process of exchanging DNA segments between molecules; it can follow a transfer event but is not itself the name for a phage carrying host DNA.

## title
Lysogenic conversion: new properties from prophage genes

## question
In lysogenic conversion, new properties are conferred in the bacterial cells by products of?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Bacterial genes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The bacterium's own pre-existing genes are not what confer the new property in lysogenic conversion -- the new property specifically comes from genes the cell did not have before, carried by the newly integrated prophage.

## answer_b
Sex factor genes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sex factor (F plasmid) genes mediate conjugation, an entirely separate gene-transfer mechanism unrelated to a temperate phage's integrated genome.

## answer_c
Prophage genes

## explanation_c
Correct. Lysogenic conversion is, by definition, the process by which a temperate bacteriophage's genes -- once integrated into the bacterial chromosome as a prophage -- alter the host bacterium's phenotype, conferring new properties (such as new toxin-producing ability) that the cell did not have before lysogeny. This phage-gene origin is exactly what distinguishes lysogenic conversion from ordinary bacterial gene expression, plasmid-carried traits, or conjugation-related sex-factor genes.

## answer_d
Plasmids

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Plasmid-carried genes are a separate, non-integrating source of accessory traits; lysogenic conversion specifically names the phenotype change driven by a prophage's own genes.

## answer_e
All of the above

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Only prophage genes are the defining source of lysogenic conversion's new properties; bacterial genes, sex factor genes and plasmids are each a distinct mechanism, not jointly responsible for this specific process.

Prophage
