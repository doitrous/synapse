## title
Carcinoma in situ has not yet invaded the basement membrane

## question
An epithelial neoplasm exhibiting all the cellular features associated with malignancy, but which has not yet invaded through the epithelial basement membrane is called:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
carcinosarcoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Carcinosarcoma is a distinct, mixed malignant tumour containing both carcinomatous and sarcomatous elements, not the pre-invasive lesion this stem describes.

## answer_b
carcinoma in citue

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This option is simply a misspelling of carcinoma in situ, not a different named entity; the correctly spelled option is the one the printed key marks.

## answer_c
anaplastic

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Anaplastic describes a degree of poor cellular differentiation, not the specific pre-invasive-versus-invasive distinction this stem is testing.

## answer_d
carcinoma in situ

## explanation_d
Correct. Carcinoma in situ (CIS) represents a pre-invasive stage of carcinoma, showing full-thickness epithelial atypia (severe dysplasia) with all the cytological features of malignancy, but without invasion of the basement membrane. The basement membrane, not cytological appearance, is the feature that separates CIS from an actual invasive carcinoma, and once it is breached the CIS phase ends and a truly invasive malignant tumour begins.

## title
The spleen is not among the commonly affected metastatic sites

## question
Which of the following statements is not true about distant metastases:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Lungs and liver are common sites for metastases.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (as the false statement) -- this statement is true. The liver and lungs are among the department book's named common sites of haematogenous metastasis.

## answer_b
Histology of metastatic tumors usually resembles that of the primary tumor.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (as the false statement) -- this statement is true. A metastatic tumour's histology usually resembles its primary site of origin, which is how pathologists trace an unknown metastasis back to its source.

## answer_c
Omental metastases are commonly accompanied by hemorrhagic ascites

## explanation_c
Incorrect (as the false statement) -- this statement is true. Omental metastases commonly produce haemorrhagic ascites in the department's teaching.

## answer_d
Skeletal muscles and intestine are rarely affected by metastases.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (as the false statement) -- this statement is true. Skeletal muscle and intestine are explicitly listed as rare sites of metastasis, despite being richly vascular.

## answer_e
Spleen is among the most commonly affected sites.

## explanation_e
Correct (as the false statement). The department book grosses the common sites of haematogenous metastasis as liver, lungs, bone and brain, explicitly listing muscle, spleen, pancreas and intestine as RARE sites instead. So "spleen is among the most commonly affected sites" is the one statement here that is not true, despite the spleen's own rich blood supply.

## title
Carcinoma in situ is an intraepithelial carcinoma

## question
Which of the following statements is true for carcinoma in situ:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
it is mild degree of dysplasia.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Carcinoma in situ shows severe, not mild, epithelial dysplasia -- full-thickness cellular atypia with disturbed polarity.

## answer_b
It predisposes to the development of fibrosarcoma.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Carcinoma in situ, if untreated, progresses to invasive carcinoma of the same epithelial lineage, not to fibrosarcoma, a mesenchymal tumour of an entirely different lineage.

## answer_c
It is characterized by cellular atypia accompanied by basement membrane invasion.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Carcinoma in situ is defined precisely by cellular atypia WITHOUT basement membrane invasion; invasion of the basement membrane is what ends the CIS phase and marks the start of truly invasive carcinoma.

## answer_d
it is an intraepithelial carcinoma.

## explanation_d
Correct. Carcinoma in situ is an intraepithelial carcinoma: full-thickness epithelial atypia with all the cytological features of malignancy, confined entirely within the epithelium and its intact basement membrane. This intraepithelial confinement, not a mild degree of dysplasia, a predisposition to fibrosarcoma, or a grossly ulcerated appearance, is what defines it.

## answer_e
It may appear grossly as an irregular ulcer with everted edges.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. An irregular ulcer with everted edges describes the gross appearance of an invasive malignant ulcer, not the microscopic, pre-invasive entity carcinoma in situ.
