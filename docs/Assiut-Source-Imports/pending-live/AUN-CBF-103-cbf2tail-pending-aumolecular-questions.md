## title
Chargaff's rule fixes the guanine content from thymine

## question
In a DNA molecule the thymine concentration is 30%. The guanine concentration will be:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
30%

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 30% would equal adenine (A=T), not guanine.

## answer_b
10%

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 10% does not satisfy the base-pairing arithmetic for this composition.

## answer_c
40%

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 40% overcounts; it would leave no room for cytosine to equal guanine.

## answer_d
20%

## explanation_d
Correct. By Chargaff's rule in double-stranded DNA, A=T and G=C. If thymine is 30%, then adenine is also 30%, so A+T = 60% and the remaining 40% is shared equally by G and C. Therefore guanine = 20% (and cytosine = 20%).

DNA ligase connects the ends of two DNA chains

## question
The enzyme DNA ligase:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Unwinding the helix is the job of helicase, not ligase.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. RNA primers are made by primase, not ligase.

## answer_c
Introduces superhelical twists

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Introducing/relieving superhelical twists is the job of topoisomerases (gyrase), not ligase.

## answer_d
Connects the ends of two DNA chains

## explanation_d
Correct. DNA ligase seals the nick between adjacent DNA fragments by forming the phosphodiester bond that joins their ends. On the lagging strand it connects successive Okazaki fragments into a continuous strand. Joining DNA chain ends is its defining function.

## title
Okazaki fragments are formed during DNA synthesis

## question
The Okazaki fragment is related to:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Protein synthesis (translation) does not produce Okazaki fragments.

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. tRNA formation is a transcription/processing event, unrelated to Okazaki fragments.

## answer_c
mRNA formation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. mRNA formation is transcription; Okazaki fragments belong to replication.

## explanation_d
Correct. Okazaki fragments are the short stretches of DNA made discontinuously on the lagging strand during DNA synthesis (replication). Because the lagging-strand template runs 3'-to-5' toward the fork, it is copied in pieces that are later joined by DNA ligase. They are therefore a feature of DNA synthesis.

## title
One strand is synthesized continuously, the other discontinuously

## question
After formation of the replication fork:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Both new strands are synthesized discontinuously

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Only the lagging strand is discontinuous; the leading strand is continuous.

## answer_b
RNA primer is required only for the synthesis of one new strand

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Priming is needed on both strands (the leading strand needs at least an initial primer, and the lagging strand needs one per Okazaki fragment).

## answer_c
Both new strands are synthesized continuously

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Both strands are not continuous; the lagging strand is made in fragments.

## answer_d
One strand is synthesized continuously and the other discontinuously

## explanation_d
Correct. Because DNA polymerase synthesises only 5'-to-3', the strand matching the fork direction (leading strand) is made continuously, while the other (lagging strand) is made discontinuously as Okazaki fragments that are later joined by ligase. This semidiscontinuous pattern follows directly from the antiparallel template and polymerase directionality.

## title
DNA topoisomerase I relaxes positively supercoiled DNA

## question
DNA topoisomerase I catalyses:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Relaxation of negatively supercoiled DNA

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Relaxing negative supercoils is not the reaction the item credits to topoisomerase I here; the fork generates positive supercoils that must be relieved.

## answer_b
Relaxation of positively supercoiled DNA

## explanation_b
Correct. As helicase unwinds the duplex it drives positive supercoiling ahead of the replication fork; topoisomerase relieves this by transiently nicking and resealing the DNA, relaxing the positive supercoils so unwinding can continue. This supercoil relief is the enzyme's role at the fork. Preventing torsional strain lets replication proceed.

## answer_c
Conversion of negatively supercoiled DNA into positively supercoiled DNA

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Topoisomerase relaxes supercoiling; it does not convert negative supercoils into positive ones.

## answer_d
Conversion of the double helix into supercoiled DNA

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Topoisomerase relieves supercoiling rather than creating supercoiled DNA from a relaxed helix.
