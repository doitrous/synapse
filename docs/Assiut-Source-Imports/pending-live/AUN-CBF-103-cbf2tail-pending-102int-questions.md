## title
CK-MB isoenzyme is present mainly in heart muscle

## question
The creatine kinase MB (CK-MB) isoenzyme is present mainly in:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Brain

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Brain contains the CK-BB isoenzyme, not CK-MB.

## answer_b
Skeletal muscle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Skeletal muscle contains mainly CK-MM, with little CK-MB.

## answer_c
Kidney

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The kidney is not the characteristic tissue for CK-MB.

## answer_d
Heart muscle

## explanation_d
Correct. Creatine kinase is a dimer of M and B subunits, and the MB isoenzyme is found mainly in cardiac muscle. A rise in serum CK-MB is therefore used as a marker of myocardial injury (e.g. acute myocardial infarction). Its tissue specificity is the point being tested.

## title
Cutting the sympathetic supply to the skin causes loss of sweating

## question
Cutting the sympathetic supply to the skin results in:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Excessive sweating

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cutting the nerve removes the drive to sweat, so sweating decreases, not increases.

## answer_b
Vasoconstriction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Loss of sympathetic vasoconstrictor tone causes vasodilatation, not vasoconstriction.

## answer_c
Loss of sweating

## explanation_c
Correct. Sweat glands are supplied by sympathetic fibres that are cholinergic (they release acetylcholine). Cutting this sympathetic supply removes the secretomotor drive, so the denervated skin loses its ability to sweat (anhidrosis). This is why the answer is loss of sweating despite the fibres being sympathetic.

## answer_d
Increased salivary gland secretion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Salivary secretion is controlled by nerves to the salivary glands, not by the cutaneous sympathetic supply.

## answer_e
Piloerection

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Piloerection is produced by sympathetic activation of arrector pili; cutting the sympathetic supply abolishes it rather than causing it.

## title
Cutting the parasympathetic (vagal) supply to the thorax raises heart rate

## question
Cutting the parasympathetic supply to the thorax results in:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Increased heart rate

## explanation_a
Correct. The vagus carries the parasympathetic supply to the thoracic viscera and continuously slows the heart. Cutting it removes vagal (parasympathetic) tone, so the sinoatrial node runs faster and the heart rate increases. This is the resting tachycardia of vagal denervation.

## answer_b
Glycogenolysis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glycogenolysis is a metabolic effect of sympathetic/adrenaline action, not a result of thoracic vagal section.

## answer_c
Constipation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Constipation would follow loss of parasympathetic supply to the gut (abdominal), and even then reduced motility; it is not the thoracic effect asked about.

## answer_d
Pupillodilatation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pupillodilatation is an eye (head) effect of sympathetic activity or loss of parasympathetic input to the pupil, not of thoracic vagal section.

## answer_e
Vasodilatation of blood vessels

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Most blood vessels have no parasympathetic supply, so cutting the thoracic parasympathetic does not cause generalised vasodilatation.

## title
Sympathetic output to the bladder is noradrenergic

## question
You review the autonomic supply of various organs. Which of the following nervous outputs is noradrenergic?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Parasympathetic output to the heart

## explanation_a
Incorrect. All parasympathetic postganglionic fibres are cholinergic, so the parasympathetic supply to the heart is not noradrenergic.

## answer_b
Sympathetic output to sweat glands

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The sympathetic fibres to sweat glands are a special exception: they are cholinergic, not noradrenergic.

## answer_c
Parasympathetic output to the bronchi

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Parasympathetic output to the bronchi is cholinergic.

## answer_d
Sympathetic output to the bladder

## explanation_d
Correct. Most sympathetic postganglionic fibres release noradrenaline (they are adrenergic); the sympathetic output to the bladder is one such noradrenergic fibre. This contrasts with the cholinergic exceptions (sweat glands) and the cholinergic adrenal-medulla supply. So the sympathetic supply to the bladder is the noradrenergic answer.

## answer_e
Sympathetic output to the adrenals

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The adrenal medulla is innervated by preganglionic sympathetic fibres, which are cholinergic (acting on nicotinic receptors), not noradrenergic.
