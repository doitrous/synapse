Lipid soluble substance crosses cell membrane through the phospholipid bilayer

## question
A lipid soluble substance can pass the cell membrane through

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The phospholipid bilayer

## explanation_a
Correct. Simple diffusion moves a lipid-soluble substance passively down its concentration gradient. It crosses directly through the lipid bilayer itself, without needing a channel or carrier protein at all. The molecule's own lipid solubility is what lets it dissolve into and cross the membrane's phospholipid core.

## answer_b
Protein channels

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Protein channels are the route for ions and other charged or polar substances that cannot dissolve into the lipid bilayer, not for lipid-soluble molecules, which cross the bilayer directly instead.

## answer_c
Carrier protein

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Carrier proteins mediate facilitated diffusion and active transport of specific, usually larger or polar, molecules; a small lipid-soluble substance does not need a carrier since it can cross the bilayer directly.

## answer_d
Lipid carrier

## explanation_d
Incorrect. "Lipid carrier" is not the standard teaching mechanism for a lipid-soluble substance crossing the membrane -- the substance's own solubility in the bilayer is what lets it cross, without a distinct carrier molecule.
