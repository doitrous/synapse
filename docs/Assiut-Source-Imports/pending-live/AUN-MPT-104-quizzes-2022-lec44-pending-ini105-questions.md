Co-trimoxazole combines trimethoprim with sulfamethoxazole, not clavulanic acid

## question
All the following are true concerning co-trimoxazole EXCEPT:

## answer_a
It inhibits two sequential steps in the synthesis of tetrahydrofolic acid

## explanation_a
Incorrect (as the EXCEPT) -- this statement is true. Sulfamethoxazole and trimethoprim block two sequential steps of the same bacterial folate synthesis pathway, giving co-trimoxazole its synergistic effect.

## answer_b
It is the treatment of choice for dugs caused by pneumocystis cranii

## explanation_b
Incorrect (as the EXCEPT) -- this statement is true. Co-trimoxazole is the treatment of choice for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia.

## answer_c
It is a combination of Trimethoprim and clavulanic acid

## explanation_c
Correct (as the EXCEPT). Co-trimoxazole is a combination of trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole, not clavulanic acid -- clavulanic acid is instead a beta-lactamase inhibitor paired with amoxicillin (co-amoxiclav), an entirely different combination drug. This is the false statement among the options, since sulfamethoxazole, not clavulanic acid, is co-trimoxazole's actual second component.

## answer_d
It can cause rashes

## explanation_d
Incorrect (as the EXCEPT) -- this statement is true. Skin rashes, including severe hypersensitivity reactions, are a recognised co-trimoxazole adverse effect.
