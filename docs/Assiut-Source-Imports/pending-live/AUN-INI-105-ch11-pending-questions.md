## title
Adult Ascaris may be found in faeces

## question
Ova and Sometimes adult Parasites in faeces are found in?

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Fasciola hepatica

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fasciola hepatica is diagnosed by its egg on stool or duodenal-aspirate examination; the small adult fluke is not a stool finding.

## answer_b
Heterophyes heterophyes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Heterophyes heterophyes is diagnosed by its small egg on stool examination; the tiny adult fluke is not a stool finding.

## answer_c
Hymenolepis nana

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hymenolepis nana is diagnosed by eggs alone in stool; its small adult worm is not a practical stool finding.

## answer_d
Ascaris lumbricoides

## explanation_d
Correct. Ascaris lumbricoides adult worms may themselves be passed in stool or vomited and directly identified, in addition to the more usual egg finding -- the adult worm itself can be a diagnostic finding, unlike Ancylostomiasis, which by contrast is diagnosed in routine practice by finding eggs, since its adult worms remain firmly attached to the intestinal mucosa. The large size of the adult female (up to 20-35 cm) makes it easily recognisable when passed.

Enterobius causes nocturnal enuresis

## question
The commonest parasite causing nocturnal enuresis in children is?

## subject

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides does not classically cause perianal irritation or nocturnal enuresis; its symptoms relate to intestinal bulk or pulmonary migration.

## answer_b
Ancylostoma duodenale

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ancylostoma duodenale causes iron-deficiency anaemia and skin (ground itch) symptoms, not nocturnal enuresis.

## answer_c

This clinical picture is one of the most frequently tested associations for this species.

Cutaneous larva migrans resulting from exposure to the following parasite?

A

Ancylostoma caninum

Cutaneous larva migrans is an itchy, serpiginous, slowly advancing red track in the skin caused by an animal hookworm larva (classically Ancylostoma braziliense or A. caninum, dog/cat hookworms) that cannot penetrate past the human dermis. Necator americanus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Necator americanus is the other human hookworm; like A. duodenale, its larva completes normal human infection rather than causing cutaneous larva migrans.

## answer_d
None of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ancylostoma caninum (option A) is the correct cause, so 'none of the above' is wrong.

## question
Mode of transmission of Wuchereria bancrofti by female culex?

## subject

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Cyclodevelopmental

## explanation_a
Correct. Culex transmits Wuchereria bancrofti cyclodevelopmentally: the ingested microfilaria develops through successive larval stages (L1 to L3) within the mosquito to reach the infective filariform larva, but does not multiply in number along the way -- one ingested microfilaria yields, at most, one infective larva. This distinguishes cyclodevelopmental transmission from cyclopropagative transmission, in which the organism both develops and multiplies within the vector.

## answer_b
Cyclopropagative

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cyclopropagative transmission (development plus multiplication in the vector) describes organisms such as the malaria parasite in Anopheles, not the filarial larva in Culex, which develops without multiplying.

## answer_c
Mechanical

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mechanical transmission (no development within the vector, simple physical carriage) does not apply to filariasis, which requires larval development inside the mosquito.

## answer_d
Transovarian

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Transovarian transmission (passage of the organism to the vector's own offspring via the egg) is not how Wuchereria bancrofti is transmitted by Culex.
