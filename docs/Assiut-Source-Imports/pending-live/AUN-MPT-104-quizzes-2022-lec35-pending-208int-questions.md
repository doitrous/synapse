Moist gangrene lacks a line of demarcation

Moist gangrene is characterized by:

## answer_a
Line of demarcation is not present

## explanation_a
Correct. Dry gangrene, caused by arterial occlusion alone, develops a well-developed red line of demarcation and a later line of separation, since its slow, dry course allows a clear inflammatory boundary to form between gangrenous and viable tissue. Moist gangrene, caused by combined arterial and venous occlusion, spreads rapidly through blood-engorged tissue favouring bacterial putrefaction, so it does not develop this clear demarcation. Toxaemia is severe (not minimal), the general condition is markedly bad (not preserved), and putrefaction is extensive (not minimal) in moist gangrene -- the opposite of what the other three options claim.

## answer_b
Toxaemia is minimal

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Toxaemia is severe, not minimal, in moist gangrene, because rapid bacterial putrefaction in blood-engorged tissue releases substantial toxin.

## answer_c
General condition is not so bad

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The general condition is markedly bad, not preserved, in moist gangrene, reflecting its severe toxaemia and rapid tissue destruction.

## answer_d
Putrefaction is minimal

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Putrefaction is extensive, not minimal, in moist gangrene, since the blood-engorged tissue favours rapid bacterial growth.
