## title
Firm collagen-rich post-stab-wound nodule is keloid formation

## question
A 19-year-old man incurs a stab wound to the chest. The wound is treated in the emergency room. Two months later there is a firm, 3 x 2 cm nodular mass with intact overlying epithelium in the region of the wound. On examination the scar is firm, but not tender, with no erythema. This mass is excised and microscopically shows fibroblasts with abundant collagen. Which of the following mechanisms has most likely produced this series of events?

## answer_a
Keloid formation

## explanation_a
Correct. A firm, non-tender, non-erythematous nodule under intact epithelium, showing fibroblasts with abundant collagen on excision, matches keloid formation. Keloid is an exaggerated variant of normal repair in which fibroblasts lay down excess, disorganised collagen beyond the original wound margin. Its bland, painless, non-inflamed appearance distinguishes it from the other options here.

## answer_b
Foreign body response from suturing

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A foreign-body response to retained suture material would show macrophages and multinucleated giant cells around refractile foreign material on histology, not a picture of fibroblasts with abundant collagen alone.

## answer_c
Fistula formation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A fistula is an abnormal communicating tract between two epithelial or endothelial surfaces, not a solid, firm, collagen-rich nodule under intact skin.

## answer_d
Staphylococcal wound infection

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A staphylococcal wound infection would present with erythema, tenderness and typically purulent discharge, none of which this scar shows -- it is firm, non-tender and non-erythematous.
