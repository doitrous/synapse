Epstein-Barr virus is associated with nasopharyngeal carcinoma

## question
Epstein-Barr virus is associated with development of:

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Gastric carcinoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Gastric carcinoma's characteristic infectious association is H. pylori, not EBV, in this teaching.

## answer_b
Oesophageal carcinoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Oesophageal carcinoma is not the characteristic EBV-associated malignancy.

## answer_c
Laryngeal carcinoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Laryngeal carcinoma is not the characteristic EBV-associated malignancy; it is more strongly linked to smoking and alcohol.

## answer_d
Nasopharyngeal carcinoma

## explanation_d
Correct. Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) is causally implicated in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, along with Burkitt's lymphoma, other B-cell lymphomas and some cases of Hodgkin lymphoma. EBV either integrates a viral oncogene into host DNA or produces proteins that inactivate tumor suppressor gene products, and nasopharyngeal carcinoma is one of its best-established malignant associations, distinct from gastric, oesophageal or laryngeal carcinoma.

## title
Tumor suppressor genes prevent malignant transformation

## question
Genes that function in a manner that prevents malignant transformation:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Proto-oncognes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Proto-oncogenes are the normal, unmutated precursor genes that promote controlled cell growth; they do not themselves prevent malignant transformation, and become growth-promoting oncogenes only on gain-of-function mutation.

## answer_b
Oncogenes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Oncogenes actively promote autonomous cell growth once activated, the opposite of preventing malignant transformation.

## answer_c
Tumor suppressor genes

## explanation_c
Correct. Tumor suppressor genes -- "governors" such as RB, which restrain proliferation, and "guardians" such as TP53, which sense DNA damage -- function to prevent malignant transformation. Loss-of-function mutation of tumor suppressor genes removes this restraint, contributing to cancer development, in contrast to oncogenes, which actively drive it when activated.

## answer_d
Antiapoptotic genes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Antiapoptotic genes promote cell survival by blocking programmed cell death; overactivity can contribute to cancer, so they do not function to prevent malignant transformation.

## title
Oncogenes promote autonomous cell growth in cancer cells

## question
Genes that promote autonomous cell growth in cancer cells are called:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Tumor suppressor genes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Tumor suppressor genes restrain proliferation or sense DNA damage; they act to prevent, not promote, autonomous growth.

## answer_b
DNA repair genes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. DNA repair genes maintain genomic integrity by correcting DNA damage; their loss increases mutation accumulation, but they are not themselves the genes that actively drive autonomous growth.

## answer_c
Oncogenes

## explanation_c
Correct. Oncogenes are genes that promote autonomous cell growth in cancer cells, arising when a normal proto-oncogene acquires a gain-of-function mutation. This drives uncontrolled proliferation independent of normal growth signals, the opposite role from tumor suppressor genes, which instead restrain proliferation or sense DNA damage.

## answer_d
Antiapoptotic genes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Antiapoptotic genes block programmed cell death rather than directly promoting autonomous growth; this is a related but distinct mechanism from the one the printed key names.

Aflatoxin from Aspergillus flavus causes liver cancer

## question
Toxic metabolites produced by the fungus Aspergillus flavus contaminating food stuffs are involvement in development of which of the following cancers?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Uterine tumors

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Aflatoxin's carcinogenic target organ is the liver, not the uterus.

## answer_b
Liver cancer

## explanation_b
Correct. Aflatoxin, a chemical carcinogen produced by the fungus Aspergillus flavus contaminating grain and nut food stuffs, causes hepatocellular carcinoma (liver cancer). This is a food-contaminant, not a synthetic industrial, chemical carcinogen, and its liver-specific target distinguishes it from the other options here.

## answer_c
Bone tumors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Aflatoxin's carcinogenic target organ is the liver, not bone.

## answer_d
Skin cancer

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Aflatoxin's carcinogenic target organ is the liver, not skin; chronic UV exposure, not a fungal food contaminant, is the characteristic skin cancer risk factor.
