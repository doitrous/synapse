## title
Mycoplasma resistant to penicillin

## question
Mycoplasmas are bacterial cells that?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Fail to reproduce on artificial media

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Mycoplasma can be cultured on enriched artificial (cell-free) media, unlike obligate intracellular bacteria such as Chlamydia or Rickettsia.

## answer_b
Have a rigid cell wall

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Mycoplasma is genetically incapable of synthesising peptidoglycan and so permanently lacks a rigid cell wall -- its pleomorphic shape is a direct consequence.

## answer_c
Are resistant to penicillin

## explanation_c
Correct. Penicillin and other beta-lactams work by blocking peptidoglycan cross-linking in the bacterial cell wall. Mycoplasma has no peptidoglycan wall at all, so it has no target for the drug. It is therefore intrinsically resistant to penicillin, not by an acquired mechanism but simply because the drug's target is absent.

## answer_d
Stain well with Gram's stain

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gram staining depends on differential retention of crystal violet by a peptidoglycan cell wall -- with no cell wall, Mycoplasma does not stain reliably by Gram's method.

## title
Fungal cell wall major components

## question
Which statement regarding fungal cell walls is correct?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Major components are proteins as chitin, glucan, mannans

## explanation_a
Correct. The medically important fungi have a cell wall whose major components are chitin, glucan and mannan (mannoproteins). This is a mixture of structural polysaccharides and glycoproteins. It is chemically and structurally distinct from bacterial peptidoglycan, the basis of the bacterial cell wall.

## answer_b
Cell wall is not essential for fungal viability of survival

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The fungal cell wall is essential for viability -- it maintains cell shape and osmotic integrity, and disrupting it (as echinocandin antifungals do) is lethal to the fungus.

## answer_c
Ligands associated with cell wall mediate attachment to host cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect (in this context). While cell-wall-associated ligands can mediate host attachment for some fungi, the question is asking for the wall's actual chemical composition, which A names directly.

## answer_d
Cell wall components are the targets for the major classes of antifungal drugs as polyenes and azoles

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Polyenes and azoles target the fungal cell MEMBRANE (ergosterol), not the cell wall -- echinocandins are the antifungal class that targets the cell wall (beta-glucan synthase), so this option misattributes the wall as the target of the wrong drug classes.

## answer_e
Cell wall components rarely stimulate immune response

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Fungal cell wall components (such as beta-glucan and mannan) are recognized by host pattern-recognition receptors and do stimulate an immune response -- they do not "rarely" do so.
