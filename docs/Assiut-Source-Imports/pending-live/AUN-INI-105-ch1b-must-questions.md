# Item

## title
Impaired defence turns normal flora into opportunistic pathogens

## question
Normal flora cause infection when body defense is impaired is called?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Saprophytic

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A saprophyte lives on dead or decaying organic matter in the environment, not on or in a living host, so the term does not describe a normal-flora organism turning pathogenic in an immunocompromised patient.

## answer_b
Opportunistic pathogens

## explanation_b
Correct. Members of a person's own normal flora ordinarily coexist harmlessly with the host, but they can cause disease specifically when host defences are compromised, as in immunocompromised patients. This condition-dependent behaviour is exactly what defines an opportunistic pathogen, distinguishing it from a true (obligate) pathogen that frequently causes disease even in healthy subjects.

## answer_c
Endo-parasites

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "Endo-parasite" describes a parasite living inside the host's body, a parasitology term, not the standard microbiology term for a normal-flora organism that becomes pathogenic under compromised host defences.

## answer_d
pathogen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A true (obligate) pathogen typically causes disease in healthy subjects as its usual behaviour, unlike an opportunistic organism, which specifically requires a compromised host to become clinically significant.

## title
Molds feeding on dead matter are saprophytes

## question
The molds obtained nutrition from dead and decaying matter which are called ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Saprophytes

## explanation_a
Correct. Organisms, including moulds, that feed on dead animal or plant material and thereby decompose it are called saprophytes. Saprophytes are free-living decomposers, not organisms dependent on a living host. This decomposer role is the defining feature of a saprophyte.

## answer_b
Parasites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A parasite depends on a living host for nutrition, typically at the host's expense -- the reverse of feeding on dead, decaying matter.

## answer_c
Commensals

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A commensal lives in or on a living host without harming it, benefiting from that living association -- not from decomposing dead matter.

## answer_d
None of these

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Since "saprophytes" correctly names organisms feeding on dead and decaying matter, "none of these" is wrong.
