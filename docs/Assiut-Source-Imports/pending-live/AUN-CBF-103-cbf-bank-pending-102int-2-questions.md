## title
Homeostasis: receptor detects change and sends message to control center

## question
Regarding Homeostasis mechanism, which of the following detects change and sends a message to the control center:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Receptor

## explanation_a
Correct. In a homeostatic control system, the receptor is the component that detects the change, the stimulus, in the internal environment. It then sends that information onward as a message to the control centre. The control centre is what then coordinates the appropriate response, downstream of the receptor's own detecting role.

## answer_b
Stimulus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The stimulus is the change itself that occurs in the internal environment, not the component that detects it and reports it onward -- that detecting-and-reporting role belongs to the receptor.

## answer_c
Effector

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The effector is the component that carries out the response at the end of the control loop, not the component that first detects the change.

## answer_d
Response

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The response is the effector's output, the last step of the loop, not the detection step this question asks about.

## title
Positive feedback increases or speeds up the original change

## question
Regarding Homeostasis mechanism, which of the following Increases or speeds up original change

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Negative feedback

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Negative feedback drives a response that reverses or stops the original change, moving the variable back toward its original value, the opposite of amplifying it.

## answer_b
Positive feedback

## explanation_b
Correct. Positive feedback makes the response progressively larger, amplifying rather than reversing the original change. The body uses positive feedback in a few specific processes that need to accelerate to completion rapidly, such as blood clotting and uterine contractions during childbirth, rather than as the general mechanism of homeostatic regulation.

## answer_c
Involuntary control

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "Involuntary control" is not itself the term for the amplifying, self-accelerating loop this question describes -- that specific pattern is positive feedback.

## answer_d
Voluntary control

## explanation_d
Incorrect. "Voluntary control" describes control under conscious direction, not the automatic, self-accelerating amplification this question describes, which is positive feedback.

## title
Keeping the internal body environment constant is homeostasis

## question
keeping the internal body environment constant is the ……..:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Haemostasis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Haemostasis is the process of stopping bleeding (clot formation and vascular/platelet responses), a different word that sounds similar but names an unrelated process.

## answer_b
Homeostasis

## explanation_b
Correct. Homeostasis is the term for keeping the conditions of the internal environment constant. Almost all body systems work toward this goal, mainly through negative feedback control loops, and failure of homeostasis often leads to disease or death.

## answer_c
Haemolysis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Haemolysis is the destruction (rupture) of red blood cells, an unrelated process to the constancy of the internal environment.

## answer_d
Feedback

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Feedback is one of the mechanisms (alongside the receptor-control centre-effector loop) that homeostasis uses to achieve constancy, but it is not itself the name for the overall goal of keeping the internal environment constant -- that name is homeostasis.
