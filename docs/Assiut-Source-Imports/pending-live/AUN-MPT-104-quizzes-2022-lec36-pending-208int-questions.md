## title
Fosinopril can cause angioedema

## question
Which antihypertensive medication can cause the rare side effect of angioedema?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Prazosin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Prazosin, an alpha1-blocker, is not associated with ACE-mediated bradykinin accumulation and does not cause angioedema by this mechanism.

## answer_b
Fosinopril

## explanation_b
Correct. ACE inhibitors such as fosinopril decrease bradykinin degradation as well as reducing angiotensin II formation. The resulting bradykinin accumulation is why ACE inhibitors, unlike other antihypertensive classes, carry a recognised, if rare, risk of angioedema. Prazosin, propranolol and amlodipine act through entirely different mechanisms (alpha1 blockade, beta blockade, calcium channel blockade respectively) that do not raise bradykinin levels.

## answer_c
Propranolol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Propranolol, a beta-blocker, does not affect bradykinin metabolism and is not characteristically associated with angioedema.

## answer_d
Amlodipine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amlodipine, a calcium channel blocker, does not affect bradykinin metabolism and is not characteristically associated with angioedema.

## title
Lisinopril causes a persistent dry cough

## question
A 45-year-old man was started on therapy for hypertension and developed a persistent, dry cough. Which is most likely responsible for this side effect?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Nifedipine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Nifedipine, a calcium channel blocker, is not associated with ACE-mediated bradykinin accumulation and is not the characteristic cause of a persistent dry cough.

## answer_b
Lisinopril

## explanation_b
Correct. ACE inhibitors such as lisinopril decrease bradykinin degradation, and the resulting accumulation of bradykinin (and substance P) in the airways produces a persistent, dry, irritant cough in a substantial minority of patients -- the most common manifestation of ACE inhibitor intolerance. This mechanism-specific effect is not shared by nifedipine, losartan or atenolol.

## answer_c
Losartan

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Losartan, an ARB, acts downstream of ACE and does not inhibit bradykinin degradation, so it does not characteristically cause this cough (an advantage of ARBs over ACE inhibitors).

## answer_d
Atenolol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Atenolol, a beta-blocker, does not affect bradykinin metabolism and is not the characteristic cause of a persistent dry cough.
