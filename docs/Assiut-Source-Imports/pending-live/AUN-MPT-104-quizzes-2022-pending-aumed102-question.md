## title
Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor as an ionotropic receptor

## question
One of the following is an example of an ionotropic receptor:

## subject

## status

## owner

## correct_answer

## answer_a
Cholinergic muscarinic receptors

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cholinergic muscarinic receptors are G-protein coupled (metabotropic) receptors, not ionotropic -- they act through a G-protein and a second messenger, on a slower (seconds-scale) time course than a directly-linked ion channel.

## answer_b
Cholinergic nicotinic receptors

## explanation_b
Correct. Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors are ligand-gated ion channels, not G-protein-coupled receptors: the same receptor family sits at the skeletal-muscle motor end-plate (mediating neuromuscular transmission) and in the autonomic ganglia (mediating fast synaptic transmission between pre- and postganglionic neurons). Ligand binding directly opens an integral ion channel, producing a fast, brief depolarisation within milliseconds -- the defining feature of an ionotropic receptor, and distinct from the muscarinic receptors at parasympathetic effector organs, which are G-protein coupled instead.

## answer_c
beta-adrenergic receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Beta-adrenergic receptors are G-protein coupled receptors, not ionotropic -- catecholamine binding activates a G-protein and a second-messenger cascade (via adenylyl cyclase/cAMP), not a directly-linked ion channel.

## answer_d
alpha-adrenergic receptors

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Alpha-adrenergic receptors are also G-protein coupled receptors, not ionotropic, for the same reason as the beta-adrenergic option: catecholamine binding works through a G-protein and second messenger, not a directly-gated channel.
