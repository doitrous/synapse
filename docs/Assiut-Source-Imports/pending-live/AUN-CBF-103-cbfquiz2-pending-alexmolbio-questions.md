## title
Amanita mushroom toxin and RNA polymerase II

## question
A child returns from a mushroom hunt in Northern Michigan, begins vomiting, and by the next morning is hospitalized with jaundice, hyperammonemia, and delirium. A toxicologist is called, who suggests poisoning from Amanita phylloides, a mushroom containing the toxin alpha-amanitin, which inhibits RNA polymerase II. She suggests that the child's liver failure is most likely due to inhibition of the synthesis of which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Large rRNA synthesis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Large ribosomal RNA (28S, 18S, 5.8S) is synthesised by RNA polymerase I, not RNA polymerase II, so alpha-amanitin's inhibition of Pol II would not directly explain a loss of large rRNA synthesis.

## answer_b
Small RNA and large rRNA synthesis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Large rRNA is made by Pol I; small nuclear RNAs are made partly by Pol II and partly by Pol III, so this option does not correctly isolate the Pol II-specific product.

## answer_c
mRNA

## explanation_c
Correct. Each eukaryotic RNA polymerase transcribes its own class of genes: RNA polymerase I makes the large ribosomal RNAs, RNA polymerase II makes messenger RNA (and some small nuclear RNAs), and RNA polymerase III makes tRNA and the small 5S rRNA. Alpha-amanitin is a potent, relatively selective inhibitor of RNA polymerase II, so poisoning with it collapses mRNA synthesis specifically, halting the production of new proteins genome-wide. Because the liver is a highly synthetically active organ constantly replacing its proteome, this abrupt loss of mRNA (and downstream protein) synthesis is exactly what drives the fulminant hepatic failure characteristic of Amanita phylloides poisoning.

## answer_d
tRNA and protein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. tRNA is made by RNA polymerase III, not RNA polymerase II, so alpha-amanitin's Pol II inhibition does not directly explain a loss of tRNA synthesis (though protein synthesis is affected indirectly, through the loss of new mRNA).

## answer_e
Mitochondrial RNA synthesis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Mitochondrial RNA is synthesised by a separate, mitochondria-specific RNA polymerase encoded by the nuclear genome, distinct from the three nuclear RNA polymerases (I, II, III) and not the target alpha-amanitin's Pol II inhibition acts on.

## title
Removal of introns from mRNA precursors

## question
Which one of the following best describes the process of removal of introns from mRNA precursors?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
RNA catalyzed in the absence of protein

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Splicing is carried out by spliceosomes, ribonucleoprotein complexes that combine small nuclear RNAs with numerous associated proteins; it is not an RNA-alone reaction free of protein.

## answer_b
Requires no enzymes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Splicing very much requires a dedicated enzymatic/catalytic machinery (the spliceosome); it does not proceed without enzymes.

## answer_c
Controlled by RNA polymerase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. RNA polymerase's job is transcription, synthesising the pre-mRNA in the first place; it is not the machinery that subsequently removes introns from that transcript.

## answer_d
Carried out by spliceosomes

## explanation_d
Correct. Introns are removed from a pre-mRNA transcript, and the flanking exons joined together, by the spliceosome, a large ribonucleoprotein complex built from small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs, such as U1, U2, U4, U5, U6) bound to their associated proteins (small nuclear ribonucleoproteins, snRNPs). The spliceosome recognises specific sequences at the intron-exon boundaries, precisely excises each intron, and ligates the exons, producing the mature mRNA that then leaves the nucleus. This spliceosome-mediated mechanism is what makes possible the mRNA processing step of intron removal, distinct from the earlier transcription step carried out by RNA polymerase.

## answer_e
Regulated by RNA helicase

## explanation_e
Incorrect. RNA helicase unwinds RNA secondary structure and assists various RNA-processing steps generally, but it is not itself the machinery that carries out intron removal; that is the spliceosome's role.

## title
Deriving the RNA sequence from a DNA template strand

## question
The sequence of the template DNA strand is 5'-GATATCCATTAGTGAC-3'. Which one of the following is the sequence of the RNA produced?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
5'-GTCACTAATGGATATC-3'

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This sequence uses thymine (T) instead of uracil (U), so it cannot be the RNA product; it also is not the correct base-pairing complement of the given template.

## answer_b
5'-CTATAGGTAATCACTG-3'

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This sequence also uses thymine (T) instead of uracil (U), so it cannot be an RNA sequence at all.

## answer_c
5'-CUAUAGGUAAUCACUG-3'

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This sequence correctly uses uracil, but its base order is not the antiparallel complement of the given template strand read in the correct 5' to 3' direction; it looks like a coding-strand-style sequence written the wrong way round rather than the actual RNA transcript.

## answer_d
5'-GUCACUAAUGGAUAUC-3'

## explanation_d
Correct. RNA polymerase reads the template strand 3' to 5' and synthesises RNA 5' to 3', with each RNA base complementary to the template base opposite it (A pairs with U, T pairs with A, G pairs with C, C pairs with G). Complementing the given template (5'-GATATCCATTAGTGAC-3') base by base and then expressing the result in the 5' to 3' direction of the newly made RNA strand gives 5'-GUCACUAAUGGAUAUC-3', which matches this option exactly. This antiparallel, complementary, thymine-to-uracil-substituted relationship between template and transcript is the basic rule of transcription that this question is testing.

## answer_e
5'-CAGUGAUUACCUAUAG-3'

## explanation_e
Incorrect. This sequence uses uracil correctly but its base order does not match the antiparallel complement of the given template strand.
