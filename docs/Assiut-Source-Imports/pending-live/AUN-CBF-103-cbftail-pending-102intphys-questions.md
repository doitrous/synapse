## title
The somatic motor pathway is a single-neuron system

## question
Regarding the somatic nervous system, which of the following is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It controls the functions of skeletal muscle

## explanation_a
Correct. The somatic motor pathway from the spinal cord to skeletal muscle is a single-neuron (one-neuron) system, in contrast to the two-neuron (preganglionic then postganglionic) autonomic efferent pathway; its effector organ is specifically skeletal muscle, which it controls under voluntary command. This single-neuron arrangement, with no intervening ganglion, is part of why somatic reflexes and voluntary movements are faster than autonomic responses to the same stimulus. The autonomic system's extra synaptic relay, by contrast, buys it the ability to modulate its output pharmacologically at the ganglion, a site the somatic pathway has no equivalent of.

## answer_b
Its effect is rapid and intense

## explanation_b
Incorrect. While somatic conduction is fast, describing the effect simply as "rapid and intense" does not capture what distinguishes the somatic system -- its defining feature tested here is the single-neuron pathway to skeletal muscle, not a vague speed/intensity claim.

## answer_c
It controls the functions of plain muscle, exocrine glands and cardiac muscle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Control of plain (smooth) muscle, exocrine glands and cardiac muscle is the autonomic nervous system's job, not the somatic system's, which is restricted to skeletal muscle.

## answer_d
It controls involuntary actions

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The somatic nervous system controls voluntary, not involuntary, actions; involuntary control of visceral effectors is the autonomic nervous system's role.
