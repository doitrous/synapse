## title
Chloramphenicol's limited use owes to aplastic anaemia risk

## question
The main reason for limited use of chloramphenicol is the high incidence of:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Nephrotoxicity

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Nephrotoxicity is not chloramphenicol's dose-limiting concern; it is more characteristic of aminoglycosides.

## answer_b
Hepatic toxicity

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hepatic toxicity is not the main reason chloramphenicol's use is limited.

## answer_c
Aplastic Aneamia

## explanation_c
Correct. Chloramphenicol inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 50S ribosomal subunit, but its most serious clinical drawback -- the main reason its use is now limited -- is bone marrow suppression, including a rare but often fatal, idiosyncratic form of aplastic anaemia. This risk, not nephrotoxicity, hepatic toxicity or ototoxicity, is why chloramphenicol is reserved for situations where no safer alternative exists.

## answer_d
Ototoxicity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ototoxicity is characteristic of aminoglycosides, not chloramphenicol's dose-limiting concern.

## title
Aminoglycosides misread mRNA by binding the 30S subunit

## question
Aminoglycosides inhibit bacterial protein synthesis by:

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Binding to 30S ribosome leading to inhibition of Trna

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Aminoglycosides bind the 30S subunit, correctly, but they cause misreading of the mRNA code, not inhibition of tRNA itself.

## answer_b
Binding to 50S ribosome leading to inhibition of transpeptidation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Binding the 50S ribosome to inhibit transpeptidation describes chloramphenicol's mechanism, not the aminoglycoside 30S mechanism this question asks about.

## answer_c
Binding to 50S ribosome preventing translocation of peptide chain

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Binding the 50S ribosome to prevent peptide chain translocation describes macrolides' mechanism, not aminoglycosides.

## answer_d
Binding to 30S ribosome leading to misreading of mRNA code

## explanation_d
Correct. Aminoglycosides irreversibly bind the bacterial ribosome's 30S subunit, distorting it so that mRNA is misread and abnormal, non-functional proteins are produced -- a disruption severe enough to be bactericidal. This distinguishes aminoglycosides from 50S-targeting protein synthesis inhibitors such as chloramphenicol (transpeptidation inhibition) and macrolides (translocation blockade).
