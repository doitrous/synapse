## title
Why Hymenolepis nana is the commonest cestode

## question
Hymenolepis nana is the commonest cestode because of ?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Eggs pass immature

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hymenolepis nana's eggs are passed mature and directly infective, not immature -- this is part of what allows the direct life cycle, but it is not itself the reason the species is the commonest cestode.

## answer_b
Auto infection

## explanation_b
Correct. Hymenolepis nana's capacity for autoinfection -- the egg hatching and re-infecting the same host internally, without ever leaving the body -- lets a single infected person build up very heavy worm burdens and sustain transmission easily, making it the commonest cestode infection, especially in children. This is why H. nana infection, unlike the other cestodes in this chapter, can persist and intensify within a single untreated host.

## answer_c
inhabits the large intestine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hymenolepis nana inhabits the small intestine, not the large intestine, like the other intestinal cestodes of man.

## answer_d
The smallest tapeworm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Being the smallest intestinal tapeworm is a true but separate fact about H. nana; small size is not what drives its high prevalence, autoinfection is.

Undercooked beef containing cysticercous cellulose

Incorrect. Eating cysticercus cellulosae in undercooked beef is not how it is normally acquired (it is found in pork, not beef); this option also describes taeniasis by larval ingestion, not cysticercosis by egg ingestion.
