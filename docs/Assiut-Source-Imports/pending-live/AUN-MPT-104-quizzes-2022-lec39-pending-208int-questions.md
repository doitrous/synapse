## title
Histological grade describes degree of differentiation

## question
The histological grade of malignant neoplasm describes:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
its degree of differentiation

## explanation_a
Correct. Grading and staging measure two separate axes of a malignant tumour. Histological grade specifically scores a tumour's degree of differentiation, how closely its cells resemble the normal tissue they arose from, on a scale from well differentiated (grade I) to undifferentiated/anaplastic (grade IV). Its ability to spread is instead captured by TNM staging (tumour size, nodal involvement, distant metastasis), a distinct classification from grade.

## answer_b
its ability to spread

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The ability to spread is captured by TNM staging, not by histological grade.

## answer_c
Its effect on the host

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A tumour's effect on the host is a clinical consequence, not what histological grade itself measures.

## answer_d
Its rate of growth

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Rate of growth is not what histological grade specifically measures; grade measures degree of differentiation.

## title
TNM staging: T4 N1 M1

## question
A 48-year-old woman goes to her physician for a routine physical examination. A 4 cm diameter non-tender mass is palpated in her right breast. The mass appears fixed to the chest wall. Another 2 cm non-tender mass is palpable in the left axilla. A chest radiograph reveals multiple 0.5 to 2 cm nodules in both lungs. Which of the following classifications best indicates the stage of her disease?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
T2 N1 M0

## explanation_a
Incorrect. T2 understates the primary tumour's extent (fixation to the chest wall is a T4 feature), M0 misses the bilateral lung nodules (metastatic disease, M1).

## answer_b
T1 N1 M0

## explanation_b
Incorrect. T1 understates the primary tumour's size and chest-wall fixation, and M0 misses the bilateral lung nodules (M1).

## answer_c
T1 N0 M1

## explanation_c
Incorrect. T1 understates the primary tumour's size and chest-wall fixation, and N0 misses the palpable axillary node (N1).

## answer_d
T3 N0 M0

## explanation_d
Incorrect. T3 understates the tumour's chest-wall fixation (a T4 feature), N0 misses the axillary node, and M0 misses the lung nodules.

## answer_e
T4 N1 M1

## explanation_e
Correct. The TNM system stages cancer by primary tumour extent (T), regional lymph node involvement (N) and distant metastasis (M). A 4 cm breast mass fixed to the chest wall meets T4 criteria (chest-wall fixation, not merely size, drives this); a palpable 2 cm axillary node is regional nodal involvement, N1; and multiple bilateral lung nodules are distant metastatic disease, M1. Together these give T4 N1 M1, the most advanced stage among the five options.
