## title
Polymorphs are NOT a chronic inflammatory cell

## question
The following are NOT chronic inflammatory cells:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Macrophages

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Macrophages are one of the three named chronic-inflammatory cells -- they are the key cell of chronic inflammation, not the excluded one.

## answer_b
Plasma cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Plasma cells (antibody-secreting B-lymphocyte derivatives) are also a chronic-inflammatory cell, part of the same triad as macrophages and lymphocytes.

## answer_c
Lymphocytes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Lymphocytes are the third member of the chronic-inflammatory-cell triad, activated to secrete the cytokines that sustain the chronic response.

## answer_d
Polymorphs

## explanation_d
Correct. Polymorphs (neutrophils) are the hallmark cell of acute, not chronic, inflammation. Chronic inflammation is instead dominated by mononuclear cells -- macrophages, lymphocytes and plasma cells -- so polymorphs are specifically the one NOT counted among the chronic inflammatory cells here.

## title
Granuloma pathogenesis is a delayed hypersensitivity reaction

## question
Which of the following best explains the pathogenesis of granuloma?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Atopic reaction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An atopic reaction is an immediate, IgE-mediated (type I) hypersensitivity response, not the T-cell-driven mechanism that builds a granuloma.

## answer_b
Cytotoxic reaction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A cytotoxic reaction (type II hypersensitivity) is antibody-mediated destruction of a target cell, an entirely different mechanism from granuloma formation.

## answer_c
Delayed hypersensitivity reaction

## explanation_c
Correct. Granuloma pathogenesis is a delayed (type IV) hypersensitivity reaction: a macrophage presents a persistent antigen to T lymphocytes, and the lymphokines they release activate and aggregate macrophages into the epithelioid-cell granuloma. This T-cell-mediated, delayed mechanism is what best explains how a granuloma forms.

## answer_d
Anaphylactic reaction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Anaphylactic reactions are severe, immediate (type I) hypersensitivity responses, not the delayed, cell-mediated process underlying granuloma formation.

## title
Bilharzial granuloma depends on type IV hypersensitivity

## question
Bilharzial granuloma depend on:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Type III hypersensitivity

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Type III (immune-complex) hypersensitivity is antigen-antibody complex deposition causing tissue damage, not the mechanism that builds the bilharzial granuloma around a schistosome ovum.

## answer_b
Type IV hypersensitivity

## explanation_b
Correct. Like every granuloma, the bilharzial granuloma around a Schistosoma ovum forms through a type IV (delayed, T-cell-mediated) hypersensitivity reaction. Macrophages present ovum antigen to T lymphocytes, whose lymphokines activate and aggregate macrophages around the trapped egg. This is the same pathogenesis tested generically for granuloma formation, here applied to its bilharzial cause.

## answer_c
Type I hypersensitivity

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Type I (immediate, IgE-mediated) hypersensitivity underlies atopic and anaphylactic reactions, not the delayed, macrophage-driven granulomatous response to a schistosome ovum.

## answer_d
Type II hypersensitivity

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Type II (antibody-mediated cytotoxic) hypersensitivity destroys a targeted cell directly, an unrelated mechanism from the T-cell/macrophage pathway that forms a granuloma.
