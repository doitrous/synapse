## title
Sympathetic stimulation increases heart rate

## question
Stimulation of the sympathetic nervous system causes:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Broncho-constriction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sympathetic stimulation dilates the bronchi, not constricts them, as part of the general preparation for exertion; bronchoconstriction is instead a parasympathetic effect.

## answer_b
Increased heart rate

## explanation_b
Correct. Sympathetic discharge speeds and strengthens the heartbeat (positive chronotropy and inotropy), one of its cardinal effects in preparing the body for fight, flight or exertion, alongside dilating its own coronary vessels to meet the heart's increased oxygen demand. This is part of a coordinated pattern in which the sympathetic system simultaneously raises alertness, mobilises fuel and redirects blood flow toward the organs an emergency response needs most. The heart's increased rate and force are what let it deliver that redirected blood supply fast enough to be useful.

## answer_c
Increased gastric HCl and pepsin secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Increased gastric acid and pepsin secretion is a parasympathetic (vagal) effect; sympathetic stimulation instead inhibits gastrointestinal secretion and motility.

## answer_d
Decreased glycogenolysis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sympathetic stimulation increases, not decreases, glycogenolysis (via epinephrine and direct sympathetic effects on the liver), mobilising glucose for the fight-or-flight response.

## title
Sympathetic stimulation to the pelvis does not relax the vas deferens

## question
Stimulation of sympathetic outflow to the pelvis produces all of the following EXCEPT:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Retention of urine

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this is a TRUE sympathetic effect, not the exception). Sympathetic stimulation to the pelvis does cause retention of urine, by contracting the internal urethral sphincter and relaxing the bladder wall.

## answer_b
Vasoconstriction of blood vessels of the erectile tissue of the penis and clitoris

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this is a TRUE sympathetic effect). Sympathetic stimulation vasoconstricts the erectile tissue of the penis and clitoris, which is why sympathetic dominance (as in anxiety) impairs erection.

## answer_c
Inhibition of the plain muscle of the wall of the rectum and contraction of the internal anal sphincter

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this is a TRUE sympathetic effect). Sympathetic stimulation inhibits rectal wall muscle and contracts the internal anal sphincter, favouring retention (continence) over defecation.

## answer_d
Relaxation of the plain muscles of the vas deferens, seminal vesicles and prostate

## explanation_d
Correct -- this is the FALSE statement (the EXCEPT). Sympathetic fibres to the pelvis leave the cord as splanchnic nerves and mediate ejaculation specifically by CONTRACTING, not relaxing, the smooth muscle of the vas deferens, seminal vesicles and prostate, propelling their secretions into the urethra; "relaxation" of these structures is the opposite of what sympathetic stimulation actually does. This contraction is the emission phase of ejaculation, distinct from the parasympathetically driven vasodilatation that produces erection. Confusing the two phases, and which autonomic division drives each, is a common source of error on exactly this kind of question.

## title
All preganglionic autonomic fibres are cholinergic

## question
Regarding the parasympathetic nervous system, which of the following is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Its effector organ is skeletal muscle

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The parasympathetic system's effector organs are smooth muscle, cardiac muscle and glands, not skeletal muscle, which is innervated by the somatic motor system instead.

## answer_b
Its stimulation causes urine retention

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Parasympathetic stimulation causes bladder emptying (micturition) by contracting the detrusor and relaxing the internal sphincter, not urine retention, which is a sympathetic effect.

## answer_c
It has a vasodilator effect on skeletal muscle blood vessels during rest

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Skeletal muscle blood vessels at rest are under sympathetic (largely vasoconstrictor tone) control; the parasympathetic system does not supply skeletal muscle vasculature.

## answer_d
It is involved in stress and emergency conditions

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Stress and emergency (fight-or-flight) responses are the sympathetic system's role, not the parasympathetic system's, which instead favours "rest and digest" functions.

## answer_e
All its preganglionic fibres are cholinergic

## explanation_e
Correct. Autonomic fibres are named for the transmitter they release: cholinergic fibres are every preganglionic fibre of both divisions, every postganglionic parasympathetic fibre, and the two sympathetic exceptions (sweat glands and some vasodilator fibres); adrenergic fibres are the remaining postganglionic sympathetic fibres. So it is correct without qualification that all parasympathetic preganglionic fibres are cholinergic.

## title
Parasympathetic dominance dilates the vessels of external genitalia causing erection

## question
When a person is relaxed, which of the following occurs in the pelvic viscera?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pupilloconstriction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pupilloconstriction is indeed a parasympathetic effect (via cranial nerve III), but it is not a pelvic-viscera effect, which is specifically what this question asks about.

## answer_b
Contraction of the plain muscles of the vas deferens, seminal vesicles and prostate, causing ejaculation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Contraction of the vas deferens, seminal vesicles and prostate causing ejaculation is a sympathetic effect, not a parasympathetic one produced during relaxation.

## answer_c
Bronchoconstriction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bronchoconstriction is a parasympathetic (vagal, thoracic) effect, not a pelvic-viscera effect.

## answer_d
Dilatation of the vessels of the external genitalia, causing erection

## explanation_d
Correct. Parasympathetic outflow to the pelvis leaves the cord as the pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2-S4) and, in the relaxed state, produces vasodilatation of the vessels of the external genitalia, which is erection -- alongside its other pelvic functions of defecation and micturition. This vasodilatation is the direct opposite of the sympathetic vasoconstriction of the same vessels, which is why erection specifically requires parasympathetic dominance and is impaired when sympathetic tone (as in anxiety) predominates instead. The other pelvic parasympathetic functions, bladder emptying and defecation, follow the same relaxed, "rest and digest" pattern.

## answer_e
Retention of urine and stools

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Retention of urine and stools is a sympathetic effect (favouring continence); parasympathetic dominance instead favours micturition and defecation, the opposite of retention.

## title
Injury to S2-S4 abolishes erection

## question
A patient sustains an injury to the first four sacral segments of the spinal cord. What is the effect of this injury on him?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Lack of erection of the penis

## explanation_a
Correct. Parasympathetic outflow to the pelvis leaves the spinal cord specifically from the second, third and fourth sacral segments (S2-S4) as the pelvic splanchnic nerves; destroying these segments removes the parasympathetic supply that produces penile vasodilatation, so erection is lost. Micturition and defecation, which depend on the same sacral parasympathetic outflow, are typically disturbed as well after this kind of injury. This localisation -- S2-S4 specifically, not the thoracolumbar cord -- is what makes the sacral segments the ones to name when a lesion abolishes erection.

## answer_b
Hyperglycaemia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hyperglycaemia is driven by sympathetic (thoracolumbar) and adrenal medullary activity, not by the sacral parasympathetic outflow; an S2-S4 lesion would not directly cause hyperglycaemia.

## answer_c
Bronchoconstriction

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bronchoconstriction is mediated by the vagus (a cranial, not sacral, parasympathetic outflow to the thorax); an S2-S4 injury does not affect bronchial innervation.

## answer_d
Disturbance of sweat production

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sweating is a sympathetic (thoracolumbar, cholinergic-sympathetic) function, not a sacral parasympathetic one; an S2-S4 lesion would not primarily disturb sweat production.

## answer_e
Relaxation of the external anal and urethral sphincters

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Loss of the sacral parasympathetic supply impairs micturition/defecation and erection rather than relaxing the external sphincters, which are under somatic (pudendal nerve) voluntary control, not directly abolished by this lesion.

## question
Which of the following receptors is most likely to be affected by smoking?

## subject

## explanation_a

Alpha 2 receptors

## title
An alpha-adrenergic blocker produces pupillary constriction

## question
Pupil size is an important indicator of brainstem function. Which of the following results in pupillary constriction?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
A blocker of beta adrenergic receptors

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pupil dilation is mediated by alpha-adrenergic, not beta-adrenergic, receptors on the iris dilator muscle, so blocking beta receptors does not remove the mechanism responsible for dilation.

## answer_b
A blocker of muscarinic receptors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Blocking muscarinic receptors (as with atropine) removes parasympathetic constrictor tone, producing dilation (mydriasis), the opposite of constriction.

## answer_c
A blocker of nicotinic receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Nicotinic receptors act at the ganglia, not directly on the iris muscles; blocking them does not selectively produce pupillary constriction.

## answer_d
Emotional excitement

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Emotional excitement raises sympathetic tone, which dilates the pupil, not constricts it.

## answer_e
A blocker of alpha adrenergic receptors

## explanation_e
Correct. Sympathetic stimulation dilates the pupil by contracting the iris dilator muscle via alpha-adrenergic receptors; blocking those alpha-adrenergic receptors removes this dilating influence, leaving unopposed parasympathetic (muscarinic) constrictor tone and producing pupillary constriction (miosis). This logic of "remove one side of a push-pull pair and the other side wins" is the same reasoning that explains why a muscarinic blocker instead produces dilation. Pupil size is a useful brainstem sign precisely because it reflects this balance between sympathetic dilator and parasympathetic constrictor tone.

## title
Sickle cell anemia: the underlying mutation mechanism

## question
Sickle cell anemia is caused by a specific mutation in the gene for beta-globin, one of the two globin proteins that complex with heme to form hemoglobin. The sickle cell anemia mutation is known to produce a single amino acid change, glutamic acid to valine, at position 6 of the beta-globin peptide chain. Which of the following is the most likely mechanism for this mutation?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Single-base substitution

## explanation_a
Correct. Sickle cell anemia results from a single-base substitution in the beta-globin gene (GAG to GTG), which changes the sixth codon from one specifying glutamic acid to one specifying valine. A base substitution is the commonest type of gene mutation, and here it swaps a polar, negatively charged glutamate for a nonpolar valine at a surface position of the beta chain -- creating a hydrophobic "sticky patch" that drives haemoglobin S polymerisation under low oxygen tension, the basis of sickling.

## answer_b
Crossing over

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Crossing over is a meiotic recombination event between homologous chromosomes, not the mechanism that produces a single point substitution within one gene's coding sequence.

## answer_c
Single base insertion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An insertion would shift the reading frame downstream of the insertion (a frameshift), producing a very different, usually much more disruptive protein than the single defined amino acid substitution seen in sickle cell disease.

## answer_d
Three-base deletion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A three-base (single-codon) in-frame deletion would remove an entire amino acid rather than substituting one amino acid for another, which is not what is observed in sickle haemoglobin.

## answer_e
Two-base insertion

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A two-base insertion would cause a frameshift, scrambling every downstream codon, not the single, precise amino acid substitution characteristic of sickle cell disease.

## title
Sickle cell anemia as a textbook mutation type

## question
Sickle cell anemia is a well-studied example for which one of the following mutation types?

## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Deletion

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sickle cell anemia does not involve loss of any nucleotides; the beta-globin gene sequence is the same length, just one base substituted.

## answer_b
Missense

## explanation_b
Correct. A base substitution in a coding region is missense if it changes the amino acid specified. Sickle cell anemia's GAG-to-GTG substitution changes codon 6 from glutamate to valine -- a different amino acid is inserted, which is the definition of a missense mutation. This makes sickle cell anemia the textbook example of a missense mutation, contrasted with nonsense mutations (which create a premature stop codon, as in some thalassaemias) and silent mutations (which change the codon but not the amino acid).

## answer_c
Nonsense

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A nonsense mutation converts an amino acid codon into a stop codon, causing premature termination; the sickle cell substitution still codes for an amino acid (just a different one), so it is missense, not nonsense.

## answer_d
Frameshift

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A frameshift results from an insertion or deletion that is not a multiple of three, shifting the reading frame; sickle cell anemia is a single-base substitution that leaves the reading frame intact.

## answer_e
Insertion

## explanation_e
Incorrect. No extra nucleotide is added in the sickle cell mutation; it is a substitution, not an insertion.
