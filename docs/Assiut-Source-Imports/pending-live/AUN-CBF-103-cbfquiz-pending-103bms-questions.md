## title
Wernicke's disease and beriberi reversed by which vitamin

## question
Both Wernicke's disease and beriberi can be reversed by administering:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Pyridoxine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) deficiency produces a different clinical picture (convulsions via impaired GABA synthesis); it does not reverse Wernicke's disease or beriberi.

## answer_b
Vitamin B12

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Vitamin B12 deficiency produces megaloblastic anaemia and subacute combined degeneration, an unrelated clinical picture; it does not reverse Wernicke's disease or beriberi.

## answer_c
Thiamin

## explanation_c
Correct. Thiamine (vitamin B1), as its coenzyme thiamine pyrophosphate, is required for oxidative decarboxylation reactions central to glucose metabolism, and its deficiency underlies both Wernicke's disease (a CNS presentation) and beriberi (a cardiovascular/peripheral-nerve presentation). Administering thiamine reverses both, since both trace back to the same underlying deficiency.

## answer_d
Retinol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Retinol (vitamin A) deficiency produces night blindness and xerophthalmia; it does not reverse Wernicke's disease or beriberi.

## title
Riboflavin deficiency and angular stomatitis

## question
Riboflavin deficiency can cause:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Diarrhoea

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Diarrhoea is part of pellagra's classic triad (niacin deficiency), not a characteristic finding of riboflavin deficiency.

## answer_b
None of these

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the answer. Riboflavin deficiency does have a characteristic clinical finding among the options listed (angular stomatitis), so "none of these" is not correct.

## answer_c
Peripheral neuritis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Peripheral neuritis is more characteristic of thiamine (B1) or B6 deficiency than of riboflavin deficiency.

## answer_d
Angular stomatitis

## explanation_d
Correct. Riboflavin (vitamin B2) deficiency characteristically causes angular stomatitis -- cracking and inflammation at the corners of the mouth. It occurs alongside glossitis and corneal vascularisation as part of the same deficiency picture. All three reflect riboflavin's role, as FAD and FMN, in the metabolism of rapidly turning-over epithelial tissue.
