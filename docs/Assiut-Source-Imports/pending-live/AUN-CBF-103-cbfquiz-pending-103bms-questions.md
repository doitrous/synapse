## title
Wernicke's disease and beriberi reversed by which vitamin

## question
Both Wernicke's disease and beriberi can be reversed by administering:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Pyridoxine

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) deficiency produces a different clinical picture (convulsions via impaired GABA synthesis); it does not reverse Wernicke's disease or beriberi.

## answer_b
Vitamin B12

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Vitamin B12 deficiency produces megaloblastic anaemia and subacute combined degeneration, an unrelated clinical picture; it does not reverse Wernicke's disease or beriberi.

## answer_c
Thiamin

## explanation_c
Correct. Thiamine (vitamin B1), as its coenzyme thiamine pyrophosphate, is required for oxidative decarboxylation reactions central to glucose metabolism, and its deficiency underlies both Wernicke's disease (a CNS presentation) and beriberi (a cardiovascular/peripheral-nerve presentation). Administering thiamine reverses both, since both trace back to the same underlying deficiency.

## answer_d
Retinol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Retinol (vitamin A) deficiency produces night blindness and xerophthalmia; it does not reverse Wernicke's disease or beriberi.

The heart preferentially converts lactate into glucose

## explanation_e
Incorrect. It is the liver, not the heart, that preferentially converts lactate back into glucose; the heart instead tends to take up and oxidise lactate as a fuel.
