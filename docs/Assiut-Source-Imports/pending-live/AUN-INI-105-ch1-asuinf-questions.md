## title
Capsule protects against phagocytosis

## question
Outer covering in some bacteria that protects them from phagocytosis by host cells is?

## subject
inf

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Capsule

## explanation_a
Correct. The polysaccharide (or, in Bacillus anthracis, polypeptide) capsule that surrounds many bacteria is a major virulence factor precisely because it resists phagocytosis by host immune cells -- it physically masks surface molecules that phagocytes would otherwise recognise and bind. This antiphagocytic role, alongside its use in organism identification/serotyping and as a vaccine antigen (e.g. pneumococcal, meningococcal and Hib vaccines), is the capsule's defining function. It is an outer covering additional to, and distinct from, the cell wall beneath it.

## answer_b
Cell wall

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The cell wall gives the bacterium its shape and rigidity and is a site of action for some antibiotics; protecting the cell from phagocytosis is specifically the capsule's role, not the wall's.

## answer_c
Flagellum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The flagellum is the organelle of motility, propelling the cell through liquid; it plays no role in resisting phagocytosis.

## answer_d
Peptidoglycan

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peptidoglycan is the structural mesh within the cell wall that gives it rigidity; it is not the outer antiphagocytic covering being described here.

## question
A bacterial cell wall does all of the following EXCEPT?

## subject

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect (true statement). Peptidoglycan, the wall's structural mesh, is exactly what gives the bacterial cell its characteristic shape and rigidity.

## answer_b
Is the site of action for some antibiotics

## explanation_b
Incorrect (true statement). Beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins) and others act by inhibiting peptidoglycan synthesis, making the cell wall a genuine antibiotic target.

Protection from phagocytosis is specifically the capsule's job, not the cell wall's -- the wall instead provides shape/rigidity, is an antibiotic target, and (via its Gram-negative LPS component) contributes to disease symptoms, but resisting phagocytic engulfment is a separate, capsule-specific function.

## subject

Exotoxin properties: the exception

## question
All are true about exotoxins EXCEPT?

## subject
inf

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Coded by chromosome

## explanation_a
Correct. This is the false statement, so it is the EXCEPT answer. Many exotoxin genes are carried on plasmids or lysogenic bacteriophages rather than exclusively on the bacterial chromosome (e.g. diphtheria toxin is phage-encoded, and several enterotoxins are plasmid-encoded), so "coded by chromosome" is not a universal exotoxin property.

## answer_b
Formed of Protein

## explanation_b
Incorrect (true statement). Exotoxins are, by definition, secreted polypeptide/protein toxins.

## answer_c
Thermo labile

## explanation_c
Incorrect (true statement). Being proteins, exotoxins are heat labile -- their toxic activity is destroyed by heat, unlike heat-stable endotoxin.

## answer_d
Act on specific organs

## explanation_d
Incorrect (true statement). Exotoxins typically show specific action, targeting a particular tissue, cell type or receptor, unlike the broad, nonspecific effects of endotoxin.
