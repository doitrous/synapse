The liver is the site of ketogenesis

## question
Which of the following is a site of ketogenesis?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Kidney

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The kidney is a minor gluconeogenic organ and uses ketone bodies, but it is not the principal site of ketogenesis.

## answer_b
Skeletal muscle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Skeletal muscle oxidises ketone bodies for fuel; it does not synthesise them.

## answer_c
Liver

## explanation_c
Correct. Ketogenesis (synthesis of acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate from acetyl-CoA) occurs in liver mitochondria, because the liver has the HMG-CoA synthase and lyase needed. The liver exports ketone bodies for other tissues to use. It is the characteristic ketogenic organ.

Brain

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The brain uses ketone bodies during prolonged fasting but does not produce them.

## answer_e
Cardiac muscle

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Cardiac muscle readily oxidises ketone bodies but does not synthesise them.

## title
Thiophorase deficiency prevents ketone-body utilisation

## question
Absence of which of the following causes inability to use ketone bodies as an energy source (as in the liver, which normally lacks it)?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Thiophorase

## explanation_a
Correct. Thiophorase (succinyl-CoA:acetoacetate CoA transferase) activates acetoacetate to acetoacetyl-CoA in extrahepatic tissues, the essential first step of ketone-body utilisation. Its absence prevents a tissue from using ketone bodies; the liver normally lacks thiophorase, which is exactly why it cannot use the ketone bodies it exports. Incorrect. HMG-CoA lyase is needed to make ketone bodies (ketogenesis), not to use them.

## answer_d
Beta-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Beta-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase interconverts the two ketone bodies; its absence is not the classic cause of inability to use them.

## answer_e
Succinate dehydrogenase

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Succinate dehydrogenase is a TCA-cycle/respiratory-chain enzyme, unrelated to ketone-body activation.

## question
Insulin increases the activity of acetyl-CoA carboxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme of fatty acid biosynthesis. Which of the following best describes this regulatory enzyme?

fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Malonyl-CoA is the product of the enzyme, not its activator; the enzyme is activated by citrate and by dephosphorylation (insulin).

## answer_b

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Acetyl-CoA carboxylase makes malonyl-CoA from acetyl-CoA, not the reverse.

It catalyzes a reaction that requires biotin and ATP

Correct. Acetyl-CoA carboxylase catalyses the carboxylation of acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA, the committed and rate-limiting step of fatty acid synthesis. Incorrect. It is activated by citrate and dephosphorylation, not by carboxylation of the enzyme itself.

## answer_e
It catalyzes a reaction that condenses an acetyl group with a malonyl group

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Condensation of an acetyl group with a malonyl group is a fatty acid synthase reaction, not the carboxylase step.

Hydrolase
