## title
Secondary spermatocyte chromosome complement

## question
A secondary spermatocyte typically contains which of the following chromosomal arrangements?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
46 duplicated chromosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 46 duplicated chromosomes describes the primary spermatocyte, before the first meiotic division has halved the chromosome number.

## answer_b
46 single chromosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 46 single chromosomes does not correspond to any normal stage of spermatogenesis; the chromosome number is halved to 23 at the first meiotic division while the chromosomes are still duplicated.

## answer_c
23 single chromosomes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 23 single chromosomes describes the spermatid, produced only after the second meiotic division separates the sister chromatids; a secondary spermatocyte has not yet undergone that division.

## answer_d
23 duplicated chromosomes

## explanation_d
Correct. The first meiotic division separates homologous chromosomes (reductional), halving the chromosome number to 23 but leaving each chromosome still duplicated into two sister chromatids, so a secondary spermatocyte has 23 duplicated chromosomes (46 chromatids total) until the second meiotic division separates the chromatids. Only the second meiotic division, which separates the sister chromatids rather than the homologues, converts this into the genuinely single-chromatid spermatid.

## title
Phosphate group attaches to the 5' carbon of the pentose sugar

## question
In a standard nucleotide, the phosphate group is covalently joined to which specific carbon of the pentose sugar?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Carbon 2

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Carbon 2 of the pentose sugar (in deoxyribose) simply lacks a hydroxyl group compared with ribose; it is not the attachment site for the phosphate group.

## answer_b
Carbon 3

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Carbon 3 carries the free hydroxyl group that a neighbouring nucleotide's 5' phosphate links to during polymerization; it is not itself the phosphate's attachment site on the same nucleotide.

## answer_c
Carbon 4

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Carbon 4 is part of the sugar ring structure and helps position the 5' carbon exocyclically, but the phosphate group attaches at carbon 5, not carbon 4.

## answer_d
Carbon 5

## explanation_d
Correct. The phosphate group of a nucleotide is attached to the 5' carbon of the pentose sugar, and this 5'-phosphate is what forms the phosphodiester bond linking one nucleotide to the 3'-hydroxyl of the next, giving the nucleic acid strand its 5'-to-3' directionality. This 5' phosphate is also the reason a nucleic acid strand has an inherent 5'-to-3' directionality, running from a free 5' phosphate end to a free 3' hydroxyl end.

## title
Cyclin-dependent kinases as internal cell cycle signals

## question
Which of the following is considered one of the internal signals that tightly regulate progression through the cell cycle?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Growth factors

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Growth factors are external (extracellular) signals that influence whether a cell enters the cycle at all, not the internal molecular machinery that drives progression once the cycle is underway.

## answer_b
Cytokines

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cytokines are extracellular signalling molecules, typically involved in immune communication, not the internal regulatory machinery of the cell cycle itself.

## answer_c
Environmental stresses

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Environmental stresses are external factors that can trigger checkpoint arrest, but they are not themselves the internal regulatory signal that drives normal cycle progression.

## answer_d
Cyclin-dependent kinases

## explanation_d
Correct. Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), activated by binding their partner cyclins, are the internal molecular signal that phosphorylates target proteins and drives the cell past successive checkpoints through G1, S, G2 and M — the cell's own built-in progression machinery, not an external cue. Because different cyclins peak at different times, sequential cyclin-CDK complexes impose an ordered, one-way progression through the cycle's stages.

## title
One primary spermatocyte yields four sperm

## question
A single primary spermatocyte cell undergoes complete meiosis to produce how many individual sperm cells?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
One sperm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. One sperm would be the outcome only if meiosis discarded most of its products, as in oogenesis; spermatogenesis is symmetric at every division and produces more than a single sperm.

## answer_b
Two sperm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Two sperm would describe the yield of only the first meiotic division (two secondary spermatocytes); each of those two must still complete a second meiotic division, doubling the final yield.

## answer_c
Four sperm

## explanation_c
Correct. The first meiotic division of one primary spermatocyte yields two secondary spermatocytes, and each of those completes the second meiotic division to yield two spermatids apiece, for four spermatids (and ultimately four mature sperm) in total, since spermatogenesis discards no products at either division. Each of the four spermatids is genetically distinct, shuffled by crossing-over and independent assortment, before maturing into a functional sperm.

## answer_d
Eight sperm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Eight sperm would require a third division; spermatogenesis involves exactly two meiotic divisions from each primary spermatocyte, yielding four, not eight, products.

## title
Homologous chromosome pairing enables genetic recombination

## question
Homologous chromosome pairing is a critically important event in meiosis because it is strictly necessary for which process?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Inheritable genetic predisposition in general

## explanation_a
Incorrect. General inheritance of traits does not itself require homologous pairing as a mechanism; homologous pairing's specific structural role is to enable the exchange of DNA segments between chromatids.

## answer_b
Genetic recombination (crossing-over)

## explanation_b
Correct. During prophase of meiosis I, homologous chromosomes physically pair (synapsis) along their length via the synaptonemal complex, and only while aligned in this way can non-sister chromatids exchange corresponding DNA segments at chiasmata — genetic recombination — producing new combinations of maternal and paternal alleles. Without this length-wise pairing, non-sister chromatids could never align closely enough to exchange DNA segments in the first place.

## answer_c
The cell's rapid DNA replication

## explanation_c
Incorrect. DNA replication occurs earlier, during the S phase preceding meiosis, and does not require homologous chromosome pairing; pairing is a prophase I event, well after replication is complete.

## answer_d
Targeted cellular DNA repair

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Targeted DNA repair is a distinct cellular process from meiotic recombination and does not depend on homologous chromosome pairing in the meiotic sense described here.

## title
Anaphase I separates homologous chromosomes

## question
Which of the following critical chromosomal events occurs during anaphase I of meiosis?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Sister chromatids separate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sister chromatid separation is the defining event of anaphase II (and of mitotic anaphase), not anaphase I; at anaphase I, sister chromatids remain joined at their centromere.

## answer_b
Homologous chromosomes separate

## explanation_b
Correct. At anaphase I, the spindle pulls each pair of homologous chromosomes apart, sending one full, still-duplicated homologue to each pole — this is what makes meiosis I a reductional division, halving the chromosome number while sister chromatids stay together. It is only at the mechanically similar anaphase II, later, that sister chromatids are finally pulled apart from each other.

## answer_c
Chromosomes uncoil

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Chromosome uncoiling (decondensation) is a telophase event, occurring after the chromosomes have already reached the poles, not during the anaphase movement itself.

## answer_d
The nuclear envelope reforms

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Nuclear envelope reformation is also a telophase event, following anaphase's chromosome movement, not occurring during anaphase I itself.

## title
The metaphase checkpoint verifies spindle attachment

## question
Which specific cell cycle checkpoint verifies proper spindle attachment to the chromosomes before allowing the cell to proceed?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
G1 checkpoint

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The G1 checkpoint verifies that the cell has adequate size, nutrients and an intact genome before committing to DNA replication; it does not monitor spindle attachment, which has not yet occurred at that stage.

## answer_b
Metaphase (spindle-assembly) checkpoint

## explanation_b
Correct. The metaphase (spindle-assembly) checkpoint monitors whether every chromosome's kinetochore is correctly attached to spindle microtubules from opposite poles, and it blocks entry into anaphase until every chromosome is properly bi-oriented, preventing chromosome missegregation. Only once every chromosome is correctly bi-oriented does the checkpoint release its block, allowing sister chromatids to separate at anaphase.

## answer_c
G2 checkpoint

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The G2 checkpoint verifies that DNA replication is complete and undamaged before the cell enters mitosis; spindle attachment has not yet been established at that stage either.

## answer_d
S phase checkpoint

## explanation_d
Incorrect. There is no dedicated 'S phase checkpoint' monitoring spindle attachment; DNA-damage surveillance during S phase is a separate mechanism from the metaphase checkpoint's spindle-attachment role.

Which of the following cellular organelles contains its own DNA?

## subject

## title
Homologous pairing's evolutionary purpose: recombination

## question
What is the main evolutionary purpose served by the pairing of homologous chromosomes during meiosis I?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Separation of sister chromatids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sister chromatid separation is a mechanical event of anaphase II, not the evolutionary purpose of homologous pairing at prophase I, which serves a different, recombination-enabling function.

## answer_b
Formation of the mitotic spindle

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Spindle formation is a general mitotic and meiotic apparatus event, not specific to, or the purpose of, homologous chromosome pairing.

Occurrence of genetic recombination

## explanation_c
Correct. Homologous pairing (synapsis) aligns maternal and paternal chromosomes closely enough for crossing-over to occur between non-sister chromatids, and this recombination shuffles alleles onto new chromosome combinations — the principal evolutionary benefit of pairing, generating genetic diversity among gametes beyond what independent assortment alone provides. Without synapsis holding the homologues in close alignment, this exchange of DNA segments between non-sister chromatids could not occur.

## answer_d
Condensation of chromosomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chromosome condensation is a general feature of both mitotic and meiotic prophase, not a purpose specific to homologous pairing.

## title
Synapsis: pairing of homologous chromosomes in prophase I

## question
What exactly occurs during the biological process of synapsis in the prophase of the first meiotic division?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Separation of sister chromatids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sister chromatid separation happens much later, at anaphase II, not during prophase I synapsis, which involves an entirely different pair of structures — homologous chromosomes, not chromatids of the same chromosome.

## answer_b
Pairing of homologous chromosomes

## explanation_b
Correct. Synapsis is the process, specific to prophase I of meiosis, in which each chromosome pairs closely with its homologous partner along its full length, held together by the synaptonemal complex, forming a structure called a bivalent and enabling crossing-over between them. This pairing has no equivalent step in mitosis, where homologous chromosomes never pair with one another at all.

## answer_c
Division of the cellular cytoplasm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Division of the cytoplasm (cytokinesis) is a separate, later event that follows nuclear division, not something that occurs during prophase I synapsis.

## answer_d
Condensation of the nuclear envelope

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The nuclear envelope actually breaks down, rather than condenses, as prophase I progresses toward metaphase I; this is unrelated to the pairing process of synapsis.

## title
Synapsis defined: pairing along the chromosome's length

## question
What does the biological term 'synapsis' specifically mean during cell division?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pairing of sister chromatids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Synapsis pairs homologous chromosomes (one maternal, one paternal copy of the same chromosome), not the sister chromatids of a single already-duplicated chromosome.

## answer_b
Pairing of homologous chromosomes at the centromere only

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Synapsis is not restricted to the centromere; the defining feature is that pairing occurs along the entire length of each homologous chromosome, not at a single point.

## answer_c
Alignment of non-homologous structures across the equator

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Synapsis specifically pairs homologous structures, not non-homologous ones, and it is not an equatorial alignment process (that describes metaphase positioning, a separate event).

## answer_d
Pairing of homologous chromosomes along their length

## explanation_d
Correct. Synapsis is the intimate pairing of homologous chromosomes along their entire length during prophase I of meiosis, mediated by the synaptonemal complex, forming the bivalent (tetrad) structure that permits crossing-over between non-sister chromatids. The paired structure formed by each homologous pair in this way is called a bivalent, or tetrad when counting all four chromatids.

Mitosis serves tissue repair in multicellular organisms

## question
Multicellular organisms perform mitotic division primarily for which of the following reasons?

Incorrect. Sexual reproduction depends on gametes produced by meiosis, not mitosis; mitosis is not the division type responsible for gamete formation.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Gametogenesis (production of sperm and eggs) depends on meiosis, not mitosis, so it is not the reason multicellular organisms undergo mitotic division.

## title
Telomerase is a reverse transcriptase

## question
Which of the following enzyme classifications best describes the biochemical nature of telomerase?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
DNA-dependent RNA polymerase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A DNA-dependent RNA polymerase synthesizes RNA from a DNA template — essentially the opposite of what telomerase does, since telomerase synthesizes DNA from an RNA template.

## answer_b
Reverse transcriptase

## explanation_b
Correct. Telomerase is a reverse transcriptase — an RNA-dependent DNA polymerase — that carries its own built-in RNA template as part of the enzyme complex and uses it to synthesize new telomeric DNA repeats onto the 3' end of chromosomes, compensating for the DNA lost with each round of replication. Its activity is high in germ cells and stem cells but low or absent in most differentiated somatic cells, one reason those cells have a finite replicative lifespan.

## answer_c
Specialized RNA endonuclease

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Telomerase does not function as an endonuclease (which would cut nucleic acid internally); its catalytic role is polymerization of new DNA, not cleavage.

## answer_d
DNA restriction ligase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. There is no such combined 'restriction ligase' enzyme class, and telomerase's function (RNA-templated DNA synthesis) has nothing to do with restriction-enzyme cutting or DNA ligation.

## title
DNA replication produces two daughter molecules

## question
During the process of DNA replication, how many new, complete DNA molecules are normally produced from one original parent molecule?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
One new copy

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A single new copy would leave the original strand still paired only with itself; semiconservative replication instead separates both original strands and builds a new complementary strand on each.

## answer_b
Two

## explanation_b
Correct. DNA replication is semiconservative: the two strands of the parent double helix separate, and each serves as a template for one new complementary strand, producing exactly two daughter DNA molecules, each with one original (conserved) strand paired with one newly synthesized strand. This 'one old, one new' pairing in each daughter molecule is exactly what the Meselson-Stahl experiment demonstrated, ruling out fully conservative or dispersive models.

## answer_c
Three copies

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Three copies would require an additional, unexplained replication event; a single round of DNA replication from one parent molecule always yields exactly two daughter molecules.

## answer_d
Four copies

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Four copies would only arise after two successive rounds of replication; a single replication event produces two, not four, daughter molecules.

## title
Fever from infection is caused by resetting the hypothalamic set point

## question
Which of the following pathophysiological mechanisms is fundamentally responsible for the development of a fever during an infection?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
A marked decrease in the basal metabolic rate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fever is associated with an increase, not a decrease, in metabolic rate, as the body actively generates heat to reach the new, higher set point.

## answer_b
Resetting of the hypothalamic thermoregulatory set point upward

## explanation_b
Correct. Pyrogens released during infection act on the hypothalamus to reset its thermoregulatory set point upward; because actual core temperature is now below this new target, the hypothalamus drives shivering and vasoconstriction until temperature rises to match it — this is fever. Recognising fever as a regulated shift in the set point, rather than a breakdown of thermoregulation, is the key distinction this question is testing.

## answer_c
Complete inactivation of the sweat glands

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sweat gland inactivation is not the fundamental mechanism of fever; it can contribute to reduced heat loss during the rising phase, but the driving event is the set point being reset upward.

## answer_d
Marked dilation of peripheral blood vessels

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fever onset is associated with peripheral vasoconstriction, which conserves heat, not vasodilation, which would dissipate it.

QST-SCUFBS102-S2-PHYSIOLOGY-SODIUM-CHANNELS-INACTIVE

## title
Sodium channel inactivation underlies the absolute refractory period

## question
The absolute refractory period of an action potential occurs primarily because of which specific ion channel mechanism?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Potassium channels are successfully inactivated

## explanation_a
Incorrect. It is sodium channels, not potassium channels, whose inactivated state underlies the absolute refractory period; potassium channels are, if anything, still open and contributing to repolarization at this time.

## answer_b
Voltage-gated sodium channels are inactivated

## explanation_b
Correct. During and immediately after depolarization, the great majority of voltage-gated sodium channels sit in the inactivated state, in which their inactivation gate blocks the pore regardless of voltage, so no stimulus, however strong, can trigger a second action potential until enough channels have returned to the closed, activatable state. Only once enough sodium channels have returned from inactivated to the closed, resting state does excitability begin to recover.

## answer_c
Sodium channels are only partially active

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Describing sodium channels as merely 'partially active' understates the mechanism; during the absolute refractory period they are specifically inactivated (unavailable), not simply reduced in activity.

## answer_d
Potassium channels are completely, partially active

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Potassium channel state is not what defines the absolute refractory period; the defining, rate-limiting event is sodium channel inactivation.

## title
Strenuous exercise raises systolic blood pressure

## question
Performing maximal, strenuous exercise typically produces a significant increase in which of the following cardiovascular parameters?

## subject
cvs

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Total peripheral (systemic vascular) resistance

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Total peripheral resistance actually falls during dynamic exercise, because vasodilation in the exercising skeletal muscle beds lowers overall resistance rather than raising it.

## answer_b
Systolic blood pressure

## explanation_b
Correct. The sharp rise in cardiac output during strenuous exercise, driven by increased heart rate and stroke volume, substantially raises systolic blood pressure, while diastolic pressure changes little because of the offsetting fall in peripheral resistance. This selective rise in systolic, rather than diastolic, pressure is exactly the pattern examiners expect for the cardiovascular response to dynamic exercise.

Total body water volume

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Acute exercise does not meaningfully change total body water volume over the short term; fluid shifts and sweat losses are comparatively small relative to total body water.

## answer_d
Daily urinary output volume

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Urinary output typically decreases, not increases, during and immediately after strenuous exercise, as renal blood flow is reduced in favour of the exercising muscles.
