Epithelium's main physiological function

## question
What is the main physiological function of epithelial tissue?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Contraction and movement

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Contraction and movement are the defining physiological role of muscle tissue, not epithelium.

## answer_b
Absorption and secretion

## explanation_b
Correct. Of the four basic tissue types, epithelium's defining physiological job is absorption and secretion. It does this by lining surfaces and cavities, and by lining or forming glands, to move substances across a barrier in either direction. Intestinal absorption and glandular secretion are the two clearest worked examples of this shared function.

Protection and support

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Protection and mechanical support are chiefly connective tissue's role, achieved through its abundant extracellular matrix.

## answer_d
Conducting electrical impulses

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Conducting electrical impulses is the defining role of nervous tissue, not epithelium.

## title
Where continuous capillaries with narrow clefts are found

## question
Continuous capillaries characterized by having narrow intercellular clefts are typically found in which of the following tissues?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The large liver and spleen sinusoids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The liver and spleen are the classic example of SINUSOIDAL capillaries — wide-open gaps and a discontinuous basement membrane for whole-cell and plasma-protein traffic — the structural opposite of the sealed continuous type.

## answer_b
Lung and adipose tissue

## explanation_b
Correct. Continuous (somatic) capillaries have an unbroken endothelium with only narrow intercellular clefts between adjacent cells. They are built for tissue where solutes cross gradually rather than in bulk. Lung and adipose tissue, along with muscle and skin, are the classic examples of this continuous type.

## answer_c
The filtering renal glomeruli structures

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Renal glomeruli are lined by fenestrated capillaries, with pores covered by diaphragms for fast fluid filtration — a third, intermediate type between continuous and sinusoidal.

## answer_d
The hormone secreting endocrine gland tissues

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Endocrine glands are also supplied by fenestrated capillaries, allowing rapid passage of hormones into the bloodstream — not the continuous type this question asks about.

The defining feature of connective tissue

## question
Which of the following is considered a main characteristic feature of connective tissue?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Absence of any blood vessels

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Avascularity is a feature of epithelium (which is nourished by diffusion from underlying connective tissue), not connective tissue itself, which is generally well vascularised.

## answer_b
Presence of extracellular matrix

## explanation_b
Correct. Connective tissue is defined by having relatively few cells widely separated by an abundant extracellular matrix — ground substance plus fibres — that the cells themselves secrete. This is the opposite arrangement from epithelium, whose cells sit closely packed with minimal matrix between them. The sheer bulk of matrix relative to cells is what a histology slide of connective tissue shows at a glance.

Cells closely packed together

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Closely packed cells with minimal intervening material describe epithelium, not connective tissue.

## answer_d
Resting on a basement membrane

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Resting on a basement membrane is a feature of epithelium, which sits on this specialised layer separating it from the underlying connective tissue.

## title
The primary function of smooth endoplasmic reticulum

## question
What is the primary function of the smooth endoplasmic reticulum in a cell?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Packaging newly synthesized cellular proteins

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Packaging finished secretory protein is a Golgi apparatus function, not one performed by the smooth ER itself, even though the smooth ER's membranes feed material into the Golgi.

## answer_b
Digesting old cellular waste materials

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Digesting cellular waste is the lysosome's job, using its own set of hydrolytic enzymes — a separate organelle from smooth ER.

## answer_c
Regulating calcium concentration inside the cell

## explanation_c
Correct. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum has several functions depending on the cell type, including lipid and steroid synthesis and detoxification of drugs and toxins. Especially in muscle cells, where it is specialised as the sarcoplasmic reticulum, it regulates intracellular calcium concentration. It does this by sequestering and releasing calcium ions to drive and then terminate contraction.

## answer_d
Producing energy molecules for the cell

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ATP production is the mitochondrion's role, achieved through oxidative phosphorylation — unrelated to the smooth ER's membrane network.

## title
Switching off G protein signalling

## question
In G protein coupled receptors the G protein action is switched off by which one of the following specific biochemical processes?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The extremely fast direct total activation of the target functional protein

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Activation of the target protein is part of the signal being ON, downstream of an already-active G protein — not the step that switches it off.

## answer_b
The completely full alpha subunit totally replacement of GDP for GTP

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Exchanging GDP for GTP on the alpha subunit is exactly what turns the G protein ON, the reverse of the switch-off step being asked about.

## answer_c
The extremely rapid total dissociation of the beta and delta subunits from alpha

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Dissociation of the beta-gamma pair from alpha happens when the G protein is activated, not when it is switched off.

## answer_d
Hydrolysis of GTP

## explanation_d
Correct. A G protein-coupled receptor activates its heterotrimeric G protein by prompting the alpha subunit to exchange bound GDP for GTP, letting alpha dissociate and activate its downstream target. Signalling switches off when the alpha subunit's own intrinsic GTPase activity hydrolyses that bound GTP back to GDP, allowing alpha to re-associate with beta-gamma and return the complex to its resting state.

Incorrect. Tyrosine kinase receptors are another surface-membrane mechanism, used by hormones such as insulin, not by lipid-soluble steroid hormones.

## title
Defining the product of an enzymatic reaction

## question
In an enzymatic reaction how is the product defined?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The biomolecule acted upon by an enzyme

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The molecule an enzyme acts upon at the start is the SUBSTRATE, not the product — this describes the beginning of the reaction, not its result.

## answer_b
Result of reaction between enzyme and substrate

## explanation_b
Correct. An enzymatic reaction has three basic terms. The substrate is what the enzyme acts on at the start, the transition state is a brief high-energy intermediate mid-reaction, and the product is what the substrate becomes once the enzyme has finished acting on it — the result of the reaction between enzyme and substrate.

## answer_c
The initial unstable transition state molecule

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The transition state is a fleeting, high-energy intermediate the substrate passes through mid-reaction, not the final, stable result the reaction produces.

## answer_d
The specific cofactor organically attached to the enzyme molecule

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A cofactor, when one is needed, is a separate helper molecule the enzyme uses during catalysis, not the product of the reaction itself.

The sugar type that determines ABO antigen specificity

## question
What is the primary type of carbohydrate molecules that determine the ABO blood group antigens on the red blood cell surface?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The complex disaccharides

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Disaccharides (two linked sugar units, like lactose or sucrose) are not what determines ABO antigen specificity.

## answer_b
Monosaccharides

## explanation_b
Correct. While the ABO antigen as a whole is a branched oligosaccharide chain, blood-group specificity is set by which single further monosaccharide a person's own glycosyltransferase adds onto the shared H-antigen core: an extra N-acetylgalactosamine gives the A antigen, an extra galactose gives the B antigen, and no further addition leaves the O phenotype. So the primary carbohydrate TYPE that determines specificity — the one differing residue a transferase enzyme adds — is a monosaccharide.

## answer_c
The branched polysaccharides

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Polysaccharides are long chains of many repeating monosaccharide units (like glycogen or starch), a different scale of molecule from the single terminal sugar that sets ABO specificity.

## answer_d
Large stored glycogen molecules

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Glycogen is the body's storage polysaccharide of glucose, unrelated to red-cell surface antigen specificity.

## title
How a secondary spermatocyte becomes a spermatid

## question
By which specific cellular division process does a secondary spermatocyte produce a spermatid with a haploid number of chromosomes?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The first meiotic division

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The first meiotic division is what already happened to create the secondary spermatocyte from the primary spermatocyte, separating homologous chromosomes and halving the chromosome number to 23 (still duplicated).

## answer_b
Second meiotic division

## explanation_b
Correct. Each secondary spermatocyte (23 duplicated chromosomes) completes the second meiotic division — separating sister chromatids, with no further DNA replication beforehand — to give two spermatids, each with a genuinely haploid 23 single chromosomes. The first division is reductional (halves the number); the second is equational (separates sister chromatids), and it is specifically the second division that turns a secondary spermatocyte into a spermatid.

## answer_c
The standard mitotic cellular division

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells and plays no part in the reductional divisions of spermatogenesis.

## answer_d
The simple binary fission process

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Binary fission is a prokaryotic (bacterial) mode of cell division, not a process human germ cells use.
