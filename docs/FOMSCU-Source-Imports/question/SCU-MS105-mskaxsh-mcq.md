## title
Boundaries of the apex of the axilla

## question
Which structures form the boundaries of the apex of the axilla?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Clavicle, first rib, superior border of scapula

## explanation_a
Correct. The apex of the axilla, also called the cervico-axillary canal, is the gateway between the root of the neck and the axilla. It is bounded anteriorly by the clavicle (with subclavius), posteriorly by the upper border of the scapula, and medially by the outer border of the first rib. The axillary vessels and the trunks and divisions of the brachial plexus all pass through this narrow canal.

## answer_b
Clavicle, second rib, superior angle of scapula

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The second rib and the superior angle of the scapula are not part of the apex's boundaries; the true boundaries use the first rib and the upper border of the scapula, not its superior angle specifically.

## answer_c
Coracoid process, humerus, clavicle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The coracoid process and humerus relate to the lateral wall and shoulder joint region, not the apex, and the clavicle alone is insufficient to define the apex's three boundaries.

## answer_d
First rib, sternum, coracoid process

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The sternum and coracoid process play no role in the apex's boundaries; the clavicle is a boundary, but paired with the first rib and scapula, not the sternum and coracoid process.

## subject

Correct. The cords of the brachial plexus are named according to their position relative to which structure?

Subscapularis muscle
