## label
Histone acetylation typically targets lysine residues

## canonical_key
chromatin.histone-acetylation.lysine-residues

## definition
Histone acetyltransferases add an acetyl group to the epsilon-amino group of lysine side chains on histone tails, neutralising their positive charge. Arginine, asparagine and leucine are not the residues typically targeted by this modification.

## explicit_objective
State that histone acetylation typically occurs on lysine residues.

## pitfalls
Confusing lysine (the standard acetylation target) with arginine, which carries a different, guanidinium side chain and is instead a common site for methylation.

## label
DNA methylation is associated with repressing, not inducing, gene activity

## canonical_key
epigenetics.dna-methylation.represses-not-induces

## definition
DNA methyltransferases add a methyl group to cytosine (forming 5-methylcytosine), usually at cytosines next to a guanine (CpG sites, often clustered as CpG islands near promoters). Rather than inducing the activity of the affected gene, promoter-region methylation is characteristically associated with silencing/repressing transcription, and is one mechanism regulating gene expression during differentiation.

## explicit_objective
State that DNA methylation at gene promoters is associated with repressing (not inducing) that gene's activity.

## pitfalls
Assuming methylation is a generically "activating" mark -- promoter methylation is the classic epigenetic silencing mechanism, the opposite direction from induction.

## label
Histone acetylation neutralises lysine's positive charge, not a negative one

## canonical_key
chromatin.histone-acetylation.charge-mechanism

## definition
A lysine side chain's epsilon-amino group is positively charged under physiological conditions, and it is this positive charge that normally strengthens the electrostatic attraction between histones and the negatively charged DNA backbone. Acetylation removes that positive charge (by converting the amino group to an uncharged amide), loosening histone-DNA contacts -- not, as a mislabelled statement might claim, removing a "negative" charge from the lysine itself.

## explicit_objective
State that histone acetylation works by removing the positive charge on lysine's epsilon-amino group, reducing histone-DNA electrostatic attraction.

## pitfalls
Reading a statement that says acetylation removes a "negative charge... of the lysine" as correct -- lysine's side-chain amino group is positively charged, so the charge removed by acetylation is positive, not negative.

## label
Enhancers are trans-acting regulatory sequences that can act at long, variable distances on either DNA strand

## canonical_key
gene-regulation.enhancers.definition-and-location

## definition
Enhancers are cis-acting DNA sequences (bound by trans-acting proteins) that increase transcription of a target gene; they can sit close to or many thousands of base pairs from the promoter they regulate, upstream or downstream, and can function on either DNA strand, typically by DNA looping to contact the promoter-bound transcription machinery. They are not themselves sites of DNA acetylation, and they do not act by inhibiting transcription-factor binding to the promoter.

## explicit_objective
State that enhancers can be close to or thousands of base pairs from the promoter (upstream or downstream) and can occur on either DNA strand.

## pitfalls
Calling enhancers "trans-acting" (they are cis-acting DNA elements bound by trans-acting proteins) or assuming they act only when adjacent to the promoter.
