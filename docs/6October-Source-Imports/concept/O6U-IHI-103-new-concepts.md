The neutrophil's nucleus is multilobed (typically 3-5 lobes)

## canonical_key
neutrophil.nucleus.multilobed

## definition
The neutrophil is named for its granule staining but identified on a blood film mainly by
its nucleus: a single nucleus divided into three to five lobes connected by thin chromatin
strands, giving the "polymorphonuclear" appearance. This distinguishes it at a glance from
the bilobed eosinophil, the obscured/S-shaped basophil nucleus, and the rounded nuclei of
lymphocytes and monocytes.

State that the neutrophil's nucleus is multilobed (3-5 lobes), and use nuclear shape to

## label
Eosinophils carry acidophilic specific granules with a crystalline core, and a bilobed nucleus

## canonical_key
eosinophil.identification.acidophilic-crystalloid-granules-bilobed-nucleus

## definition
The eosinophil is identified by two features together: a bilobed nucleus (rarely three
lobes), and coarse, uniformly sized specific granules that stain strongly acidophilic (eosin
pink) and, on electron microscopy, show a dense crystalline core (crystalloid) inside a
less-dense matrix -- the only granulocyte whose specific granules have this internal
crystalline structure. Eosinophils normally make up about 1-4% of the total leukocyte count,
not the 20-25% a distractor may offer, and are not converted into plasma cells (a separate,
B-lymphocyte-derived lineage).

## label
The orthochromatophilic erythroblast is the maturation stage with mainly acidophilic cytoplasm

## canonical_key
erythroblast.maturation.orthochromatophilic-stage-acidophilic

## definition
Across the erythroblast maturation series -- proerythroblast, basophilic erythroblast,
polychromatophilic erythroblast, orthochromatophilic erythroblast -- cytoplasmic staining
shifts steadily from basophilic (ribosome-rich, protein-synthesising) toward acidophilic
(haemoglobin-rich) as the cell matures. The orthochromatophilic erythroblast, the last
nucleated stage before the reticulocyte, is named for reaching that end point: its cytoplasm
stains mainly acidophilic because haemoglobin has by then almost fully replaced the
ribosomal machinery that made the earlier stages basophilic.

## explicit_objective
Place the orthochromatophilic erythroblast as the maturation stage with mainly acidophilic

## label
RBC maturation condenses and extrudes the nucleus, not lobulates it, while ribosomal cytoplasm falls and haemoglobin rises

## canonical_key
erythrocyte.maturation.nuclear-condensation-and-extrusion

## definition
As an erythroblast matures toward an erythrocyte, three changes proceed together: overall
cell size decreases; the nucleus becomes smaller and progressively condensed (heterochromatic)
until it is extruded from the cell entirely at the orthochromatophilic-erythroblast stage --
it is never lobulated, since lobulation is a granulocyte nuclear feature, not an erythroid
one; and the ribosome-rich, basophilic cytoplasm is progressively replaced by haemoglobin, so
basophilia falls as acidophilia (and haemoglobin content) rises.

## explicit_objective
List the three parallel changes of RBC maturation -- shrinking cell size, nuclear
condensation and extrusion (not lobulation), and falling ribosomal cytoplasm as haemoglobin
rises.

basophil.nucleus.bilobed-obscured-by-granules

## definition
The basophil is the least common leukocyte (about 0.5% of the total count) and is identified
by coarse, irregular, deeply basophilic (metachromatic with toluidine blue) specific granules
that are so numerous they typically overlie and obscure the nucleus. The nucleus itself is
bilobed or S-shaped, like the eosinophil's, not a simple central rounded nucleus -- a rounded
nucleus belongs to a monocyte or lymphocyte, not a basophil.

## label
Monocytes are agranular and phagocytic, a separate lineage from lymphocytes

## canonical_key
monocyte.identification.agranular-phagocytic-myeloid-lineage

## definition
The monocyte is an agranular leukocyte -- it carries no specific granules, unlike the
granulocytes -- with a large, kidney- or horseshoe-shaped nucleus and abundant pale-blue
cytoplasm. Its defining function is phagocytosis: it circulates briefly before migrating
into tissues and differentiating into a macrophage. Monocytes are myeloid-lineage cells and
are not the mother cell of lymphocytes, which are a separate lymphoid lineage arising from
their own bone-marrow stem cell.

## explicit_objective
State that the monocyte is agranular and phagocytic, and that it belongs to the myeloid
lineage, distinct from -- and not the precursor of -- lymphocytes.
