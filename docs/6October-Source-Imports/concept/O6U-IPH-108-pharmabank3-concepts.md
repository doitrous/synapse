## label
Norepinephrine's pressor effect includes reflex bradycardia, not positive chronotropism

## canonical_key
teaching.pharma.ans.norepinephrine-pressor-mechanism-reflex-bradycardia

## definition
Norepinephrine raises blood pressure through alpha-1-mediated vasoconstriction and beta-1-mediated positive inotropism, and the resulting hypertension triggers a reflex, baroreceptor-mediated bradycardia that offsets its own direct beta-1 positive chronotropic potential. The net observed heart-rate effect of norepinephrine is therefore reflex slowing, not a positive chronotropic rise, even though norepinephrine has genuine direct beta-1 chronotropic activity. Norepinephrine's beta-1 activity also stimulates renin secretion from the juxtaglomerular apparatus, raising angiotensin II and further sustaining the pressor effect.

## explicit_objective
State that norepinephrine's net heart-rate effect is reflex bradycardia, not positive chronotropism, despite its direct beta-1 chronotropic activity, and that its pressor mechanism also includes renin/angiotensin II stimulation.

## canonical_key

## label
Epinephrine is never given orally, and is unsuitable for acute congestive glaucoma

## canonical_key
teaching.pharma.ans.epinephrine-routes-and-indications

## definition
Epinephrine, a catecholamine rapidly destroyed by gut and liver first-pass metabolism, is poorly and unreliably absorbed from the GI tract and so is never given orally for a systemic effect; it is instead given parenterally (subcutaneous, intramuscular, intravenous, intracardiac in extremis), by inhalation for local airway effect, or topically for local vasoconstriction and haemostasis. Epinephrine's genuine therapeutic uses include bronchospasm-related respiratory distress, topical haemostasis, cardiopulmonary resuscitation, and reversal of hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions, but it is unsuitable, even contraindicated, in acute congestive (angle-closure) glaucoma, since its mydriatic effect risks worsening angle closure. Epinephrine's ophthalmic use is instead confined to chronic open-angle (simple) glaucoma, where it lowers aqueous formation without provoking angle closure.

Brimonidine, a selective alpha-2 agonist, is preferred to epinephrine in chronic simple glaucoma

## canonical_key
teaching.pharma.ans.brimonidine-preferred-chronic-simple-glaucoma

## definition
Brimonidine, a selective alpha-2 agonist, lowers intraocular pressure in chronic simple (open-angle) glaucoma both by decreasing aqueous humor production, through ciliary-body vasoconstriction, and by increasing uveoscleral outflow, without producing the pupillary dilation a nonselective sympathomimetic such as epinephrine would cause. This selective alpha-2 action lets brimonidine achieve the same aqueous-lowering vasoconstrictive effect as epinephrine while avoiding epinephrine's mydriatic risk of precipitating angle closure in a susceptible eye. Epinephrine's ophthalmic use has consequently been largely superseded in practice by more selective, better-tolerated agents such as brimonidine.

## canonical_key
teaching.pharma.ans.clonidine-classification-urgency-hypertension

## definition
Clonidine, a centrally-acting alpha-2 agonist, stimulates central and presynaptic alpha-2 autoreceptors to reduce sympathetic outflow from the brainstem, the opposite pharmacologic role from a peripherally-acting sympathomimetic such as phenylephrine, dobutamine, salbutamol or amphetamine, and it is conventionally classified separately from that peripheral sympathomimetic group. Its central sympatho-inhibitory action lowers blood pressure relatively quickly by the oral route, making systemic clonidine a recognised option for urgency hypertension (significantly elevated blood pressure without acute end-organ damage, managed with oral agents rather than an emergency IV infusion). This primary systemic antihypertensive role is distinct from clonidine's other niche indications.

Classify clonidine as a centrally-acting alpha-2 agonist rather than a peripheral sympathomimetic, and identify urgency hypertension as its primary systemic therapeutic use.

## definition

Dose reduction is recommended when lignocaine is co-administered with a beta blocker, even though the mechanism is haemodynamic rather than classic enzyme-inhibition interaction.

Alpha-1 receptors mediate contraction in bladder-neck, vas deferens, iris dilator (radial/papillae) and vascular smooth muscle, so blocking them relaxes each of these tissues -- the basis of an alpha blocker's use in BPH, and its vasodilator and pupil-sparing effects. Bronchial smooth-muscle tone, by contrast, is controlled mainly by parasympathetic (bronchoconstrictor) and beta-2-adrenergic (bronchodilator) influences, not by alpha receptors, so alpha-receptor blockade has no meaningful relaxant action on bronchial smooth muscle. This is why alpha blockers, unlike beta-2 agonists, have no bronchodilator role, and an alpha blocker's smooth-muscle-relaxing effects are confined to the tissues where alpha-1 receptors genuinely mediate contraction.

## explicit_objective
State that alpha-receptor blockade relaxes bladder-neck, vas deferens, iris dilator and vascular smooth muscle, but has no relaxant action on bronchial smooth muscle.

Tamsulosin's alpha-1A selectivity and alpha-2-autoreceptor sparing give it minimal reflex tachycardia

## canonical_key
teaching.pharma.ans.tamsulosin-alpha1a-selectivity-minimal-reflex-tachycardia

## definition
Tamsulosin's selectivity for alpha-1A (and alpha-1D) receptors, which predominate in prostatic and bladder-neck tissue rather than in blood vessels generally, means it produces much less systemic vasodilation/hypotension than a nonselective alpha-1 blocker such as prazosin; since hypotension is the trigger for baroreceptor-mediated reflex tachycardia, less hypotension means less reflex tachycardia. Tamsulosin also spares presynaptic alpha-2 autoreceptors, which normally inhibit further norepinephrine release, so that inhibitory brake on sympathetic outflow remains intact, further limiting the compensatory tachycardic response, unlike phenoxybenzamine, which additionally blocks alpha-2 autoreceptors and so characteristically causes marked reflex tachycardia. This combination of minimal hypotension and preserved alpha-2 autoreceptor feedback is why tamsulosin causes the least reflex tachycardia among alpha blockers.

## label
Alpha-receptor-blockade vasodilation occurs in both the arterial and venous circulations

## canonical_key
teaching.pharma.ans.alpha-blockade-vasodilation-arteries-and-veins

## definition
Alpha-1 receptors are present on smooth muscle in both the arterial and venous circulations, mediating vasoconstriction in each; blocking them therefore produces "balanced" vasodilation across both arterial and venous beds, not a selective effect on one alone. This balanced arterial-plus-venous dilation is exactly why selective alpha-1 blockers such as prazosin characteristically cause first-dose postural hypotension: dilating the venous capacitance bed reduces venous return, while dilating the arterial bed reduces peripheral resistance, and the combination produces a marked early blood-pressure drop before compensatory reflexes adjust. This balanced-dilation mechanism directly underlies prazosin's first-dose-hypotension adverse effect.

## label
Floppy iris syndrome is a direct iris alpha-1-blockade effect, distinct from prazosin's vasodilation-mediated adverse effects

## canonical_key
teaching.pharma.ans.prazosin-floppy-iris-syndrome-distinct-mechanism

## definition
Prazosin's balanced arterial/venous vasodilation genuinely produces headache (meningeal/cerebral vasodilation), nasal stuffiness (nasal mucosal vasodilation), first-dose postural hypotension (the characteristic early adverse effect of balanced dilation), and reflex tachycardia (the baroreceptor response to that hypotension). Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome, by contrast, is caused by alpha-1A-receptor blockade specifically in the iris dilator (pupillary dilator) smooth muscle, which loses its normal tone during cataract surgery -- a direct receptor-blockade effect on iris smooth muscle, not a consequence of prazosin's systemic vasodilation. Floppy iris syndrome is most classically associated with tamsulosin but is relevant to any alpha-1 blocker, including prazosin, given its own distinct, iris-specific mechanism.

## explicit_objective
Distinguish floppy iris syndrome, a direct iris-alpha-1-blockade effect, from prazosin's vasodilation-mediated adverse effects (headache, nasal stuffiness, first-dose hypotension, reflex tachycardia).
