## label
The TATA box marks the transcription start site in eukaryotic promoters

## canonical_key
transcription.tata-box.marks-transcription-start-site

## definition
The TATA box is a core promoter element found roughly 25-30 base pairs upstream of the transcription start site in many eukaryotic protein-coding genes. It is recognized by the TATA-binding protein (TBP), which nucleates assembly of the general transcription factors and RNA polymerase II there, positioning the polymerase to begin transcription at the correct nucleotide. It marks the start of transcription, not of replication or translation.

## explicit_objective
State that the TATA box marks the transcription start site in eukaryotic promoters.

## pitfalls
Confusing the TATA box's role (marking the transcription start point) with replication origins or the translation start codon (AUG).

## label
Sigma factor positions bacterial RNA polymerase correctly on the promoter

## canonical_key
transcription.sigma-factor.positions-rna-polymerase-on-template

## definition
Sigma factor is the subunit that joins the bacterial RNA polymerase core enzyme to form the holoenzyme. Its role is to recognize the promoter sequence (including the Pribnow/-10 box) and correctly position the polymerase there so transcription starts at the right nucleotide. Sigma typically dissociates once initiation is under way, leaving the core enzyme to carry out elongation; it does not itself catalyze RNA synthesis, terminate transcription, or unwind the DNA.

## explicit_objective
State that the sigma factor positions bacterial RNA polymerase correctly on the promoter.

## pitfalls
Crediting sigma factor with catalyzing RNA synthesis or terminating transcription -- its role is promoter recognition and positioning at initiation.

RNA synthesis always proceeds exclusively in the 5' to 3' direction

## canonical_key
transcription.rna-synthesis.direction-five-to-three-exclusively

## definition
Like DNA polymerase, RNA polymerase can only extend a chain from a free 3'-hydroxyl end, so RNA synthesis is always 5' to 3', for every gene and every RNA polymerase. RNA polymerase, unlike DNA polymerase, does not require a primer to initiate transcription. ## explicit_objective
State that RNA synthesis always proceeds 5' to 3' and does not require a primer or use TTP.

## pitfalls
Assuming RNA polymerase needs a primer (it does not) or that RNA uses TTP instead of UTP (RNA uses uracil, not thymine).

Introns are removed by splicing and untranslated regions (5'UTR, 3'UTR) lie outside the coding sequence, so neither counts toward the span between the start and stop codons in a mature, processed mRNA. This lets the coding-region length be estimated directly from the protein's amino-acid count, independent of intron size or UTR length.

## explicit_objective
Calculate a processed mRNA's coding-region length from the number of encoded amino acids, excluding introns and untranslated regions.

## pitfalls
Including intron length or UTR length when calculating the coding sequence's span between the start and stop codons -- both lie outside the mature coding region.
