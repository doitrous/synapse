## summary
Atropine and its substitutes are the antidote for muscarinic-excess poisoning (mushroom toxicity, cholinomimetic overdose, organophosphate poisoning), but atropine's own overdose produces a distinct toxidrome (flushing, hyperthermia, delirium, mydriasis, tachycardia) treated with CNS-penetrant physostigmine rather than neostigmine. Atropine's competitive muscarinic blockade gives it a bronchodilator niche in COPD/asthma/reflex bronchospasm/pre-anesthetic secretion control, but not in acute pulmonary edema. Topical ocular antimuscarinics (tropicamide, cyclopentolate, homatropine) are systemically-absorbed mydriatics, unlike quaternary glycopyrrolate; scopolamine resembles atropine but is more sedating and does not cause diaphoresis; and pirenzepine is the prototype M1-selective antagonist.

## sections
### Definition
This article continues the atropine/antimuscarinic pharmacology taught in ART-O6U-IPH-ATROPINE-ANTIMUSCARINIC-PHARMACOLOGY, covering atropine's antidotal role, its own overdose toxidrome, its bronchodilator niche, ocular antimuscarinic pharmacokinetics, scopolamine's specific profile, and M1-receptor selectivity.

### Mechanism
Atropine's single mechanism -- competitive, reversible muscarinic receptor blockade -- underlies every effect covered here: it lets atropine competitively displace excess muscarinic stimulation from mushroom toxins, cholinomimetics or organophosphates, and the same mechanism produces its own toxidrome when atropine itself is given in excess (tachycardia, hyperthermia, delirium, mydriasis) rather than a toxidrome that mimics muscarinic excess. Ocular antimuscarinics act locally at the iris sphincter but, being lipid-soluble tertiary amines (tropicamide, cyclopentolate, homatropine), are absorbed systemically from the conjunctival sac; quaternary glycopyrrolate cannot cross into the eye and so has minimal pupil effect, which is exactly why it is chosen pre-anesthetically when mydriasis is undesirable. Scopolamine's greater lipid solubility gives it more prominent CNS penetration than atropine, producing sedation and vivid dreams, while every antimuscarinic (scopolamine included) decreases sweating rather than increasing it. Pirenzepine's M1 selectivity, by contrast to atropine's non-selective blockade, targets gastric parietal-cell and enteric-ganglionic M1 receptors specifically.

### Key determinants
Whether a fact concerns atropine's antidotal action, atropine's own overdose, its bronchodilator niche, an ocular antimuscarinic's absorption, scopolamine's CNS profile, or M1 selectivity determines which specific drug, receptor subtype or clinical setting the fact applies to.

### Clinical significance
Confusing atropine's antidotal role with its own toxidrome, mismatching physostigmine and neostigmine for atropine poisoning, assuming systemic atropine suits chronic COPD/asthma maintenance, or missing that glycopyrrolate spares the pupil each carry real clinical consequences.

## summary
Beta-1 receptors are absent from smooth muscle, and bronchial/ciliary/pupillary-constrictor smooth muscle lack alpha receptors entirely, while vascular endothelial M3 receptors mediate atropine-blockable, direct-agonist-induced vasodilation. Alpha-2 receptors inhibit lipolysis, sympathetic outflow and insulin release and provide presynaptic autoinhibition, while beta-2 receptors raise blood glucose via glycogenolysis and facilitate lipolysis. Amphetamine, imipramine, maprotyline and cocaine block norepinephrine reuptake, unlike the direct agonist dobutamine. Tyrosine hydroxylase is rate-limiting for catecholamine synthesis, norepinephrine's action ends mainly by reuptake, and urinary VMA is the key pheochromocytoma marker. Selective MAO-A inhibitors treat depression while MAO-B inhibitors treat Parkinsonism.

## sections
### Definition
This article covers a set of adrenergic and muscarinic receptor-pharmacology facts from "pharma MCQs bank .pdf" pp.121-138: tissue-distribution quirks, alpha-2/beta-2 peripheral effects, the classification of norepinephrine-uptake-blocking drugs, catecholamine synthesis/termination/diagnosis, and MAO isoform selectivity.

### Mechanism
Receptor distribution is tissue- and subtype-specific rather than uniform: beta-1 receptors concentrate in cardiac tissue rather than smooth muscle, and bronchial/ciliary/pupillary-constrictor smooth muscle carry no alpha receptors at all, while vascular endothelium's M3 receptors respond to a circulating or locally-applied muscarinic agonist (not genuine innervation) to release nitric oxide and vasodilate, an effect atropine blocks. Alpha-2 and beta-2 receptors act in opposite metabolic directions consistent with their Gi/Gs coupling -- alpha-2 inhibiting lipolysis, sympathetic outflow and insulin release while also mediating presynaptic autoinhibition of further norepinephrine release, and beta-2 instead driving glycogenolysis and facilitating lipolysis. Norepinephrine's synaptic action is terminated mainly by presynaptic reuptake (the same transporter that amphetamine, imipramine, maprotyline and cocaine block, though dobutamine, a direct beta-1 agonist, does not act through it), with enzymatic COMT/MAO metabolism as a secondary pathway, and tyrosine hydroxylase catalyzes catecholamine synthesis' rate-limiting step upstream of the separate sympathetic-nerve (norepinephrine) and adrenal-medullary (epinephrine) endpoints. Selective MAO-A/MAO-B inhibition exploits the same two-isoform system for different indications (depression versus Parkinsonism) based on which monoamine substrate each isoform preferentially clears.

### Key determinants
Which receptor subtype, which tissue, and which side of a synthesis/release/reuptake/metabolism pathway a given fact concerns determines whether it is a genuine distribution quirk, a peripheral metabolic effect, an uptake-blockade mechanism, or a synthesis/diagnostic fact.

### Clinical significance
Misreading a receptor-distribution exception, confusing alpha-2's inhibitory profile with beta-2's stimulatory one, missing that a direct agonist need not block reuptake, or swapping the MAO-A/MAO-B indication pairing each carries real clinical or exam consequences.

## summary
Amphetamine acts indirectly (releasing and blocking reuptake of norepinephrine) without direct receptor agonism, while ephedrine shares most of these actions but does not significantly block reuptake. Amphetamine congeners map to distinct indications (methylphenidate/ADHD, modafinil/narcolepsy), and atomoxetine lacks abuse potential. Epinephrine is first-line for anaphylaxis, is not contraindicated by diabetes, and reverses with alpha-blocker pretreatment. Clonidine is used systemically (not topically) for blood pressure, and alpha-methyldopa needs metabolic activation to bind alpha-2 receptors. Anorexigens cause cardiotoxicity but not heart block. Dobutamine's catecholamine structure limits its route/duration; dopamine is preferred in specific settings. Sympathomimetics have distinct gynecological and ophthalmic uses.

## sections
### Definition
This article covers sympathomimetic drug-class pharmacology from "pharma MCQs bank .pdf" pp.144-159: indirect/mixed-acting mechanisms, congener-specific indications, epinephrine's clinical pharmacology, alpha-2 agonist classification, anorexigen cardiotoxicity, catecholamine inotrope selection, and gynecological/ophthalmic uses.

### Mechanism
Sympathomimetics act through a spectrum from purely indirect (amphetamine: releasing norepinephrine and blocking its reuptake) through mixed-acting (ephedrine: releasing norepinephrine and weak direct agonism, without significant reuptake blockade) to purely direct-acting catecholamines (epinephrine, dobutamine, dopamine), each profile determining a different clinical niche. Amphetamine congeners are matched to indications by which effect (CNS stimulation, appetite suppression, wakefulness promotion) predominates, and atomoxetine's selective-reuptake-inhibitor mechanism (rather than a releasing mechanism) is what spares it abuse potential. Epinephrine's combined alpha-1/beta-1/beta-2 action makes it uniquely suited to anaphylaxis, and pretreatment with an alpha blocker unmasks its beta-2 vasodilation as the classic epinephrine-reversal phenomenon. Alpha-2 agonists split into topically-applied vasoconstrictors and clonidine's systemic antihypertensive use, while alpha-methyldopa's prodrug conversion to alpha-methylnorepinephrine is what actually engages the alpha-2 receptor. Anorexigen cardiotoxicity and catecholamine inotrope selection (dobutamine's pharmacokinetic limits, dopamine's dose- and setting-dependent preference over dobutamine or isoprenaline) each follow from the specific receptor and metabolic profile of the drug in question, as do the distinct obstetric and ophthalmic uses of beta-2 and other sympathomimetic agonists.

### Key determinants
Whether a sympathomimetic acts directly, indirectly or by a mixed mechanism, and which receptor subtype or clinical setting a given fact concerns, determines which specific drug and indication pairing is correct.

### Clinical significance
Mismatching an amphetamine congener's indication, missing that atomoxetine lacks abuse potential, assuming diabetes contraindicates epinephrine, confusing clonidine's systemic use with a topical alpha-2 agonist's local one, or picking the wrong inotrope for renal impairment or heart block each carry real clinical consequences.

## hold_these
Amphetamine acts indirectly (releases NE, inhibits NE reuptake, CNS stimulant, anorexigenic) without direct agonism; ephedrine shares most actions but does NOT significantly inhibit NE reuptake.
Amphetamine congeners map to indications: methylphenidate/ADHD, modafinil/narcolepsy, dextroamphetamine or phentermine/short-term obesity; atomoxetine lacks abuse potential unlike true amphetamine congeners.

Sympathomimetic gynecological uses: premature labor, threatened abortion (not contraction ring); ophthalmic uses: fundus exam, spring catarrh, simple glaucoma.

Assuming diabetes mellitus is an absolute contraindication to epinephrine.
