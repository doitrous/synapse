## title
Incorrect statement about eukaryotic DNA polymerases

## question
Regarding DNA polymerases in eukaryotes, one of the following is NOT correct:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Polymerase α forms a complex with primase that catalyzes synthesis of RNA primers.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Polymerase α works as a complex with primase, and that primase activity is what synthesises the short RNA primers needed to start replication.

## answer_b
Polymerase β is concerned with DNA repair.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Polymerase β is the small, repair-associated eukaryotic polymerase, active in base excision repair rather than in bulk genome replication.

## answer_c
Polymerase γ is concerned with replication of mitochondrial DNAs.

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Polymerase γ is the dedicated mitochondrial DNA polymerase, replicating the mitochondrial genome independently of the nuclear replication machinery.

## answer_d
Polymerase ε catalyzes removal of RNA primer

## explanation_d
Correct — this is the false statement being asked for. Removal of RNA primers in eukaryotic replication is carried out by RNase H1 and FEN1 (flap endonuclease 1), not by polymerase ε. Polymerase ε is instead a high-fidelity, proofreading-capable polymerase associated with leading-strand-type synthesis, so crediting it with primer excision is the error that makes this option the one that is NOT correct.

## title
Correct statement about lagging-strand elongation

## question
Which statement about the elongation of the lagging strand during DNA replication is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It is synthesized in a 3' --> 5' direction.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. All DNA synthesis, including the lagging strand's, proceeds 5' to 3'; there is no known DNA polymerase that extends a strand 3' to 5'.

## answer_b
It progresses (grows) toward the replication fork.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Because synthesis is always 5' to 3' but the lagging-strand template runs the opposite way relative to the fork, the lagging strand is actually built in short pieces that grow away from the fork, not toward it.

## answer_c
It requires a short RNA primer to proceed.

## explanation_c
Correct. Each Okazaki fragment on the lagging strand needs its own short RNA primer, laid down by primase, before DNA polymerase can extend it — unlike the leading strand, which only needs one primer at the origin. This repeated priming is what makes lagging-strand synthesis discontinuous. It is one of the clearest distinguishing features between leading- and lagging-strand replication.

## answer_d
It is synthesized continuously.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Continuous synthesis describes the leading strand; the lagging strand is built discontinuously, as a series of separately primed Okazaki fragments later joined by ligase.

## title
Cell-cycle phase of DNA replication

## question
During which phase of the cell cycle is DNA replicated?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
G1 phase.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. G1 is a growth/preparatory gap phase before replication begins; the cell builds proteins and organelles here but does not yet copy its DNA.

## answer_b
S phase.

## explanation_b
Correct. The S (synthesis) phase of the cell cycle is defined by DNA replication: the entire genome is duplicated once, converting each chromosome from one to two sister chromatids. It sits between the G1 and G2 gap phases, and its completion is checked before the cell is allowed to enter mitosis.

## answer_c
G2 phase.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. G2 is the second gap phase, after replication is complete, during which the cell prepares for mitosis (e.g. further growth and checkpoint control) rather than copying DNA.

## answer_d
M phase.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. M phase is mitosis itself, when the already-duplicated chromosomes are segregated into two daughter cells; no new DNA synthesis occurs here.

## title
Role of topoisomerases in eukaryotic replication

## question
What is the role of topoisomerases in eukaryotic DNA replication?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Topoisomerase enzymes cut, uncoil and reseal the double stranded DNA.

## explanation_a
Correct. Topoisomerases relieve the positive supercoiling that builds up ahead of the advancing replication fork by transiently cutting one or both DNA strands, letting the helix rotate or pass through the break to release torsional strain, and then resealing the cut. This cut-uncoil-reseal cycle is what lets helicase keep unwinding the parental duplex without the DNA snarling on itself. Without topoisomerase activity, the accumulating supercoils would stall the fork.

## answer_b
Topoisomerase enzymes bind to the origin of replication sites within double stranded DNA.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Binding origins of replication is the role of origin-recognition proteins (e.g. the origin recognition complex), not topoisomerases.

## answer_c
Topoisomerase enzymes open up the double stranded DNA at the replication fork.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Opening up (unwinding) the double helix at the fork is the job of helicase, which breaks the hydrogen bonds between strands; topoisomerases act ahead of the fork on supercoiling, not on strand separation itself.

## answer_d
Topoisomerase enzymes join the Okazaki fragments together with phosphodiester bonds.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Joining Okazaki fragments with phosphodiester bonds is the role of DNA ligase, a distinct enzyme from topoisomerase.

## title
What DNA polymerase activity does not require

## question
DNA polymerase activity does not require one of the following:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
A template DNA.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this is required). DNA polymerase reads an existing strand as a template to decide which base to add next, so a template DNA strand is essential.

## answer_b
A primer with a free 5'-hydroxyl group.

## explanation_b
Correct — this is the one NOT required. DNA polymerase does not need a free 5'-hydroxyl on the primer; it adds new nucleotides onto the primer's free 3'-hydroxyl end, extending the chain in the 5' to 3' direction. A 5'-OH plays no role in the polymerisation reaction itself, which is why this is the requirement that does not actually apply.

## answer_c
dATP, dCTP, dGTP, and dTTP.

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. this is required). The four deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dATP, dCTP, dGTP, dTTP) are the actual building blocks incorporated into the new strand.

## answer_d
A primer with a free 3'-hydroxyl group.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. this is required). DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides onto a free 3'-hydroxyl group; this is the chemical basis of its 5'-to-3' synthesis direction and why it cannot start a chain de novo.

The direction of DNA synthesis is:

## subject
fnd

Incorrect. The 5'-to-3' rule is universal across organisms; it is a fundamental property of how DNA polymerases work, not something that varies by species.

## title
Synthesis pattern after fork formation

## question
After formation of the replication fork:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Both strands are synthesized discontinuously

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Only the lagging strand is synthesised discontinuously; the leading strand is synthesised continuously in one direction from a single primer.

## answer_b
One strand is synthesized continuously and the other discontinuously

## explanation_b
Correct. Because both template strands are antiparallel but DNA polymerase can only extend 5' to 3', one new strand (the leading strand) is built continuously toward the fork, while the other (the lagging strand) is built discontinuously, as a series of Okazaki fragments primed separately and later joined. This asymmetry is called semi-discontinuous replication.

## answer_c
Both strands are synthesized continuously

## explanation_c
Incorrect. If both strands were synthesised continuously, the lagging strand's template (which runs the 'wrong' way relative to the fork) could not be copied 5' to 3' without discontinuous fragments.

## answer_d
RNA primer is required only for the synthesis of one new strand.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Both new strands require RNA priming — the leading strand needs one primer at the origin, and the lagging strand needs a fresh primer for every Okazaki fragment, so priming is not limited to a single strand.

## title
Enzyme that forms the prokaryotic RNA primer

## question
Prokaryotic RNA primer is formed by:

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
DNA polymerase α.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Polymerase α is a eukaryotic enzyme (paired with its own primase activity); it is not the prokaryotic primer-forming enzyme this question asks about.

## answer_b
DNA polymerase III.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. DNA polymerase III is the main prokaryotic replicative polymerase, but it cannot start a new strand de novo — it extends the primer that primase has already laid down.

## answer_d
Telomerase.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Telomerase is a eukaryotic enzyme that extends the ends of linear chromosomes; it plays no role in prokaryotic replication, and prokaryotic chromosomes are typically circular and have no telomeres to maintain.

## title
Enzyme not required in DNA repair

## question
One of the following is not required in DNA repair:

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
An endonuclease.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this is required). An endonuclease makes the internal cut that excises the damaged stretch of DNA, opening the site for repair synthesis.

## answer_b
An exonuclease.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this is required). An exonuclease trims away the excised, damaged nucleotides from the cut end before new DNA is synthesised.

## answer_c
Topoisomerase.

## explanation_c
Correct. Topoisomerase relieves supercoiling ahead of replication forks and during other processes that unwind large stretches of DNA, but standard excision-repair pathways (base or nucleotide excision repair) do not depend on it — the repair patch is short and does not generate the topological strain that topoisomerase exists to resolve. This makes topoisomerase the enzyme in this list that repair does not require.

## answer_d
Repair DNA polymerase.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. this is required). A repair DNA polymerase fills in the gap left after the damaged stretch is excised, using the intact complementary strand as template.

## title
Enzyme that removes RNA primers in prokaryotic replication

## question
The following enzyme is responsible for removal of RNA primers during prokaryotic replication:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
DNA polymerase I

## explanation_a
Correct. DNA polymerase I has a 5'-to-3' exonuclease activity that removes the RNA primers left behind by primase, and it simultaneously fills the resulting gaps with DNA using its polymerase activity. This is the classic nick-translation step of prokaryotic replication, occurring immediately before DNA ligase seals the remaining nick. It is a small, repair-capable polymerase distinct from the main replicative polymerase III.

## answer_b
RNA primase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Primase is the enzyme that lays down the RNA primer in the first place; it does not remove it.

## answer_c
DNA polymerase III

## explanation_c
Incorrect. DNA polymerase III is the main replicative enzyme that extends the primer with new DNA; primer removal is a separate step carried out by polymerase I.

## answer_d
RNAse H

## explanation_d
Incorrect. RNase H can degrade RNA within an RNA:DNA hybrid in some organisms, but in the standard prokaryotic replication pathway taught here, primer excision and gap-filling is credited to DNA polymerase I's 5'→3' exonuclease activity.

## title
Eukaryote-versus-prokaryote replication difference

## question
Eukaryotes differ from prokaryote in mechanism of DNA replication in:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Use of DNA primer rather than RNA primer

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Both eukaryotes and prokaryotes use a short RNA primer, not a DNA primer, to initiate synthesis; this is not a point of difference between them.

## answer_b
Different enzyme for synthesis of lagging and leading strand

## explanation_b
Correct. Prokaryotes use one polymerase, DNA polymerase III, for both the leading and lagging strands. Eukaryotes instead use separate polymerases (classically ε- and δ-associated activities) for the two strands. This division of labour between two different enzymes is a genuine mechanistic difference between the two replication systems.

## answer_c
Discontinuous rather than semi-discontinuous replication

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Both eukaryotic and prokaryotic replication are semi-discontinuous (continuous leading strand, discontinuous lagging strand); neither system is purely discontinuous on both strands.

## answer_d
Unidirectional rather than semi-discontinuous replication

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Replication is semi-discontinuous (not unidirectional) in both eukaryotes and prokaryotes at each fork; this option also misstates the comparison being drawn.

Where Okazaki fragments occur and how they are joined

## question
Okazaki fragments occur on the _________ and are bonded together by _________.

Claude

C

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The leading strand is synthesised as one continuous piece, not as separate fragments; Okazaki fragments are a lagging-strand feature.

mRNA, anticodons

## explanation_b
Incorrect. mRNA and anticodons belong to translation, not to DNA replication or Okazaki fragment formation.

Lagging strand, ligase

Correct. Okazaki fragments are the short DNA pieces produced during discontinuous synthesis of the lagging strand, each starting with its own RNA primer. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. tRNA belongs to translation, not replication, and polymerase extends strands rather than sealing nicks between finished fragments — that final sealing step is ligase's role.

## title
Molecule that serves as the DNA replication primer

## question
Given that DNA polymerases are unable to synthesize DNA without a primer, what molecule serves as the primer for these enzymes during DNA replication?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Five carbon sugars

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A five-carbon sugar alone is not a nucleic acid and provides no base-paired 3'-OH end for a polymerase to extend from.

## answer_b
Deoxyribose alone

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Deoxyribose by itself, without attached bases or phosphates, cannot base-pair with the template strand and so cannot function as a primer.

## answer_c
A short RNA molecule

## explanation_c
Correct. Because DNA polymerases can only extend an existing 3'-OH end and cannot start a new chain from scratch, primase first lays down a short RNA molecule that base-pairs with the template. DNA polymerase then extends from this RNA primer's 3' end, and the RNA is later excised and replaced with DNA.

## answer_d
Proteins with free hydroxyl groups

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Primers are nucleic acid, not protein; a protein with free hydroxyl groups could not base-pair with the DNA template to be extended in sequence.
