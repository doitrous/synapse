## title
Germ-layer origin of epithelial tissue

## question
Epithelial tissue is derived from which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Ectoderm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ectoderm gives rise to some epithelia — skin, and the oral and anal canal linings — but not all of them.

## answer_b
Endoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Endoderm gives rise to the gut tube's lining and its glandular derivatives, but not to every epithelium in the body.

## answer_c
Mesoderm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mesoderm gives rise to endothelium and mesothelium, but not to the surface epithelia derived from ectoderm and endoderm.

## answer_d
Three germ layers

## explanation_d
Correct. Epithelial tissue arises from all three germ layers depending on the site: ectoderm for skin and the oral/anal canal linings, endoderm for the gut tube and its glandular outgrowths, and mesoderm for endothelium and mesothelium. A question naming one germ layer as the source of epithelium is testing whether a student mistakenly generalises from the most familiar example, the ectodermal skin, to every epithelium in the body. Recognising all three origins is the foundation for later linking a specific epithelium to its embryological derivation.

## title
Naming a gland by its branched duct

## question
Which of the following is the term for a gland with a branched duct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Compound gland

## explanation_a
Correct. A compound gland is defined by having a branched duct system — the duct divides before reaching the secretory units, as in the pancreas and the major salivary glands. This is a separate classifying axis from whether a gland has a duct at all (exocrine, endocrine or mixed), and from the shape of its secretory units (tubular, acinar or alveolar). Recognising duct branching as the specific criterion for "compound" prevents it being confused with either of those other two classification axes.

## answer_b
Coiled gland

## explanation_b
Incorrect. "Coiled" describes the shape of a secretory unit (as in a coiled tubular gland such as a sweat gland), not the branching of its duct.

## answer_c
Simple gland

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A simple gland has a single, unbranched duct reaching the surface — the opposite of the branched-duct criterion the question asks for.

## answer_d
Simple branched gland

## explanation_d
Incorrect. "Simple branched gland" describes a gland with one duct serving several secretory units, which is a different (and less commonly examined) category from a compound gland's branched duct.

Cell of origin of macrophages

## question
Which cell is the origin of macrophages?

## subject

## status

## explanation_a

## explanation_b
Incorrect. B lymphocytes are a separate lineage of the immune system, committed to antibody production, not to phagocytic macrophage function.

Blood monocytes

Adipoblasts

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adipoblasts are the precursor of adipocytes (fat-storing cells), an entirely different differentiation pathway from the monocyte-macrophage lineage.

## title
Fusion of primary lysosomes with undigested remnants

## question
Which of the following is the term for the fusion of primary lysosomes with undigested remnants?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Heterolysosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A heterolysosome is a primary lysosome fused with a phagosome (material taken in from outside the cell) — a specific type of secondary lysosome named by what it fused with, not the end state of undigested remnants.

## answer_b
Multivesicular bodies

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A multivesicular body is a primary lysosome fused with a pinocytic vesicle (fluid taken in from outside), again named by what it fused with rather than by its undigested end state.

## answer_c
Autolysosomes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An autolysosome is a primary lysosome fused with the cell's own worn-out organelles, a third named-by-origin secondary lysosome — not the end-state term the question asks for.

## answer_d
Residual bodies

## explanation_d
Correct. Whichever secondary lysosome forms — heterolysosome, multivesicular body or autolysosome — what is left undigested after its digestive work becomes a residual body. The residual body is either discharged from the cell by exocytosis or retained as lipofuscin, the age pigment that accumulates particularly in non-dividing cells such as cardiac muscle and neurons. This end-state terminology is distinct from the three origin-based names of secondary lysosomes.

## title
Organelle handling drug detoxification

## question
Enzymes used for detoxification of some drugs are attached to which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Smooth endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_a
Correct. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER) is a membranous organelle of branching, ribosome-free tubules concerned with lipid and steroid synthesis, and it also carries the enzymes that detoxify drugs — well developed in cells such as hepatocytes that do both jobs. On light microscopy it does not stain as a distinct structure but, where abundant, gives the cytoplasm an acidophilic tinge, distinguishing it from the basophilic cytoplasm of protein-exporting cells. Its lack of bound ribosomes, continuous with rough ER, is the structural feature that separates it from the rough ER named in option D.

## answer_b
Lysosomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lysosomes digest material that has been taken into the cell or worn-out organelles; drug detoxification is a distinct SER-based function, not a lysosomal one.

## answer_c
Mitochondria

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mitochondria generate ATP through oxidative metabolism; they are not the site of drug-detoxifying enzymes.

## answer_d
Rough endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Rough endoplasmic reticulum, studded with ribosomes, synthesises and segregates protein for export — a different job from the ribosome-free smooth ER's drug-detoxification role.

If you are asked to put the arm in pronation, the palm of the hand should be facing which of the following?

## subject

Anterior

## explanation_a

Incorrect. Superiorly describes an up/down (vertical) direction, not the rotational movement of the forearm that pronation produces.

Incorrect. "Lipid channels" through which both protein and lipid substances diffuse are not a real feature of the plasma membrane — the membrane is a lipid bilayer with embedded proteins, not a channel system for lipids and proteins together.

## answer_d
Two layers of proteins one is hydrophilic and the other is hydrophobic

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The membrane's phospholipid bilayer, not a protein bilayer, is the structure with a hydrophilic side and a hydrophobic side; describing two protein layers this way misattributes the amphipathic property.
