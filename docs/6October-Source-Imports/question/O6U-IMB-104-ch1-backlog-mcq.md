## title
Nucleotide to nucleoside conversion

## question
A nucleotide is converted into a nucleoside by the removal of a:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Purine base

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Removing the base would leave a bare sugar-phosphate, not a nucleoside; a nucleoside must still carry its base.

## answer_b
Pyrimidine base

## explanation_b
Incorrect. As with a purine base, removing the pyrimidine base would leave a sugar-phosphate rather than a nucleoside.

## answer_c
Pentose

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The pentose sugar is a defining part of a nucleoside (base + sugar); removing it would leave a free base, not a nucleoside.

## answer_d
Phosphate

## explanation_d
Correct. A nucleotide is a nucleoside (base + pentose sugar) with one or more phosphate groups attached. Hydrolysing that phosphate off leaves the base-sugar unit, which is exactly the definition of a nucleoside. This is the reverse of phosphorylating a nucleoside to build a nucleotide.

## question
If a DNA molecule contains 30% thymine, then what is its guanine content?

Incorrect. 30% would only be correct if it equalled thymine's own percentage, but this question asks for guanine, whose percentage is fixed by cytosine (G=C), not by thymine.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 40% is the combined G+C percentage (100% - 60% A+T), not guanine's own percentage; guanine alone is half of that, 20%.

The bonds that link the base pairs in the DNA double helix are

## subject

Incorrect. Hydrophobic interactions contribute to base-stacking between adjacent bases along a strand, but they are not the bonds directly linking a base pair across the two strands.
