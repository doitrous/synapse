## title
Defective virus and helper virus

## question
What is the defining characteristic of a defective virus?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It needs a helper virus for productive multiplication

## explanation_a
Correct. A defective virus lacks sufficient or complete nucleic acid to independently direct a full replication cycle, so it depends on co-infection with a fully competent helper virus, which supplies the missing function, before it can complete productive multiplication. Hepatitis D virus's dependence on concurrent Hepatitis B infection is the classic clinical example of this relationship.

## answer_b
It is a virus particle that contains sufficient nucleic acid

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This is the opposite of the definition: a defective virus is defined by lacking, not containing, sufficient nucleic acid to replicate on its own.

## answer_c
It can cause infection alone, without any other virus present

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A defective virus specifically cannot cause productive infection alone -- that is exactly why it requires a helper virus.

## answer_d
It can code for its own coat protein without assistance

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A defective virus typically cannot code for everything it needs, including a functional coat protein, on its own.

## title
Taenia solium epidemiology

## question
Which of the following statements about Taenia solium is true?

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

A

## answer_a
It is cosmopolitan, found wherever pork or pork products are eaten

## explanation_a
Correct. Taenia solium (the pork tapeworm) has a cosmopolitan distribution, occurring wherever pork or pork products are eaten and sanitation allows the pig-to-human transmission cycle to continue. Recognising this broad, pork-linked distribution matters clinically, since it predicts where the more dangerous larval-stage disease, cysticercosis, also clusters.

## answer_b
It is transmitted through ingestion of raw fish

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Raw fish transmits fish tapeworm (Diphyllobothrium latum), not Taenia solium, which is a pork-associated cestode.

It is transmitted through ingestion of contaminated vegetables

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Contaminated vegetables are a route for faecal-oral protozoa and some nematode eggs, not for Taenia solium, whose route is undercooked pork.

## answer_d
It is a disease specific to group infection outbreaks

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Taenia solium is not defined as a group-infection-specific disease; its epidemiology tracks pork consumption, not outbreak clustering.

Cells that cannot be passaged more than once

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A continuous line's defining feature is indefinite, not single, passage capability.

## question
How should you deal with a patient under droplet precaution?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
A mask is worn during close contact

## explanation_a
Correct. Droplet precautions are for pathogens spread by large respiratory droplets over short distances; the key control measure is wearing a surgical mask when in close contact with the patient. Recognising mask-in-close-contact as the defining droplet measure helps distinguish it quickly from airborne precautions, which instead require a negative-pressure room.

## answer_b
The patient is isolated in a private negative-pressured room

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A private negative-pressured room is an airborne-precaution measure, used for pathogens that travel on small aerosolised particles over longer distances, not the droplet-precaution measure.

## answer_c
The patient must be discharged from the hospital

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Discharging the patient is never an infection-control measure and does not address ongoing transmission risk to others.

## answer_d
The patient is isolated in a private positive-pressured room

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A positive-pressured room is used to protect an immunocompromised patient from the environment (protective isolation), not to contain a patient who is a source of droplet transmission.
