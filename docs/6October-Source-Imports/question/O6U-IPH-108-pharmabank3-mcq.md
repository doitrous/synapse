## title
Low-dose dopamine causes renal vasodilation via D1 receptors

## question
Low-dose dopamine causes renal, mesenteric, and coronary vasodilatation through:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Beta-1 receptors

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Beta-1 stimulation is dopamine's intermediate-dose action (increased cardiac output via inotropy), not its low-dose renal/splanchnic vasodilator effect.

## answer_b
Beta-2 receptors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dopamine has no meaningful beta-2 agonist activity at any clinically used dose.

## answer_c
Alpha-1 receptors

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Alpha-1 stimulation is dopamine's high-dose action, producing generalized vasoconstriction rather than the selective renal/mesenteric/coronary vasodilation seen at low dose.

## answer_d
Alpha-2 receptors

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dopamine does not act through alpha-2 receptors to produce this effect.

## answer_e
D-receptors

## explanation_e
Correct. At a low, "renal dose" infusion rate, dopamine selectively stimulates dopaminergic D1 receptors in the renal, mesenteric and coronary vascular beds, producing local vasodilation that increases blood flow to these organs. This D1-mediated action is distinct from dopamine's beta-1-mediated inotropic effect at intermediate doses and its alpha-1-mediated vasoconstrictor effect at high doses, making dopamine's receptor selectivity a dose-dependent phenomenon. This is exactly why low-dose dopamine was historically used to try to preserve renal perfusion in shock states, even though the D1-mediated benefit is confined to this specific low-dose window.

## answer_b
Isoprenaline

## explanation_b
Correct. Isoprenaline (isoproterenol) is a pure, nonselective beta-agonist with essentially no alpha activity, so its dominant effects are positive chronotropy and dromotropy (increased SA node rate and AV conduction) without the vasoconstriction epinephrine or norepinephrine would add. ## answer_e

Incorrect. Dobutamine's selective beta-1 inotropic action supports cardiac output in cardiogenic shock and heart failure, not the chronotropic support needed in heart block.

Correct. Incorrect. Norepinephrine's beta-1 stimulation of the juxtaglomerular apparatus genuinely increases renin secretion, contributing to sustained pressure elevation via the renin-angiotensin system.

## answer_e
Increases angiotensin II level

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The renin secretion norepinephrine stimulates raises angiotensin II, a further genuine pressor mechanism.
