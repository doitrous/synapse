## title
Solution with excess hydroxyl ions

## question
Which of the following is a solution that contains hydroxyl ions in excess of hydrogen ions?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Acidic solution

## explanation_a
Incorrect. An acidic solution has hydrogen-ion concentration in excess of hydroxyl-ion concentration, the reverse of what the question describes.

## answer_b
Basic solution

## explanation_b
Correct. A basic solution has pH above 7, with hydrogen-ion concentration below hydroxyl-ion concentration -- exactly the hydroxyl-in-excess state the question describes. This is a general-chemistry classification, distinct from (though related to) the human body's own tightly regulated blood pH set-point of 7.35-7.45.

## answer_c
Hypotonic solution

## explanation_c
Incorrect. "Hypotonic" describes a solution's relative solute concentration compared to another solution (relevant to osmosis), not its hydrogen/hydroxyl ion balance.

## answer_d
Neutral solution

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A neutral solution has equal hydrogen-ion and hydroxyl-ion concentrations, not an excess of either.

## title
Acid-base consequence of severe exercise

## question
Severe muscle exercise usually leads to which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Metabolic acidosis

## explanation_a
Correct. Severe muscular exercise outpaces oxidative metabolism's oxygen supply, so muscle shifts to anaerobic glycolysis and produces lactic acid faster than it can be buffered or cleared. This fixed (non-volatile) acid load drops blood pH by a metabolic, not respiratory, route -- the raised breathing rate seen during exercise is the compensatory response to this acid load, not its cause.

## answer_b
Respiratory acidosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Respiratory acidosis results from CO2 retention (inadequate ventilation), which is the opposite of what happens during exercise, when ventilation increases.

## answer_c
Metabolic alkalosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Metabolic alkalosis results from a base excess or acid loss (such as prolonged vomiting), not from the acid-producing anaerobic metabolism of exercise.

## answer_d
Respiratory alkalosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Respiratory alkalosis results from hyperventilation blowing off too much CO2 relative to the body's acid production, which is not the primary process during severe exercise.

## title
Amino-acid composition of glutathione

## question
Which group of amino acids contribute to the structure of glutathione?

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Glycine is a genuine component of glutathione, but alanine and tryptophan are not — this combination substitutes two wrong residues.

## answer_b
Glutamic, histidine and proline

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Neither histidine nor proline are components of glutathione; only the correct answer's three residues are.

## answer_c
Glutamic, aspartic and ornithine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Aspartic acid and ornithine are not components of glutathione, though glutamic acid is.

Glutamic, cysteine and glycine

## explanation_d
Correct. Glutathione is the tripeptide glutamate-cysteine-glycine. Its biologically active group is the sulfhydryl (-SH) contributed by the cysteine residue, which is oxidised in its redox reactions. This antioxidant role is why glutathione is central to protecting cells against oxidative and toxic damage.

## title
Direction of water movement in osmosis

## question
Solution A is more concentrated than solution B, and a semi-permeable membrane separates them. Which of the following is correct about this condition?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Water will pass from solution A to solution B by osmosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Osmosis moves water toward the more concentrated (higher-solute) side, not away from it, so water does not move from A (more concentrated) to B.

## answer_b
Water will pass from solution B to solution A by osmosis

## explanation_b
Correct. Osmosis is the movement of solvent (water) across a semipermeable membrane toward the side with the higher solute concentration, diluting it and equalising the concentration gradient. Since solution A is more concentrated, water moves from the less concentrated solution B into A.

## answer_c
Molecules will pass from solution A to solution B by active transport

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The scenario describes a semipermeable membrane, which is defined by letting water (and not the solute) cross — it does not describe active transport of solute molecules, which requires a specific carrier and energy input.

## answer_d
Molecules will pass from solution B to solution A passively

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The question specifies a semipermeable membrane, which by definition restricts solute molecules from crossing passively; the movement described is water by osmosis, not solute molecules.

## title
Effect of parasympathetic supply to the pelvis

## question
The parasympathetic supply to the pelvis may lead to which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Contract wall of the urinary bladder and rectum

## explanation_a
Correct. Parasympathetic outflow to the pelvis leaves the cord as the pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2-S4). These synapse in terminal ganglia near the target organs and, on activation, contract the detrusor muscle of the bladder wall and the rectal wall, producing micturition and defecation respectively. The same outflow also mediates erection and female genital vasodilatation.

## answer_b
Relax wall of urinary bladder and rectum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Relaxation of the bladder/rectal wall is a sympathetic (storage-phase) effect, the opposite of the parasympathetic (voiding-phase) effect being tested.

## answer_c
Contract sphincter of urinary bladder

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Contracting the bladder sphincter (to retain urine) is a sympathetic effect; the parasympathetic system instead relaxes the internal sphincter to permit voiding.

## answer_d
Ejaculation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ejaculation is primarily a sympathetic function; the parasympathetic pelvic outflow instead mediates erection, defecation and micturition.

## title
Indication for atropine

## question
Atropine is given as a treatment for which of the following conditions?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Spasmodic contractions leading to colics

## explanation_a
Correct. Atropine is a muscarinic (antimuscarinic) receptor antagonist that, by blocking parasympathetic muscarinic tone on visceral smooth muscle, relaxes spasmodic contractions of the gut and biliary/renal tract -- the mechanism behind its use for colicky abdominal pain. Note this printed key is a plurality only (81/217, 37%), so it is worth a second look even though the underlying pharmacology is standard.

## answer_b
To decrease rapid heart rate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Atropine increases, not decreases, heart rate by blocking vagal muscarinic tone on the SA node -- the opposite of this distractor.

## answer_c
To constrict the pupil

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Atropine dilates, not constricts, the pupil by blocking the iris sphincter's muscarinic tone -- the opposite of this distractor.

## answer_d
To cause stronger erection

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Atropine has no established indication for producing erection; erection is primarily a parasympathetic-mediated process that atropine would tend to blunt, not enhance.

## title
Drug class for bronchial asthma

## question
A person with bronchial asthma (narrowing of the bronchi) is most likely to be given which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Sympathomimetic

## explanation_a
Correct. Bronchial asthma narrows the airway through bronchial smooth-muscle contraction. Sympathomimetics (beta-2 adrenergic agonists) relax bronchial smooth muscle, producing bronchodilation, which is why they are the drug class used to relieve an asthma attack.

## answer_b
Sympatholytic

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A sympatholytic would block sympathetic (bronchodilating) tone, tending to worsen rather than relieve bronchoconstriction.

## answer_c
Parasympathomimetic

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A parasympathomimetic would enhance parasympathetic (bronchoconstricting) tone, worsening the narrowing rather than relieving it.

## answer_d
Phentolamine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phentolamine is an alpha-adrenergic blocker, acting on vascular smooth muscle rather than producing the beta-2-mediated bronchodilation asthma treatment requires.
