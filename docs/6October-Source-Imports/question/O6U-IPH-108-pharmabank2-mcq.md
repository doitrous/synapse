## title
Atropine treats mushroom poisoning

## question
Indicate a drug which is effective in the treatment of mushroom (muscarine) poisoning:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Atropine

## explanation_a
Correct. Many poisonous mushrooms contain muscarine, a direct muscarinic agonist, and their toxicity produces a muscarinic-excess picture (salivation, lacrimation, GI cramping, bradycardia). Atropine, by competitively blocking the same muscarinic receptors, is the antidote of choice. It reverses the muscarinic-excess picture regardless of whether the excess acetylcholine-like stimulation comes from muscarine itself, a cholinesterase inhibitor, or a direct-acting cholinomimetic overdose.

## answer_b
Pralidoxime

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pralidoxime reactivates acetylcholinesterase after organophosphate binding; it has no role in a direct muscarinic-agonist poisoning such as mushroom (muscarine) toxicity, where the enzyme itself is not inhibited.

## answer_c
Homatropine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Homatropine is itself an antimuscarinic mydriatic used ophthalmically, not the systemic antidote used for mushroom poisoning.

## answer_d
Diazepam

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Diazepam is a benzodiazepine used for seizures or agitation; it does not antagonize the muscarinic-excess picture of mushroom poisoning.

## answer_e
Dicyclomine

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Dicyclomine is itself an antimuscarinic antispasmodic, not the systemic antidote used for this specific indication.
