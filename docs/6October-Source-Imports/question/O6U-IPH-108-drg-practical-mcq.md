## title
Astringent action

## question
A substance with a vasoconstrictor and protein-denaturing effect is said to have a/an ___ action.

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Astringent

## explanation_a
Correct. An astringent is defined specifically by this combined action: vasoconstriction of superficial blood vessels together with denaturation (precipitation) of surface proteins. Together these two effects shrink and toughen exposed tissue, reduce secretions, and help control minor bleeding or oozing. This mechanism-based definition is what distinguishes an astringent from other topical agent categories.

## answer_b
Emollient

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An emollient softens and soothes the skin surface rather than constricting vessels or denaturing protein.

## answer_c
Antiseptic

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An antiseptic acts by killing or inhibiting microorganisms, not by vasoconstriction or protein denaturation.

## answer_d
Rubefacient

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A rubefacient causes localized redness and warmth through vasodilation, the opposite of an astringent's vasoconstriction.

Water

## explanation_a
Correct. Atropine sulfate is the salt form of the alkaloid atropine, and converting the free-base alkaloid to its sulfate salt makes it soluble in water, unlike the free base itself. This water solubility is exactly why atropine is formulated and dispensed clinically as atropine sulfate rather than as the free base, allowing aqueous injectable and eye-drop preparations. Free-base alkaloids in general are insoluble in water, which is the whole reason clinically used alkaloid drugs are converted to salt forms.
