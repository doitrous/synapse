## title
Molecules facilitating RNA polymerase II association with core promoters

## question
Which types of molecules interact with eukaryotic mRNA gene core promoter sites to facilitate the association of RNA polymerase II?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Termination factors.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Termination factors act at the end of transcription, not to help RNA polymerase II first bind the promoter.

## answer_b
General, or basal transcription factors (i.e., the GTFs).

## explanation_b
Correct. The general (basal) transcription factors are the proteins that assemble at the core promoter and enable RNA polymerase II to bind and initiate transcription there. For RNA polymerase II these include TFIIA, TFIIB, TFIID (containing TBP), TFIIE, TFIIF and TFIIH. They are required at essentially every RNA polymerase II promoter, unlike gene-specific regulatory proteins such as activators.

## answer_c
Elongation factors.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Elongation factors act after initiation, helping RNA polymerase move along the gene body, not to help it first bind the promoter.

## answer_d
GTPases.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. GTPases are a broad enzyme class hydrolysing GTP in many different pathways; they are not specifically the molecules that dock RNA polymerase II onto core promoters.

## title
Which histone amino acid is typically acetylated

## question
Which of the following histone amino acids are typically acetylated?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Lysine

## explanation_a
Correct. Histone acetyltransferases add an acetyl group to the epsilon-amino group of lysine side chains on histone tails. This neutralises the lysine's normal positive charge. Neutralising that charge loosens the electrostatic contacts between the histone and the negatively charged DNA backbone, making the local chromatin more accessible.

## answer_b
Arginine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Arginine carries a different, guanidinium side chain and is instead a common site for methylation, not the standard acetylation target.

## answer_c
Asparagine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Asparagine's amide side chain is not a site of histone acetylation.

## answer_d
Leucine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Leucine's hydrophobic side chain has no accessible group for acetylation.

## title
What eukaryotic mRNA feature contributes to message half-life

## question
Which of the following features of eukaryotic mRNA contribute importantly to message half-life?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
5' UTR sequences

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The 5' UTR carries regulatory sequence for translation efficiency, but it is not the feature primarily responsible for protecting the transcript from degradation.

## answer_b
The promoter

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The promoter is a DNA element that directs where transcription starts; it is not part of the mature mRNA and has no direct effect on that transcript's stability.

## answer_c
The operator

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The operator is a bacterial DNA regulatory element controlling transcription; it is not a feature of eukaryotic mRNA molecules at all.

## answer_d
The poly (A) tail

## explanation_d
Correct. The 3' poly(A) tail, added during mRNA processing, protects the transcript from 3'-to-5' exonuclease degradation (working together with the 5' cap, which protects the other end). This protection is a major reason mature, processed mRNAs are far more stable -- have a longer half-life -- than an unprotected transcript would be.

## title
The incorrect statement about DNA methylation regulation

## question
Concerning epigenetic regulation of eukaryotic gene expression via DNA methylation, One of the following statements is Incorrect:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Methylation of cytosine to form 5'methylcytosine by a DNA methyltransferase.

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this statement IS correct). DNA methyltransferases do methylate cytosine to form 5-methylcytosine.

## answer_b
The target cytosine is usually located next to a guanine residue, forming a CpG island, which is often near or in the promoter region of a gene.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this statement IS correct). Methylated cytosines are typically found next to guanine, at CpG sites often clustered as CpG islands near gene promoters.

## answer_c
It is associated with inducing the activities of certain genes.

## explanation_c
Correct (this is the incorrect statement). Promoter-region DNA methylation is characteristically associated with silencing/repressing a gene's activity, not inducing it. Methylated CpG islands recruit proteins and chromatin states that shut transcription down. That is the opposite direction from what this statement claims, which is why it is the one that is incorrect.

## answer_d
It is a mechanism for regulating gene expression during cell differentiation, particularly in fetal development.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. this statement IS correct). DNA methylation patterns are indeed established and changed during cell differentiation, including fetal development, as part of normal epigenetic regulation.

## title
The incorrect statement about chromatin remodeling

## question
Concerning chromatin remodeling One of the following statements is Incorrect:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Is the changes in the state of the chromatin, to make the desired region accessible to RNAP and other proteins (eg, transcriptional factors).

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this statement IS correct). Chromatin remodeling is indeed the process of changing chromatin state to make a region accessible to RNA polymerase and other proteins.

## answer_b
Histone acetylation is one of the mechanisms of chromatin remodeling.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this statement IS correct). Histone acetylation is one of the well-established mechanisms of chromatin remodeling.

## answer_c
Histone acetylation removes a negative charge from the ε-amino group of the lysine, thereby reducing the electrostatic interactions between the histones and the negatively charged DNA.

## explanation_c
Correct (this is the incorrect statement). A lysine side chain's epsilon-amino group is positively charged, not negatively charged, under physiological conditions, and it is this positive charge that normally strengthens histone-DNA electrostatic attraction. Acetylation removes that positive charge (converting the amino group to an uncharged amide), loosening histone-DNA contacts -- so the statement's claim that acetylation removes a "negative" charge from the lysine reverses the actual charge involved.

## answer_d
The acetyl groups can be removed by histone deacetylases (HDACs) to form inactive chromatin.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. this statement IS correct). Histone deacetylases (HDACs) do remove acetyl groups, restoring the positive charge and favouring more condensed, inactive chromatin.

## title
The correct statement about enhancers

## question
Enhancers:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Are trans-acting sequences.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Enhancers are cis-acting DNA sequences (they act on the same DNA molecule they sit on), bound by trans-acting proteins -- the sequence itself is not "trans-acting".

## answer_b
They act by binding certain proteins that inhibit the binding of transcription factors to the promoter.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Enhancers act by binding activator proteins that help recruit or stabilise the transcription machinery at the promoter, not by inhibiting transcription-factor binding.

## answer_c
They can be close to or thousands of base pairs away from the promoter (upstream or downstream) and they can occur on either strand of DNA.

## explanation_c
Correct. Enhancers can sit close to or many thousands of base pairs from the promoter they regulate, upstream or downstream. They can also function on either DNA strand. Both properties are explained by DNA looping, which lets an enhancer contact the promoter-bound transcription machinery regardless of the distance or orientation involved.

## answer_d
Are sites of DNA acetylation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. DNA itself is not the substrate of the acetylation discussed in this chapter -- histone protein tails are acetylated, not the DNA sequence, and enhancers are not defined as acetylation sites.
