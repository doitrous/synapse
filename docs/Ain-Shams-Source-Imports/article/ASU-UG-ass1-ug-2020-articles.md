## summary
Renal stones composed of uric acid, which form in the setting of persistently acidic, uric-acid-supersaturated urine such as occurs in gout, are classically smooth-surfaced and yellow (in contrast to the rough, spiculated appearance of calcium oxalate stones), and, like struvite stones, can enlarge to fill the renal pelvis and calyces in a branching "staghorn" configuration.

### Definition

### Mechanism
Renal stones composed of uric acid, which form in the setting of persistently acidic, uric-acid-supersaturated urine such as occurs in gout, are classically smooth-surfaced and yellow (in contrast to the rough, spiculated appearance of calcium oxalate stones), and, like struvite stones, can enlarge to fill the renal pelvis and calyces in a branching "staghorn" configuration.

### Key determinants
Assuming only struvite (infection) stones form a staghorn calculus; pure uric acid stones, as seen in gout, can also enlarge into a staghorn configuration, and are distinguished by their smooth, yellow gross appearance and radiolucency rather than the rougher struvite stone.
