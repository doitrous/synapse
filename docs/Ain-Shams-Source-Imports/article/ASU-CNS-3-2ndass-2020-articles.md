## sections
### Definition
Meningioma is a tumour of arachnoid cap cells that arises attached to the dura mater (a dural-based mass). Histologically, meningioma cells classically form whorled (concentric, onion-skin) patterns of spindle-shaped cells, distinct from the hyalinisation/calcification of those whorl centres into psammoma bodies (a separate, secondary histological feature seen in a subset of meningiomas). The combination of firm dural attachment and whorled architecture is the classic gross/microscopic recognition pattern for this tumour.

### Mechanism
Identify meningioma from its classic dural-based attachment and whorled spindle-cell histological pattern.

### Key determinants
Confusing meningioma's whorled spindle-cell architecture and dural attachment (the tumour's basic recognition pattern) with the separate, secondary feature of psammoma body calcification within some whorl centres.

### Clinical significance
Meningioma is the most common extra-axial (dural-based) primary CNS tumour in adults, typically benign (WHO grade I) and surgically resectable.

### Common misconceptions

## summary
This ASU Year 3 CNS slice covers: The amygdala is the limbic structure central to processing and generating fear. In experimental animals, electrical stimulation of the amygdala produces a stereotyped fear/defensive-avoidance response (freezing, autonomic arousal, avoidance behaviour), while bilateral amygdala lesions blunt fear and produce placidity, as in the Klüver-Bucy syndrome. This functional role is distinct from the amygdala's anatomical connections (e.g. via the stria terminalis to the hypothalamus), which describe its wiring rather than its behavioural output on stimulation.

## sections
### Definition
The amygdala is the limbic structure central to processing and generating fear. In experimental animals, electrical stimulation of the amygdala produces a stereotyped fear/defensive-avoidance response (freezing, autonomic arousal, avoidance behaviour), while bilateral amygdala lesions blunt fear and produce placidity, as in the Klüver-Bucy syndrome. This functional role is distinct from the amygdala's anatomical connections (e.g. via the stria terminalis to the hypothalamus), which describe its wiring rather than its behavioural output on stimulation.

### Mechanism
State that stimulation of the amygdala produces a fear/defensive-avoidance behavioural response.

### Key determinants
Confusing the amygdala's stimulation effect (fear/defensive response) with its lesion effect (placidity, blunted fear, as in Klüver-Bucy syndrome) — stimulation and ablation of the same structure produce opposite behavioural changes.

### Clinical significance
The amygdala's role in fear generation underlies its involvement in anxiety disorders and PTSD.

### Common misconceptions
Confusing the amygdala's stimulation effect (fear/defensive response) with its lesion effect (placidity, blunted fear, as in Klüver-Bucy syndrome) — stimulation and ablation of the same structure produce opposite behavioural changes.

## sections
### Definition
While awaiting CSF culture results, empiric antibiotic therapy for suspected bacterial meningitis in an adult over about 50 years old combines vancomycin (covering resistant/penicillin-non-susceptible Streptococcus pneumoniae, including MRSA), a third-generation cephalosporin such as ceftriaxone (covering typical meningitis pathogens including Neisseria meningitidis and susceptible S. pneumoniae), and ampicillin (added specifically to cover Listeria monocytogenes, a pathogen of particular concern in older and immunocompromised patients that is intrinsically resistant to cephalosporins).

### Mechanism
State that empiric bacterial meningitis therapy in an older adult combines vancomycin, ceftriaxone and ampicillin, with ampicillin added for Listeria coverage.

### Key determinants
Forgetting that ampicillin is added specifically for Listeria coverage in older/immunocompromised patients, since Listeria is intrinsically resistant to the cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) that otherwise covers most typical meningitis pathogens.

### Clinical significance
Prompt, correctly-targeted empiric therapy (including Listeria coverage in older patients) is critical, since bacterial meningitis has high mortality if treatment is delayed or incorrectly targeted.

### Common misconceptions
