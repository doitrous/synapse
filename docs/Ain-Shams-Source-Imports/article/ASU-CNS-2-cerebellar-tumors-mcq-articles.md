### Definition
Medulloblastoma is the classic embryonal cerebellar tumour of childhood, histologically highly cellular with sheets of small round blue cells showing anaplasia, brisk mitotic activity and Homer Wright rosettes (tumour cells radially arranged around a central neuropil core). It is graded WHO Grade IV, the highest malignancy grade, yet is characteristically radiosensitive, a property exploited in adjuvant treatment after surgical resection.

### Mechanism
Identify medulloblastoma from the combination of Homer Wright rosettes, anaplasia/mitoses, WHO Grade IV grading and radiosensitivity.

### Key determinants
Do not confuse Homer Wright rosettes (medulloblastoma) with Flexner-Wintersteiner rosettes (retinoblastoma/pineoblastoma) or true ependymal rosettes (ependymoma).

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

## summary

## sections
### Definition
Hemangioblastoma, a slow-growing benign cerebellar tumour (WHO Grade I) often associated with von Hippel-Lindau disease, is composed microscopically of two components: large, lipid-vacuolated ("foamy") stromal cells and a dense network of thin-walled, delicate capillary-sized blood vessels, giving the tumour its highly vascular character. The stromal cells are thought to be the true neoplastic element, with the vasculature a secondary, reactive proliferation.

### Mechanism
State that hemangioblastoma's histology shows large vacuolated stromal cells amid thin-walled capillary channels.

### Key determinants
Do not confuse the vacuolated stromal cells of hemangioblastoma with foamy macrophages of a demyelinating lesion, or with metastatic clear-cell renal cell carcinoma, which can mimic hemangioblastoma histologically.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse the vacuolated stromal cells of hemangioblastoma with foamy macrophages of a demyelinating lesion, or with metastatic clear-cell renal cell carcinoma, which can mimic hemangioblastoma histologically.

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: isochromosome 17q is the most common cytogenetic abnormality in medulloblastoma, loss of 17p (TP53 locus) with duplication of 17q.

## sections
### Definition
The single most common cytogenetic abnormality in medulloblastoma is isochromosome 17q, in which the short arm of chromosome 17 (17p, containing the TP53 tumour-suppressor locus) is lost and replaced by a duplicated long arm (17q). This karyotypic finding is distinct from the molecular-subtype markers used in the newer WHO integrated classification.

### Mechanism
State that isochromosome 17q is the genetic abnormality most commonly associated with medulloblastoma.

### Key determinants
Do not confuse the karyotypic isochromosome 17q finding with the WNT/SHH molecular-subtype markers used in the newer WHO integrated classification — they are different diagnostic axes.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse the karyotypic isochromosome 17q finding with the WNT/SHH molecular-subtype markers used in the newer WHO integrated classification.

## hold_these
Isochromosome 17q (loss of 17p with duplication of 17q) is the genetic abnormality most commonly associated with medulloblastoma.

## lose_the_mark
Do not confuse isochromosome 17q with the WNT/SHH molecular-subtype markers used in the newer WHO integrated classification.

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: nuclear beta-catenin immunopositivity identifies the WNT-activated molecular subtype of medulloblastoma, one of four WHO-recognised subtypes.

## sections
### Definition
The WHO integrated diagnosis of medulloblastoma recognises four core molecular subtypes: WNT-activated, SHH-activated, Group 3 and Group 4. Nuclear (rather than membranous or cytoplasmic) beta-catenin immunopositivity is the immunohistochemical hallmark of the WNT-activated subtype, and this subtype carries the most favourable prognosis of the four.

### Mechanism
Identify nuclear beta-catenin positivity as diagnostic of the WNT-activated molecular subtype of medulloblastoma.

### Key determinants
Do not confuse nuclear beta-catenin (WNT-activated subtype) with GAB1 or YAP1 immunopositivity, which mark the SHH-activated subtype instead.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse nuclear beta-catenin (WNT-activated subtype) with GAB1 or YAP1 immunopositivity, which mark the SHH-activated subtype instead.

## hold_these
Nuclear beta-catenin positivity on immunohistochemistry identifies the WNT-activated molecular subtype of medulloblastoma.

## lose_the_mark
Do not confuse nuclear beta-catenin (WNT-activated) with GAB1/YAP1 (SHH-activated).

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: the classic gross appearance of hemangioblastoma is a well-circumscribed, reddish vermian mass bulging into the fourth ventricle.

## sections
### Definition
Grossly, cerebellar hemangioblastoma is a slow-growing, well-circumscribed mass, characteristically located in the vermis, and because of its rich vascularity has a distinctive reddish cut surface; larger tumours bulge into and can obstruct the fourth ventricle, producing obstructive hydrocephalus.

### Mechanism
Recognise the gross appearance of hemangioblastoma as a well-circumscribed reddish vermian mass bulging into the fourth ventricle.

### Key determinants
Do not confuse the reddish, highly vascular gross appearance of hemangioblastoma with the greyish, gelatinous appearance of pilocytic astrocytoma.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse the reddish, highly vascular gross appearance of hemangioblastoma with the greyish, gelatinous appearance of pilocytic astrocytoma.

## hold_these
A well-circumscribed, reddish, highly vascular mass in the cerebellar vermis bulging into the fourth ventricle is the classic gross appearance of hemangioblastoma.

## lose_the_mark
Do not confuse hemangioblastoma's reddish gross appearance with pilocytic astrocytoma's greyish, gelatinous one.

### Key determinants

### Clinical significance

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: medulloblastoma accounts for approximately 20% of childhood brain tumours, the most common malignant paediatric brain tumour.

## sections
### Definition
Medulloblastoma is the most common malignant paediatric brain tumour, accounting for approximately 20% of childhood brain tumours and around 40% of paediatric posterior fossa tumours. It arises in the cerebellum, most often the vermis.

### Mechanism
State that medulloblastoma accounts for approximately 20% of childhood brain tumours.

### Key determinants
Do not confuse this figure with medulloblastoma's ranking among malignant paediatric brain tumours (most common) versus among all paediatric brain tumours (pilocytic astrocytoma is more common overall).

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse this figure with medulloblastoma's ranking among malignant paediatric brain tumours versus among all paediatric brain tumours.

## hold_these
Medulloblastoma accounts for approximately 20% of childhood brain tumours.

## lose_the_mark
Do not confuse "most common malignant" with "most common overall" (pilocytic astrocytoma).

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: inactivation of the VHL tumour suppressor gene underlies hemangioblastoma, both sporadic and as part of von Hippel-Lindau disease.

## sections
### Definition
The VHL tumour suppressor gene, located on chromosome 3p, is inactivated in hemangioblastoma, whether the tumour arises sporadically or as part of von Hippel-Lindau disease. Loss of functional VHL protein leads to stabilisation of hypoxia-inducible factors and upregulation of angiogenic factors such as VEGF, explaining the tumour's high vascularity.

### Mechanism
State that the VHL tumour suppressor gene is inactivated in hemangioblastoma.

### Key determinants
Do not confuse VHL (chromosome 3p, hemangioblastoma) with the genes implicated in medulloblastoma (for example PTCH1 or APC).

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse VHL (chromosome 3p, hemangioblastoma) with the genes implicated in medulloblastoma.

## hold_these
Inactivation of the VHL tumour suppressor gene underlies hemangioblastoma, both sporadic and as part of von Hippel-Lindau disease.

## lose_the_mark
Do not confuse VHL (hemangioblastoma) with medulloblastoma's driver genes.

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: the recognised histological variants of medulloblastoma (classic, desmoplastic/nodular, extensive nodularity, large cell/anaplastic) — clear cell is not among them.

## sections
### Definition
The WHO classification recognises several histological variants of medulloblastoma: classic, desmoplastic/nodular, medulloblastoma with extensive nodularity, and large cell/anaplastic. "Clear cell" is not among these recognised variants; clear-cell morphology in a cerebellar tumour instead should prompt consideration of other entities.

### Mechanism
State that clear cell is not a recognised morphological variant of medulloblastoma.

### Key determinants
Do not assume every distinctive cytoplasmic-clearing pattern in a cerebellar tumour is a medulloblastoma variant.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not assume every distinctive cytoplasmic-clearing pattern in a cerebellar tumour is a medulloblastoma variant.

## hold_these
Clear cell change is not a recognised morphological variant of medulloblastoma; classic, desmoplastic/nodular, large cell/anaplastic and extensively nodular are.

## lose_the_mark
Do not force clear-cell morphology into the medulloblastoma variant list.

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: the WHO integrated diagnosis of medulloblastoma requires both histopathologic and molecular/genetic features, not either alone.

## sections
### Definition
Since the WHO 2016/2021 CNS tumour classification updates, medulloblastoma is no longer diagnosed on histology alone. The integrated diagnosis combines the histological variant with the molecular subtype (WNT-activated, SHH-activated, Group 3 or Group 4).

### Mechanism
State that the WHO integrated diagnosis of medulloblastoma requires both histopathologic and molecular/genetic features.

### Key determinants
Do not rely on histology alone, or on molecular testing alone, to render the current WHO diagnosis of medulloblastoma.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not rely on histology alone, or on molecular testing alone, to render the current WHO diagnosis of medulloblastoma.

## hold_these
The WHO integrated diagnosis of medulloblastoma requires both histopathologic and molecular/genetic features, not either alone.

## lose_the_mark
Do not treat histology and molecular subtype as interchangeable or optional in the current WHO diagnosis.

## summary
This ASU Year 2 CNS slice covers: medulloblastoma's radiosensitivity, despite its high WHO grade, makes radiotherapy a key part of its treatment.

## sections
### Definition
Although medulloblastoma is a highly malignant, WHO Grade IV tumour, it is also markedly radiosensitive, meaning its cells are efficiently killed by ionising radiation. This makes craniospinal radiotherapy a cornerstone of adjuvant treatment after maximal safe surgical resection.

### Mechanism
State that medulloblastoma's radiosensitivity makes radiotherapy a key part of its treatment, despite its high WHO grade.

### Key determinants
Do not assume a WHO Grade IV tumour is necessarily radioresistant — WHO grade reflects malignant potential, not treatment responsiveness.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Do not assume a WHO Grade IV tumour is necessarily radioresistant.

## hold_these
Medulloblastoma's radiosensitivity, despite its high WHO grade, makes radiotherapy a key part of its treatment.

## lose_the_mark
Do not equate high WHO grade with radioresistance.
