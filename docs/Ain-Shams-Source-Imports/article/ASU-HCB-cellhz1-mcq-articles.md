## summary
Eight facts tested by the ASU HCB Dr. Hegazy "CELL MCQ 1 2022" scanned bank (67 questions) that are not fully covered by any existing pending cytology concept: the Golgi's function list excludes glycogenesis; the regulated and constitutive secretory pathways differ by storage and trigger; phase-contrast microscopy is the technique for watching organelle movement in living, unstained cells; glycogen alpha (rosette, liver) and beta (discrete) particles are both free, not membrane-bound; the two membrane leaflets differ in lipid, protein and receptor content but not in carrying cholesterol; a secretory vesicle's membrane originates from the Golgi rather than the plasma membrane, unlike the three endocytic vesicle types; the ciliary shaft is a 20-microtubule axoneme beaten by dynein arms; and the proteasome is the non-membranous organelle that degrades misfolded and short-lived cytosolic protein.

## sections
### Definition
The Golgi apparatus sorts, glycosylates and modifies secretory protein; it plays no part in glycogen synthesis, which is a separate cytosolic enzyme pathway.
The regulated secretory pathway stores its product in vesicles and releases it only on a trigger; the constitutive pathway has no storage step and releases continuously.
Phase-contrast microscopy converts refractive-index differences into visible contrast, so it can image living, unstained cells and their moving organelles.
Glycogen is stored as free (non-membrane-bound) cytoplasmic particles: rosette-shaped alpha particles in liver, discrete beta particles elsewhere.
The plasma membrane's outer and inner leaflets differ in lipid, protein and receptor content, but cholesterol sits in both leaflets.
A secretory vesicle buds from the Golgi; a phagosome, pinocytic vesicle and coated vesicle all bud from the plasma membrane instead.
The ciliary shaft is a membrane-covered axoneme of 20 microtubules (9 doublets plus 2 central singlets) whose beat is powered by dynein arms.
The proteasome is a non-membranous cylindrical particle of protein rings and guiding molecules that degrades abnormal, misfolded and short-lived cytosolic proteins tagged with ubiquitin.

### Mechanism
Newly synthesised secretory protein arrives at the Golgi's entry (cis) face from the rough endoplasmic reticulum, moves through the stack of saccules where its sugar chains are trimmed and extended and any precursor is proteolytically matured, and leaves from the exit (trans) face as sorted vesicles addressed to the lysosome, secretory granules or the plasma membrane; glycogen synthase and branching enzyme, by contrast, act freely in the cytosol and never pass through the Golgi.
A cell that must respond to a physiological signal (insulin release from a beta cell on a glucose rise) uses the regulated pathway so it can hold a reserve and release it only when needed; a cell exporting a constant structural product (procollagen from a fibroblast) uses the constitutive pathway because there is nothing to wait for.
Phase-contrast optics exploit the fact that different cell structures retard light passing through them by slightly different amounts even though they absorb almost no light; a phase plate converts these phase shifts into amplitude (brightness) differences the eye or camera can see, without any fixation or dye.
Glycogen granules dissolve out of a routine haematoxylin-and-eosin section, leaving empty vacuoles, so Best's carmine or the periodic acid–Schiff reaction is needed to demonstrate them in place; hepatocytes pack theirs into large rosette clusters (alpha particles) because the liver is the main glycogen store, while muscle and most other cells keep smaller, separate beta particles matched to local, rapid use.
Because the outer leaflet faces the extracellular fluid, it alone carries the glycoprotein/glycolipid cell coat and most receptor proteins that must recognise extracellular signals; the inner leaflet instead anchors the cytoskeleton. Cholesterol, being a small non-polar molecule that wedges between fatty-acid tails to control fluidity, is not confined to either face and packs into both leaflets equally.
A secretory vesicle is generated by budding from the trans-Golgi network, carrying material the cell is about to export; a phagosome, a pinocytic vesicle and a coated (receptor-mediated) vesicle are all produced by inward budding of a patch of existing plasma membrane, so their membrane is plasma-membrane in origin even before the secretory vesicle has fused with anything.
Dynein arms are ATPase motor proteins fixed along each of the axoneme's nine peripheral microtubule doublets; ATP hydrolysis drives each arm to "walk" along the adjacent doublet, and because the doublets are tethered together the sliding is converted into the coordinated bending wave of the ciliary beat, exactly as it also powers the identical axoneme of the sperm tail.
The proteasome recognises cytosolic proteins that have been tagged with a chain of ubiquitin molecules — misfolded proteins, or normal regulatory proteins such as mitotic cyclins whose job is done — and unfolds and degrades them inside its barrel-shaped core of protein rings, a job the lysosome (a membranous organelle working on extracellular/endocytosed material) does not do.

### Key determinants
"Except… glycogenesis" among Golgi functions flags this exclusion; the true Golgi functions are sorting, glycosylation and prohormone/protein modification.
"Stored until triggered" points to the regulated pathway; "continuous, no storage" points to the constitutive pathway.
"Living, unstained cell" plus "watching movement" points to phase-contrast microscopy, not EM or a routine stained light-microscopy section.
"Rosette-shaped, in liver" is the alpha particle; "discrete single granules" is the beta particle; "membrane bound" is false for either.
"Present in outer layer only" is false for cholesterol specifically, even though it is true for the cell coat and most receptors.
"Not surrounded by a membrane part of the cell membrane" singles out the secretory vesicle, because its membrane is Golgi-derived, not plasma-membrane-derived like the other three vesicle types listed.
"20 microtubules" and "dynein arms" identify ciliary shaft structure and the motor of its beat; do not confuse the shaft's 9+2 (20 microtubules) with the centriole/basal body's 9+0 triplet arrangement (27 microtubules).
"Non-membranous" plus "degrades misfolded/short-lived protein" identifies the proteasome, distinct from the lysosome (membranous, works on material brought in from outside the cytosol).

### Clinical significance
This is not a treatment rule. Its study value is telling apart eight commonly confused, stand-alone cell-biology facts that this MCQ bank tests directly, each easily muddled with a similar-sounding neighbouring fact (Golgi versus cytosolic glycogenesis, regulated versus constitutive secretion, phase-contrast versus EM, alpha versus beta glycogen particles, leaflet-restricted versus both-leaflet membrane components, secretory versus endocytic vesicle origin, ciliary shaft versus centriole microtubule count, and proteasome versus lysosome).

### Common misconceptions
Assuming any carbohydrate pathway near the Golgi (glycosylation) must include glycogen synthesis (glycogenesis) — they are unrelated pathways in different compartments.
Assuming all secreted protein is stored in granules before release — constitutively secreted protein such as procollagen is exported as it is made.
Naming electron microscopy or a routine stained section for "watching organelle movement" — only phase-contrast microscopy images living, unstained, moving cells.
Calling glycogen membrane-bound because it looks like a discrete granule — glycogen is free in the cytosol, unlike lipofuscin, which really is membrane-bound as a lysosomal residual body.
Extending the "outer surface only" rule for the cell coat and receptors to cholesterol, which is present in both membrane leaflets.
Assuming every vesicle that will eventually fuse with the plasma membrane must already be made of plasma membrane — a secretory vesicle's membrane is Golgi-derived until the moment of exocytosis.
Counting the ciliary shaft's microtubules as 27 (the centriole/basal-body number) instead of 20 (9 doublets plus 2 central singlets).
Assuming the proteasome and the lysosome are interchangeable protein-degrading organelles — the proteasome is non-membranous and works on ubiquitin-tagged cytosolic protein, the lysosome is membranous and works mainly on phagocytosed/endocytosed material.
