## sections
### Definition
The pudendal (Alcock's) canal, in the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa, carries the internal pudendal vessels and pudendal nerve, from which the inferior rectal artery and nerve branch to cross the fossa.

### Mechanism
The pudendal canal is formed by a splitting of the obturator fascia over the medial surface of obturator internus, in the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa. It transmits the internal pudendal artery and vein and the pudendal nerve from the lesser sciatic foramen to the perineum. Early in its course the inferior rectal (inferior anal) vessels and nerve arise and pass medially across the fat-filled ischioanal fossa to reach the anal canal and external anal sphincter, making them a classic content cited in relation to this canal.

### Key determinants
Confusing the pudendal canal's contents (internal pudendal vessels/nerve and their inferior rectal branches) with structures that instead pass through the obturator canal (obturator nerve/vessels) or leave the pelvis via the greater sciatic foramen (sciatic nerve, superior gluteal vessels).

## sections
### Definition
The unregressed cranial (upper) end of the paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct persists in the male as the appendix of the testis.

### Mechanism
Fetal Sertoli cells secrete Müllerian-inhibiting hormone (MIH/anti-Müllerian hormone), which causes near-complete regression of the paramesonephric ducts in the male fetus, since these ducts would otherwise form the uterus, uterine tubes and upper vagina. Regression is not absolute at either end: the cranial tip persists as the appendix of the testis (hydatid of Morgagni), and the fused caudal ends persist as the prostatic utricle. Both are clinically recognized incidental findings and occasional sites of torsion (appendix testis) or cystic dilation (prostatic utricle).

### Key determinants
Mixing up the two paramesonephric remnants in the male by cranio-caudal position (appendix of testis = cranial tip; prostatic utricle = fused caudal ends), and confusing paramesonephric remnants with mesonephric-duct remnants (appendix of epididymis, paradidymis, ductuli aberrantes).

## summary
The upper outer quadrant of the breast, which contains the greatest volume of breast tissue, drains predominantly to the anterior (pectoral) group of axillary lymph nodes.

## sections
### Definition
The upper outer quadrant of the breast, which contains the greatest volume of breast tissue, drains predominantly to the anterior (pectoral) group of axillary lymph nodes.

### Mechanism
About 75% of breast lymph drains laterally and superiorly, following the course of the lateral thoracic vessels to the anterior (pectoral) axillary nodes at the lower border of pectoralis minor; from there, lymph passes sequentially through the central and apical axillary groups. Because the upper outer quadrant holds the most glandular tissue and is the most common site of breast carcinoma, its cancers most often first involve this pectoral axillary group, which is why axillary (sentinel) node sampling targets this region during breast cancer staging.

### Key determinants
Assuming all breast quadrants drain to the same first-echelon nodes: medial-quadrant tumors drain preferentially to the parasternal (internal thoracic) nodes, not the axilla, while supraclavicular involvement represents later, not first-echelon, spread.

## summary
Primitive (primary) sex cords become histologically recognizable within the indifferent gonadal ridge by about the 7th week of gestation.

## sections
### Definition
Primitive (primary) sex cords become histologically recognizable within the indifferent gonadal ridge by about the 7th week of gestation.

### Mechanism
Primordial germ cells originate in the yolk sac wall and migrate to the genital ridges by about the 5th–6th week. Proliferation of the overlying coelomic epithelium then produces finger-like primary sex cords that grow into the underlying mesenchyme, becoming histologically distinct within the still-indifferent gonad by roughly the 7th week. Only after this does SRY expression (in an XY gonad) commit the sex cords to a testicular fate, forming seminiferous cords; in its absence, the gonad differentiates along the ovarian pathway with secondary (cortical) cord formation somewhat later.

### Key determinants
Confusing the arrival of primordial germ cells in the genital ridge (about week 5–6) with the later appearance of organized primary sex cords (about week 7), and confusing the indifferent stage with the already sex-differentiated gonad seen by week 10.

## sections
### Definition
Degarelix is a GnRH receptor antagonist that immediately suppresses gonadotropin (LH/FSH) secretion and testosterone production, without the initial testosterone flare seen with GnRH agonists.

### Mechanism
Unlike GnRH agonists, which initially over-stimulate pituitary GnRH receptors before eventual downregulation and suppression (producing a transient testosterone flare), degarelix competitively and immediately blocks the GnRH receptor. This directly and rapidly lowers LH and FSH secretion from the first dose, producing a fast, flare-free fall in serum testosterone — a clinically important advantage in men with advanced prostate cancer at risk of flare-related complications such as worsening bone pain, ureteric obstruction, or spinal cord compression.

### Key determinants
Assuming all GnRH-pathway drugs behave the same way; agonists (leuprolide, goserelin) cause an initial flare before suppression, while antagonists (degarelix) suppress immediately without a flare.

### Definition
Human chorionic gonadotropin, secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast, rescues and maintains the corpus luteum's progesterone secretion in early pregnancy until the placenta assumes steroidogenesis.

### Mechanism
After implantation, syncytiotrophoblast cells secrete hCG, which is structurally and functionally similar to LH. hCG binds the corpus luteum's LH/hCG receptors, preventing the luteolysis that would otherwise occur near the expected time of menses and sustaining luteal progesterone (and estrogen) secretion. ### Definition

### Mechanism
Suckling is a potent stimulus for prolactin release from the anterior pituitary. ### Definition

### Mechanism
Throughout pregnancy the placenta progressively increases estrogen (chiefly estriol) output, with an especially steep late-pregnancy rise. ### Mechanism

### Definition
Use of oral contraceptive pills is a recognized predisposing factor for symptomatic Trichomonas vaginalis vaginitis, through alteration of vaginal pH and glycogen content.

### Mechanism
Trichomonas vaginalis, a flagellated protozoan, proliferates preferentially in a vaginal environment with elevated pH and altered estrogen-dependent glycogen availability. Hormonal changes from oral contraceptive use (like those of pregnancy) can shift the vaginal milieu in a way that favors organism survival and symptomatic infection, alongside the more fundamental predisposing role of disrupted lactobacillus-dominant flora, which normally maintains a protective low vaginal pH through lactic acid production.

### Key determinants
Assuming vaginal hygiene measures such as douching are protective — disrupting the normal lactobacillus flora with douching or antibiotics is itself a recognized risk factor for vaginal infection, not a protective behavior.
