## summary
This ASU Year 3 CNS slice covers: Most muscles supplied by cranial nerve motor nuclei receive bilateral corticobulbar (upper motor neuron) input, so a unilateral upper motor neuron lesion produces little clinical deficit in those muscles. Genioglossus is an important exception: it receives corticobulbar input from the contralateral cerebral hemisphere only. Consequently, a unilateral upper motor neuron lesion (e.g. a stroke affecting the corticobulbar tract) causes the protruded tongue to deviate toward the side of the lesion (weak genioglossus fails to push the tongue to the opposite, unaffected side) — the basis of the classic "tongue deviates toward the lesion in a UMN lesion, away from the lesion in an LMN (hypoglossal nerve) lesion" clinical rule.

## summary
This ASU Year 3 CNS slice covers: The Pacinian corpuscle is a large, encapsulated mechanoreceptor with a characteristic "onion-skin" arrangement of concentric lamellae, chiefly responsible for detecting vibration and deep pressure; the Meissner corpuscle is a smaller, more superficial encapsulated receptor for light touch/flutter, with a different internal architecture in which flattened lamellar cells interdigitate around the nerve terminal in a more coiled/branching pattern. ## hold_these
The Pacinian corpuscle is a large, encapsulated mechanoreceptor with a characteristic "onion-skin" arrangement of concentric lamellae, chiefly responsible for detecting vibration and deep pressure; the Meissner corpuscle is a smaller, more superficial encapsulated receptor for light touch/flutter, with a different internal architecture in which flattened lamellar cells interdigitate around the nerve terminal in a more coiled/branching pattern. A defining structural feature of the Pacinian corpuscle is that its single sensory (afferent) nerve fibre runs axially, straight through the centre of the corpuscle's concentric lamellae, a histological distinction from the Meissner corpuscle's terminal, which does not show this same simple axial course.

## lose_the_mark
Confusing the Pacinian corpuscle's structural feature (axial nerve fibre through concentric onion-skin lamellae, detects vibration/deep pressure) with the Meissner corpuscle's distinct structure and function (light touch/flutter, coiled nerve terminal among interdigitating lamellar cells, more superficial location).
