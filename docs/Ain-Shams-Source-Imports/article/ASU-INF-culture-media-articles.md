## summary
This ASU Year 1 infection slice records Sabouraud dextrose agar as a fungal-isolation medium with source-listed selective features. It is drafted for local curriculum alignment and still needs independent microbiology evidence before publication.

## sections
### Definition
The ASU Year 1 infection practical teaches that Sabouraud dextrose agar contains Sabouraud's dextrose broth at pH 5.6 plus agar, includes chloramphinicol, cyclohexidine and acidic pH as selective features, and is used for isolation of fungi.

### Mechanism
The selected native page lists chloramphinicol to minimise bacterial contamination, cyclohexidine to inhibit growth of saprophytic fungi and acidic pH to inhibit many species of bacteria.

### Key determinants
The use stated in the selected source is isolation of fungi; this batch does not extend that statement to organism identification or treatment selection.

### Clinical significance
This batch records only the Sabouraud dextrose agar composition, selective features and use directly stated in the local practical source. It does not author treatment, dosing or susceptibility recommendations.

### Common misconceptions
Do not treat this draft as an independently verified mycology reference. Its current evidence chain is local ASU curriculum only.

## hold_these
Sabouraud dextrose agar is listed as a yellowish transparent solid medium made with Sabouraud's dextrose broth at pH 5.6 plus agar.
The source lists chloramphinicol, cyclohexidine and acidic pH as selective features and gives the use as isolation of fungi.

## lose_the_mark
Remembering Sabouraud dextrose agar only as "for fungi" and omitting the source-listed selective features.

The ASU Year 1 infection practical teaches that MacConkey agar supports most Gram-negative bacilli, inhibits Gram-positive organisms and differentiates Gram-negative bacilli by lactose fermentation.

### Mechanism

### Key determinants

### Clinical significance
