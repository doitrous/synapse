## summary
This ASU Year 2 cardiovascular histology slice covers three electron-microscopic details of cardiac muscle: branching myofibrils with less distinct striations, the Z-line T-tubule and diad arrangement, and abundant mitochondria arranged in chains between myofilaments. The source is a local Ain Shams lecture, so the article is drafted for curriculum alignment and still needs independent histology evidence before publication.

## sections
### Definition
Cardiac muscle myofibrils branch and anastomose, vary in diameter, and have less distinct striations than skeletal muscle.

### Mechanism
In this ASU lecture, the cardiac muscle T-tubule lies at the Z line, one per sarcomere, and forms a diad with one terminal cisterna of sarcoplasmic reticulum.

### Key determinants
Cardiac muscle mitochondria are abundant and lie in chains between myofilaments.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not author diagnosis, treatment, dosing or emergency-management claims from the lecture. It keeps to the low-risk histology facts explicitly stated in the selected source slice.

### Common misconceptions
Do not import the skeletal-muscle triad pattern into this cardiac-muscle slice. The ASU source states a cardiac muscle diad, not a triad.

Do not describe cardiac muscle striations as identical to skeletal muscle striations; the lecture states that they are less distinct.

## hold_these
Cardiac muscle myofibrils branch and anastomose, with less distinct striations than skeletal muscle.
The cardiac muscle T-tubule is at the Z line and participates in a diad, not a triad.
Cardiac muscle mitochondria are abundant and lie in chains between myofilaments.

## lose_the_mark
Calling the cardiac muscle coupling unit a triad when this ASU lecture states a diad.
Describing the cardiac muscle electron-microscopic picture without the abundant mitochondria between myofilaments.

## summary
This companion ASU Year 2 cardiovascular histology article keeps to two electron-microscopic cardiac muscle landmarks from the selected lecture page: the T-tubule at the Z line forming a diad with one terminal cisterna, and abundant mitochondria lying in chains between myofilaments.

## sections
### Definition
The cardiac muscle diad is taught here as one T-tubule with one terminal cisterna of sarcoplasmic reticulum.

### Mechanism
The lecture places the cardiac muscle T-tubule at the Z line, one per sarcomere, and links that position to the diad arrangement.

### Key determinants
The same electron-microscopic slice describes cardiac muscle mitochondria as abundant and arranged in chains between myofilaments.

### Clinical significance
This is a foundational histology comparison point, not a clinical-management rule. It helps prevent importing skeletal-muscle triad language into cardiac-muscle histology.

### Common misconceptions
Do not call the cardiac muscle coupling unit a triad in this ASU slice. The source states a diad with one T-tubule and one terminal cisterna.

Do not omit the mitochondrial arrangement when describing the cardiac muscle electron-microscopic picture from this lecture page.

## hold_these
The cardiac muscle T-tubule lies at the Z line and forms a diad with one terminal cisterna.
Cardiac muscle mitochondria are abundant and lie in chains between myofilaments.

## lose_the_mark
Calling the cardiac muscle diad a triad.
Separating the mitochondria statement from its between-myofilaments location.
