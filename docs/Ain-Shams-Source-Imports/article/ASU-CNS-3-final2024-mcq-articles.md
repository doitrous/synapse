### Mechanism
Attribute reflex rigidity of the overlying abdominal wall muscles, in conditions such as chronic gastritis, to a viscerosomatic reflex arising from convergent visceral and somatic afferent input at the same spinal segment.

### Key determinants
Confusing this protective viscerosomatic reflex (muscle rigidity from visceral disease) with referred pain (perceived cutaneous pain from visceral disease) — both arise from the same convergence principle, but one produces a motor (muscle) response and the other a sensory (pain-location) illusion.

## sections
### Definition
Naltrexone is a competitive antagonist at opioid receptors (chiefly the mu receptor); by blocking these receptors, it prevents exogenous opioids from producing their euphoric/reinforcing effect and, in alcohol use disorder, reduces alcohol's reward via the same opioid-receptor-mediated pathway. Because it removes the pharmacological reward that drives continued use without itself being an agonist, naltrexone (given orally or as a long-acting injectable) is used as maintenance therapy to help prevent relapse in both opioid and alcohol addiction, once the patient is opioid-free.

### Mechanism
Attribute naltrexone's relapse-prevention effect in opioid and alcohol addiction to competitive opioid receptor antagonism, which blocks the reinforcing effect of continued use.

### Key determinants
Confusing naltrexone (a pure opioid antagonist, used for relapse prevention/maintenance) with naloxone (a shorter-acting antagonist used for acute overdose reversal) or with methadone/buprenorphine (opioid agonists/partial agonists used for substitution therapy) — the three have related but distinct roles in opioid use disorder management.

### Definition
Dysmetria is impaired ability to judge the distance, speed, and force needed to reach an intended target during a voluntary movement, a sign of cerebellar (particularly spinocerebellar) disease reflecting loss of the cerebellum's real-time error-correction. It can present as hypermetria, in which the moving limb overshoots the intended endpoint (classically demonstrated as past-pointing on the finger-nose-finger test), or as hypometria, in which the limb falls short and stops before reaching it — both are manifestations of the same underlying deficit in movement calibration, just in opposite directions.

### Mechanism
Define dysmetria as impaired distance/force judgment in cerebellar disease, encompassing both hypermetria (overshoot) and hypometria (undershoot).

### Key determinants
Treating dysmetria as synonymous with only overshoot (hypermetria) — undershoot (hypometria) is an equally valid, if less commonly emphasised, form of the same cerebellar sign.

## summary
