### Definition
Local extension is one of the four principal routes by which organisms reach the central nervous system, alongside haematogenous spread, direct implantation and travel along peripheral nerves. In acute bacterial (pyogenic) meningitis, cerebrospinal fluid analysis characteristically shows a markedly decreased glucose (from bacterial and neutrophil consumption), a neutrophil-predominant pleocytosis, elevated protein, and a turbid or purulent gross appearance from the high cell count. ## sections
### Definition
Brain abscess evolves through stages: early and late cerebritis (diffuse, poorly demarcated inflammation and early necrosis), followed by early and late capsule formation, in which a fibrous/collagenous capsule progressively walls off the necrotic centre. A well-developed capsule marks the chronic (late capsule) stage, a more mature, contained lesion that is generally more amenable to surgical drainage than the earlier, diffuse cerebritis stage.

### Mechanism
State that a well-developed capsule in a brain abscess indicates the chronic stage.

### Key determinants
Incorrect. The acute stage of brain abscess is diffuse cerebritis, without a well-developed capsule. Incorrect. A well-developed, mature capsule reflects the later, chronic stage rather than an intermediate subacute stage of early capsule formation. Incorrect. "Resolving" is not a standard staging term for brain abscess evolution, which is described as cerebritis (early/late) progressing to capsule formation (early/late).

### Definition
Tuberculous and fungal (e.g. cryptococcal) meningitis, despite differing causative organisms, share a similar chronic-meningitis CSF pattern: a lymphocyte-predominant pleocytosis with decreased glucose and elevated protein, reflecting the chronic granulomatous or fungal inflammatory response, which is why the two are grouped together and distinguished from each other by specific stains, cultures and antigen tests rather than by the basic CSF profile alone.

### Mechanism
State that tuberculous and fungal meningitis share a lymphocytic, decreased-glucose CSF pattern, distinguishing both from viral and pyogenic causes.

### Key determinants
Incorrect. CSF glucose is characteristically decreased, not normal, in both tuberculous and fungal meningitis. Incorrect. Both are typically chronic or subacute in course, not acute and fulminant, which better describes pyogenic bacterial meningitis. Incorrect. Fungal meningitis is, by definition, caused by fungi, not bacteria, so the causative organisms are not always bacteria.
