## summary
Thyroid follicular colloid is rich in the glycoprotein thyroglobulin, giving it a positive periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) reaction.

## sections
### Definition
Colloid is the gel-like material filling the lumen of a thyroid follicle.

### Mechanism
Its high glycoprotein (thyroglobulin) content reacts positively with the PAS stain, a standard histochemical identifier of colloid on light microscopy.

### Key determinants
PAS positivity is a fixed structural/histochemical feature of colloid, distinct from the amount of colloid present, which varies with the follicle's activity state.

### Clinical significance
PAS staining helps confirm thyroid follicular colloid on histology slides and in differentiating thyroid tissue from other structures.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse fixed PAS positivity (a staining property) with the variable colloid amount seen in hyperactive vs hypoactive follicles.

## hold_these
Thyroid follicular colloid is PAS-positive because of its glycoprotein (thyroglobulin) content.

## lose_the_mark
Describing colloid as PAS-negative or attributing its staining to a non-glycoprotein component.

## summary
Steroid-secreting cells are ultrastructurally identified by abundant smooth ER, tubular mitochondria and cytoplasmic lipid droplets.

## sections
### Definition
Steroid hormone-secreting cells (adrenal cortical, gonadal steroidogenic cells) share a characteristic ultrastructure.

### Mechanism
Abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum hosts the enzymatic steps converting cholesterol to steroid hormones; mitochondria with tubular/vesicular cristae perform the initial cholesterol side-chain cleavage; lipid droplets store the cholesterol precursor.

### Key determinants
The combination of all three features together, not any single one, is what identifies a cell as steroidogenic on electron microscopy.

### Clinical significance
Recognising this ultrastructural triad helps identify steroid-secreting tissue (adrenal cortex, gonads) on electron micrographs and distinguishes it from peptide-hormone-secreting cells (rough ER, secretory granules).

### Common misconceptions
Do not name only one feature (e.g., lipid droplets alone) as sufficient — the full triad (smooth ER, mitochondria, lipid) together defines steroidogenic ultrastructure.

## hold_these
Steroid-secreting cells have abundant smooth ER, mitochondria and lipid droplets.

## lose_the_mark
Naming rough ER and secretory granules (a peptide-hormone-cell profile) instead of smooth ER and lipid droplets.

## summary
The adenohypophysis connects to the hypothalamus via the hypophyseal portal venous system, not a direct neural tract.

## sections
### Definition
The adenohypophysis (anterior pituitary) receives hypothalamic regulation through a vascular route.

### Mechanism
Hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones enter a primary capillary plexus in the median eminence, drain via the hypophyseal portal veins, and reach a secondary capillary plexus in the anterior pituitary to act on its cells.

### Key determinants
This contrasts with the neurohypophysis (posterior pituitary), which is connected to the hypothalamus by a direct neural tract — the hypothalamo-hypophyseal axon tract from the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei.

### Clinical significance
Understanding this dual connection (portal vessels for the anterior lobe, direct axons for the posterior lobe) explains why anterior and posterior pituitary hormones are regulated so differently.

### Common misconceptions
Do not attribute a direct neural connection to the adenohypophysis — that describes the neurohypophysis instead.

## hold_these
The adenohypophysis is connected to the hypothalamus by the hypophyseal portal venous system.
The neurohypophysis, by contrast, has a direct neural (axonal) connection.

## lose_the_mark
Stating that the adenohypophysis is connected to the hypothalamus by direct nerve tracts.

## summary
Parathyroid oxyphil cells are packed with mitochondria, which produces their deeply acidophilic staining.

## sections
### Definition
Oxyphil cells are the larger, less numerous of the two parathyroid parenchymal cell types.

### Mechanism
They contain numerous mitochondria, which is what gives them their deeply acidophilic (eosinophilic) cytoplasmic staining on light microscopy.

### Key determinants
Mitochondrial content is the underlying determinant of the staining property — more mitochondria means deeper acidophilia.

### Clinical significance
Recognising oxyphil cells (deeply acidophilic, mitochondria-rich) versus chief cells (lipid/glycogen-rich, less intensely stained) is a standard parathyroid histology distinction.

### Common misconceptions
Do not confuse oxyphil cells with chief cells, which have lipid and glycogen content rather than abundant mitochondria.

## hold_these
Oxyphil cells contain numerous mitochondria.
This mitochondrial content produces their deeply acidophilic staining.

## lose_the_mark
Describing oxyphil cells as containing lipid and glycogen (that is the chief cell profile).

## summary
Pituicytes are modified astrocyte-like glial cells that support the axons and axon terminals of the posterior pituitary.

## sections
### Definition
Pituicytes are the glial cell population of the neurohypophysis (posterior pituitary).

### Mechanism
They provide structural and metabolic support to the unmyelinated axons and axon terminals of the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract, without themselves secreting hormone.

### Key determinants
Their supportive, non-secretory role distinguishes pituicytes from the neurosecretory axon terminals (Herring bodies) they surround.

### Clinical significance
Recognising pituicytes as supportive rather than secretory clarifies that oxytocin/vasopressin release comes from the axon terminals, not the glial cells around them.

### Common misconceptions
Do not attribute hormone secretion to pituicytes — that is the role of the neurosecretory axon terminals they support.

## hold_these
Pituicytes are supportive glial cells of the posterior pituitary, not hormone-secreting cells.

## lose_the_mark
Describing pituicytes as the cells that secrete oxytocin or vasopressin.
