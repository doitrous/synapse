## summary
This ASU Year 2 respiratory histology slice covers the cartilage-supported part of airway patency: the trachea is listed with C-shaped hyaline cartilage, extrapulmonary bronchi with complete hyaline cartilage, and intrapulmonary bronchi with separate cartilage plates.

## sections
### Definition
Tracheal patency is supported by C-shaped hyaline cartilage in the ASU respiratory histology source.

### Mechanism
Extrapulmonary bronchi are listed with complete hyaline cartilage, while intrapulmonary bronchi are listed with cartilage plates.

### Key determinants
The teaching point is a progression in support pattern across conducting airways: C-shaped cartilage in trachea, complete cartilage in extrapulmonary bronchi, and cartilage plates in intrapulmonary bronchi.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not author diagnosis, treatment, dosing or emergency-management claims. It keeps to the low-risk histology classification stated in the selected local source slice.

### Common misconceptions
Do not describe all airway cartilage with one pattern. The ASU source separates trachea, extrapulmonary bronchi and intrapulmonary bronchi.

Do not describe intrapulmonary bronchi as having the same complete cartilage pattern as extrapulmonary bronchi; the source states cartilage plates.

## hold_these
The trachea is taught with C-shaped hyaline cartilage for patency.
Extrapulmonary bronchi are taught with complete hyaline cartilage.
Intrapulmonary bronchi are taught with cartilage plates.

## lose_the_mark
Collapsing trachea, extrapulmonary bronchi and intrapulmonary bronchi into one undifferentiated cartilage pattern.
Calling intrapulmonary bronchial support complete cartilage when this ASU source states cartilage plates.

## summary
This companion ASU Year 2 respiratory histology article keeps to the bronchiolar line of the airway-patency slide: bronchioles are described as having no cartilage, with surfactant-like fluid secreted from Clara cells.

## sections
### Definition
Bronchioles are taught as having no cartilage, with surfactant-like fluid secreted from Clara cells in the patency context.

### Mechanism
The ASU slide contrasts bronchioles with cartilage-supported larger conducting airways by placing the phrase no cartilage directly in the bronchiolar patency line.

### Key determinants
The distinctive bronchiolar point in this slice is the absence of cartilage, paired with the source's wording about surfactant-like fluid from Clara cells.

### Clinical significance
This is a foundational histology comparison point, not a management rule. It helps keep bronchioles separate from cartilage-supported airways in the local ASU teaching sequence.

### Common misconceptions
Do not describe bronchioles as having cartilage in this ASU histology slice. The source explicitly says no cartilage.

Do not replace the source term Clara cells with an unsupported synonym in this batch; the local source uses Clara cells.

The same slide links bronchiolar patency with surfactant-like fluid secreted from Clara cells.
