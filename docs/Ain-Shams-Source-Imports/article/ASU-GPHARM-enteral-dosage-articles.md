## summary
This ASU Year 1 General Pharmacology article covers the local slide map of enteral dosage routes and the first oral dosage-form split into liquid and solid forms.

## sections
### Definition
In the ASU slide, enteral dosage routes are oral, buccal, sublingual, rectal and gavage.

### Mechanism
This article is a classification slice, not a pharmacokinetic mechanism claim. It keeps to the route names and oral dosage-form framing stated in the local source.

### Key determinants
The same source frames oral dosage forms as drugs taken by mouth and swallowed for local or systemic effect, then separates them into liquid and solid forms.

### Clinical significance
This is foundational dosage-form vocabulary for ASU-GPHARM. It does not recommend a drug, dose, treatment route or emergency action.

### Common misconceptions
Do not collapse enteral dosage into oral administration alone; the ASU route map also names buccal, sublingual, rectal and gavage.

## summary
This ASU Year 1 General Pharmacology article covers three oral dosage-form distinctions from the local slide deck: syrup/elixir/tincture, suspension and enteric-coated tablets.

## sections
### Definition
For oral solutions, the ASU slide distinguishes syrup as a sweetened aqueous solution, elixir as a sweetened hydroalcoholic solution and tincture as a hydroalcoholic solution containing 10–20% alcohol.

### Mechanism
The suspension slide defines suspension as insoluble solids suspended in water and adds that it should be shaken before use.

### Key determinants
The enteric-coated tablet slide states that the tablet resists dissolution in the acidic stomach environment and dissolves in the alkaline juices of the intestine.

### Clinical significance
This is a dosage-form classification slice. It does not recommend a medicine, dose, treatment route or emergency action.

### Common misconceptions
Do not call every oral liquid a solution: in the ASU slide, suspension is a separate category because insoluble solids are suspended in water.

## body

## hold_these
Syrup is the ASU slide's sweetened aqueous solution.
Elixir and tincture are hydroalcoholic solution categories in the ASU slide.
Suspension contains insoluble solids suspended in water and should be shaken before use.
Enteric-coated tablets resist stomach acid and dissolve in intestinal alkaline juice.

## lose_the_mark
Treating suspension as the same category as solution.
Describing enteric-coated tablets as dissolving in the stomach.
