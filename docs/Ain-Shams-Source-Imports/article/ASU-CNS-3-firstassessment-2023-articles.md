## sections
### Definition
Parkinson disease is a neurodegenerative disorder characterised histologically by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and by intracytoplasmic Lewy bodies — eosinophilic inclusions composed principally of aggregated, misfolded alpha-synuclein protein — within surviving neurons.

### Mechanism
State that Parkinson disease is characterised histologically by Lewy bodies and loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra, underlying its bradykinesia.

### Key determinants
This nigrostriatal dopaminergic loss underlies the disease's cardinal motor features (bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity and postural instability) and is the physiological target of dopamine-replacement therapies such as levodopa.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not extend the source's plain fact into diagnosis, treatment or management claims beyond what the compilation itself states.

### Common misconceptions
Confusing Parkinson disease's substantia nigra Lewy bodies (alpha-synuclein) with Huntington disease's caudate atrophy or Alzheimer disease's amyloid plaques and tau tangles.
