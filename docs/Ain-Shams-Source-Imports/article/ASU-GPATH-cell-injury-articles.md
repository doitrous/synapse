## summary
This ASU Year 1 General Pathology practical slice records the slide's text-only observation rule: low-power examination is for the overall change in tissue composition or pattern. It is a local curriculum draft and still needs independent pathology evidence before publication.

## sections
### Definition
In the ASU practical slide, low-power examination is used for the overall change in tissue composition or pattern.

### Mechanism
The practical workflow begins with a broad tissue-pattern question before moving to cellular detail. This article records only the low-power wording that is explicitly present in the source text.

### Key determinants
The determinant in this slice is magnification level: low power is tied to the overall tissue composition or pattern, not to a final diagnosis made from an image.

### Clinical significance
This is a foundational practical-observation point. It supports later pathology practical work by reminding students to start with the whole tissue pattern before naming finer microscopic features.

### Common misconceptions
Do not infer a diagnosis from the slide image in this batch. The evidence here supports only the text statement about low-power assessment of tissue composition or pattern.

## summary
This ASU Year 1 General Pathology practical slice records the slide's definition of hyperplasia and the slide-listed textual features for endometrial hyperplasia. It deliberately avoids adding image-derived findings or independent pathology claims.

## sections
### Definition
In the ASU practical slide, hyperplasia means increased organ size due to increased cell number.

### Mechanism
The source frames hyperplasia as a cellular adaptation. This article keeps the mechanism limited to the local slide wording: organ size increases because cell number increases.

### Key determinants
For endometrial hyperplasia, the ASU slide lists glands and stroma, cellular stroma, cystically dilated glands and cuboidal lining.

### Clinical significance
This is a foundational pathology-practical slice. Its study value is recognising the local curriculum wording for hyperplasia and the listed endometrial-hyperplasia features before any broader diagnostic or management claims are added from independent references.

### Common misconceptions
Do not convert this text-only slice into an image diagnosis exercise.

Do not define hyperplasia as increased cell size; the ASU slide defines it by increased cell number.
