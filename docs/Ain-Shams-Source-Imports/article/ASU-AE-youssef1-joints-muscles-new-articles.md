## sections
### Definition
The temporomandibular joint (TMJ) is a synovial joint between the mandibular condyle and the temporal bone's mandibular fossa, divided into upper and lower compartments by a fibrocartilaginous articular disc. Temporalis is a fan-shaped (triangular) muscle of mastication, arising broadly over the temporal fossa and converging to a narrow insertion on the coronoid process of the mandible.

### Mechanism
The TMJ's disc lets the joint perform two different movements in series: rotation of the condyle on the disc's underside (in the lower compartment) for simple opening/closing, and gliding of the disc-condyle unit against the temporal bone (in the upper compartment) for protrusion, retraction and the wider excursion of full mouth opening. Temporalis's triangular shape reflects its action: the broad fan of fibres over the temporal fossa converges force down onto the single narrow coronoid-process insertion, closing the jaw with the whole muscle's pull concentrated at one point, the same geometric principle by which a fan-shaped sail concentrates a broad area of wind onto a single mast.

### Key determinants
Among common joint examples (superior radioulnar, ankle, metacarpophalangeal, carpometacarpal of the thumb), the TMJ is the one built with an intra-articular disc. Among muscle shape classes (triangular/fan-shaped, parallel/strap, pennate), temporalis is the standard worked example of the triangular class, distinct from strap muscles such as sartorius and from purely pennate muscles such as deltoid or flexor pollicis longus.

### Clinical significance
This article does not make prescribing or diagnostic-workup claims. Its study value is foundational: the TMJ's disc is the anatomical basis for the audible/palpable "click" of disc displacement in temporomandibular joint dysfunction, and a fan-shaped muscle's broad origin is why temporalis can be palpated contracting over a wide area of the temple during clenching.

### Common misconceptions
Do not assume every synovial joint carries an intra-articular disc -- among this bank's own distractor joints, only the TMJ does.

Do not conflate a muscle's shape class (triangular, parallel/strap) with its fibre-direction class (unipennate, bipennate, multipennate) -- these are two separate classification schemes, and temporalis is the worked example for the shape class specifically in this question.
