## summary
This ASU Year 1 Locomotor practical slice teaches Jobe test as the supraspinatus special test in the shoulder-examination sequence. It is drafted from local curriculum text and remains evidence-gated pending independent musculoskeletal examination review.

## sections
### Purpose and indications
Use this local practical slice to recognise the ASU shoulder-examination setup for Jobe test. It teaches a named manoeuvre for supraspinatus assessment, not the full differential diagnosis of shoulder pain.

### Preparation and consent
The selected source page does not provide a preparation or consent checklist for this special test. No additional steps are supplied from outside the authorised source.

### Equipment
The selected source page does not specify equipment.

### Step-by-step procedure
In this ASU shoulder-examination practical, Jobe test assesses supraspinatus by placing both arms in 90 degrees abduction and 30 degrees flexion with thumbs down, then resisting downward pressure.

### Safety and complications
This local practical source is not independent clinical verification. It should not be treated as a complete safety, pain-limitation or contraindication standard.

### Communication points
The source instructs that the patient places both arms in the described position and resists the examiner's downward pressure. The selected page supports the movement setup only.

### Assessment criteria
The learner should identify Jobe test as the supraspinatus special test and reproduce the arm position and resisted downward pressure described in the ASU source.

## hold_these
Jobe test is the selected ASU shoulder special test for supraspinatus.
The source position is 90 degrees abduction, 30 degrees flexion, thumbs down and resisted downward pressure.

## lose_the_mark
Answering with generic rotator-cuff muscle composition instead of the Jobe manoeuvre.
Omitting the thumbs-down resisted position from the setup.
