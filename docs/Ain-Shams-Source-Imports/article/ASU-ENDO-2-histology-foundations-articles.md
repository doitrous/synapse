## summary
This ASU Year 2 endocrine histology slice records the local thyroid-follicle morphology contrast: normal follicles have cuboidal cells, hyperactive follicles have columnar cells with smaller follicles and little colloid, and hypoactive follicles have flat squamous cells with distension and abundant colloid.

## sections
### Definition
In the ASU Zahra histology source, normal thyroid follicular cells are cuboidal; hyperactive follicles have columnar cells with smaller follicles and little colloid; hypoactive follicles have flat squamous cells with distended follicles and abundant colloid.

### Mechanism
The ASU source presents epithelial height, follicle size and colloid amount as morphology changes associated with follicular activity state.

### Key determinants
The determinants in this source slice are follicular-cell shape, follicle size and colloid amount; they should be read as normal histology morphology rather than as a disease diagnosis.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not author disease, diagnosis, treatment, dosing or emergency-management claims. It keeps to the histology contrast stated in the selected local source slice.

### Common misconceptions
Do not collapse normal, hyperactive and hypoactive follicular appearances into one undifferentiated thyroid follicle pattern.

Do not merge this normal histology record into thyroid disease or thyroid carcinoma concepts; those records answer a different question.

## hold_these
Normal functioning follicular cells are cuboidal in this ASU source.
Hyperactive follicles are taught as columnar cells, smaller follicles and little colloid.
Hypoactive follicles are taught as flat squamous cells, distension and abundant colloid.

## lose_the_mark
Calling all thyroid follicles cuboidal regardless of activity state.
Treating this normal morphology contrast as a disease-pathology record.

## summary
This ASU Year 2 endocrine histology slice records two local thyroid cell-function statements: follicular cells synthesize, store and release thyroid hormones through thyroglobulin, while parafollicular C-cells sit between the basement membrane and follicular cells, do not reach the lumen, and secrete calcitonin.

## sections
### Definition
The ASU Zahra source teaches that follicular cells synthesize and store thyroglobulin in the follicular lumen, iodinate it, then under TSH endocytose thyroglobulin and release T3 and T4 after lysosomal breakdown.

### Mechanism
The source describes a cycle in which glycoprotein is formed in rER and Golgi apparatus, discharged into the follicular lumen, iodinated and stored as iodinated thyroglobulin, then taken back up under TSH for lysosomal release of T3 and T4.

### Key determinants
The determinants in the follicular-cell pathway are luminal thyroglobulin storage, iodide uptake and oxidation, iodinated thyroglobulin formation, TSH-stimulated endocytosis and lysosomal breakdown.

### Parafollicular C-cells
The ASU Zahra source places parafollicular C-cells between the basement membrane and follicular cells, not reaching the follicular lumen, and states that they secrete calcitonin.

### Clinical significance
This batch does not author disease, diagnosis, treatment, dosing or emergency-management claims. It keeps to the cell-location and cell-process statements directly stated in the selected local histology source.

### Common misconceptions
Do not skip the luminal thyroglobulin storage step when describing the follicular-cell pathway in this ASU source.

Do not place parafollicular C-cells in the follicular lumen; the source says they do not reach the lumen.

## hold_these
Follicular cells synthesize and store thyroglobulin in the follicular lumen before hormone release.
TSH is linked in this source to endocytosis of thyroglobulin and lysosomal release of T3 and T4.
Parafollicular C-cells do not reach the follicular lumen and secrete calcitonin.

## lose_the_mark
Saying finished T3 and T4 are simply exocytosed without the thyroglobulin uptake and lysosomal step taught here.
Placing C-cells inside the follicular lumen.
