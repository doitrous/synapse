## summary
Thirteen facts tested by the ASU HCB Dr. Ahmed Zahra "MCQ IN HISTOLOGY" cytology bank (322 questions across Parts A/B/C) that are not covered by any existing pending cytology concept: heterochromatin's properties (coiled, electron-dense, inactive) and euchromatin's (extended, active, not resolved by light microscopy); nucleolus-associated heterochromatin as a chromatin mass distinct from the nucleolar organizer region; the nuclear envelope's true features and that it is not itself acidophilic; the nuclear pore complex as the non-membranous protein assembly filling the pore, distinct from the pore itself; the centriole's self-replicating property, shared with mitochondria but not the Golgi; that the Golgi and coated vesicles (not lysosomes) maintain and renew the plasma membrane; the classification of secondary lysosomes into multivesicular body, residual body and heterolysosome, excluding the unrelated Barr body; the red-granule light-microscopic appearance of secretory vesicles; the classification of glycogen and lipid as stored food versus carbon particles as an exogenous pigment; the complete-13-protofilament subunit A versus incomplete subunit B of the axonemal doublet microtubule; the single-long-flagellum-versus-multiple-short-cilia comparison; skeletal muscle as the classic multinucleated cell formed by myoblast fusion; and the ribosome's 20-30 nm electron-dense, non-membranous EM appearance.

## sections
### Definition
Heterochromatin is the highly coiled, electron-dense, transcriptionally inactive form of chromatin; euchromatin is its loosely extended, transcriptionally active counterpart, which is not distinctly resolved by light microscopy because it is not densely packed enough to form a visible clump.
A rim of condensed, nucleolus-associated heterochromatin typically surrounds the nucleolus, separate from the nucleolar organizer region (the rDNA-carrying chromosomal locus).
The nuclear envelope is a double-walled membrane, perforated by nuclear pores and separated by a perinuclear space; it is not itself an acidophilic structure.
The nuclear pore complex is the large, non-membranous, multi-protein cylindrical assembly that fills each nuclear pore and regulates traffic across the envelope.
Centrioles are self-replicating organelles, duplicating once per cell cycle in the same way mitochondria divide by fission; the Golgi complex has no equivalent self-duplication mechanism.
The plasma membrane is maintained and renewed by Golgi-derived vesicles supplying new membrane and by coated vesicles retrieving and recycling existing membrane; lysosomes play no part in this membrane traffic.
Secondary lysosomes are subclassified as heterolysosomes, multivesicular bodies and residual bodies, depending on what has fused with the primary lysosome and how far digestion has progressed; the Barr body (an inactivated X chromosome) is an unrelated nuclear structure.
By light microscopy, protein-rich secretory vesicles typically stain as red (acidophilic) granules.
Cytoplasmic inclusions are grouped into stored food (glycogen, lipid), endogenous pigment (melanin, lipofuscin, haemoglobin) and exogenous pigment (carotene, inhaled carbon particles).
The axonemal doublet microtubule of a cilium or flagellum is built from a complete subunit A (13 protofilaments, like an ordinary microtubule) fused to an incomplete subunit B that shares part of its wall with subunit A.
A ciliated cell bears many short cilia; a flagellated cell (the spermatozoon) bears a single, much longer flagellum, though both structures share the same 9+2 axoneme.
Skeletal muscle fibres are the classic multinucleated human cell, formed developmentally by the fusion of many mononucleated myoblasts.
On electron microscopy, ribosomes appear as small (20-30 nm), electron-dense granules; despite their close association with the rough endoplasmic reticulum, ribosomes themselves are non-membranous.

### Mechanism
Dense supercoiling of heterochromatin's DNA-histone packaging blocks the transcription machinery's access, so only the loosely packed euchromatin is available for RNA polymerase to transcribe; this is also why heterochromatin, but not euchromatin, condenses enough to be seen as a distinct dark mass by light microscopy.
The nucleolus itself has no membrane, so the condensed chromatin found against its surface (nucleolus-associated heterochromatin) is simply ordinary peripheral-type heterochromatin that happens to lie next to the nucleolar organizer region rather than being part of it.
The nuclear envelope's two membranes fuse at each nuclear pore, and it is this fused rim that the nuclear pore complex — an assembly of roughly thirty nucleoporin proteins arranged as a non-membranous cylinder — occupies, forming a selective channel for nucleocytoplasmic transport.
A centriole nucleates the growth of a new, perpendicular daughter centriole once per cell cycle (S phase), the same autonomous-duplication logic mitochondria use when they divide by fission; the Golgi, by contrast, is continuously rebuilt from membrane and protein supplied by the rER-to-Golgi and endocytic traffic rather than duplicating itself.
New plasma membrane, synthesised and packaged by the Golgi, reaches the cell surface via secretory and transfer vesicles; membrane retrieved from the surface for recycling or further processing does so via clathrin-coated vesicles, keeping total membrane area in balance without any lysosomal involvement.
A primary lysosome fusing with a phagosome or pinocytic vesicle becomes a heterolysosome; one containing small internalised vesicles is a multivesicular body; one left holding undigested residue is a residual body — all three are secondary lysosomes, unlike the Barr body, which is inactivated X-chromosome chromatin with no lysosomal relationship at all.
Secretory (zymogen) granules are packed with concentrated protein, which takes up eosin strongly, giving them their characteristic red appearance under the light microscope in a cell such as a pancreatic acinar cell.
Glycogen and lipid droplets are reserve fuel the cell can mobilise on demand, which is why they are classed as stored food rather than pigment; carbon particles, inhaled from air pollution or smoke and phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages, accumulate permanently as an exogenous pigment (anthracosis) because the cell cannot metabolise or excrete them.
Each axonemal doublet's subunit A closes into a full 13-protofilament tube exactly like an ordinary cytoplasmic microtubule, while subunit B, fused onto it, is built from fewer protofilaments and shares part of subunit A's wall instead of closing its own — this asymmetry is what lets dynein arms, anchored on subunit A, walk along the neighbouring doublet's subunit B to generate the ciliary beat.
Both cilia and flagella are built on the identical 9+2 microtubule axoneme, but a ciliated epithelium (such as the trachea) needs many short cilia beating in a coordinated wave to move surface fluid, whereas the single sperm flagellum needs to be long to propel the whole cell forward.
During skeletal muscle development, individual mononucleated myoblasts align and fuse their plasma membranes into one continuous myotube, carrying forward each contributing myoblast's own nucleus, so a mature skeletal muscle fibre ends up with many nuclei lying peripherally beneath the sarcolemma.
Ribosomes are ribonucleoprotein particles (rRNA plus protein, as a large and small subunit) with no lipid bilayer of their own, so on electron microscopy they appear simply as small electron-dense granules, whether they are free in the cytosol or docked on the cytoplasmic face of the rough endoplasmic reticulum.

### Key determinants
"Highly coiled, electron-dense" plus "inactive" identifies heterochromatin; "extended" plus "active, not LM-visible" identifies euchromatin.
"Attached to the nucleolus" (a chromatin mass) is nucleolus-associated heterochromatin; "carries the rDNA genes" (a chromosomal locus) is the nucleolar organizer region.
"Double membrane, pores, perinuclear space" are true nuclear-envelope features; "acidophilic" is the false one.
"Non-membranous cylindrical structure that fits the pore" is the nuclear pore complex, not simply "the pores" themselves.
"Self-duplicating" among the organelles offered picks out centrioles and mitochondria; the Golgi is never the self-duplicating answer.
"Maintain/renew the membrane" picks Golgi + coated vesicles; lysosomes are the excluded option whenever this pairing is tested.
"Except… Barr body" flags the false member of a secondary-lysosome list; the true members are heterolysosome, multivesicular body, residual body.
"By LM… red granules" identifies a protein-rich secretory vesicle.
"Stored food" (glycogen, lipid) versus "exogenous pigment" (carotene, carbon particles) versus "endogenous pigment" (melanin, lipofuscin, haemoglobin) sorts every cell-inclusion option in this cluster.
"Subunit A = 13 protofilaments, complete" versus "subunit B = incomplete, shares A's wall" is the axonemal-doublet-specific fact, distinct from an ordinary microtubule's 13 protofilaments.
"Single and longer" is the flagellum-versus-cilium answer; "multiple and shorter" describes cilia.
"Multinucleated" points to skeletal muscle fibre among the cell types offered, formed by myoblast fusion, not to nerve, liver, steroid-forming or epithelial cells.
"20-30 nm electron-dense granule" plus "non-membranous" identifies ribosome EM appearance; "membranous" is the false statement whenever ribosomes are the subject.

### Clinical significance
This is not a treatment rule. Its study value is telling apart thirteen commonly confused, stand-alone cell-biology facts this MCQ bank tests directly, several with a genuine clinical anchor: skeletal muscle's multinucleation underlies its regenerative reliance on satellite (myoblast-like) cells, and correctly separating stored food from pigment classifies conditions such as anthracosis (carbon particle accumulation) and lipofuscin-associated cellular ageing.

### Common misconceptions
Assuming euchromatin, because it is the "active" form, must be the one visibly stained and easy to see by light microscopy — it is heterochromatin, the inactive form, that condenses enough to be seen as a distinct mass.
Treating nucleolus-associated heterochromatin and the nucleolar organizer region as the same structure — one is condensed chromatin lying beside the nucleolus, the other is the rDNA-carrying chromosomal locus.
Calling the nuclear envelope acidophilic because it sits beside chromatin and cytoplasmic structures that often are — the envelope itself is not classically described as an acidophilic structure.
Using "nuclear pore" and "nuclear pore complex" interchangeably — the pore is the perforation, the complex is the protein structure filling it.
Assuming the Golgi self-duplicates the way centrioles and mitochondria do — it is instead continuously rebuilt from delivered membrane and protein.
Crediting lysosomes with a role in membrane maintenance and renewal because they are membrane-bound and vesicle-related — their role is digestive, not membrane-supplying.
Classing the Barr body as a type of secondary lysosome because it appears in the same answer list — it is inactivated X-chromosome chromatin, unrelated to the lysosomal system.
Confusing a secretory vesicle's red (acidophilic) LM appearance with the blue/purple basophilia of a ribosome-rich cell.
Calling glycogen or lipid a pigment, or calling carbon particles endogenous because they persist permanently in tissue.
Applying the ordinary microtubule's 13-protofilament count to both halves of the axonemal doublet, when only subunit A is complete.
Assuming a flagellum is simply "a long cilium" without noting the number difference (single versus multiple) alongside the length difference.
Naming hepatocytes as the classic multinucleated cell because a minority are binucleate — true multinucleation from cell fusion is skeletal muscle's defining feature.
Assuming a ribosome is membranous because it is so often shown docked on the rER's membrane.
