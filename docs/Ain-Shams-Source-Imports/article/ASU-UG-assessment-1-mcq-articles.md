## summary
A decrease in plasma oncotic pressure increases glomerular filtration rate.

## sections
### Definition
A decrease in plasma oncotic pressure increases glomerular filtration rate.

### Mechanism
GFR is driven by the net filtration pressure across the glomerular capillary, which equals the glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure minus Bowman's capsule hydrostatic pressure minus the glomerular capillary oncotic pressure (the main opposing force to filtration). Lowering plasma oncotic pressure — as happens with hypoalbuminemia — reduces this opposing force, so more of the hydrostatic pressure is available to drive fluid out of the capillary, increasing GFR.

### Key determinants
Forgetting that oncotic pressure opposes filtration, so a fall in oncotic pressure raises GFR rather than lowering it.

### Definition
Bartter syndrome causes hypokalemia through loop-of-Henle salt wasting and secondary hyperaldosteronism.

### Mechanism
Bartter syndrome is an inherited tubulopathy caused by defective salt reabsorption in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, functionally mimicking chronic loop diuretic use. This produces volume depletion that activates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, and the resulting hyperaldosteronism drives renal potassium wasting, so patients characteristically present with hypokalemia together with metabolic alkalosis.

### Key determinants
Forgetting that a loop-of-Henle salt-wasting tubulopathy produces the same secondary hyperaldosteronism/hypokalemia picture as chronic loop diuretic use.

## sections
### Definition
A rise in glomerular filtration rate increases peritubular capillary oncotic pressure, driving glomerulotubular balance.

### Mechanism
When GFR rises relative to renal plasma flow, the filtration fraction (GFR/RPF) increases, meaning a larger share of plasma water leaves the glomerular capillaries as filtrate while plasma proteins stay behind in the blood that continues on to the peritubular capillaries. This concentrates protein in the post-glomerular blood, raising peritubular capillary oncotic pressure, which in turn enhances proximal tubular fluid reabsorption — the physiological basis of glomerulotubular balance, which keeps the fraction of filtered load reabsorbed roughly constant despite changes in GFR.

### Key determinants
Missing the link between filtration fraction and peritubular capillary oncotic pressure, which is the physiological mechanism underlying glomerulotubular balance.

## sections
### Definition
The ascending limb of the vasa recta removes water from the renal medullary interstitium.

### Mechanism
The vasa recta act as passive countercurrent exchangers that preserve, rather than dissipate, the medullary osmotic gradient. As blood ascends back toward the cortex in the ascending limb of the vasa recta, it re-enters progressively less hyperosmotic interstitium, so water that had entered the descending limb (and solute that diffused in along the way) moves back out of the vessel into the blood, effectively removing the excess water that entered the medulla and carrying it away.

### Key determinants
Mixing up the ascending and descending limbs of the vasa recta — water leaves the blood on the way down and re-enters (removing the excess) on the way up.

## summary

## sections
### Definition
Ammonia is an especially effective, adaptable urinary buffer in the kidney.

### Mechanism
The renal tubular epithelium (chiefly the proximal tubule) synthesizes ammonia from glutamine and secretes it into the tubular lumen, where it combines with secreted H+ to form ammonium (NH4+), which is then trapped in the lumen (since the epithelium is far less permeable to the charged NH4+ than to neutral NH3) and excreted in the urine. Because the kidney can increase ammonia synthesis and secretion on demand — unlike the fixed buffering capacity of phosphate — the ammonia buffer system gives the kidney a large, adaptable capacity to excrete the daily acid load and regenerate bicarbonate, making it especially effective specifically in this organ.

### Key determinants
Treating ammonia as a generic circulating buffer rather than recognizing it as a renal tubular mechanism whose real value is that the kidney can adaptively increase its production to handle a larger acid load.

## sections
### Definition
Renal autoregulation of blood flow and GFR is largely myogenic in origin, with a tubuloglomerular feedback contribution.

### Mechanism
Renal autoregulation keeps renal blood flow and GFR relatively constant across a wide range of arterial pressures (roughly 80-180 mmHg) through two intrinsic mechanisms: the myogenic response, in which afferent arteriolar smooth muscle constricts in response to stretch from increased pressure, and tubuloglomerular feedback, in which increased NaCl delivery to the macula densa (from a higher GFR) triggers afferent arteriolar constriction. Because these mechanisms are intrinsic to the kidney itself, autoregulation persists even in a denervated or isolated, perfused kidney.

### Key determinants

## sections
### Definition
Only a small fraction of filtered water is reabsorbed in the medullary collecting duct, under ADH control.

### Mechanism
Correct. Of the water filtered at the glomerulus, roughly two-thirds is reabsorbed obligatorily in the proximal convoluted tubule, with further amounts reabsorbed in the descending thin limb and, when ADH is present, in the distal tubule and cortical collecting duct; only a small remaining percentage of the original filtered water reaches the medullary collecting duct, where ADH-driven aquaporin-2 insertion allows a further, variable, but quantitatively small fraction to be reabsorbed to produce concentrated urine. This final, ADH-dependent step in the medullary collecting duct is what allows fine control of urine concentration and body water balance, even though it involves only a modest fraction of the total filtered water. Without ADH, this segment remains relatively water-impermeable and that last increment of water is instead excreted as dilute urine.

### Definition
Membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis shows tram-track basement membrane splitting with subendothelial deposits.

### Mechanism
Correct. Membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis is characterized by mesangial cell proliferation and mesangial interposition into the glomerular capillary wall, together with subendothelial immune complex deposits; on silver or PAS staining, the interposed mesangium and reduplicated basement membrane produce the classic 'tram-track' (double-contour) appearance. This combination of proliferative and membrane-splitting changes, driven by subendothelial deposits, is what distinguishes MPGN from purely proliferative or purely membranous glomerular diseases and often produces a mixed nephritic-nephrotic clinical picture. Type I MPGN in particular shows this tram-track pattern with subendothelial deposits most classically.

### Key determinants

21-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency causes aldosterone deficiency and salt-wasting hyponatremia.

### Mechanism

This salt-wasting presentation is a medical emergency in neonates if unrecognized.

Anti-GBM disease causes segmental necrosis with crescent formation from parietal epithelial proliferation.

### Mechanism
Correct. Because the antibody attacks a structural basement membrane antigen directly, the resulting injury is typically severe and rapidly destructive if untreated.

### Definition
Chronic bronchiectasis is a classic cause of secondary (AA) amyloid nephropathy presenting with nephrotic syndrome.

### Mechanism
Correct. Chronic suppurative or inflammatory conditions such as bronchiectasis sustain persistently elevated levels of the acute-phase reactant serum amyloid A protein, fragments of which can be deposited extracellularly in tissues (including the glomeruli) as AA amyloid; renal AA amyloidosis characteristically produces heavy, nephrotic-range proteinuria and progressive kidney disease. This secondary (reactive) amyloidosis is a recognized long-term complication of any chronic inflammatory or infective disease process, and bronchiectasis with recurrent or chronic pulmonary suppuration is a classic predisposing condition taught alongside rheumatoid arthritis and chronic osteomyelitis. Renal biopsy in this setting classically shows Congo red-positive, apple-green birefringent deposits under polarized light.

### Definition
The pelviureteric junction, the first site of ureteric narrowing, lies at approximately the level of the transpyloric plane.

### Mechanism
Correct. Because it is the first, and narrowest, of the three classic sites of physiological ureteric narrowing, the PUJ is a common site for a stone descending from the renal pelvis to become impacted, and its level corresponds closely to the transpyloric plane. This anatomical correlation is a standard teaching point linking surface/vertebral landmarks to the sites of ureteric stone impaction.

### Key determinants

### Definition
Gout-associated uric acid stones are smooth, yellow-brown and radiolucent, and can occasionally form staghorn calculi.

### Mechanism
Correct. Uric acid stones, which form in patients with gout or other hyperuricosuric states, are classically smooth and yellow-brown and, because they lack calcium, are radiolucent on plain X-ray (visible on CT or ultrasound instead); while pure struvite (infection) stones are the most typical cause of large staghorn calculi, sufficiently persistent uric acid stone formation can occasionally also fill and cast the renal pelvis and calyces in a staghorn-like configuration. Uric acid stones form characteristically in persistently acidic urine, which favors precipitation of the poorly soluble undissociated uric acid form. Management therefore includes urinary alkalinization in addition to reducing uric acid production or excretion.

### Key determinants

### Definition
Calcium oxalate stones are characteristically rough and brownish-black, pigmented by incorporated blood breakdown products.

### Mechanism
Correct. Calcium oxalate stones are the most common type of renal stone and are classically rough, spiculated, and brownish-black in color, a pigmentation attributed to the incorporation of blood breakdown products (hemosiderin) from the mucosal trauma these irregular stones cause as they form and pass. They are also strongly radio-opaque on plain X-ray because of their calcium content, in contrast to the radiolucent uric acid stones. Their rough surface is what predisposes to hematuria and pain even before the stone has fully obstructed the urinary tract.

### Key determinants
Incorrect. Uric acid stones are smooth and yellow-brown, not the rough, brownish-black stones described.
