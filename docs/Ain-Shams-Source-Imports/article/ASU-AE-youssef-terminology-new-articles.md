## sections
### Definition
Langer's lines are the natural lines of tension mapped over the skin surface. The axis of toe abduction and adduction is the line a toe moves towards or away from. The carpus is the set of small bones between the forearm and the metacarpals.

### Mechanism
Langer's lines run in the direction of the collagen fibre bundles of the reticular dermis -- not the elastic fibres, not the skin creases that form over flexing joints, and not the ridge pattern of fingerprints, each of which is a separate surface marking with its own cause. A surgical incision placed parallel to these lines cuts across fewer collagen bundles, gaps less under tension and heals with a finer scar than one placed across them. For toe movements, the hand and the foot use different reference axes: the hand's is the middle (third) finger, but the foot's is the second toe, because the second toe -- not a geometric "middle" toe -- lies on the long axis of the foot; adduction of a toe is movement towards the second toe, abduction is movement away from it. For the wrist, the carpus is built of eight bones in two rows of four: proximally the scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum and pisiform (the pisiform itself a sesamoid bone lying in the tendon of flexor carpi ulnaris), and distally the trapezium, trapezoid, capitate and hamate.

### Key determinants
Keep three distinctions straight: (1) Langer's lines are about dermal collagen fibre direction, not skin creases, elastic fibres or fingerprints; (2) the foot's toe-movement axis is the second toe, not the middle toe by analogy with the hand; (3) the carpus has eight bones, not seven -- count both rows.

### Clinical significance
This article does not make prescribing or diagnostic-workup claims. Its study value is foundational: surgeons plan skin incisions parallel to Langer's lines to minimise scarring and wound tension, and correctly counting and naming the carpal bones underlies reading a wrist radiograph and localising a fracture (for example, the scaphoid) to the correct row.

### Common misconceptions
Do not equate Langer's lines with skin creases -- creases mark where skin folds over a flexing joint, which is a different surface feature with a different cause.

Do not default to the hand's middle-finger axis when describing toe movements -- the foot's reference line is the second toe.

Do not undercount the carpal bones as seven -- there are eight, four in each row.
