## summary
This ASU Year 3 CNS slice covers: The pharyngeal (branchial) pouches are paired embryonic endodermal outpocketings that give rise to distinct adult structures. The fourth pharyngeal pouch gives rise to the superior parathyroid glands (along with the ultimobranchial body, which contributes calcitonin-secreting C cells to the thyroid). This is distinct from the third pharyngeal pouch, which gives rise to the inferior parathyroid glands and the thymus — the third pouch's parathyroid derivative migrates further caudally during development than the fourth's, which is why the "inferior" parathyroids (from the third pouch) can end up more variable in final position than the "superior" ones (from the fourth pouch).

## summary
This ASU Year 3 CNS slice covers: The great cerebral vein (of Galen) is a short, midline venous structure formed by the union of the two internal cerebral veins, which then curves posteriorly around the splenium of the corpus callosum before draining into the straight sinus. Along this posterior course, it lies within the cisterna ambiens (the ambient/quadrigeminal cistern region), a CSF-filled subarachnoid space that wraps around the midbrain; this cisternal location is a distinct anatomical fact from the vein's formation (union of the two internal cerebral veins) and matters clinically because it is a recognised site for vein-of-Galen malformations to present within this cistern.

## summary
This ASU Year 3 CNS slice covers: The hypothalamus is the body's principal thermoregulatory centre, with anterior and posterior regions playing complementary roles: the anterior hypothalamus (anterior/preoptic area) functions as the heat-loss centre, triggering cooling responses (cutaneous vasodilation, sweating) when core temperature rises, while the posterior hypothalamus functions as the heat-conservation/heat-generation centre. ## summary
This ASU Year 3 CNS slice covers: Memory consolidation is the process by which a newly formed, initially fragile short-term memory trace is stabilised into a durable long-term memory. This process depends on the limbic system, chiefly the hippocampus and adjacent medial temporal lobe structures (parahippocampal, entorhinal cortex) together with the fornix and mammillary bodies of the Papez circuit, which bind together the distributed cortical representations of an experience into a lasting memory over time (a process aided by sleep). Damage to these limbic structures, as in bilateral hippocampal lesions, characteristically impairs the ability to consolidate new memories (anterograde amnesia) while often sparing older, already-consolidated memories.

### Definition
Memory consolidation is the process by which a newly formed, initially fragile short-term memory trace is stabilised into a durable long-term memory. This process depends on the limbic system, chiefly the hippocampus and adjacent medial temporal lobe structures (parahippocampal, entorhinal cortex) together with the fornix and mammillary bodies of the Papez circuit, which bind together the distributed cortical representations of an experience into a lasting memory over time (a process aided by sleep). Damage to these limbic structures, as in bilateral hippocampal lesions, characteristically impairs the ability to consolidate new memories (anterograde amnesia) while often sparing older, already-consolidated memories.
