## title
The inferior salivatory nucleus supplies parasympathetic innervation to which gland?

## question
The inferior salivatory nucleus, via the glossopharyngeal nerve, lesser petrosal nerve and otic ganglion, supplies preganglionic parasympathetic innervation to which gland?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The parotid gland

## explanation_a
Correct. The inferior salivatory nucleus gives rise to preganglionic parasympathetic fibres travelling with the glossopharyngeal nerve, then via its tympanic branch and the lesser petrosal nerve, to synapse in the otic ganglion; postganglionic fibres join the auriculotemporal nerve to supply the parotid gland. This is a distinctive single-target parasympathetic route, contrasted with the superior salivatory nucleus, which supplies the other salivary and lacrimal glands via the facial nerve.

## answer_b
The submandibular gland

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The submandibular gland is supplied by the superior salivatory nucleus via the facial nerve, chorda tympani and submandibular ganglion, not the inferior salivatory nucleus.

## answer_c
The lacrimal gland

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The lacrimal gland is supplied by the superior salivatory nucleus via the facial nerve, greater petrosal nerve and pterygopalatine ganglion, not the inferior salivatory nucleus.

## answer_d
The sublingual gland

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The sublingual gland, like the submandibular gland, is supplied by the superior salivatory nucleus via the facial nerve and submandibular ganglion, not the inferior salivatory nucleus.

## title
Which anticholinergic drug is used to treat drug-induced (extrapyramidal) tremor?

## question
A patient develops drug-induced extrapyramidal tremor after starting a typical antipsychotic. Which anticholinergic drug is used to treat it?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Benztropine

## explanation_a
Correct. Benztropine is a centrally acting anticholinergic (muscarinic antagonist) that restores the normal dopamine-acetylcholine balance in the basal ganglia when disrupted by dopamine-receptor-blocking drugs, treating the resulting extrapyramidal tremor, rigidity and acute dystonic reactions. Its mechanism works by reducing the relative cholinergic excess from pharmacological dopamine blockade, rather than by directly restoring dopamine.

## answer_b
Levodopa

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Levodopa restores dopamine itself and is used for idiopathic Parkinson disease; it is not the treatment of choice for drug-induced tremor from dopamine-receptor blockade, since it would counteract the offending antipsychotic's mechanism.

## answer_c
Propranolol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Propranolol is used for essential tremor and for the somatic symptoms of anxiety, not specifically for drug-induced extrapyramidal tremor.

## answer_d
Amantadine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Amantadine has some antiparkinsonian activity via dopamine release but is not the classic anticholinergic first-line agent for acute drug-induced extrapyramidal symptoms.

## title
Which glucose transporter do astrocytes use to take up glucose from cerebral capillary blood?

## question
Astrocytes, whose end-feet envelop cerebral capillaries as part of the blood-brain barrier, take up glucose from the capillary blood using which facilitative glucose transporter?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
GLUT1

## explanation_a
Correct. Astrocytes express the insulin-independent facilitative glucose transporter GLUT1 at high density on their perivascular end-feet and on the capillary endothelium, supporting the astrocyte-neuron lactate shuttle model in which astrocytes metabolise glucose glycolytically to lactate and export it to neurons as an additional fuel source. GLUT1 is distinct from GLUT3, the transporter that predominates on neurons themselves.

## answer_b
GLUT3

## explanation_b
Incorrect. GLUT3 is the high-affinity glucose transporter that predominates on neurons themselves, not on astrocytes/endothelium.

## answer_c
GLUT4

## explanation_c
Incorrect. GLUT4 is the insulin-dependent glucose transporter of skeletal muscle and adipose tissue, not the astrocyte/blood-brain barrier transporter.

## answer_d
SGLT1

## explanation_d
Incorrect. SGLT1 is a sodium-glucose cotransporter found chiefly in intestinal epithelium and renal tubules, not the facilitative transporter used by astrocytes for capillary glucose uptake.

Sympathetic (thoracolumbar) outflow from the spinal cord extends caudally as far as which level?

## question
The sympathetic (thoracolumbar) preganglionic outflow, arising from the lateral horn of the spinal cord, extends caudally as far as which spinal segment?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
L2 (second lumbar)

## explanation_a
Correct. Sympathetic (thoracolumbar) outflow arises from preganglionic neurons in the lateral horn from T1 down to L2, the caudal limit of this column; no further preganglionic sympathetic cell bodies are found in the spinal cord below L2. This fixed T1-L2 extent underlies the paravertebral sympathetic chain's own longer cranial and caudal extensions, since ascending and descending fibres within the chain redistribute this output to targets at other levels.

## answer_b
S2 (second sacral)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. S2 marks part of the sacral (parasympathetic) outflow, not the caudal limit of the sympathetic thoracolumbar column.

## answer_c
L5 (fifth lumbar)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. L5 is caudal to the true limit of sympathetic outflow, which ends at L2.

## answer_d
T12 (twelfth thoracic)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. T12 is within the sympathetic outflow range but is not its caudal limit, which extends two further segments to L2.

## title
What type of sensory receptor is found in the cornea?

## question
The cornea, densely innervated by the ophthalmic division of the trigeminal nerve, is supplied almost exclusively by which type of sensory receptor?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Free nerve endings

## explanation_a
Correct. The cornea's sensory endings consist almost exclusively of bare (free) nerve endings rather than the encapsulated, specialised mechanoreceptors found in the skin. Because free nerve endings are predominantly nociceptors, the cornea is one of the most pain-sensitive tissues in the body, and even light touch is chiefly perceived as pain, which is the physiological basis of the protective corneal (blink) reflex.

## answer_b
Pacinian corpuscles

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pacinian corpuscles are encapsulated mechanoreceptors detecting vibration and deep pressure in the skin and deep tissues, not the receptor type found in the cornea.

## answer_c
Meissner's corpuscles

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Meissner's corpuscles are encapsulated mechanoreceptors detecting light touch in glabrous skin, not present in the cornea.

## answer_d
Merkel discs

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Merkel discs are encapsulated mechanoreceptors detecting sustained pressure/texture in the skin, not the cornea's exclusively free-nerve-ending innervation.

## title
Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, classically in chronic alcoholics, results from deficiency of which vitamin?

## question
Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, classically occurring in chronic alcoholics, results from deficiency of which vitamin?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Thiamine (vitamin B1)

## explanation_a
Correct. Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome results from thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency, most classically in chronic alcoholics because of poor intake, impaired absorption and reduced hepatic storage. The acute phase (Wernicke encephalopathy) presents with confusion, ophthalmoplegia/nystagmus and ataxia from injury concentrated in the mammillary bodies and medial thalamus, and can progress to the amnestic Korsakoff psychosis if untreated; because thiamine is a required cofactor for glucose metabolism, it must be given before or with IV glucose to avoid precipitating or worsening the encephalopathy.

## answer_b
Cobalamin (vitamin B12)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cobalamin (B12) deficiency classically causes subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord and megaloblastic anaemia, not the Wernicke-Korsakoff triad.

## answer_c
Niacin (vitamin B3)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Niacin (B3) deficiency causes pellagra (dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia), a distinct clinical picture from Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome.

## answer_d
Folate (vitamin B9)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Folate (B9) deficiency causes megaloblastic anaemia and neural tube defects in pregnancy, not the Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome.
