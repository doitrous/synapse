# Item

QST-ASULOCO-PURINE-PURINE-Q01

## title
Gout vignette -- which pathway

## question
A patient develops severe pain in the right big toe, and uric acid crystals are present in his urine. This patient's pain is directly caused by the overproduction of the end product of which of the following metabolic pathways?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
De novo pyrimidine biosynthesis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pyrimidine biosynthesis produces bases used in DNA/RNA, not uric acid; its overproduction does not cause gout.

## answer_b
Pyrimidine degradation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pyrimidine degradation produces water-soluble end products (beta-alanine, beta-aminoisobutyrate), not uric acid.

## answer_c
De novo purine biosynthesis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. De novo purine biosynthesis makes new purine nucleotides; while an overactive de novo pathway can indirectly raise uric acid downstream, the uric acid itself is the end product of the degradation (catabolism) pathway, which is what the question is asking about directly.

## answer_d
Purine salvage

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Purine salvage recycles existing bases back into nucleotides rather than degrading them to uric acid; a failure of salvage can indirectly raise uric acid by pushing flux toward de novo synthesis and degradation, but salvage itself is not the pathway whose end product is uric acid.

## answer_e
Purine degradation

## explanation_e
Correct. Uric acid is the direct end product of purine degradation (catabolism) in humans, who lack uricase. Its overproduction, or under-excretion, leads to hyperuricaemia and, when urate crystals deposit in a joint (classically the first metatarsophalangeal joint of the big toe), the acute painful arthritis of gout described in this vignette.

## title
Which statement best describes xanthine

## question
Which statement best describes xanthine?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It covalently binds to allopurinol

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Allopurinol acts as a competitive substrate/inhibitor of xanthine oxidase (it is metabolised to oxypurinol, which binds the enzyme's active site); xanthine itself does not covalently bind allopurinol.

## answer_b
It is oxidised to form uric acid

## explanation_b
Correct. Xanthine oxidase oxidises xanthine to uric acid, the terminal step of purine catabolism -- this is xanthine's defining metabolic fate. Confusing which direction the enzyme acts on xanthine is the recurring trap this question tests.

## answer_c
It is oxidised to form hypoxanthine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This reverses the actual direction: hypoxanthine is oxidised to xanthine (by xanthine oxidase), not the other way around; xanthine is downstream of hypoxanthine, not upstream of it.

## answer_d
It is a substrate rather than a product for xanthine oxidase enzyme

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Xanthine is both a product (of hypoxanthine oxidation) and a substrate (for its own oxidation to uric acid) for xanthine oxidase -- it is not exclusively a substrate; describing it as 'a substrate rather than a product' misses that xanthine oxidase acts on it twice, at two different points in the same pathway.

## title
Common intermediate in purine degradation to uric acid

## question
A common intermediate in the degradation of all purines to uric acid is:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Hypoxanthine

## explanation_a
Correct. Both adenine-derived and guanine-derived purines converge on hypoxanthine before their final oxidation to uric acid: adenosine is deaminated and dephosphorylated down to hypoxanthine, while guanine is deaminated directly to xanthine (which itself derives from hypoxanthine oxidation for the adenine-derived route) -- hypoxanthine is the shared node both routes pass through en route to xanthine and then uric acid. Recognising this shared node is what lets a single enzyme, xanthine oxidase, finish off both purine bases.

## answer_b
Adenosine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Adenosine is specific to the adenine-derived route; the guanine-derived route does not pass through adenosine.

## answer_c
Inosine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Inosine is an intermediate specific to the adenine-derived route (formed from adenosine by ADA), not a point where both purine routes converge.

## answer_d
Ribose 1-phosphate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ribose 1-phosphate is a byproduct released during nucleoside phosphorolysis (by PNP), not itself a purine base intermediate in the degradation sequence.

## answer_e
Xanthine

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Xanthine is indeed a shared later intermediate (both hypoxanthine and guanine converge on xanthine before uric acid), but hypoxanthine is the earlier, more classically cited common intermediate on this exam's printed key.

## title
Recurrent infection with low T and B cells -- enzyme's role

## question
A child was noted to have recurrent respiratory infection; his lab demonstrates a decrease in T and B cells. The enzyme that is defective in this patient is important in which of the following?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Convert ribonucleotide to deoxyribonucleotide

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ribonucleotide reductase converts ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides, an unrelated enzyme not implicated in this immunodeficiency.

## answer_b
Formation of AMP

## explanation_b
Incorrect. AMP formation is a salvage/synthesis reaction (e.g. by adenylosuccinate synthetase or adenine phosphoribosyltransferase), not the reaction whose failure causes this combined T- and B-cell picture.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Incorrect. UMP synthesis is a pyrimidine pathway step, unrelated to this purine-pathway immunodeficiency.

## explanation_d
Correct. A decrease in both T and B lymphocytes with recurrent infection is the classic picture of adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency, one cause of severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID). ADA normally converts adenosine to inosine; without it, deoxyadenosine accumulates to toxic levels that are selectively lethal to developing lymphocytes of both lineages.

The drug allopurinol is an analogue of which of the following?

## title
Lesch-Nyhan syndrome -- defective pathway

## question
A 7-year-old boy suffers from mental retardation and self-mutilation and has increased levels of serum uric acid. These symptoms are characteristic of Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, which is due to a defect in:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Salvage pathway for pyrimidine biosynthesis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is a purine, not a pyrimidine, salvage disorder.

## answer_b
De novo synthesis of pyrimidines

## explanation_b
Incorrect. De novo pyrimidine synthesis is unrelated to this purine-salvage disease.

## answer_c
Xanthine oxidase

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A xanthine oxidase defect would lower, not raise, uric acid (as seen in the rare condition xanthinuria), the opposite of this vignette's hyperuricaemia.

## answer_d
HGPRT (Hypoxanthine Guanine Phosphoribosyl Transferase)

## explanation_d
Correct. Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is caused by a near-total deficiency of HGPRT, the enzyme that salvages hypoxanthine and guanine back to their nucleotides. Loss of salvage forces purine synthesis down the wasteful de novo pathway, raising uric acid, and produces the syndrome's characteristic triad of hyperuricaemia, choreoathetosis and compulsive self-mutilation, inherited X-linked.

## answer_e
Purine degradation

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Purine degradation itself is intact in Lesch-Nyhan syndrome; the defect is in salvage, which secondarily increases the load going through degradation to uric acid.

## title
Reaction catalysed by HGPRT

## question
Which reaction is catalysed by HGPRT (hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase)?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Nucleotide to nucleoside

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Nucleotide-to-nucleoside conversion is 5'-nucleotidase's reaction, not HGPRT's.

## answer_b
Guanosine to guanine + ribose-1-phosphate

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Guanosine-to-guanine-plus-ribose-1-phosphate is purine nucleoside phosphorylase's (PNP's) reaction, not HGPRT's.

## answer_c
Adenosine to inosine

Incorrect. Adenosine-to-inosine is adenosine deaminase's (ADA's) reaction, not HGPRT's.

No other listed enzyme performs this particular one-step base-to-nucleotide conversion.
