## title
Mechanism of action of denosumab

## question
What is the mechanism of action of denosumab?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
RANKL inhibitor

## explanation_a
Correct. Denosumab is a human monoclonal antibody that binds RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand), preventing it from activating RANK on osteoclast precursors. By blocking RANKL, denosumab inhibits osteoclast formation, function and survival, reducing bone resorption. It is given by subcutaneous injection and used in osteoporosis and bone metastases.

## answer_b
Binds hydroxyapatite and induces osteoclast apoptosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Binding hydroxyapatite and inducing osteoclast apoptosis is the mechanism of bisphosphonates, not denosumab — denosumab acts as an antibody against RANKL, not by binding bone mineral directly.

## answer_c
Calcitonin receptor agonist

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Calcitonin, not denosumab, acts as a calcitonin receptor agonist; calcitonin also reduces bone resorption but through a different receptor pathway on osteoclasts.

## answer_d
Estrogen receptor agonist

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Estrogen receptor agonists (e.g., raloxifene as a selective modulator) reduce bone turnover through the estrogen receptor pathway, unrelated to denosumab's RANKL-antibody mechanism.

## title
Beta blockers in hyperthyroidism treatment

## question
Which of the following is true about beta blockers in the treatment of hyperthyroidism?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
They inhibit thyroid hormone synthesis by blocking peroxidase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Inhibiting thyroid peroxidase and blocking hormone synthesis is the mechanism of thionamides such as methimazole and PTU, not beta blockers, which have no direct effect on thyroid hormone synthesis.

## answer_b
They block sympathetic overactivity and are cardioprotective

## explanation_b
Correct. Beta-adrenergic blockers, typically propranolol, are used as adjunct symptomatic therapy in hyperthyroidism: they block the peripheral sympathetic manifestations of excess thyroid hormone (tremor, tachycardia, palpitations, anxiety, heat intolerance) and are cardioprotective by reducing heart rate and myocardial oxygen demand, lowering the risk of tachyarrhythmia. They do not reduce thyroid hormone synthesis and are not curative on their own.

## answer_c
They deplete iodine stores in the gland

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Beta blockers have no iodine-depleting action; iodine handling is unaffected by adrenergic blockade.

## answer_d
They are curative for hyperthyroidism

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Beta blockers are purely symptomatic and cardioprotective adjuncts; definitive therapy requires thionamides, radioiodine, or surgery.

## title
Correct clinical use of Lugol's iodine

## question
What is the correct clinical use of Lugol's iodine?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Long-term definitive treatment of chronic hyperthyroidism

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Lugol's iodine is not used as chronic definitive therapy — its antithyroid effect is transient, and prolonged dosing leads to escape from the inhibitory effect, so it is unsuitable for long-term hyperthyroidism management.

## answer_b
Given for 7-10 days before subtotal thyroidectomy to reduce gland size and vascularity

## explanation_b
Correct. Lugol's iodine (strong iodine solution) is given for 7-10 days immediately before subtotal/near-total thyroidectomy in hyperthyroid patients. High-dose iodine transiently inhibits thyroid hormone release and reduces the size and vascularity of the hyperplastic gland, making surgery technically easier and reducing intraoperative blood loss.

## answer_c
Prevention of endemic iodine-deficiency goitre

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Prevention of endemic iodine-deficiency goitre is achieved through dietary iodisation programmes (e.g., iodised salt), not short-course pharmacologic Lugol's iodine.

## answer_d
Given postoperatively to prevent hypocalcaemia after thyroidectomy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Postoperative hypocalcaemia after thyroidectomy (from parathyroid injury) is managed with calcium and vitamin D supplementation, not iodine.

False statement about methimazole in pregnancy

## question
Which of the following statements about methimazole is NOT true?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is contraindicated in all stages of pregnancy

## explanation_a
Correct — this is the FALSE statement. Methimazole is avoided in the first trimester because of an association with rare embryopathy (aplasia cutis, choanal/oesophageal atresia), so PTU is preferred instead despite its own hepatotoxicity risk; but from the second trimester onward, methimazole is generally preferred over PTU because of PTU's greater hepatotoxicity risk. Because its use is trimester-dependent rather than avoided throughout pregnancy, the statement that it is "contraindicated in all stages of pregnancy" is not true.

## answer_b
It is generally avoided in the first trimester in favour of propylthiouracil (PTU)

Incorrect as an answer to this question — this statement is TRUE. Methimazole is indeed avoided in the first trimester because of its embryopathy association, with PTU preferred instead.

## answer_c
It is generally preferred over PTU from the second trimester onward

## explanation_c
Incorrect as an answer to this question — this statement is TRUE. From the second trimester onward, methimazole is generally the preferred thionamide over PTU because of PTU's greater hepatotoxicity risk.

## answer_d
It inhibits thyroid peroxidase, reducing new thyroid hormone synthesis

## explanation_d
Incorrect as an answer to this question — this statement is TRUE. Methimazole's mechanism of action is inhibition of thyroid peroxidase, which reduces new thyroid hormone synthesis.

## title
Classifying bromocriptine

## question
Which of the following best describes bromocriptine?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
An orally active dopamine agonist

## explanation_a
Correct. Bromocriptine is an ergot-derived dopamine D2 receptor agonist that is orally active. By activating dopamine receptors on lactotroph cells, it mimics the tonic inhibitory action of hypothalamic dopamine on prolactin secretion, making it a first-line drug for hyperprolactinaemia (e.g., prolactinoma).

## answer_b
An injectable somatostatin analogue

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Injectable somatostatin analogues (e.g., octreotide) are used for acromegaly and carcinoid symptom control, not hyperprolactinaemia, and are given by injection rather than orally like bromocriptine.

## answer_c
A gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist

## explanation_c
Incorrect. GnRH agonists (e.g., leuprolide) act on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis and are unrelated to bromocriptine's dopaminergic mechanism.

## answer_d
A dopamine receptor antagonist

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A dopamine receptor antagonist (e.g., metoclopramide) would raise, not lower, prolactin — the opposite effect of bromocriptine.

It is available as an oral tablet

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Oral administration is the route for tolvaptan, another vaptan; conivaptan itself is not formulated for oral use.

## answer_b
It is administered only by intravenous infusion

## explanation_b
Correct. Conivaptan is a non-selective vasopressin V1a/V2 receptor antagonist used to treat euvolaemic and hypervolaemic hyponatraemia (e.g., in SIADH). Incorrect. Vaptans, including conivaptan, are contraindicated in hypovolaemic hyponatraemia — they are used for euvolaemic and hypervolaemic hyponatraemia, and would worsen volume depletion if given in a hypovolaemic patient.

False statement about bisphosphonates and renal function

## question

## subject
endo

A

## answer_a
They can be safely given regardless of renal function

## explanation_a
Correct — this is the FALSE statement. Bisphosphonates are renally cleared and are not safely given in patients with significant renal impairment (commonly around a creatinine clearance below approximately 30-35 mL/min, drug-specific), because reduced clearance raises nephrotoxicity risk and compounds hypocalcaemia risk. They require caution and dose adjustment in significant renal impairment

They are contraindicated in active peptic ulcer disease

## answer_d
Oral formulations should be taken with a full glass of water while remaining upright

## explanation_d
Incorrect as an answer to this question — this statement is TRUE. Patients are instructed to take oral bisphosphonates with a full glass of water, on an empty stomach, and to remain upright for 30-60 minutes to reduce oesophageal irritation.

Adverse effect of octreotide in carcinoid tumour treatment

## question

## title
Insulin preparation for once-daily dosing

## question
Which of the following insulin preparations can be used with once-daily dosing as basal insulin?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Insulin glargine

## explanation_a
Correct. Insulin glargine is a recombinant long-acting insulin analogue engineered to be released slowly and continuously from the subcutaneous injection site, producing a relatively flat, peakless plasma insulin profile lasting approximately 20-24 hours. This pharmacokinetic profile is what allows once-daily dosing as basal insulin replacement.

## answer_b
Regular insulin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Regular (short-acting) insulin has a much shorter duration of action and requires multiple daily doses, not once-daily dosing.

## answer_c
NPH insulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. NPH is an intermediate-acting insulin with a more pronounced peak and a shorter duration than glargine, typically requiring twice-daily dosing.

## answer_d
Insulin lispro

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Insulin lispro is a rapid-acting mealtime insulin with a short duration of action, used for prandial coverage rather than once-daily basal dosing.

## title
Primary action of metformin

## question
Which of the following is true about the primary action of metformin?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It decreases hepatic glucose production

## explanation_a
Correct. Metformin, a biguanide, lowers blood glucose primarily by activating AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) in hepatocytes, which suppresses gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis and thereby decreases hepatic glucose output. This insulin-independent mechanism is why metformin does not cause hypoglycaemia as monotherapy.

## answer_b
It stimulates pancreatic insulin secretion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Stimulating pancreatic insulin secretion is the mechanism of sulfonylureas, not metformin, which does not act as an insulin secretagogue.

## answer_c
It increases renal glucose excretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Increasing renal glucose excretion is the mechanism of SGLT2 inhibitors such as canagliflozin, not metformin.

## answer_d
It delays carbohydrate absorption in the gut

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Delaying carbohydrate absorption in the gut is the mechanism of alpha-glucosidase inhibitors such as acarbose, not metformin's primary action.
