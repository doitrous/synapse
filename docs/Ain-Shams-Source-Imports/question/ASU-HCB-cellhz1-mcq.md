## title
Component not part of cell-membrane molecular structure

## question
Molecular structure of cell membrane include the following except:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Phospholipids bilayer.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The phospholipid bilayer is the structural backbone of the plasma membrane, so it is a true membrane component, not the exception.

## answer_b
Ribophorines.

## explanation_b
Correct. Ribophorins are receptor proteins on the outer, cytoplasmic surface of the rough endoplasmic reticulum that bind the large ribosomal subunit; they are a rough-ER feature, not a component of the plasma membrane's own molecular structure. Confusing the two is easy because both are membranous organelles studded with functional proteins, but the plasma membrane's protein components are peripheral and integral proteins, not ribophorins. This is the deliberately placed distractor testing whether the student can separate plasma-membrane composition from rough-ER composition.

## answer_c
Cholesterol

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cholesterol lies among the phospholipid fatty-acid tails and stabilises the membrane, so it is a genuine structural component.

## answer_d
Integral proteins.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Integral (transmembrane) proteins are one of the two protein classes built into the membrane, so they are a true component.

## title
Meaning of "unit membrane"

## question
Unit membrane means:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Trilaminar appearance in E.M.

## explanation_a
Correct. "Unit membrane" is the electron-microscopic term for the plasma membrane's trilaminar appearance: two electron-dense lines (the polar phospholipid heads and associated protein) separated by an electron-lucent middle line (the hydrophobic fatty-acid tails). This trilaminar image is only resolved by electron microscopy, at roughly 7.5-10 nm thickness, and is the structural basis for calling every cell's surface membrane a "unit" of the same basic three-layered design. It is not visible with the light microscope and is not demonstrated by any light-microscopic stain.

## answer_b
Phospholipids layer.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A phospholipid layer is only one component of the membrane, not the term for its overall trilaminar EM appearance.

## answer_c
One electron dense line with its associated cell coat.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This describes a single dense line, not the three-layered (trilaminar) unit-membrane image.

## answer_d
Cell boundaries as seen by L.M.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cell boundaries are not resolved as a distinct structure by light microscopy at all; the unit membrane is an electron-microscopic finding.

Correct. The cell coat (glycocalyx) is a carbohydrate layer of glycoproteins and glycolipids on the outer membrane surface only; cholesterol is a lipid that sits among the phospholipid fatty-acid tails within the bilayer itself, not in the carbohydrate coat external to it. Placing cholesterol in the cell coat mixes up two separate membrane-associated structures with different compositions and locations. This is the false statement the question is testing.

Incorrect. This is true: cholesterol's principal role is to stabilise the membrane by restricting excessive phospholipid movement.

## answer_d
Keep the phospholipid molecules packed together.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This is true: by wedging between fatty-acid tails, cholesterol keeps the phospholipid molecules more closely and uniformly packed.

Incorrect. Cell recognition is a true cell-coat function: its surface molecules, including blood-group antigens, let one cell identify another.

## title
True statement about integral membrane proteins

## question
Integral membrane proteins:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Are loosely attached to the outer part of phospholipid bilayer.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Loose attachment describes peripheral, not integral, proteins; integral proteins cross the bilayer rather than sitting loosely on it.

## answer_b
Are not attached to the inner part of phospholipid bilayer.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Integral (transmembrane) proteins span the bilayer and so are attached to, and pass through, both the inner and outer parts.

## answer_c
Are the main cause for stability of the membrane.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Cholesterol, not integral protein, is the principal stabiliser of membrane fluidity; integral proteins carry out transport and signalling roles instead.

## answer_d
May Act as channel protein.

## explanation_d
Correct. Because an integral protein spans the full thickness of the bilayer, it can form a continuous pore or channel across the membrane, allowing ions and small polar molecules such as water to cross; this channel-forming role, together with acting as a carrier or pump, is one of the defining functions of integral membrane proteins. A peripheral protein, confined to one surface, cannot form a transmembrane channel. This is why channel-protein function is used to identify the integral class specifically.
