# Item

QST-NEU-ASU-AE-YOUSSEF1-ROOT-SENSORY

## title
Purely sensory part of a spinal nerve

## question
Which part of a spinal nerve is formed of purely sensory fibres?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Ventral ramus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The ventral ramus lies distal to the union of the two roots, so like the whole spinal nerve trunk it is a mixed nerve, carrying both motor and sensory fibres, not purely sensory ones.

## answer_b
Dorsal root

## explanation_b
Correct. The dorsal (posterior) root is the one purely sensory component of a spinal nerve: it carries only afferent fibres, whose cell bodies sit in the dorsal root ganglion just before the root joins the ventral root. This sensory purity exists only at the root itself, proximal to the union that forms the mixed spinal nerve trunk -- once the roots unite, and again once the nerve splits into dorsal and ventral rami, motor and sensory fibres travel together. The companion question in this set asks for the purely motor part, which is the ventral root, the direct counterpart of this fact.

## answer_c
Ventral root

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The ventral (anterior) root is the purely motor counterpart of the dorsal root, carrying only efferent fibres from the ventral grey horn -- the opposite of what this stem asks for.

## answer_d
Dorsal ramus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Like the ventral ramus, the dorsal ramus lies distal to the union of the roots and is a mixed nerve, not a purely sensory one.

## answer_e
Nerve trunk

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The nerve trunk is the mixed nerve formed after the dorsal and ventral roots unite, carrying both sensory and motor fibres together.

## title
Level of the sympathetic outflow

## question
The sympathetic outflow is:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Cervical

## explanation_a
Incorrect. No division of the autonomic outflow is described as purely cervical; the sympathetic preganglionic cell bodies begin no higher than the first thoracic segment.

## answer_b
Cranio-sacral

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cranio-sacral describes the parasympathetic outflow, not the sympathetic one -- the two divisions leave the CNS at opposite levels.

## answer_c
Sacral

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sacral segments (S2-S4) contribute to the parasympathetic outflow, not the sympathetic outflow.

## answer_d
Lumbo-sacral

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lumbo-sacral is not the recognised autonomic outflow level for either division; it conflates parts of both the sympathetic (lower thoracic to upper lumbar) and parasympathetic (sacral) ranges.

## answer_e
Thoraco-lumbar

## explanation_e
Correct. Preganglionic sympathetic neurons have their cell bodies in the lateral horn of the spinal cord from segment T1 down to about L2 or L3, which is why the sympathetic outflow is named thoraco-lumbar. This is the anatomical basis for contrasting it with the parasympathetic system's cranio-sacral outflow, from the brainstem cranial nerve nuclei and S2-S4. Confusing the two labels is one of the commonest errors in autonomic anatomy, since each name points to the exact opposite levels of the other division.

## title
Preganglionic sympathetic fibres in the thoracic spinal nerve

## question
Which part of a thoracic spinal nerve contains preganglionic sympathetic fibres?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Dorsal root

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The dorsal root carries only somatic sensory (afferent) fibres; sympathetic fibres are efferent and travel via the ventral, not dorsal, root.

## answer_b
Dorsal root ganglion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The dorsal root ganglion houses the cell bodies of somatic sensory neurons, not preganglionic sympathetic neurons, whose cell bodies instead sit in the lateral horn of the spinal cord.

## answer_c
Ventral root

## explanation_c
Correct. Preganglionic sympathetic neurons have their cell bodies in the lateral horn of the thoracolumbar spinal cord. Their axons leave the cord in the ventral root, alongside the somatic motor fibres, travel a short distance in the mixed spinal nerve trunk, and then branch off via the white ramus communicans to reach the sympathetic chain ganglion. At the level this question asks about -- the spinal nerve itself, before that branching -- the preganglionic sympathetic fibres are still inside the ventral root.

## answer_d
Dorsal ramus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The dorsal ramus is a branch of the mixed spinal nerve trunk distal to where the sympathetic fibres would already have left via the white ramus communicans; it is not the structure carrying preganglionic sympathetic fibres at the level this question asks about.

## answer_e
Grey ramus communicans

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The grey ramus communicans carries postganglionic, not preganglionic, sympathetic fibres, returning from the sympathetic chain ganglion back to the spinal nerve for distribution to the body wall and limbs.

## subject

## answer_a
Cervical

## explanation_a
Incorrect. No division of the autonomic outflow is described as purely cervical.

## answer_b
Cranio-sacral

## explanation_b
Correct. The parasympathetic outflow leaves the CNS at two separate, non-contiguous levels: preganglionic fibres arise from the nuclei of cranial nerves III, VII, IX and X in the brainstem, and from the lateral grey matter of spinal cord segments S2-S4. Together these two sites give the outflow its name, cranio-sacral, distinguishing it from the sympathetic system's single continuous thoracolumbar band (T1 to L2/L3). Sacral

Incorrect. Sacral segments S2-S4 are only the caudal half of the parasympathetic outflow; the cranial nerve component is equally part of the definition, which is why the outflow is called cranio-sacral rather than simply sacral.

## answer_d
Lumbo-sacral

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lumbo-sacral is not the recognised parasympathetic outflow level; the sacral segments contribute, but the cranial nerve nuclei, not lumbar segments, provide the rostral component.

## answer_e
Thoraco-lumbar

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Thoraco-lumbar describes the sympathetic outflow, the opposite division from the one this stem asks about.

QST-NEU-ASU-AE-YOUSSEF1-GANGLIA-PLEXUS-CORRECT

## title
Correct statement about the nervous system

## question
As regards the nervous system, choose the correct statement:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Nerve plexuses are formed by dorsal rami of spinal nerves

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Nerve plexuses (cervical, brachial, lumbar, sacral) are formed by the ventral rami of spinal nerves, not the dorsal rami, which instead supply the back segmentally without joining a plexus.

## answer_b
Pia matter is the outermost one of the meninges

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The pia mater is the innermost of the three meningeal layers, closely investing the neural tissue itself; the dura mater is the outermost layer.

## answer_c
Sympathetic system has cranio-sacral outflow

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The sympathetic system has a thoraco-lumbar outflow; cranio-sacral describes the parasympathetic system.

## answer_d
Ganglia are groups of nerve cells outside CNS

## explanation_d
Correct. A ganglion is a group of nerve cell bodies located outside the central nervous system, in contrast to a nucleus, the equivalent grouping inside the CNS. Dorsal root ganglia (sensory) and autonomic ganglia (sympathetic chain, parasympathetic terminal ganglia) are all examples of this same structural category. This paired vocabulary -- ganglion outside the CNS, nucleus inside it -- is one of the basic organising distinctions of nervous system anatomy.

## answer_e
Ventral rami of spinal nerves are purely motor

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The ventral rami of spinal nerves are mixed nerves, carrying both motor and sensory fibres, not purely motor ones; it is only the ventral root, proximal to the rami, that is purely motor.
