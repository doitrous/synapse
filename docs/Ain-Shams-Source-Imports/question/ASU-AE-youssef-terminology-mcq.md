# Item

QST-MSK-ASU-AE-YOUSSEF-PLANE-LEFT-PARASAGITTAL

## title
Plane dividing the body into a large right and a small left part

## question
The plane that divides the body into a large right part & a small left part is:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Median

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The median plane runs exactly through the midline and divides the body into two equal right and left halves, not a large and a small part.

## answer_b
Right parasagittal

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A plane offset to the right of the median line removes a thin slice from the right side, leaving a large left part and a small right part -- the mirror image of what this stem describes.

## answer_c
Left parasagittal

## explanation_c
Correct. A parasagittal plane runs parallel and close to the median plane, so it always produces one large and one small half rather than two equal halves. A plane to the left of the median line removes only a thin slice from the left side, leaving a large right part and a small left part -- the exact division this stem describes. This is the same plane class tested from the opposite direction elsewhere in this set: planes are distinguished by the two parts each one leaves behind, not merely by whether they run vertically.

## answer_d
Coronal

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The coronal (frontal) plane is vertical but runs at right angles to the median plane, dividing the body into anterior and posterior parts, not right and left.

## answer_e
Transverse

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The transverse (horizontal) plane divides the body into upper and lower parts, not right and left.

QST-MSK-ASU-AE-YOUSSEF-EPIPHYSEAL-PLATE-LENGTH

## title
Structure responsible for bone growth in length

## question
Which of the following is responsible for the growth of the bone in length?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Periosteum

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The periosteum is responsible for growth in diameter (width), not length -- its osteoblasts lay down new bone on the outer surface of the shaft.

## answer_b
Endosteum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The endosteum lines the medullary cavity and is involved in internal remodelling and repair, not in lengthening the bone.

## answer_c
Epiphysis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The epiphysis is the expanded bony end itself, not the growth-producing cartilage -- that role belongs specifically to the epiphyseal plate within it.

## answer_d
Epiphyseal plate

## explanation_d
Correct. A long bone increases in length by proliferation of the cartilage cells within its epiphyseal plates, one near each end of the shaft. As these cartilage cells divide and are progressively replaced by bone on the diaphyseal side, the shaft is pushed further from the two epiphyses, lengthening the whole bone. This is the growth-in-length mechanism, distinct from growth in width, which instead comes from the periosteum -- the pairing tested by a companion question in this same set.

## answer_e
Diaphysis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The diaphysis is the shaft that lengthens as a result of growth, not the structure that drives the lengthening.

## title
Bone type of the lunate

## question
What is the type of the lunate (carpal) bone?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Long

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Long bones have two expanded ends and a shaft, a form the lunate -- a small carpal bone -- does not have.

## answer_b
Sesamoid

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sesamoid bones are small nodules embedded within a tendon, such as the patella or the pisiform; the lunate is a true carpal bone of the wrist row, not a tendon nodule.

## answer_c
Pneumatic

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pneumatic bones contain air cavities, as in the skull bones around the nose; the lunate has no such air spaces.

## answer_d
Flat

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Flat bones are two thin plates of compact bone around a spongy layer, as in the scapula or skull cap -- a different internal structure from the lunate's uniformly spongy short-bone build.

## answer_e
Short

## explanation_e
Correct. Bones are classified by shape into six classes, and the carpal bones -- including the lunate -- belong to the short-bone class: spongy bone under a thin shell of compact bone, roughly cuboidal in shape. The lunate sits in the proximal row of the carpus, between the scaphoid and the triquetrum, and like every other carpal bone it is a short bone regardless of its individual size or crescent shape. Size and shape of the whole bone do not decide the classification -- what matters is the internal spongy-under-a-thin-shell structure common to every short bone.

The parasagittal plane divides the body into:

## explanation_a

Right & left unequal halves

## explanation_b
Correct. By definition a parasagittal plane is any plane parallel to, but offset from, the true median plane. ## answer_e
Upper & lower unequal parts

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Upper and lower unequal parts still describes a transverse-type division, not the right/left division a parasagittal plane gives.

QST-MSK-ASU-AE-YOUSSEF-TOE-ADDUCTION

## title
Direction of toe adduction

## question
Adduction of toes occurs in which direction?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Towards the middle toe

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The middle toe is not the foot's reference axis for toe movements; that role belongs specifically to the second toe.

## answer_b
Away from big toe

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Away from the big toe describes neither adduction nor abduction correctly with respect to the true reference axis, the second toe.

## answer_c
Towards big toe

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Towards the big toe would describe abduction of a toe lateral to it relative to the second-toe axis, not adduction.

## answer_d
Towards the 2nd toe

## explanation_d
Correct. Unlike the hand, where finger abduction and adduction are described relative to the middle (third) finger, the foot uses the second toe as its reference axis, because the second toe lies on the long axis of the foot. Adduction of a toe is therefore movement towards the second toe, and abduction is movement away from it -- the toe-equivalent of the hand's middle-finger rule, but built around a different digit. This swapped reference axis is a common source of error for students who default to the hand's own convention.

## answer_e
Towards midline of body

## explanation_e
Incorrect. "Towards the midline of the body" is the hip-level term, as in adduction of the whole limb, not the toe-level convention, which uses the second toe as its own local axis.

## title
Structure responsible for bone growth in diameter

## question
Which part of the bone is responsible for its growth in diameter (width)?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Primary center of ossification

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The primary centre of ossification is where a bone first turns from cartilage into bone before birth, not the structure that widens an already-formed long bone.

## answer_b
Periosteum

## explanation_b
Correct. The inner (osteogenic) layer of the periosteum contains osteoblasts that lay down new bone circumferentially on the outer surface of the shaft, so the bone increases in diameter (width) as it grows. This is the width-growth partner to the epiphyseal plate's length-growth role: a long bone lengthens at its epiphyseal plates and widens from its periosteum. Confusing the two roles, or answering "epiphyseal plate" here, is the trap this question is built to catch.

Endosteum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The endosteum lines the inner (medullary) surface of the bone and is involved in remodelling that surface, not in adding bone to the outer diameter.

## answer_d
Epiphyseal plate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The epiphyseal plate lengthens the bone; it does not widen the shaft.

## answer_e
Metaphysis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The metaphysis is the highly active region of the shaft just under the epiphyseal plate -- a site of active remodelling, not itself the structure responsible for width growth.

Incorrect. The patella, a sesamoid bone, ossifies endochondrally from cartilage, not from a membrane.

## answer_c
Sternum

## explanation_c
Correct. The sternum is one of the defining bones of the axial skeleton, together with the skull, hyoid, ribs and vertebral column -- it forms the anterior midline of the thoracic cage, articulating with the costal cartilages of the ribs. This is the mirror fact to the earlier question in this set asking which named bone is NOT axial (the scapula): here the sternum is squarely inside the axial group, unlike every limb or girdle bone offered as a distractor.
