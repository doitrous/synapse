## title
Which histological findings are characteristic of Parkinson disease?

## question
A patient with bradykinesia, resting tremor and rigidity dies, and post-mortem examination of the brain shows loss of pigmented neurons in the substantia nigra along with intracytoplasmic eosinophilic inclusions in surviving neurons. Which protein chiefly composes these inclusions?

## answer_a
Alpha-synuclein

## explanation_a
Correct. Parkinson disease is characterised histologically by loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and by intracytoplasmic Lewy bodies — eosinophilic inclusions composed principally of aggregated, misfolded alpha-synuclein protein — within surviving neurons. This nigrostriatal dopaminergic loss underlies the disease's cardinal motor features, including the bradykinesia, tremor and rigidity described.

## answer_b
Beta-amyloid

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Beta-amyloid is the core protein of the extracellular neuritic plaques of Alzheimer disease, not the intracytoplasmic Lewy bodies of Parkinson disease.

## answer_c
Hyperphosphorylated tau

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hyperphosphorylated tau forms the neurofibrillary tangles of Alzheimer disease, a different neurodegenerative disease with a different histological signature.

## answer_d
Huntingtin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Huntingtin is the protein whose CAG-repeat-expanded gene causes Huntington disease, characterised by caudate atrophy, not Lewy bodies.
