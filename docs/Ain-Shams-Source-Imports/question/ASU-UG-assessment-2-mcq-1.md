## title
A newborn with masculinized (ambiguous) female external genitalia is found to have hyponatremia and hyperkalemia. Which enzyme deficiency is most consistent with this presentation?

## question
A newborn with masculinized (ambiguous) female external genitalia is found to have hyponatremia and hyperkalemia. Which enzyme deficiency is most consistent with this presentation?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
21-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency

## explanation_a
Correct. Classic salt-wasting 21-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency (the most common cause of congenital adrenal hyperplasia) blocks cortisol and aldosterone synthesis, shunting precursors into the androgen pathway; the resulting excess fetal androgens virilize a genetically female fetus's external genitalia, while the aldosterone deficiency causes a salt-wasting crisis with hyponatremia and hyperkalemia in the newborn period. This combination of ambiguous genitalia in a genetic female plus salt-wasting electrolyte disturbance is the classic exam presentation for this enzyme defect.

## answer_b
5-alpha-reductase deficiency

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 5-alpha-reductase deficiency impairs conversion of testosterone to dihydrotestosterone and causes undervirilized external genitalia in genetic males, not virilization of a female fetus, and it does not cause a salt-wasting electrolyte disturbance.

## answer_c
Aromatase deficiency

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Aromatase deficiency impairs estrogen synthesis and can virilize a female fetus through unopposed androgen action, but it does not impair aldosterone synthesis and so does not produce this salt-wasting electrolyte picture.

## answer_d
17-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 17-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency causes excess mineralocorticoid effect with hypertension and hypokalemia (the opposite electrolyte pattern) and undervirilization, not virilization, of a genetic female.

## subject

## vignette

A

## answer_a
Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)

## explanation_a
Correct. Yolk sac (endodermal sinus) tumor is the most common testicular germ cell tumor of infancy and early childhood, and it characteristically secretes alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), which serves as a reliable serum marker for diagnosis and treatment monitoring. Histologically it is distinguished by glomeruloid Schiller-Duval bodies, structures resembling primitive glomeruli that reflect the tumor's yolk-sac-like differentiation. This marker-histology pairing is a classic pediatric oncology teaching point.

Beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (beta-hCG)

Incorrect. Beta-hCG is the classic marker for choriocarcinoma, a different, more aggressive germ cell tumor type, not for yolk sac tumor.

## answer_c
Prostate-specific antigen (PSA)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. PSA is a marker for prostatic tissue (benign or malignant) and has no relevance to a pediatric testicular germ cell tumor.

## answer_d
CA-125

## explanation_d
Incorrect. CA-125 is an ovarian tumor marker, most associated with epithelial ovarian carcinoma, not with testicular germ cell tumors.

## question

## subject

A

## answer_a
Lichen sclerosis

## explanation_a
Correct. Lichen sclerosis is a chronic inflammatory dermatosis of the vulva, most common in postmenopausal women, that produces a thin, whitish, parchment-like epidermis overlying a densely fibrotic (sclerotic) dermis. It carries a small but real long-term risk of progression to vulvar squamous cell carcinoma, so biopsy-confirmed cases need ongoing follow-up. ## answer_d
Extramammary Paget disease

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Extramammary Paget disease shows large, pale malignant epithelial cells infiltrating the epidermis (Paget cells), a distinct histologic picture from lichen sclerosis's epidermal thinning and dermal fibrosis.

## title
A transurethral resection of the prostate specimen shows catheter-related fibrosis and a lymphocyte-predominant infiltrate, with a negative urine culture. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## question
A transurethral resection of the prostate specimen shows catheter-related fibrosis and a lymphocyte-predominant infiltrate, with a negative urine culture. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Chronic (culture-negative) cystitis

## explanation_a
Correct. Chronic cystitis can arise from chronic irritation and instrumentation, such as long-standing catheter use or a transurethral resection specimen, rather than from an active bacterial infection; a negative urine culture together with catheter-related submucosal fibrosis and a predominantly lymphocytic infiltrate favors this chronic, non-infective process. This pattern is distinguished from acute bacterial cystitis, which is culture-positive with a predominantly neutrophilic infiltrate. Recognizing this culture-negative, lymphocyte-predominant pattern avoids unnecessary antibiotic treatment.

## answer_b
Acute bacterial cystitis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Acute bacterial cystitis is defined by a positive urine culture and a predominantly neutrophilic (not lymphocytic) inflammatory infiltrate, the opposite of what is described here.

## answer_c
Emphysematous cystitis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Emphysematous cystitis is a gas-forming infection typically seen in diabetic patients, with gas visible within the bladder wall on imaging, not a culture-negative lymphocytic pattern.

## answer_d
Malacoplakia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Malacoplakia is characterized by von Hansemann histiocytes with Michaelis-Gutmann bodies, a distinct histologic picture from catheter-related fibrosis with a lymphocytic infiltrate.

## title
A bladder biopsy shows keratinized epithelium invading the underlying stroma in sheets and nests, with many cells showing keratin pearls. What does this histologic pattern indicate?

## question
A bladder biopsy shows keratinized epithelium invading the underlying stroma in sheets and nests, with many cells showing keratin pearls. What does this histologic pattern indicate?

## subject

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Invasive, well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma

## explanation_a
Correct. Squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder is graded by how closely the tumor recapitulates normal squamous epithelium; keratinized sheets and nests of malignant cells invading the stroma, with keratin pearls (concentric whorls of keratinization), are the classic hallmark of well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma. Keratin pearl formation reflects a high degree of squamous differentiation, not a more aggressive or poorly differentiated tumor. Recognizing this histologic marker of good differentiation is a key uropathology teaching point.

## answer_b
Invasive, poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma lacks obvious keratinization and forms less organized sheets of atypical cells, the opposite of the prominent keratin pearl formation described here.

## answer_c
Non-invasive (in situ) squamous carcinoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Non-invasive (in situ) squamous carcinoma remains confined to the epithelium without breaching the basement membrane, whereas this specimen explicitly shows invasion of the underlying stroma.

## answer_d
Transitional (urothelial) cell carcinoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Transitional (urothelial) cell carcinoma arises from and resembles normal urothelium, without the keratinized squamous morphology and keratin pearls described in this specimen.

## title
An Egyptian man with a history of urinary schistosomiasis has a bladder mass; microscopy shows keratinizing changes of the epithelium. Which histologic type of bladder carcinoma is most likely?

## question
An Egyptian man with a history of urinary schistosomiasis has a bladder mass; microscopy shows keratinizing changes of the epithelium. Which histologic type of bladder carcinoma is most likely?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Squamous cell carcinoma

## explanation_a
Correct. Chronic infection with Schistosoma haematobium, endemic in Egypt, causes persistent bladder wall inflammation from deposited ova, driving squamous metaplasia of the normally transitional bladder epithelium; this metaplastic epithelium is a well-recognized precursor to squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder. Microscopic keratinization of the epithelium in a patient with this history and geography is a classic exam pairing, distinct from the transitional cell carcinoma that dominates in non-endemic regions. This etiologic association is one of the best-known chronic-inflammation-to-metaplasia-to-carcinoma sequences in pathology teaching.

## answer_b
Transitional (urothelial) cell carcinoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Transitional (urothelial) cell carcinoma is the dominant bladder cancer type in non-schistosomiasis-endemic regions, not the classic schistosomiasis-associated type, which is squamous.

## answer_c
Adenocarcinoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Adenocarcinoma of the bladder is a distinct, less common subtype usually linked to urachal remnants or intestinal metaplasia, not the classic schistosomiasis association.

## answer_d
Small cell carcinoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Small cell carcinoma of the bladder is a rare, highly aggressive neuroendocrine tumor type, not the classic schistosomiasis-associated histology.

A 59-year-old man with flank pain and microscopic hematuria is found to have a 6 cm variegated renal mass; nephrectomy shows nests of cells with clear cytoplasm (clear cell renal cell carcinoma). Which of the following is LEAST likely to be an associated finding?

## question
A 59-year-old man with flank pain and microscopic hematuria is found to have a 6 cm variegated renal mass; nephrectomy shows nests of cells with clear cytoplasm (clear cell renal cell carcinoma). Which of the following is LEAST likely to be an associated finding?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Hepatitis

## explanation_a
Correct. Clear cell renal cell carcinoma is well known for a range of paraneoplastic manifestations, including fever, polycythemia, hypercalcemia, and a reversible hepatic dysfunction syndrome (Stauffer syndrome) that resolves after tumor resection. True hepatitis, meaning genuine hepatic inflammation from an infectious or toxic cause, is not among these classic RCC-associated findings, which is why it is the correct answer to a "least likely" question among these options. Distinguishing Stauffer syndrome's abnormal liver function tests from true hepatitis is the key discriminating point here.

## answer_b
Polycythemia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Polycythemia, from ectopic erythropoietin secretion by the tumor, is a well-recognized paraneoplastic finding in renal cell carcinoma, making it a likely (not least likely) association.

## answer_c
Hypercalcemia

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hypercalcemia, from tumor secretion of parathyroid hormone-related peptide, is a well-recognized paraneoplastic finding in renal cell carcinoma.

## answer_d
Fever

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fever is a classic constitutional paraneoplastic finding in renal cell carcinoma, part of its recognized presentation alongside flank pain, hematuria, and a palpable mass.

## answer_d
A family history of polycystic kidney disease

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A family history of polycystic kidney disease reflects a fixed genetic risk that cannot be modified, unlike obesity.

Among adults, men have the lowest baseline susceptibility to urinary tract infection, mainly because the male urethra is considerably longer than the female urethra and lies farther from the perianal/vaginal flora that seed most ascending UTIs, and normal prostatic secretions have some antibacterial activity. Male UTI risk does rise later in life with benign prostatic hyperplasia and instrumentation, but this does not change the general baseline comparison. Women, by contrast, are far more susceptible throughout most of adult life because of their short urethra.

## answer_b
Adult females

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Adult women, because of their short urethra and its proximity to periurethral flora, are substantially more susceptible to UTI than adult men, not less.

## answer_c
Pregnant women

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pregnancy further increases UTI susceptibility, through urinary stasis from progesterone-mediated ureteral relaxation and mechanical compression by the gravid uterus, making pregnant women more, not less, susceptible.

## answer_d
Sexually active young women

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sexual activity increases UTI risk by introducing periurethral bacteria into the bladder (the mechanism behind "honeymoon cystitis"), making sexually active young women more, not less, susceptible.

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Control of hypertension

## explanation_a
Correct. Primary prevention aims to stop disease before it starts by controlling underlying risk factors, and because hypertension is one of the leading causes of chronic kidney disease and its progression to end-stage renal disease, effective blood pressure control is a core primary prevention strategy for renal disease. This is distinguished from strategies aimed at people who already have early or established disease.

## answer_b
Screening asymptomatic adults for urine albumin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Screening asymptomatic individuals for urine albumin to catch early, otherwise silent kidney disease is secondary prevention (early detection), not primary prevention.

## answer_c
Dialysis for established renal failure

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Dialysis manages patients who already have established renal failure, which is tertiary prevention (limiting the impact of established disease), not primary prevention.

## answer_d
Renal transplantation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Renal transplantation, like dialysis, is a tertiary-prevention-level intervention for patients with established end-stage renal disease, not a primary prevention measure.
