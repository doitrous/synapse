## title
Organelle that forms zymogen granules

## question
On examining a cell of pancreatic acini by EM, you observe multiple zymogen granules in its cytoplasm. What is the organelle responsible for their formation?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Lysosome

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The lysosome digests material rather than packaging secretory granules, so it is not where zymogen granules are assembled.

## answer_b
sER

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum handles lipid synthesis and detoxification, not the packaging of a protein secretory granule.

## answer_c
Golgi

## explanation_c
Correct. The Golgi apparatus is the exit-face organelle that packs, concentrates and buds off secretory products — in a pancreatic acinar cell this is exactly how the zymogen granules of digestive enzyme precursors are formed. The Golgi receives the enzyme protein made on rough endoplasmic reticulum and delivered by transfer vesicles to its entry (cis) face, then concentrates and packages it into a membrane-bound granule that leaves from the exit (trans) face. This trans-face budding of a concentrated, membrane-bound secretory granule is precisely the zymogen-granule-formation step this question tests.

## answer_d
Polysomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Polysomes are free ribosome clusters that translate protein; they do not package or concentrate the finished protein into a membrane-bound granule.

## title
Vesicle delivering rER protein to the Golgi

## question
Which of the following structures can deliver proteins synthesized by ribosomes of rER to the Golgi apparatus?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Coated vesicles

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Coated (clathrin-coated) vesicles are chiefly associated with receptor-mediated endocytosis at the plasma membrane and with some Golgi output, not with the rER-to-Golgi delivery step.

## answer_b
Pinocytotic vesicles

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Pinocytotic vesicles bring extracellular fluid into the cell from the plasma membrane; they play no part in moving newly made protein from the rER.

## answer_c
Phagocytic vesicles

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Phagocytic vesicles engulf solid extracellular particles; they are unrelated to intracellular rER-to-Golgi transport.

## answer_d
Transfer vesicles

## explanation_d
Correct. Transfer vesicles bud from the rough endoplasmic reticulum carrying newly synthesised protein and fuse with the entry (cis) face of the Golgi apparatus. This is a Golgi input, not a Golgi product — the most often tested distinction in this topic — and it is the specific mechanism by which rER protein reaches the Golgi for further processing. Recognising "transfer vesicle" as the rER-to-Golgi carrier, as opposed to "secretory vesicle" which carries a finished Golgi product outward, is exactly what this question checks.

## answer_e
Secretory vesicles

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Secretory vesicles carry the finished, packaged product away from the Golgi's exit face; they do not carry protein into the Golgi from the rER.

## title
Organelle digesting phagocytosed bacteria

## question
In dealing with phagocytosed bacteria which of the following organelles are Primarily involved?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
SER

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum handles lipid metabolism and detoxification, not intracellular digestion of engulfed bacteria.

## answer_b
Peroxisomes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Peroxisomes detoxify hydrogen peroxide and oxidise long-chain fatty acids; they are not the organelle primarily responsible for digesting phagocytosed material.

## answer_c
Endosomes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Endosomes are an intermediate compartment in the endocytic pathway, but the actual digestion is carried out once lysosomal enzymes are delivered, not by the endosome itself.

## answer_d
RER

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Rough endoplasmic reticulum synthesises secretory and lysosomal proteins; it does not itself digest engulfed bacteria.

## answer_e
Lysosomes

## explanation_e
Correct. Lysosomes are the membrane-bound organelles containing hydrolytic enzymes — acid phosphatase, protease, sulfatase, phospholipase, nuclease — that are abundant in phagocytic cells such as macrophages and neutrophils and are specifically responsible for digesting phagocytosed bacteria and viruses. The phagosome containing the engulfed bacterium fuses with a lysosome to form a phagolysosome, inside which the hydrolytic enzymes destroy the microbe. This is the organelle's principal defensive function and the reason lysosomes are concentrated in phagocytic white blood cells.

## title
Route of hydrolytic enzymes from Golgi to lysosome

## question
Hydrolytic enzymes are transferred from Golgi apparatus to which of the following organelles?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Phagosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A phagosome is formed at the plasma membrane around an engulfed particle; it does not receive hydrolytic enzymes directly from the Golgi.

## answer_b
Endosomes

## explanation_b
Correct. The Golgi apparatus buds off hydrolytic enzymes toward endosomes, which mature into lysosomes as the enzymes accumulate and the compartment acidifies — the Golgi-to-endosome route is the standard path by which a primary lysosome is formed. This route is why the mature, active digestive compartment is described as forming through Golgi-to-endosome enzyme delivery rather than direct Golgi-to-lysosome transfer. Recognising "endosome" as this intermediate step, rather than jumping straight to "lysosome", is exactly the distinction this question is testing.

Secretory vesicles

Incorrect. Secretory vesicles carry a cell's export product to the plasma membrane for exocytosis; they are a separate Golgi output from the lysosomal-enzyme route.

## answer_d
Peroxisomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peroxisomes bud from the rough endoplasmic reticulum, not from the Golgi, and their enzymes are made on free ribosomes rather than routed through the Golgi.

## answer_e
Proteasomes

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The proteasome is a separate, non-membranous cytosolic complex that is not built by Golgi budding or delivery at all.

## title
Organelle holding age (lipofuscin) pigment

## question
A 90 years-old woman dies. Autopsy of her heart reveals cardiac cells with golden cytoplasmic granules. This aging pigment accumulates primarily within which of the following organelles?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Endosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Endosomes are an intermediate stage of the endocytic pathway, not the long-term site where the indigestible age pigment accumulates.

## answer_b
Golgi apparatus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The Golgi apparatus packages and modifies protein; it is not where undigested residual material is retained.

## answer_c
Lysosomes

## explanation_c
Correct. What is left undigested inside a secondary lysosome becomes a residual body, which either leaves the cell by exocytosis or stays inside it as lipofuscin — the golden-brown pigment that accumulates with age, particularly in long-lived, non-dividing cells such as cardiac muscle and nerve cells. A 90-year-old's cardiac myocytes are exactly the classic site for this finding, since these cells never divide to dilute the accumulating residual bodies. This is why lipofuscin is described as the "wear-and-tear" or aging pigment of lysosomal origin.

## answer_d
Peroxisomes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peroxisomes handle oxidative detoxification; they are not the site of lipofuscin storage.

## answer_e
Endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The endoplasmic reticulum synthesises protein and lipid; it does not accumulate undigested residual material as a pigment.

## title
How lysosomes are demonstrated histologically

## question
Lysosomes are specifically stained using which of the following techniques?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Histochemical

## explanation_a
Correct. Lysosomes cannot be seen in a routine haematoxylin-and-eosin section and need a histochemical reaction — the acid phosphatase reaction — to be identified, because their content of hydrolytic enzymes is what is being detected rather than a structural stain. The acid phosphatase histochemical reaction localises the enzyme activity itself, producing a visible reaction product exactly where lysosomes are present. This is why "histochemical" is the specific demonstration method associated with lysosomes rather than a routine or immunological stain.

## answer_b
Immunocytochemical

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Immunocytochemistry detects a specific protein antigen using an antibody; it is not the classical technique used to demonstrate lysosomes, which relies instead on detecting enzyme activity.

## answer_c
Tissue culture

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Tissue culture is a method for growing living cells, not a staining technique for demonstrating an organelle in a section.

## answer_d
Vital

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Vital staining colours living tissue with a dye taken up during life; it is not the specific method used to demonstrate lysosomes.

## answer_e
Supravital

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Supravital staining is applied to cells shortly after removal from the body, again a different technique from the acid-phosphatase histochemical reaction used for lysosomes.

## subject

Has homogenous electron dense content

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. a true statement). A primary lysosome does have a homogeneous, electron-dense content on EM, since it has not yet fused with any digestive substrate.

Contain inactive lytic enzymes

Incorrect (i.e. a true statement). The enzymes of a primary lysosome are inactive/unused until the lysosome fuses with a phagosome, pinocytic vesicle or old organelle and its enzymes are called into digestive action.

Once it fuses with a phagosome, pinocytic vesicle or old organelle it becomes a secondary lysosome, which is the active, digesting form — so describing the primary lysosome itself as "active" is the one statement that does not fit the other three true features listed.
