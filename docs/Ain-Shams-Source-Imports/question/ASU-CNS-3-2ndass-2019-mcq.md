## title
Which structure does the stria medullaris thalami connect the septal area to?

## question
The stria medullaris thalami is a white-matter bundle that connects the septal area to which structure?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Habenular nucleus

## explanation_a
Correct. The stria medullaris thalami runs along the dorsomedial margin of the thalamus and carries fibres from the septal area (and other limbic forebrain structures) to the habenular nucleus. This makes it one of the principal inputs of the habenulo-limbic system. From the habenular nucleus, the habenulointerpeduncular tract continues on to link the limbic forebrain with brainstem monoaminergic centres.

## answer_b
Mammillary body

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The septal area connects to the mammillary bodies chiefly via the fornix, not the stria medullaris thalami, which specifically targets the habenular nucleus.

## answer_c
Amygdala

## explanation_c
Incorrect. It is the stria terminalis, a separate tract, that connects the amygdala to the hypothalamus — not the stria medullaris thalami, which links the septal area to the habenular nucleus.

## answer_d
Hypothalamus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The stria medullaris thalami's target is the habenular nucleus, not the hypothalamus directly.

## title
Which local anaesthetic is unique for its intrinsic vasoconstrictor effect?

## question
Which local anaesthetic is unique among clinically used local anaesthetics for producing intrinsic vasoconstriction at its site of application, rather than requiring an added vasoconstrictor?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Cocaine

## explanation_a
Correct. Cocaine blocks presynaptic reuptake of noradrenaline in addition to blocking sodium channels. This produces local vasoconstriction as an intrinsic property of the drug itself, unlike other local anaesthetics. This dual action is exploited in ENT/nasal surgery, where topical cocaine provides both anaesthesia and haemostasis.

## answer_b
Lidocaine

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lidocaine causes vasodilation and is typically combined with an added vasoconstrictor such as adrenaline to prolong its action, rather than possessing intrinsic vasoconstrictor activity.

## answer_c
Bupivacaine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bupivacaine, like lidocaine, causes vasodilation and needs an added vasoconstrictor; it has no intrinsic vasoconstrictor effect.

## answer_d
Procaine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Procaine similarly lacks intrinsic vasoconstrictor activity and does not share cocaine's noradrenaline-reuptake-blocking mechanism.

## title
Which memory system holds the opening words of a sentence while reading continues?

## question
While reading a sentence, the first two words are held in mind as the rest of the sentence is processed and integrated. Which memory system is responsible for this?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Long-term memory

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Long-term memory stores information over much longer timescales (days to a lifetime); it is not what actively holds the first words of a sentence for the few seconds needed to finish reading it.

## answer_b
Sensory memory

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sensory memory is a very brief (sub-second), pre-attentive buffer of raw sensory input, too short-lived to hold the first words while the rest of a sentence is read.

## answer_c
Episodic memory

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Episodic memory stores personally experienced events over the long term, not the moment-to-moment holding of words during ongoing sentence comprehension.

## answer_d
Semantic memory

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Semantic memory stores general facts and word meanings long-term; it is not the active buffer holding specific just-read words during comprehension.

## answer_e
Working memory

## explanation_e
Correct. Working memory is the short-term, limited-capacity system that actively holds and manipulates information for an ongoing task. Here, it holds the sentence's first words online so they can be integrated with the words that follow. This is a classic illustration of working memory's role in language comprehension, distinct from long-term storage systems.

## title
What autonomic effect follows stimulation of the posterior hypothalamus?

## question
Stimulation of the posterior (lateral) hypothalamus produces which autonomic effect?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Tachycardia via sympathetic activation

## explanation_a
Correct. The posterior (lateral) hypothalamus is the principal hypothalamic centre for sympathetic outflow. Its stimulation produces tachycardia together with vasoconstriction and shivering, but not sweating. This contrasts with the anterior hypothalamus, which is chiefly associated with parasympathetic, heat-dissipating responses including sweating.

## answer_b
Bradycardia via parasympathetic activation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Bradycardia via parasympathetic activation is more characteristic of anterior hypothalamic stimulation, not the sympathetically-driven posterior hypothalamus.

## answer_c
Increased sweating

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Sweating is a cholinergic sympathetic response linked to anterior hypothalamic heat-dissipating pathways, and is explicitly not produced by posterior hypothalamic stimulation.

## answer_d
Miosis (pupillary constriction)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sympathetic activation from the posterior hypothalamus produces pupillary dilation (mydriasis), not constriction (miosis).
