QST-OBS-ASU-AE-EMBRYO2-PLACENTAL-BARRIER-EXCLUDES

## title
Layer excluded from the placental barrier

## question
The placental barrier does not include:

## subject
obs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The syncytiotrophoblast

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this layer IS part of the barrier). The syncytiotrophoblast is the outer, maternal-blood-facing layer of the placental barrier.

## answer_b
The cytotrophoblast

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this layer IS part of the barrier, at least in early pregnancy). The cytotrophoblast contributes to the barrier until it becomes discontinuous later in pregnancy as the barrier thins.

## answer_c
The primary mesoderm

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. this layer IS part of the barrier). The primary (connective tissue) mesoderm, with the villus's fetal capillaries embedded in it, forms part of the barrier.

## answer_d
The endothelium of maternal blood vessels

## explanation_d
Correct. Maternal blood bathes the chorionic villi directly within the intervillous space -- it is not contained within its own maternal endothelium at the villus interface, unlike fetal blood, which stays inside fetal capillaries throughout. The placental barrier is therefore built entirely from fetal-derived tissue (trophoblast, connective tissue mesoderm, and fetal capillary endothelium); no maternal vessel wall is part of it.

## answer_e
The endothelium of fetal blood vessels

## explanation_e
Incorrect (i.e. this layer IS part of the barrier). The endothelium of the fetal capillaries within the villus is the innermost layer of the barrier, in contact with fetal blood.

## title
Site of implantation causing placenta praevia

## question
Placenta praevia results from implantation of the blastocyst in the:

## subject
obs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Pelvic peritoneum

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Implantation in the pelvic peritoneum describes a form of abdominal (extrauterine) ectopic pregnancy, not placenta praevia, which is by definition an intrauterine implantation site.

## answer_b
Medial part of uterine tube

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The uterine tube is where tubal ectopic pregnancy occurs, not placenta praevia.

## answer_c
Lateral part of uterine tube

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Like the medial part, the lateral (ampullary) part of the uterine tube is a tubal ectopic pregnancy site, not placenta praevia.

## answer_d
Upper part of the uterus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Implantation in the upper part of the uterus (the fundus or body) is the normal implantation site and does not cause placenta praevia.

## answer_e
Lower part of the uterus

## explanation_e
Correct. Placenta praevia occurs when the blastocyst implants in the lower part of the uterus, so that the developing placenta lies over or near the internal cervical os. Because this places the placenta in the path the fetus must pass through at delivery, placenta praevia is a recognised cause of antepartum haemorrhage and can obstruct a vaginal delivery.

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO2-POLYSPERMY-BLOCK

## title
Mechanism preventing polyspermy

## question
Prevents penetration of the oocyte by more than one sperm:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Acrosome reaction

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The acrosome reaction is what lets the fertilising sperm itself penetrate the corona radiata and zona pellucida; it is not the block that stops subsequent sperm.

## answer_b
Sperm capacitation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Capacitation is a prerequisite the sperm must undergo before it can fertilise at all; it plays no role in blocking additional sperm afterward.

## answer_c
Vitelline membrane

## explanation_c
Correct. Fusion of the first sperm with the oocyte membrane triggers cortical granule release, which alters the oocyte's surrounding coat. This bank's own terminology labels the resulting barrier the vitelline (membrane) block, more commonly taught as the zona reaction. Either name describes the same event: it prevents any further sperm from penetrating and fertilising the same oocyte (polyspermy).

## answer_d
Cervical plug

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cervical plug is a mucus barrier at the cervix relevant to ascending infection and early pregnancy, not to blocking polyspermy at the oocyte itself.

## answer_e
Decidual reaction

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The decidual reaction is the endometrial stromal-cell change that supports implantation, occurring later and at a different site than the polyspermy block.

## question
Implantation of blastocyst is completed in which post-fertilization day?

## subject
dev

## status

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Day 5 is roughly when the blastocyst forms and is still free in the uterine cavity, well before implantation begins.

7th

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Day 7 is approximately when implantation begins (the blastocyst starts to attach and the trophoblast starts to invade), not when it is completed.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Day 13 is beyond the completion of implantation and instead falls within the events of the second week that follow it (bilaminar disc and extraembryonic mesoderm formation).

## title
Origin of the fetal part of the placenta

## question
The fetal part of placenta is derived from:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Decidua capsularis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Decidua capsularis is the maternal endometrium covering the implanted conceptus on the uterine-lumen side; it thins and eventually fuses with decidua parietalis, contributing nothing to the placenta.

## answer_b
Decidua basalis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Decidua basalis is maternal tissue -- it forms the maternal part of the placenta, not the fetal part this question asks about.

## answer_c
Decidua parietalis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Decidua parietalis lines the rest of the uterine cavity away from the implantation site and takes no part in the placenta at all.

## answer_d
Chorion frondosum

## explanation_d
Correct. The chorion frondosum is the region of chorion adjacent to the decidua basalis, where the chorionic villi remain well developed and continue to branch; it persists as the fetal (villous) part of the placenta. Its counterpart, the chorion laeve, is the rest of the chorion, whose villi degenerate as it is pushed away from the richly vascular decidua basalis.

## answer_e
Chorion leave

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The chorion laeve ("smooth chorion") is the part whose villi degenerate; it does not contribute to the placenta and instead fuses with the decidua parietalis to form part of the fetal membranes.

## explanation_b

Fate of the blastocoele

## question
The blastocoele becomes the:

## subject

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

Amniotic sac

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The amniotic sac develops from a separate cavity that appears within the inner cell mass (between the epiblast and the overlying cytotrophoblast), not from the blastocoele.

Yolk sac

## explanation_b
Correct. The blastocoele is the fluid-filled cavity of the blastocyst. Intraembryonic coelom

Incorrect. The intraembryonic coelom forms later still, within the lateral plate mesoderm of the embryonic disc itself, unrelated to the blastocoele.

## answer_e
Chorionic cavity

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The chorionic cavity is the same structure as the extraembryonic coelom, formed later within the extraembryonic mesoderm rather than being the blastocoele itself.

## title
Composition of the chorionic vesicle wall

## question
The wall of chorionic vesicle is made of:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cytotrophoblast + syncytiotrophoblast

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast together make up the trophoblast alone, without the mesodermal layer that lines it once the extraembryonic mesoderm forms -- this describes an earlier stage (the wall of the blastocyst/early implanted conceptus), not the definitive chorionic vesicle.

## answer_b
Cytotrophoblast + exocoelomic membrane

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The exocoelomic membrane lines the primary yolk sac, not the chorion, and is not part of the chorionic wall.

## answer_c
Syncytiotrophoblast + exocoelomic membrane

## explanation_c
Incorrect. As in option B, the exocoelomic membrane belongs to the yolk sac lining, not the chorionic vesicle wall, and syncytiotrophoblast alone omits the cytotrophoblast layer.

## answer_d
Trophoblast + extraembryonic somatic mesoderm

## explanation_d
Correct. Once extraembryonic mesoderm forms, it lines the inside of the trophoblast. The chorion -- the definitive wall of the chorionic vesicle -- therefore consists of the trophoblast (cytotrophoblast + syncytiotrophoblast) plus this extraembryonic somatic mesoderm. This is the same somatic mesodermal layer that also lines the amnion, as distinct from the splanchnic layer that covers the yolk sac.

## answer_e
Trophoblast + extraembryonic splanchnic mesoderm

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The extraembryonic splanchnic mesoderm covers the yolk sac, not the chorion; the chorion's mesodermal component is specifically the somatic layer.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO2-CELL-FERTILIZED

## title
Identity of the cell fertilised by the sperm

## question
The cell that is fertilized by the sperm is the:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Primary oocyte

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The primary oocyte has already completed its first meiotic division, becoming a secondary oocyte, by the time it is ovulated and available for fertilisation.

## answer_b
Secondary oocyte

## explanation_b
Correct. The cell released at ovulation and penetrated by the sperm is the secondary oocyte, still arrested at metaphase of the second meiotic division. Sperm entry provides the calcium signal that triggers completion of this second division, after which the cell is properly called a mature (fertilised) ovum.

## answer_c
Mature ovum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A "mature ovum" only exists once the second meiotic division has completed -- that is, only after fertilisation has already begun, not the cell fertilisation acts upon at the outset.

## answer_d
First polar body

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The first polar body is a discarded product of the first meiotic division; it is not a gamete and cannot be fertilised.

## answer_e
Second polar body

## explanation_e

Previa

## explanation_a
Correct. Placenta praevia (previa) is the term for a placenta that has developed from a blastocyst implanted in the lower uterine segment, so that it lies over or near the internal cervical os. It is defined by abnormal implantation site, in contrast to the other named placental anomalies below, which describe abnormal attachment depth or shape rather than site.

## answer_b
Acreta

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Placenta accreta describes abnormally deep attachment of the placenta into the myometrium (with little or no intervening decidua), a depth-of-invasion abnormality, not a site abnormality.

## answer_c
Membranacea

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Placenta membranacea describes a placenta that remains abnormally thin and spread over most of the chorionic sac, a shape abnormality, not specifically a lower-segment site abnormality.

## answer_d
Succenturiata

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Placenta succenturiata describes an accessory placental lobe connected to the main placenta by vessels, a shape/number abnormality, not a lower-segment site abnormality.

## answer_e
Percreta

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Placenta percreta describes the most severe degree of abnormal attachment, in which villi penetrate through the full thickness of the myometrium and beyond, a depth-of-invasion abnormality, not specifically a site one.
