# Item

## id
QST-ASULOCO-CALCIUM-CALCIUM-Q01

## title
Correct statement about vitamin D

## question
Which one of the following statements concerning vitamin D is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Chronic renal failure requires the oral administration of 1-alpha hydroxycholecalciferol

## explanation_a
Correct. Chronic renal failure destroys the kidney's own 1-alpha-hydroxylase capacity, so plain vitamin D or even 25-hydroxyvitamin D cannot be activated; the patient must be given 1-alpha-hydroxycholecalciferol (or calcitriol itself), a preparation that bypasses the missing renal step. This is the standard treatment for renal osteodystrophy. Without it, calcium absorption and bone mineralisation stay impaired despite normal vitamin D intake.

## answer_b
It is required in the diet of individuals exposed to sunlight

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Adequate sunlight exposure lets skin synthesise sufficient vitamin D on its own (via UV conversion of 7-dehydrocholesterol), so dietary vitamin D is not strictly required in a sun-exposed individual.

## answer_c
25-Hydroxycholecalciferol is the active form of the vitamin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 25-hydroxycholecalciferol is the main circulating storage form, not the active hormone -- the active form is 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol), made by the kidney's second hydroxylation step.

## answer_d
Vitamin D opposes the effect of parathyroid hormone

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Vitamin D (as calcitriol) works together with parathyroid hormone, not against it -- both raise serum calcium, and PTH itself stimulates the renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase step that activates vitamin D.

## answer_e
A deficiency in vitamin D results in an increased secretion of calcitonin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Vitamin D deficiency lowers serum calcium, which triggers a rise in parathyroid hormone (secondary hyperparathyroidism), not an increase in calcitonin, which is instead released when calcium rises.

## title
Vitamin D preparation for resistant rickets

## question
Which Vitamin D preparation is recommended for a patient with Vitamin D resistant rickets?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Vitamin D3

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Plain vitamin D3 still requires two intact hydroxylation steps (liver 25-hydroxylase, then renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase) to become active; in vitamin-D-resistant rickets the renal step is the one that is missing.

## answer_b
24,25 Vitamin D3

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 24,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 is an inactive metabolite, not a therapeutic activation product.

## answer_c
1-Hydroxy D3

## explanation_c
Correct. Vitamin-D-resistant rickets (vitamin-D-dependent rickets type I) results from a genetic deficiency of renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase, so treatment must supply a preparation that is already 1-alpha-hydroxylated -- 1-hydroxy D3 (or calcitriol) -- bypassing the missing enzyme entirely. The same 1-alpha-hydroxylated logic applies to the chronic-renal-failure scenario in the preceding question.

## answer_d
25-Hydroxy D3

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 25-hydroxy D3 still requires the missing 1-alpha-hydroxylase step to become fully active, so it does not correct the defect.

## answer_e
1,24,25 trihydroxy D3

## explanation_e
Incorrect. 1,24,25-trihydroxy D3 is a further, minor hydroxylated metabolite, not the standard therapeutic preparation for this condition.

## title
Parathyroid hormone actions -- which is false

## question
All of the following are true about Parathyroid hormone actions, EXCEPT:

## subject
fnd

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Increase calcium level in blood

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this is true). Raising serum calcium is PTH's defining overall effect, achieved via its actions on bone, kidney and (indirectly) intestine.

## answer_b
Increase calcium reabsorption

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this is true). PTH increases renal tubular calcium reabsorption, directly conserving calcium that would otherwise be filtered and lost in urine.

## answer_c

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. this is true). PTH stimulates renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase, increasing calcitriol production, which further raises calcium via enhanced intestinal absorption.

## explanation_d
Correct as the exception. PTH actually decreases renal phosphate reabsorption (it is phosphaturic), promoting phosphate excretion in urine -- the opposite of what this option claims -- while simultaneously raising calcium, which is why PTH is said to lower the calcium-phosphate product in the kidney's handling of the two ions. Recognising this phosphaturic effect is what separates a complete list of PTH's actions from an incomplete one.

## title
Correct calcium body distribution

## question
Which of the following represents a correct calcium distribution?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It represents 0.1% of total body weight

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Calcium is actually a larger share of total body weight than 0.1% (roughly 1-2%), reflecting how much of it is locked into the skeleton.

## answer_b
1.5% of calcium is stored in bones

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Far more than 1.5% of body calcium is stored in bone -- about 99% of the total body calcium pool is skeletal.

## answer_c
99% of calcium is present in soft tissues

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Soft tissue holds only a small slice of total body calcium, not 99% -- 99% is bone, and soft tissue/extracellular fluid/plasma together make up only about 1%.

## answer_d
0.1% of calcium is present in plasma

## explanation_d
Correct. Plasma calcium is a very small fraction, roughly 0.1% of total body calcium, held within a narrow, tightly regulated range despite the enormous skeletal reservoir sitting alongside it -- this small fraction is exactly what the PTH-calcitriol-calcitonin loop is constantly adjusting. Recognising this heavy skeletal skew is essential to understanding why even small percentage shifts in bone turnover can noticeably move plasma calcium.

## answer_e
0.1% of calcium is present in teeth

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Teeth contain a meaningful mineral reserve as part of the skeletal/dental calcium pool, but this specific figure does not represent the correct distribution.

## title
Order of calcitriol's mechanism steps

## question
Arrange the following steps in the mechanism of calcitriol: 1) Binding a nuclear receptor; 2) Increase intestinal absorption of calcium; 3) Binding to a specific DNA sequence; 4) Activates transcription; 5) Calbindin synthesis. Which is the correct order?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
1,3,4,5,2

## explanation_a
Correct. As a steroid-family hormone, calcitriol first binds its nuclear receptor (1), the receptor-hormone complex then binds a specific DNA sequence (a vitamin D response element) (3), this activates transcription of target genes (4), one of which is calbindin, a calcium-binding protein whose synthesis follows (5), and the resulting calbindin increases intestinal calcium absorption (2), the hormone's overall physiological effect. Getting this receptor-then-DNA-then-transcription order right reflects the same general mechanism shared by other steroid/nuclear-receptor hormones.

## answer_b
1,3,4,2,5

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This places calcium absorption (2) before calbindin synthesis (5), but calbindin must first be made from the transcribed gene before it can do the work of increasing calcium absorption.

## answer_c
1,4,3,5,2

## explanation_c
Incorrect. This places DNA-sequence binding (3) after transcriptional activation (4), but the receptor must bind its specific DNA response element before it can activate transcription of the target gene.

## answer_d
1,4,3,2,5

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Like option C, this reverses DNA binding and transcriptional activation, and it also places calcium absorption before calbindin synthesis, getting two steps out of order.

Hormone acting at high-normal calcium

## question
Which of the following hormones is acting when calcium level is 11 mg/dL?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
PTH

## explanation_a
Incorrect. PTH is secreted in response to a falling (low) calcium, not a high-normal value like 11 mg/dL.

## answer_b
Calcitriol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Calcitriol production is driven upstream by PTH's response to low calcium; it is not the hormone whose secretion is triggered by a high-normal calcium value.

## answer_c
Calcitonin

## explanation_c
Correct. At 11 mg/dL, calcium sits at the upper end of (or just above) the normal range, which is exactly the stimulus that triggers calcitonin release from thyroid parafollicular cells -- calcitonin acts to bring an elevated calcium back down by suppressing osteoclastic bone resorption. Recognising which direction calcium is moving is the key to picking the right hormone in this kind of numeric vignette.

## answer_d
Both A and B

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Both PTH and calcitriol are the hormones mobilised by low calcium, the opposite scenario to the high-normal value given here.

## title
Hormone acting at low calcium

## question
Which of the following hormones is acting when calcium level is 7 mg/dL?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
PTH

## explanation_a
Incorrect alone. PTH is indeed released at a low calcium such as 7 mg/dL, but calcitriol production is also driven up in parallel (PTH itself stimulates renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase), so PTH alone is an incomplete answer.

## answer_b
Calcitriol

## explanation_b
Incorrect alone. Calcitriol is likewise mobilised at low calcium (via PTH-stimulated renal activation), but naming calcitriol alone omits PTH's own direct actions.

## answer_c
Calcitonin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Calcitonin is released in response to a rising, not a falling, calcium -- it would not be the active hormone at a low value like 7 mg/dL.

## answer_d
Both A and B

## explanation_d
Correct. A low calcium of 7 mg/dL triggers PTH release, and PTH in turn stimulates renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase to raise calcitriol production -- both hormones act together in this scenario, PTH directly on bone and kidney, and calcitriol on the intestine, to restore serum calcium. Recognising that both hormones act together, not just one, is the key distinction this question tests.
