## question
Antiphospholipid syndrome, is an autoimmune disease against

## subject

## vignette

C

## answer_a
Fatty acids

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Antibodies in this syndrome are not directed against free fatty acids; fatty acids are not the antigenic target the disease is named for.

## answer_b
Surfactant

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lung surfactant is a different clinical topic entirely (relevant to neonatal respiratory distress syndrome), not the autoimmune target in antiphospholipid syndrome.

## answer_c
Acidic phospholipids

## explanation_c
Correct. Antiphospholipid syndrome is an autoimmune disease in which the body forms antibodies against acidic (negatively charged) phospholipids — a class that includes phosphatidylserine, phosphatidylinositol, phosphatidylglycerol and cardiolipin. These autoantibodies predispose to abnormal blood clotting (thrombosis) and to pregnancy complications such as recurrent miscarriage.

## answer_d
Cholesterol

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cholesterol is a neutral, uncharged lipid, not one of the acidic phospholipids this syndrome's antibodies target; it is not the antigen this disease is defined by.

Which out of the following fatty acids is a precursor of prostaglandins?

## question
Which out of the following fatty acids is a precursor of prostaglandins?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Arachidonic acid

## explanation_a
Correct. Arachidonic acid, a 20-carbon omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acid released from membrane phospholipids by phospholipase A2, is the direct substrate for prostaglandin H synthase (cyclo-oxygenase), which converts it into the cyclic eicosanoids — the prostaglandins and thromboxanes. Lipoxygenase acts on the same arachidonic acid pool to make the acyclic leukotrienes, so arachidonic acid is the single starting point for the whole eicosanoid family.

## answer_b
Oleic acid

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Oleic acid is a monounsaturated omega-9 fatty acid that is not a substrate for prostaglandin H synthase; it plays no role in eicosanoid synthesis.

## answer_c
Linoleic acid

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Linoleic acid is the essential omega-6 dietary precursor that the body elongates and desaturates to make arachidonic acid, but linoleic acid itself is one metabolic step removed from being the direct eicosanoid precursor — that role belongs to arachidonic acid.

## answer_d
Linolenic acid

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Alpha-linolenic acid is the essential omega-3 precursor of a separate eicosanoid family (via eicosapentaenoic acid), not the omega-6 pathway that produces the classic prostaglandins this question asks about.

Cholesterol is a precursor for all of the following except

## question
Cholesterol is a precursor for all of the following except

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Bile acids

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the "except" answer. Bile acids (such as cholic acid) are synthesised from cholesterol in the liver, so cholesterol is genuinely their precursor.

## answer_b
Steroid hormones

## explanation_b
Incorrect as the "except" answer. Every steroid hormone (cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone) is synthesised starting from cholesterol; the steroid nucleus itself comes from cholesterol's own ring structure.

## answer_c
Sex hormones

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the "except" answer. The sex hormones (testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone) are a subset of the steroid hormones and are made from cholesterol via the same steroidogenic pathway.

## answer_d
Vitamin D

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the "except" answer. Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) is synthesised in the skin from 7-dehydrocholesterol, itself derived from cholesterol, under the action of UV light — cholesterol is genuinely vitamin D's precursor.

## answer_e
Glycerol

## explanation_e
Correct. Glycerol is a simple three-carbon alcohol that forms the backbone of triacylglycerols and glycerophospholipids; it is synthesised from glycolytic or gluconeogenic intermediates (such as dihydroxyacetone phosphate), not from cholesterol. Because cholesterol's steroid ring system has no metabolic path to glycerol's simple carbon backbone, glycerol is the one substance in this list that cholesterol is not a precursor of.

## question
Glycerophospholipids include all of the following except:

## subject

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Cephalin

## explanation_a

## answer_b
Lecithin

## question
During Hydrolysis of Lecithin, all the following will be produced except:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Saturated and unsaturated fatty acid

## explanation_a
Incorrect as the exception. Lecithin's glycerol backbone carries two fatty acid chains, typically one saturated and one unsaturated, and hydrolysis genuinely releases these fatty acids, so this is a real product, not the exception.

## answer_b
Sphingosine

## explanation_b
Correct. Lecithin (phosphatidylcholine) is a glycerophospholipid, built on a glycerol backbone, not on sphingosine — sphingosine is instead the backbone of the structurally different sphingolipid family, such as sphingomyelin. Because lecithin never contained sphingosine to begin with, hydrolysing it cannot release sphingosine as a product; this is exactly the glycerol-versus-sphingosine distinction that separates glycerophospholipids from sphingophospholipids. Complete hydrolysis of lecithin instead yields glycerol, two fatty acids, phosphoric acid and choline.

## answer_c
Glycerol

## explanation_c
Incorrect as the exception. Glycerol is lecithin's structural backbone, and hydrolysis genuinely releases it once the ester bonds to the fatty acids and phosphate are broken, so it is a real product, not the exception.

## answer_d
Phosphoric acid

## explanation_d
Incorrect as the exception. Phosphoric acid is released when the phosphodiester bonds linking glycerol to the phosphate head group and choline are hydrolysed, so it is a real hydrolysis product, not the exception.

## answer_e
Choline

## explanation_e
Incorrect as the exception. Choline is lecithin's head group, released as a free molecule once the phosphodiester bond joining it to the phosphate is hydrolysed, so it is a real product, not the exception.
