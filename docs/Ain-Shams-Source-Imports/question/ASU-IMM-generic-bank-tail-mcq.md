## question
Which of the following induces secondary signals augmenting antigen recognition signals of the BCR complex and is important for B cell activation?

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Complement component (C3d) acting on complement receptor 2 on B lymphocytes

## explanation_a
Incorrect alone. C3d engaging complement receptor 2 (CR2/CD21) genuinely is one of the two costimulatory second signals — but option c (PAMP-TLR engagement) is also genuine, so 'a alone' is incomplete.

## answer_b
CTLA-4 on activated T helper cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. CTLA-4 is an inhibitory checkpoint receptor expressed on activated T cells that dampens T-cell costimulation; it plays no role in delivering a B-cell second signal.

## answer_c
PAMPs on microbes acting on Toll like receptors on B-lymphocytes

## explanation_c
Incorrect alone. PAMP recognition by Toll-like receptors on B cells genuinely is one of the two costimulatory second signals — but option a (C3d-CR2) is also genuine, so 'c alone' is incomplete.

## answer_d
Both a and b

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Because CTLA-4 (b) is not a genuine B-cell costimulatory signal — it is a T-cell inhibitory checkpoint molecule — 'both a and b' cannot be correct.

## answer_e
Both a and c

## explanation_e
Correct. Naive B-cell activation is augmented by two distinct, genuine second-signal pathways alongside BCR-antigen engagement: C3d (deposited on antigen during complement activation) engaging complement receptor 2 on the B cell, and pathogen-associated molecular patterns on microbes engaging Toll-like receptors on the B cell. CTLA-4 is unrelated to either pathway, since it is a T-cell inhibitory receptor, not a B-cell signalling molecule, which is exactly why 'both a and c' — not 'both a and b' — is the correct pairing.

## title
The phenomenon of affinity maturation occurring in prolonged and repeated infections is due to:

## question
The phenomenon of affinity maturation of antibodies occurring in prolonged and repeated infections is due to:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
High mutation rate of proliferating B cells at late phase of humoral immune response

## explanation_a
Correct. In prolonged or repeated infections, germinal-centre B cells proliferating at the late phase of the humoral response acquire somatic point mutations in their immunoglobulin genes at an unusually high rate, and B cells whose mutated receptor binds antigen more tightly are selectively expanded. This progressive selection for higher-affinity clones is exactly what affinity maturation means, and it explains why late, repeated or prolonged exposure yields tighter-binding antibody than an early response does.

## answer_b
PAMPs on microbes acting on Toll like receptors on B-lymphocytes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. PAMP-TLR engagement delivers an activation/costimulatory signal to the B cell; it does not drive the somatic mutation process that raises antibody affinity over time.

## answer_c
Complement component (C3d) acting on complement receptor 2 on B lymphocytes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. C3d-CR2 engagement is likewise a costimulatory activation signal, not the mechanism generating the mutations responsible for affinity maturation.

## answer_d
Costimulation of B lymphocytes by CD28 on macrophages

## explanation_d
Incorrect. CD28-mediated costimulation of B lymphocytes by macrophages is not how affinity maturation is generated; affinity maturation is driven by somatic hypermutation within proliferating germinal-centre B cells, not by this costimulatory pathway.

## question
Affinity maturation of antibodies at the late phase of humoral immune response is due to mutations in:

imm

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Ig V region

## explanation_a
Correct. Affinity maturation results from somatic hypermutation targeted to the immunoglobulin variable (V) region — the segment encoding the antigen-binding domain of both heavy and light chains. Because the mutations concentrate in the V region rather than in a constant-region exon, they can improve antigen-binding affinity for the specific epitope without altering the antibody's isotype or effector function, which is exactly the pattern affinity maturation describes.

CH1 region

## explanation_b
Incorrect. CH1 is a heavy-chain constant-region exon (part of the Fab arm's constant domain), not the site of affinity-raising mutations.

CH2 region

## answer_d
CH3 region

## explanation_d
Incorrect. CH3 is a heavy-chain constant-region exon, contributing to isotype identity and effector function, not to antigen-binding affinity.

## answer_e
CL region

## explanation_e
Incorrect. CL is the light-chain constant-region exon; affinity-maturation mutations concentrate in the variable, not the constant, light-chain region.

## question
Which of the following statements describes Monoclonal antibodies?

## subject
imm

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Antibodies sharing epitopes from different species

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Sharing epitopes across species describes cross-reactivity, not the defining property of a monoclonal antibody, which is single-clone, single-epitope specificity.

## answer_b
Antibodies against a single epitope and produced from a single clone of immortal cells

## explanation_b
Correct. A monoclonal antibody is, by definition, produced by a single clone of immortalised antibody-secreting cells and is therefore directed against one single epitope with one uniform affinity — unlike a polyclonal response, which contains antibodies of differing affinities against multiple epitopes on a single immunising antigen. This distinction between a mixed polyclonal pool and a single, uniform monoclonal product is exactly what the question's five options are testing.

## answer_c
Antibodies with different affinities for the same antigen that induced them

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Antibodies with differing affinities for the same antigen describe a polyclonal response, the opposite of the uniform, single-clone monoclonal pattern.

## answer_d
Produced by fusion of a normal plasma cell with a B cell producing antibodies against a single epitope

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The real hybridoma technique fuses a normal antibody-secreting plasma (or B) cell with an immortal myeloma (tumour) cell, not with another B cell producing antibody against a single epitope; this option misstates the fusion partner.

## answer_e
Induced in the lab by B lymphocyte activation by endotoxin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Endotoxin-driven B-lymphocyte activation in vitro is not how monoclonal antibodies are manufactured; the standard method is hybridoma fusion with an immortal myeloma cell line.

## title
The process by which IgG and/or C3b enhance phagocytosis is called:

## question
The process by which IgG and/or C3b enhance phagocytosis is called:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Opsonization

## explanation_a
Correct. Opsonization is the process in which IgG antibody and/or the complement fragment C3b coat (tag) a microbial antigen, so that phagocytes bearing Fc receptors (for IgG) or complement receptors (for C3b) recognise and engulf the coated particle far more efficiently. This antibody-and-complement-mediated tagging is exactly what 'enhancing phagocytosis' names, distinguishing it from mechanisms that act on the microbe's ability to bind host cells (neutralization) or that kill an antibody-coated host cell (ADCC).

## answer_b
Neutralization

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Neutralization is antibody blocking a microbe or toxin from binding host cells directly; it does not describe IgG/C3b-mediated enhancement of phagocytic uptake.

## answer_c
ADCC

## explanation_c
Incorrect. ADCC (antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity) is the antibody-directed killing of an already antibody-coated target cell by an effector cell such as an NK cell, not the tagging of free antigen for phagocytosis.

## answer_d
Agglutination

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Agglutination is antibody cross-linking particulate antigen into clumps; it does not describe enhanced phagocytic engulfment of a single tagged particle.

## answer_e
Sensitization

## explanation_e
Incorrect. 'Sensitization' as a distractor here refers loosely to antibody coating a target for a later effector step (as in ADCC or complement fixation), not specifically to the IgG/C3b-mediated phagocytosis-enhancement process named opsonization.

An important feature of thymus-independent antigens is:

## question
An important feature of Thymic independent antigens is:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
They are composed of a hapten conjugated to a carrier protein

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A hapten-carrier conjugate is the classic thymus-DEPENDENT antigen structure, requiring carrier-protein processing and T-cell help — the opposite of a thymus-independent antigen.

## answer_b
They have repeated epitopes that cross link membrane bound immunoglobulins on B cells

## explanation_b
Correct. Thymus-independent (TI) antigens, typically bacterial polysaccharides, characteristically carry many repeated copies of the same epitope; this repetitive structure lets a single TI antigen cross-link enough membrane-bound immunoglobulin (BCR) molecules on one B cell to deliver a strong enough signal to activate it without T-cell help. This is why a repetitive polysaccharide, but not a single-copy protein epitope, can activate a B cell without any T-cell help at all.

## answer_c
They elicit a memory response on re-exposure to them

## explanation_c
Incorrect. TI antigens do not generate memory B cells; eliciting a memory response on re-exposure is a thymus-dependent, T-cell-help-dependent feature.

## answer_d
They lead to secretion of all isotypes of antibodies

## explanation_d
Incorrect. TI responses are largely restricted to IgM, not secretion of all isotypes; isotype switching to multiple classes requires T-cell help (CD40L-CD40), a thymus-dependent feature.

## answer_e
Ubiquitous antigens in the body displayed on MHC molecules for T cells during their development

## explanation_e
Incorrect. This describes self antigens displayed on MHC during T-cell thymic education, an unrelated concept to the structural cross-linking feature of thymus-independent antigens.
