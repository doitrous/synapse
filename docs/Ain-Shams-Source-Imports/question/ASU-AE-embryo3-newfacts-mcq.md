## title
Which of these is NOT derived from ectoderm?

## question
Which of the following is NOT derived from ectoderm?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Spinal cord

## explanation_a
Incorrect as an answer to 'NOT derived from ectoderm' -- the spinal cord is part of the central nervous system, formed when the neural tube (itself folded from the ectodermal neural plate) closes, so it is very much ectodermal.

## answer_b
Epidermis and skin appendages

## explanation_b
Incorrect as an answer -- the epidermis and its appendages (hair, nails, glands) are surface ectoderm's own direct derivatives.

## answer_c
Neural crest cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect as an answer -- neural crest cells separate from the crests of the neural folds as the neural tube closes, and both the neural tube and the crest are ectodermal derivatives.

## answer_d
Sensory epithelium

## explanation_d
Incorrect as an answer -- the sensory epithelium of the ear, nose and eye (from the otic and lens/nasal placodes) is a further ectodermal derivative.

## answer_e
Adrenal cortex

## explanation_e
Correct. The adrenal (suprarenal) cortex is mesodermal, not ectodermal -- it arises from mesodermal cells of the posterior abdominal wall condensing beside the root of the dorsal mesentery, unlike the adrenal medulla (a genuinely ectodermal/neural-crest derivative that would fit the other four options here). Because the adrenal gland's two parts have different origins, 'adrenal cortex' is the one item on this ectoderm-derivative list that does not belong.

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-HEPARIN-NOT-TERATOGENIC

## title
Which drug is NOT teratogenic?

## question
Which of the following drugs is NOT teratogenic?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Anticonvulsants

## explanation_a
Incorrect as an answer -- several anticonvulsants (for example phenytoin and valproate) are recognised teratogens, associated with fetal anticonvulsant syndromes and neural tube defects, so this is a drug that IS teratogenic.

## answer_b
Tetracycline

## explanation_b
Incorrect as an answer -- tetracyclines cross the placenta and are teratogenic, classically causing discoloration and hypoplasia of fetal teeth and bone when given in later pregnancy, so this is a drug that IS teratogenic.

## answer_c
Heparin

## explanation_c
Correct. Heparin is a large, highly charged molecule that cannot cross the placenta, so it never reaches the embryo or fetus and cannot disrupt its development regardless of dose or timing. It is the standard teaching example of a drug whose safety in pregnancy is explained by lack of placental transfer rather than by any special property of the molecule itself. Warfarin, by contrast, is a small molecule that does cross the placenta and is a recognised teratogen, which is exactly why anticoagulation is switched to heparin during pregnancy.

## answer_d
Antithyroid drugs

## explanation_d
Incorrect as an answer -- antithyroid drugs (for example propylthiouracil and methimazole) cross the placenta and can affect fetal thyroid development, so this is a drug that IS teratogenic (or at least fetotoxic).

## answer_e
Valium

## explanation_e
Incorrect as an answer -- diazepam (Valium) and other benzodiazepines cross the placenta and have been linked to fetal malformation risk, so this is a drug that IS teratogenic.

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-POLYHYDRAMNIOS-GI

## title
Hydramnios and fetal esophageal atresia

## question
Hydramnios could indicate which abnormality in the fetus?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Esophageal atresia

## explanation_a
Correct. Fetal swallowing of amniotic fluid is one of the main routes by which fluid is normally removed from the amniotic cavity. Esophageal atresia leaves the esophagus blind-ending, so the fetus cannot swallow at all, and fluid accumulates faster than it is removed. This is the classic structural cause of hydramnios (polyhydramnios) that this concept's own definition names directly.

## answer_b
Agenesis of the kidneys

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Agenesis of the kidneys removes the main source of amniotic fluid (fetal urine), so it causes reduced fluid (oligohydramnios), the mirror-image finding to hydramnios, not hydramnios itself (this pairing is tested directly at embryo3-q026).

## answer_c
A large liver

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An enlarged fetal liver is not a recognised mechanism for altering amniotic fluid volume in the way swallowing or urine production are.

## answer_d
A large spleen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. An enlarged fetal spleen is likewise not a recognised cause of altered amniotic fluid volume through either the swallowing or urine-production route.

## answer_e
An anomaly in the heart

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A cardiac anomaly on its own does not interrupt either fetal swallowing or fetal urine production, the two routes that actually govern amniotic fluid volume, so it is not the mechanistic link this question is testing.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-TERATOGEN-FIRST-TRIMESTER

## title
When is teratogenic effect greatest?

## question
The teratogenic effect [of a harmful agent on the embryo/fetus] is greater during which period of pregnancy?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
On the day of conception

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Exposure on the day of conception, before the zygote has even implanted, tends to follow an all-or-none pattern -- either the conceptus is lost or it develops unaffected -- rather than producing the graded structural malformations that define the teratogenic period.

## answer_b
During the first 10-12 weeks (1st trimester)

## explanation_b
Correct. The embryonic period (roughly weeks 3-8, the bulk of the first trimester) is when the organ systems are actively being laid down, and each organ has its own narrow window of peak sensitivity within that stretch. A teratogen reaching the embryo during this period produces the most severe and most varied structural malformations, which is why this window carries the highest teratogenic risk of the whole pregnancy.

## answer_c
Between the 20th and 25th weeks

## explanation_c
Incorrect. By 20-25 weeks the fetal period is well underway and the major organs are already formed, so exposure at this stage mostly causes growth restriction or functional/physiological deficits rather than the structural malformations characteristic of first-trimester exposure.

## answer_d
Between the 25th and 30th weeks

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The 25th-30th week window is further still into the fetal period, past the stage of organ formation, so the risk of major structural malformation from a teratogen at this point is much lower than in the first trimester.

## answer_e
At the terminal stage of pregnancy

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The terminal stage of pregnancy is the least vulnerable window for structural malformation, since organogenesis is long complete; agents reaching the fetus this late are more likely to affect growth or specific organ function (for example the CNS, still maturing) than to cause the kind of malformation the teratogenic-period concept is about.
