QST-ASULOCO-COLLAGEN-COLLAGEN-Q01

## title
Vitamin cofactor for proline/lysine hydroxylation

## question
Which vitamin is required for hydroxylation of proline and lysine in collagen synthesis?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Vitamin D

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Vitamin D governs calcium/phosphate handling and bone mineralisation, not the hydroxylation chemistry of collagen's own polypeptide chain.

## answer_b
Vitamin K

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Vitamin K is a cofactor for gamma-carboxylation of glutamate residues in clotting factors and some bone matrix proteins, an entirely different post-translational reaction.

## answer_c
Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid)

## explanation_c
Correct. Prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl hydroxylase, the intracellular enzymes that hydroxylate proline and lysine residues on the procollagen alpha-chain in the rough endoplasmic reticulum, require vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid) as a cofactor to keep their iron centre in the reduced (Fe2+) state. Without vitamin C the hydroxylation reaction stalls, which is why scurvy presents with defective, poorly cross-linked collagen. Molecular oxygen and ferrous iron are also required, but vitamin C is the classic, exam-tested cofactor.

## answer_d
Vitamin A

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Vitamin A is relevant to epithelial differentiation and vision, not to collagen's hydroxylation step.

## title
Lysyl oxidase function

## question
Which of the following best describes the lysyl oxidase enzyme?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Required for allysine synthesis in collagen

## explanation_a
Incorrect alone. This is true of lysyl oxidase, but it is not the only correct statement -- the enzyme is also copper-dependent and its mechanism is specifically oxidative deamination, so 'all of the above' captures the full picture.

## answer_b
A copper-dependent enzyme

## explanation_b
Incorrect alone. Lysyl oxidase does require copper as a cofactor, but this statement alone omits the enzyme's actual catalytic action and product, so the complete answer is 'all of the above'.

## answer_c
Catalyses oxidative deamination of lysine side chains into allysine

## explanation_c
Incorrect alone. This correctly names the reaction lysyl oxidase catalyses, but it leaves out the cofactor requirement and the resulting allysine product description, both of which are also true.

## answer_d
All of the above

## explanation_d
Correct. Lysyl oxidase is a copper-dependent, extracellular enzyme that oxidatively deaminates the side chains of specific lysine and hydroxylysine residues on tropocollagen, converting them to reactive aldehydes (allysine). These aldehydes then spontaneously condense with neighbouring residues to form the covalent cross-links that give mature collagen fibrils their tensile strength, so every one of the three statements is correct.

## title
Scurvy -- which statement is false

## question
Which of the following is NOT true about scurvy?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is due to vitamin C deficiency in the diet for a few days

## explanation_a
Correct as the false statement. Scurvy does not develop after only a few days of dietary vitamin C deprivation. The body's vitamin C stores (in liver and other tissues) typically take several weeks to a few months of deficient intake to deplete before clinical scurvy appears, so a few days is far too short a window.

## answer_b
Hydroxylation of proline and lysine will be deficient

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Vitamin C is the required cofactor for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase, so its deficiency directly impairs hydroxylation of proline and lysine during collagen synthesis.

## answer_c
Collagen is weak due to decreased hydrogen bonds

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Under-hydroxylated collagen chains form fewer of the hydrogen bonds that stabilise the triple helix, so the resulting collagen is structurally weak.

## answer_d
The patient shows bleeding gums and delayed wound healing

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Bleeding gums, poor wound healing and easy bruising are the classic clinical presentation of scurvy, reflecting weak, poorly cross-linked collagen in gingival and connective tissue.

## title
Collagen structure -- which statement is false

## question
Which of the following is NOT true about collagen?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
The chain contains approximately 1000 amino acids

## explanation_a
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Each collagen alpha-chain is indeed roughly 1000 amino acid residues long.

## answer_b
Glycine is repeated every third position

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Glycine, the smallest amino acid, occupies every third position in the chain -- this is required so it can fit at the crowded centre of the triple helix.

## answer_c
It has a repeated sequence Gly-X-Y

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. this statement is true). Collagen's primary structure is a repeating Gly-X-Y motif, with proline and hydroxyproline frequently occupying the X and Y positions.

## answer_d
Proline disrupts collagen helix as an alpha helix

## explanation_d
Correct as the false statement. Collagen is not an alpha helix at all -- proline's rigid ring structure and the requirement for interchain (not intrachain) hydrogen bonding between the three supercoiled strands make collagen's triple helix a structurally distinct, further-wound helix, so describing proline as disrupting 'collagen helix as alpha helix' misstates collagen's actual structure rather than a true disruptive effect on a genuine alpha helix. This is exactly why the Gly-X-Y repeat and interchain hydrogen bonding, not an alpha-helical intrachain scheme, define collagen's stability.

## title
Order of collagen synthesis steps

## question
The following are steps of collagen synthesis: 1) Glycosylation of some hydroxylysine; 2) Hydroxylation of proline and lysine; 3) Cleavage of the signal sequence of preprocollagen in the rough endoplasmic reticulum; 4) Extracellular cleavage of procollagen molecules; 5) Assembly and secretion of procollagen to the extracellular space. Which is the correct sequence?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
1,2,3,4,5

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This places glycosylation before signal-peptide cleavage and hydroxylation, but the signal sequence must be cleaved first and hydroxylation must precede glycosylation of hydroxylysine (glycosylation modifies a residue hydroxylation has already created).

## answer_b
2,4,3,1,5

## explanation_b
Incorrect. This places extracellular cleavage (step 4) before secretion (step 5), which is impossible -- procollagen cannot be cleaved extracellularly before it has been secreted from the cell.

## answer_c
3,2,1,5,4

## explanation_c
Correct. Synthesis runs: cleave the preprocollagen signal sequence (3) -> hydroxylate proline and lysine (2) -> glycosylate hydroxylysine (1) -> assemble and secrete procollagen (5) -> cleave procollagen extracellularly (4). This is exactly the intracellular-then-extracellular order the pathway follows.

## answer_d
3,2,1,4,5

## explanation_d
Incorrect. This swaps the order of secretion (5) and extracellular cleavage (4) -- cleavage of procollagen happens extracellularly after secretion, not before it.

Leucine

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Leucine is not modified during collagen maturation.

## title
Intracellular event during collagen maturation

## question
Which of the following events occurs inside the cell during collagen maturation?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Removal of the N-terminal propeptide of procollagen

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Removal of the N-terminal propeptide happens extracellularly, after procollagen has already been secreted, by a procollagen N-peptidase.

## answer_b
Removal of the C-terminal propeptide of procollagen

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Removal of the C-terminal propeptide is likewise an extracellular event, occurring after secretion.

## answer_c
Conversion of lysine to allysine

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Conversion of lysine (and hydroxylysine) side chains to allysine is catalysed by lysyl oxidase, an extracellular enzyme, so this step happens outside the cell.

## answer_d
Cross-link formation of collagen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Cross-link formation between tropocollagen molecules follows lysyl oxidase's extracellular action and is itself extracellular.

## answer_e
Formation of disulfide bonds at the terminal pro-sequences

## explanation_e
Correct. Interchain disulfide bonds form at the C-terminal propeptide region of the three procollagen chains while they are still inside the rough endoplasmic reticulum; this disulfide-bond-driven chain alignment is what allows the three chains to correctly register and begin winding into the triple helix before secretion. This intracellular alignment step precedes and enables every later maturation event, including secretion and extracellular cross-linking.

## title
Collagen architecture in teeth

## question
What is the structural arrangement of collagen in teeth (dentine)?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Parallel fibres

## explanation_a
Correct. Like tendon, dentine's collagen is packed as parallel bundles of fibres, an arrangement that gives tensile strength along a defined axis -- the same mechanical logic that governs tendon architecture. This is the same packing logic that makes tendon collagen strong along its long axis.

## answer_b
Gel

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A gel arrangement is characteristic of the vitreous humor, where transparency and cushioning matter more than directional tensile strength.

## answer_c
Crystalline

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A near-crystalline, regularly spaced fibril arrangement is characteristic of the cornea, where it is needed for optical transparency, not of dentine.

## answer_d
Random meshwork

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A random meshwork is more characteristic of loose connective tissue or basement membrane (type IV collagen), not of the highly ordered collagen in dentine.
