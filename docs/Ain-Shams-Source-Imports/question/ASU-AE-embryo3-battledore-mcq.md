## title
Umbilical cord attached to the placental margin

## question
An umbilical cord attached to the margin of the placenta is called:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Velamentous insertion of cord

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Velamentous insertion is a different abnormal attachment, where the cord inserts into the amniotic membranes beyond the placental edge rather than into the placental disc itself, so the umbilical vessels run unprotected through the membranes before reaching the placenta -- the vessels, not the placental margin, are what is exposed, which is a different anomaly from a cord that does reach the disc but at its edge.

## answer_b
Placenta accreta

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Placenta accreta is an abnormally firm, invasive attachment of the placenta to the myometrium because the decidua basalis is deficient -- it describes how deeply the placenta has invaded the uterine wall, not where on the placental disc the umbilical cord inserts.

## answer_c
Battledore placenta

## explanation_c
Correct. Battledore placenta is the name given when the umbilical cord inserts at the margin (edge) of the placental disc rather than centrally or eccentrically within it -- the resemblance to the shape of a battledore (paddle) racquet, with the handle at the rim rather than the centre, is where the name comes from. It is usually of no clinical consequence on its own, unlike velamentous insertion, which exposes unprotected vessels.

## answer_d
Succenturiate placenta

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A succenturiate (accessory) placenta is a separate small lobe of placental tissue connected to the main disc by blood vessels running through the membranes -- it describes an extra lobe, not the position where the cord attaches to the main disc.

## answer_e
False cord knot

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A false knot is a local heaping-up of Wharton's jelly or a redundant loop of an umbilical vessel that looks like a knot in the cord itself -- it is a finding along the length of the cord, unrelated to where the cord inserts into the placenta.
