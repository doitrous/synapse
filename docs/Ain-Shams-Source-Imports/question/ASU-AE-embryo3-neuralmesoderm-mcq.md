QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-RETINA-NEURAL-TUBE

## title
Neural crest derivatives EXCEPT

## question
The neural crest cells give rise to the following EXCEPT:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
Melanocytes

## explanation_a
Incorrect as an answer -- melanocytes are a genuine neural crest derivative, migrating widely through the embryo to pigment the skin and other tissues.

## answer_b
Dorsal root ganglia

## explanation_b
Incorrect as an answer -- the dorsal root (sensory) ganglia form from neural crest cells that settle beside the developing spinal cord.

## answer_c
Autonomic ganglia

## explanation_c
Incorrect as an answer -- the sympathetic and parasympathetic (autonomic) ganglia are likewise neural crest derivatives.

## answer_d
Adrenal medulla

## explanation_d
Incorrect as an answer -- the adrenal medulla's chromaffin cells are neural crest derivatives too, the same lineage that gives sympathetic ganglion cells elsewhere.

## answer_e
Retina of the eye

## explanation_e
Correct. The retina is not a neural crest derivative -- it develops as an outgrowth of the neural tube itself (the optic vesicle, and then optic cup, evaginating from the forebrain), making it part of the central nervous system's own derivative set rather than the neural crest's. This is the key distinction the whole neural-tube-versus-neural-crest split is built to test: both lineages are ectodermal, but the neural tube gives the central nervous system (including the retina, as a CNS outgrowth) while the neural crest gives the peripheral ganglia, Schwann cells, melanocytes and the adrenal medulla.

## id
QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-MELANOCYTE-NEURAL-CREST

## title
Origin of skin pigment cells

## question
Pigment cells of the skin (melanocytes) are derived from the:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Notochord

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The notochord is a separate midline axial structure that induces the neural plate and later becomes the nucleus pulposus -- it does not give rise to migratory pigment cells.

## answer_b
Surface ectoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Surface ectoderm gives the epidermis and its appendages (hair, nails, glands) directly, but the pigment-producing melanocytes within the epidermis are not surface-ectoderm derivatives themselves -- they migrate in from elsewhere.

## answer_c
Neural crest

## explanation_c
Correct. Melanocytes are neural crest derivatives. Crest cells separate from the neural fold margins as the neural tube closes and then migrate widely through the embryo, and one major migratory stream ends in the epidermis and hair follicles, differentiating there into melanocytes. This is alongside the neural crest's other destinations -- sensory and autonomic ganglia, Schwann cells and the adrenal medulla -- all part of the same migratory cell population.

## answer_d
Lateral plate mesoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Lateral plate mesoderm gives the somatic and splanchnic layers (body-wall connective tissue, serous membranes, gut/vessel smooth muscle) -- it is not the source of the epidermis's pigment cells.

## answer_e
Dermomyotome

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The dermomyotome is the somite-derived tissue that gives the dermis (dermatome) and skeletal muscle (myotome) -- a mesodermal derivative, not the source of the ectodermal-lineage melanocyte.

## subject

## explanation_e

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-KIDNEYS-INTERMEDIATE-MESODERM

## title
Kidneys and the intermediate cell mass

## question
The kidneys are derived from which part of the intraembryonic mesoderm?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cranial somites

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cranial somites are paraxial mesoderm, from the streak's cranial part, and differentiate into sclerotome, myotome and dermatome (vertebrae, muscle, dermis) -- not the urogenital system.

## answer_b
Caudal somites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Caudal somites, likewise paraxial mesoderm, follow the same sclerotome/myotome/dermatome fate as cranial somites and are not the source of the kidneys.

## answer_c
Intermediate cell mass

## explanation_c
Correct. Intraembryonic mesoderm arising from the middle of the primitive streak forms the intermediate mesoderm, also called the intermediate cell mass. It lies between the paraxial mesoderm and the lateral plate mesoderm, running the length of the embryo. This intermediate mesoderm forms the entire urogenital system -- the kidneys, ureters and gonads.

## answer_d
Visceral layer of lateral plate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The splanchnopleuric (visceral) layer of lateral plate mesoderm, from the streak's caudal part, forms the connective tissue and smooth muscle of the gut and respiratory tract, cardiac muscle and the visceral serous layers -- not the kidneys.

## answer_e
Somatic layer of lateral plate

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The somatopleuric (somatic) layer of lateral plate mesoderm forms the body-wall connective tissue and the parietal serous layers -- again not the source of the kidneys.

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-VESSELS-MESODERM

## title
Origin of arteries, veins and lymphatics

## question
The arteries, veins and lymphatic channels develop from:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Villi

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Chorionic villi are placental structures built from trophoblast and mesoderm together, not the germ layer that gives rise to the vasculature throughout the embryo generally -- villi are a site containing vessels, not the source of vessels as a rule.

## answer_b
Ectoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ectoderm gives the nervous system and the epidermis, not the vascular system -- angioblasts (the precursors of blood vessels) are not an ectodermal derivative.

## answer_c
Mesoderm

## explanation_c
Correct. Blood vessels -- arteries, veins and lymphatic channels alike -- are mesodermal derivatives. They form from mesenchymal angioblasts that aggregate into blood islands and then canalise and link up into a vascular network. This process, angiogenesis and vasculogenesis, is common to both the embryo proper and the extraembryonic membranes.

## answer_d
Amnion

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The amnion is the ectoderm-and-mesoderm membrane that lines the amniotic cavity -- it is a covering structure, not the source of the body's vessels.

## answer_e
Endoderm

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Endoderm gives the epithelial lining of the gut and respiratory tract and the parenchyma of organs such as the liver and pancreas -- not the vascular system, though endoderm-derived organs are of course richly vascularised by mesoderm-derived vessels growing into them.

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-VISCERAL-PERICARDIUM-SPLANCHNIC

## title
Origin of the visceral pericardium

## question
Visceral pericardium originates from:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Splanchnic layer of lateral plate mesoderm

## explanation_a
Correct. Lateral plate mesoderm splits into a somatopleuric (somatic) layer and a splanchnopleuric (splanchnic) layer. The splanchnic layer covers the developing gut tube and, around the heart, forms the visceral serous layer -- the visceral (epicardial) layer of the pericardium -- alongside the visceral pleura and visceral peritoneum elsewhere in the body, the same layer forming all three visceral serous coverings from a single embryological source.

## answer_b
Somatic layer of lateral plate mesoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The somatic layer of lateral plate mesoderm lines the body wall and forms the parietal serous layers (parietal pericardium, pleura, peritoneum) instead -- the layer facing the wall, not the organ.

## answer_c
Paraxial mesoderm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Paraxial mesoderm segments into somites and forms the axial skeleton, skeletal muscle and dermis -- it plays no part in forming the serous coverings of the thoracic or abdominal organs.

## answer_d
Intermediate mesoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Intermediate mesoderm forms the urogenital system (kidneys, gonads) -- unrelated to the pericardium.

## answer_e
Splanchnic layer of extraembryonic mesoderm

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Extraembryonic mesoderm lies outside the embryo proper, contributing to the connecting stalk, chorion and amnion -- the visceral pericardium is an intraembryonic structure, formed from intraembryonic (not extraembryonic) splanchnic mesoderm.
