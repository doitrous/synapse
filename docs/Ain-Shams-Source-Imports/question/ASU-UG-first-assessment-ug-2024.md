## title
A substance cleared faster than inulin but slower than PAH is filtered and partially secreted

## question
A substance's renal clearance is measured as higher than inulin clearance but lower than PAH (para-aminohippurate) clearance. What is the renal handling of this substance?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
It is freely filtered and partially secreted by the renal tubules

## explanation_a
Correct. Inulin clearance equals GFR (freely filtered, neither reabsorbed nor secreted), while PAH clearance approximates renal plasma flow (freely filtered and virtually completely secreted). A clearance value between these two therefore indicates a substance that is freely filtered and also partially secreted by the tubules, adding to its filtered load without reaching the near-complete secretion seen with PAH.

## answer_b
It is freely filtered and completely reabsorbed, with no secretion

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Net reabsorption would give a clearance LOWER than inulin (GFR), not a value between inulin and PAH clearance.

## answer_c
It is freely filtered and neither reabsorbed nor secreted, exactly like inulin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. A clearance equal to inulin's would indicate no net reabsorption or secretion; a clearance higher than inulin's rules this out.

## answer_d
It is freely filtered and completely secreted, exactly like PAH

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Complete secretion, like PAH, would give a clearance approximately equal to PAH clearance itself, not a value below it.

## title
Basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase drives sodium reabsorption across the proximal convoluted tubule

## question
Sodium reabsorption across the proximal convoluted tubule epithelium is ultimately driven by which mechanism?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase pump, which maintains the low intracellular sodium gradient that drives apical sodium entry

## explanation_a
Correct. The basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase actively pumps sodium out of the proximal tubule cell into the interstitium (in exchange for potassium), keeping intracellular sodium low. This gradient is what drives sodium's passive entry across the apical membrane via numerous cotransporters and exchangers (with glucose, amino acids, phosphate, and via Na+/H+ exchange), making the basolateral pump the ultimate energetic driver of proximal sodium reabsorption.

## answer_b
Passive diffusion down a favorable electrochemical gradient alone, without any active transport step

## explanation_b
Incorrect. While apical entry into the cell is passive (facilitated by the gradient the pump creates), overall net reabsorption is an active transport process fundamentally dependent on the ATP-consuming basolateral pump, not passive diffusion alone.

## answer_c
The H+/K+-ATPase pump located in intercalated cells of the collecting duct

## explanation_c
Incorrect. H+/K+-ATPase in intercalated cells mediates potassium reabsorption and hydrogen secretion in the distal nephron/collecting duct, not proximal tubular sodium reabsorption.

## answer_d
Aldosterone-dependent epithelial sodium channels (ENaC), as in the principal cells of the collecting duct

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ENaC channels mediate aldosterone-regulated sodium reabsorption in the principal cells of the distal tubule and collecting duct, a distinct, more distal and hormonally regulated mechanism from the constitutive Na+/K+-ATPase-driven reabsorption of the proximal tubule.

## title
Angiotensin II stimulates proximal tubular sodium-hydrogen exchange, increasing sodium and bicarbonate reabsorption

## question
Which of the following is a true statement regarding the direct renal tubular actions of angiotensin II?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Angiotensin II stimulates proximal tubular Na+/H+ exchange, increasing sodium and bicarbonate reabsorption

## explanation_a
Correct. In addition to its vascular and aldosterone-mediated effects, angiotensin II directly stimulates the apical Na+/H+ exchanger (NHE3) in the proximal tubule. This increases sodium reabsorption (paired with hydrogen secretion) and, because the secreted hydrogen ion combines with filtered bicarbonate to regenerate CO2 that re-enters the cell and is converted back to reabsorbed bicarbonate, also increases net bicarbonate reabsorption — part of the overall volume- and pressure-conserving action of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system.

## answer_b
Angiotensin II inhibits proximal tubular sodium reabsorption, promoting natriuresis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Angiotensin II promotes, rather than inhibits, proximal sodium reabsorption; it is an antinatriuretic hormone that supports volume conservation, opposite to what this option describes.

## answer_c
Angiotensin II acts only on the renal vasculature (efferent arteriole) and has no direct tubular effect

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Beyond its vascular actions, angiotensin II has well-established direct tubular effects, especially at the proximal tubule (Na+/H+ exchange) in addition to its aldosterone-mediated distal effects; it is not confined to vascular action alone.

## answer_d
Angiotensin II decreases bicarbonate reabsorption, favoring the development of metabolic acidosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Angiotensin II increases, rather than decreases, bicarbonate reabsorption via enhanced proximal Na+/H+ exchange, and its overall hemodynamic/volume effects do not characteristically favor metabolic acidosis.

## question
The left kidney is related anteriorly to the stomach, being separated from it by which peritoneal space?

## subject

## status

## owner

## answer_a
The omental bursa (lesser sac)

## explanation_a
Correct. The posterior wall of the stomach and the anterior surface of the left kidney (upper pole) are both related through the omental bursa (lesser sac). This is the peritoneal space that lies posterior to the stomach and lesser omentum, also bordered by the pancreas and spleen. The stomach is therefore an indirect (bursa-separated) anterior relation of the left kidney rather than one in direct peritoneal contact.

The greater sac (general peritoneal cavity) directly

Incorrect. The greater sac is the main peritoneal cavity anterior to the lesser sac; the stomach's posterior surface, which relates to the left kidney, faces into the lesser sac (omental bursa), not directly into the greater sac.

## answer_c
The hepatorenal recess (Morison's pouch)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The hepatorenal recess (Morison's pouch) lies on the right side, between the liver and right kidney, and is unrelated to the left kidney's relation to the stomach.

## answer_d
The rectouterine pouch (pouch of Douglas)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The rectouterine pouch (pouch of Douglas) is a pelvic peritoneal recess between the uterus and rectum, unrelated to the upper abdominal relation between the left kidney and stomach.

## title
Gartner's duct cysts are vestigial remnants of the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct

## question
Gartner's duct cysts, found along the anterolateral wall of the vagina, are vestigial remnants of which embryonic structure?

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The mesonephric (Wolffian) duct

## explanation_a
Correct. In the female, the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct normally regresses, since it is not stabilized by testosterone the way it is in the male. Residual segments can nonetheless persist along the lateral wall of the uterus, cervix, or vagina. When the vaginal-level remnant persists and dilates, it forms a Gartner's duct cyst, a classic incidental finding on pelvic examination.

## answer_b
The paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct is the structure that persists and develops in the female (forming the uterine tubes, uterus, cervix and upper vagina); Gartner's duct is instead the vestige of the duct that regresses in females (the mesonephric duct), not the one that develops.

## answer_c
The urogenital sinus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The urogenital sinus contributes to the lower vagina and vestibule, but Gartner's duct cysts specifically represent a mesonephric duct remnant, not a urogenital sinus derivative.

## answer_d
The mesonephric tubules (as opposed to the duct itself)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. It is the mesonephric duct itself (the longitudinal collecting structure), not the individual mesonephric tubules, that forms the residual, persisting Gartner's duct along the vaginal wall.

## title
A duplicated (double) ureter draining a single kidney results from early splitting of the ureteric bud

## question
A duplicated (double) ureter draining a single kidney most commonly results from which embryological event?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Early (premature) splitting of the ureteric bud, before it reaches the metanephric blastema

## explanation_a
Correct. A complete duplex (double) ureter, each with its own separate ureteric orifice, results when the ureteric bud splits early, before it induces the metanephric blastema. Two entirely separate ureteric bud stalks then each induce their own collecting system within a single, fused kidney. This is termed complete ureteral duplication.

## answer_b
Persistence of two entirely separate metanephric blastemas within one kidney

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The metanephric blastema (metanephric mesenchyme) is a single mass of intermediate mesoderm that is induced by the ureteric bud; duplication anomalies arise from abnormal branching of the ureteric bud itself, not from two separate blastemas.

## answer_c
Failure of the mesonephric duct to degenerate after the ureteric bud arises

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Persistence of mesonephric duct remnants (e.g., Gartner's duct in females, appendix epididymis in males) is a separate embryological event unrelated to ureteric duplication, which concerns abnormal branching of the ureteric bud.

## answer_d
Late splitting of the ureteric bud, after it has already entered the metanephric blastema

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Splitting of the ureteric bud AFTER it has already entered and begun inducing the metanephric blastema produces a partial (incomplete/bifid) duplication — two ureters that join before reaching the bladder — rather than the complete duplication with two separate ureteric orifices that results from early splitting.

## title
Linear IgG/C3 immunofluorescence with crescent formation defines Goodpasture syndrome (anti-GBM disease)

## question
Renal biopsy in a patient with a pulmonary-renal syndrome shows linear immunofluorescent staining for IgG and C3 along the glomerular basement membrane, together with proliferation of parietal epithelial cells forming crescents. Which diagnosis is most consistent with these findings?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Goodpasture syndrome (anti-glomerular basement membrane disease)

## explanation_a
Correct. Goodpasture syndrome (anti-GBM disease) is caused by autoantibodies directed against an epitope on type IV collagen shared by the glomerular and alveolar basement membranes. On immunofluorescence this produces a smooth, continuous linear pattern of IgG (and often C3) deposition along the GBM — distinct from the granular ('lumpy-bumpy') pattern of immune-complex diseases — and the resulting severe glomerular injury classically triggers proliferation of parietal (Bowman's capsule) epithelial cells into crescents, producing rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis with concurrent pulmonary hemorrhage.

## answer_b
Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis is an immune-complex disease showing a granular ('lumpy-bumpy'), not linear, pattern of IgG/C3 deposition, reflecting discrete subepithelial immune complex deposits rather than antibody binding diffusely along the GBM.

## answer_c
IgA nephropathy (Berger disease)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. IgA nephropathy shows mesangial, not linear GBM, immunofluorescence positivity — IgA deposits are localized to the mesangium rather than diffusely along the capillary basement membrane.

## answer_d
Membranous nephropathy

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Membranous nephropathy shows a granular, subepithelial ('spike-and-dome') pattern of immune complex deposition, not a linear pattern, and does not characteristically produce crescents.

All of the following are characteristic features of diabetic glomerulosclerosis EXCEPT:

Hyalinization of both the afferent and efferent arterioles

Incorrect. Hyaline arteriolosclerosis affecting BOTH the afferent and efferent arterioles is a distinctive feature of diabetic nephropathy (unlike hypertensive nephrosclerosis, which characteristically spares the efferent arteriole), so it does not answer this 'except' question.

## question
A patient with severe hypercalcemia and concomitant heart failure requires urgent lowering of serum calcium. Which initial treatment approach is most appropriate?

## subject
renal

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Isotonic (normal) saline to restore intravascular volume, followed by a loop diuretic to promote calciuresis, with careful monitoring given the heart failure

## explanation_a
Correct. Severe hypercalcemia is usually accompanied by volume depletion (from calcium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and reduced oral intake), so initial management is isotonic saline to restore volume and increase renal calcium excretion by improving GFR and reducing proximal tubular sodium (and paired calcium) reabsorption. A loop diuretic (e.g., furosemide) is then added, both to further promote calciuresis by blocking calcium reabsorption in the thick ascending limb and to prevent fluid overload — a particularly important consideration in a patient who also has heart failure and cannot tolerate large-volume saline without careful monitoring.

## answer_b
Thiazide diuretics, which enhance distal tubular calcium reabsorption

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Thiazide diuretics increase distal tubular calcium reabsorption and would worsen, not improve, hypercalcemia; they are contraindicated in this setting (and are instead used therapeutically to reduce urinary calcium excretion in conditions like idiopathic hypercalciuria).

## answer_c
Aggressive free-water restriction alone, without intravenous fluids or diuretics

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hypercalcemic patients are typically volume-depleted, and free-water restriction without volume repletion would fail to correct the underlying deficit and would not promote the renal calcium excretion needed to lower serum calcium.

## answer_d
Immediate administration of high-dose calcium channel blockers

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Calcium channel blockers are not a standard treatment for hypercalcemia; the priority is volume repletion and calciuresis (saline plus loop diuretic), with agents such as bisphosphonates or calcitonin considered for more definitive/sustained lowering afterward.

## question
During prolonged fasting, the kidney (specifically the renal cortex) becomes an important site of energy/glucose production through which metabolic pathway?

## subject

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Gluconeogenesis, using substrates such as glutamine and lactate, contributing significantly to glucose production alongside the liver

## explanation_a
Correct. During prolonged fasting/starvation, the renal cortex becomes a significant gluconeogenic organ, using substrates such as glutamine (also linked to renal ammoniagenesis for acid-base regulation), lactate, glycerol and amino acids to synthesize glucose. As starvation progresses, the kidney can contribute a substantial fraction (up to roughly 40–50% in prolonged fasting) of total endogenous glucose production, complementing hepatic gluconeogenesis.

## answer_b
Anaerobic glycolysis exclusively, producing lactate as its end product for export

## explanation_b
Incorrect. While the renal medulla is characteristically dependent on anaerobic glycolysis (due to its low oxygen tension), the renal CORTEX — the site relevant to fasting energy production discussed here — is instead a major site of gluconeogenesis, not glycolysis, and is a net glucose producer rather than consumer during fasting.

## answer_c
Ketogenesis, exporting ketone bodies for use by peripheral tissues

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Ketogenesis (synthesis of ketone bodies from fatty acid-derived acetyl-CoA) occurs in the liver, not the kidney; the kidney can utilize ketone bodies as fuel but is not a site of their synthesis.

## answer_d
Glycogenolysis, drawing on substantial renal glycogen reserves

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The kidney has minimal glycogen stores compared with the liver and skeletal muscle, and glycogenolysis is not a meaningful contributor to renal glucose output during fasting — the kidney's contribution instead comes from de novo gluconeogenesis.

Incorrect. The vast majority of body carnitine is intracellular, concentrated within muscle tissue, with only a small fraction circulating in plasma at any time.

## subject

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Streptococcus pyogenes (group A beta-hemolytic streptococcus), specifically nephritogenic strains

## explanation_a
Correct. Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis is a non-suppurative immune-complex complication of infection (classically pharyngitis or impetigo) with specific 'nephritogenic' strains of Streptococcus pyogenes (group A beta-hemolytic streptococcus). Incorrect. ## answer_c
Streptococcus pneumoniae

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Streptococcus pneumoniae is not the classic causative organism of post-infectious glomerulonephritis; that role belongs specifically to group A Streptococcus pyogenes.

## answer_d
Escherichia coli

## explanation_d
Incorrect. E. coli is the leading cause of urinary tract infection, not of the non-suppurative, immune-complex-mediated glomerulonephritis being described here.
