# Item

## id
QST-ASUCNS3P22022S-DEXAMETHASONE-MENINGITIS

## title
When should dexamethasone be given in bacterial meningitis to reduce neurological complications?

## question
In a patient being treated for bacterial meningitis, at what point should adjunctive dexamethasone be given to reduce the risk of neurological complications such as hearing loss?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
With or just before the first dose of antibiotics

## explanation_a
Correct. Adjunctive dexamethasone, given intravenously with or just before the first dose of antibiotic therapy, blunts the inflammatory response generated as bacterial cell walls lyse under antibiotic action, reducing the risk of neurological complications such as sensorineural hearing loss. This benefit depends critically on early, first-dose timing rather than on the antibiotic choice itself.

## answer_b
Only after 48 hours of antibiotic therapy, once the diagnosis is confirmed by culture

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Dexamethasone started after antibiotics have already been running for some time no longer meaningfully blunts the early lytic inflammatory surge, so delaying it to 48 hours loses most of its benefit.

## answer_c
Only if the patient develops seizures during treatment

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Dexamethasone's role here is prophylactic, given at the start of treatment to prevent complications, not reserved for a specific complication such as seizures once it has already occurred.

## answer_d
It should never be combined with antibiotic therapy for meningitis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adjunctive dexamethasone is a well-established part of bacterial meningitis management when timed correctly, not an agent to avoid combining with antibiotics.

## title
Which antipsychotic is preferred for treating psychosis in a patient with Parkinson disease?

## question
A patient with Parkinson disease develops visual hallucinations attributed to their dopaminergic therapy. Which antipsychotic is preferred to treat this psychosis with the lowest risk of worsening their parkinsonian motor symptoms?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Quetiapine

## explanation_a
Correct. Most antipsychotics are potent D2 receptor antagonists that would worsen this patient's parkinsonism. Quetiapine, an atypical antipsychotic with comparatively weak and rapidly-dissociating D2 receptor blockade, is preferred for psychosis in Parkinson disease instead. It controls psychotic symptoms with a substantially lower risk of exacerbating extrapyramidal motor symptoms than typical antipsychotics or most other atypical agents.

## answer_b
Haloperidol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Haloperidol is a potent typical antipsychotic with strong D2 receptor blockade, which would markedly worsen this patient's parkinsonism.

## answer_c
Risperidone

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Risperidone, though atypical, still has relatively strong D2 blockade at clinical doses and carries a meaningfully higher risk of worsening parkinsonism than quetiapine.

## answer_d
Chlorpromazine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Chlorpromazine is a typical antipsychotic with strong D2 blockade and prominent extrapyramidal side effects, unsuitable for a Parkinson disease patient.

QST-ASUCNS3P22022S-HABITUATION

## title
Which term describes the progressive decrease in response to a repeated, non-threatening stimulus?

## question
A loud but harmless noise repeated many times produces a progressively smaller startle response each time it is presented. Which form of learning does this illustrate?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Habituation

## explanation_a
Correct. Habituation is a simple, non-associative form of learning. The strength of a behavioural response to a repeated, unchanging stimulus progressively decreases as the nervous system learns that the stimulus is neither novel nor threatening and can safely be ignored. This is exactly what is described here, as the startle response wanes with repetition.

## answer_b
Classical conditioning

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Classical conditioning requires pairing a neutral stimulus with an unconditioned stimulus to create a new learned association, not the simple decline in response to one repeated stimulus described here.

## answer_c
Operant conditioning

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Operant conditioning involves learning an association between a behaviour and its consequence (reinforcement or punishment), not a passive decline in response to a repeated stimulus.

## answer_d
Sensitisation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sensitisation is the opposite phenomenon — an enhancement, not a decrease, of a response, typically after exposure to a strong or noxious stimulus.

QST-ASUCNS3P22022S-POSTERIOR-COMMUNICATING-ARTERY

## title
Which artery connects the internal carotid artery with the posterior cerebral artery?

## question
Which vessel of the circle of Willis links the internal carotid artery directly with the posterior cerebral artery, connecting the anterior (carotid) and posterior (vertebrobasilar) cerebral circulations?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The posterior communicating artery

## explanation_a
Correct. The posterior communicating artery is a short anastomotic vessel that runs backward from the internal carotid artery to join the posterior cerebral artery on each side. It forms part of the posterior half of the circle of Willis, linking the anterior (carotid) and posterior (vertebrobasilar) cerebral circulations. This anastomosis allows collateral flow between the two systems if one is compromised.

## answer_b
The anterior communicating artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The anterior communicating artery connects the two anterior cerebral arteries across the midline, not the carotid and vertebrobasilar systems.

## answer_c
The anterior choroidal artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The anterior choroidal artery is a branch of the internal carotid artery supplying deep structures such as the choroid plexus and internal capsule; it does not connect to the posterior cerebral artery directly.

## answer_d
The basilar artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The basilar artery is formed by the union of the two vertebral arteries and supplies the posterior circulation itself; it is not the anastomosis linking it to the carotid system.

## title
Which drug is used for the acute (abortive) treatment of a moderate-to-severe migraine attack?

## question
A patient with a moderate-to-severe migraine attack already in progress needs acute treatment to abort the headache. Which drug is appropriate for this purpose?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Sumatriptan

## explanation_a
Correct. Sumatriptan is a selective serotonin 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist, a 'triptan'. It is used for the acute (abortive) treatment of moderate-to-severe migraine attacks once a headache has already started. It works by causing cranial vasoconstriction and inhibiting the release of vasoactive neuropeptides from trigeminal nerve terminals.

## answer_b
Propranolol

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Propranolol is a prophylactic migraine agent, taken regularly to reduce the frequency of future attacks, not to treat one already under way.

## answer_c
Amitriptyline

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Amitriptyline is likewise used for migraine prophylaxis, not for aborting a current attack.

## answer_d
Topiramate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Topiramate is a prophylactic antiepileptic used to prevent migraine attacks, not an acute abortive treatment.

## title
Which drug is first-line disease-modifying therapy for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis?

## question
A patient newly diagnosed with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis is starting disease-modifying therapy for the first time. Which of the following is a first-line agent for this purpose?

## subject

## status

## source

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Interferon beta-1

## explanation_a
Correct. Interferon beta-1 is given by injection. It is a first-line disease-modifying therapy for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis, reducing the frequency of relapses and slowing the accumulation of new demyelinating lesions. This benefit is achieved through its immunomodulatory effects on the underlying autoimmune process.

## answer_b
Natalizumab

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Natalizumab, a monoclonal antibody, is reserved for patients whose disease remains active despite first-line therapy, or for more aggressive disease, reflecting its greater efficacy but also its greater risk, including progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy.

## answer_c
High-dose intravenous methylprednisolone

## explanation_c
Incorrect. High-dose intravenous methylprednisolone treats acute relapses of multiple sclerosis; it is not a disease-modifying therapy used to prevent future relapses.

## answer_d
Riluzole

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Riluzole is used in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, an unrelated motor neuron disease, not multiple sclerosis.
