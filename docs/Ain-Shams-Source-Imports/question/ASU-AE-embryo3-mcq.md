# Item

## id
QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-PRIMITIVE-STREAK-LAYER

## title
Layer the primitive streak develops in

## question
The primitive streak develops in which layer of embryonic disc?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Epiblast

## explanation_a
Correct. The primitive streak appears at the beginning of the third week as a linear thickening within the epiblast, formed by the proliferation and migration of epiblast cells toward the disc's midline. It is the epiblast, not the hypoblast, that goes on to give rise to all three definitive germ layers, since epiblast cells ingress through the streak to displace the hypoblast (forming endoderm) and to form the intraembryonic mesoderm, leaving the remaining epiblast as the ectoderm. Because every later germ layer traces back to this single epiblast population, the primitive streak's location in the epiblast is the starting point for the whole of gastrulation.

## answer_b
Hypoblast

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The hypoblast is displaced by ingressing epiblast cells rather than contributing to the primitive streak itself; it does not persist as endoderm, a common point of confusion.

## answer_c
Mesoderm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Mesoderm is a product that the primitive streak generates once epiblast cells migrate through it, not the layer the streak itself develops within.

## answer_d
Endoderm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Endoderm, like mesoderm, is a downstream product of gastrulation through the primitive streak, not its site of origin.

## answer_e
Notochord

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The notochord forms from cells passing through the primitive node at the streak's cranial end, a related but distinct structure from the streak itself.

## id
QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-SOMITE-DERIVATIVE-DERMIS

## title
Structure derived from somites

## question
Which of the following is derived from Somites:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Dermis

## explanation_a
Correct. Each somite splits into a ventromedial sclerotome, which forms the vertebrae, and a dorsolateral dermomyotome, which further divides into a dermatome and a myotome. The dermatome portion gives rise to the dermis of the skin over most of the body, so dermis is a genuine somite derivative alongside the axial skeleton and skeletal muscle. This dermatome/myotome/sclerotome split is the standard framework these somite-derivative questions are built around.

## answer_b
Ganglia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Sensory, sympathetic and parasympathetic ganglia are neural crest derivatives, not somite derivatives; the neural crest arises separately from the neural folds rather than from the paraxial mesoderm.

## answer_c
Pleura

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The pleura is a lateral plate mesoderm derivative (from the somatic and splanchnic layers lining the coelom), not a somite derivative.

## answer_d
Lungs

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The lungs are an endodermal outgrowth of the foregut for their epithelium, with mesenchyme from the surrounding splanchnic mesoderm; neither component traces to the somites.

## answer_e
Kidneys

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The kidneys develop from the intermediate mesoderm, a separate intraembryonic mesoderm column from the paraxial mesoderm that forms somites.

QST-OBS-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-BATTLEDORE-PLACENTA

## title
Name for cord attached to placental margin

## question
An umbilical cord attached to the margin of Placenta is called:

## subject
obs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Velamentous insertion of cord

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Velamentous insertion is when the cord inserts into the fetal membranes beyond the placental edge, leaving the vessels to run unprotected through the membranes before reaching the placenta itself, a distinct and generally riskier abnormality from a simple marginal attachment.

## answer_b
Placenta accreta

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Placenta accreta refers to abnormal placental invasion into the myometrium, an attachment abnormality of the placenta to the uterine wall, not a cord-insertion abnormality.

## answer_c
Battledore placenta

## explanation_c
Correct. When the umbilical cord inserts at the margin (edge) of the placental disc, rather than centrally or paracentrally, this is called battledore placenta. The name comes from its resemblance to a battledore (paddle) racket, with the handle (cord) attached at the rim. It is generally a benign variant, though it carries a modestly increased risk of fetal growth restriction and cord compression.

## answer_d
Succenturiata placenta

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A succenturiate placenta has one or more accessory lobes separate from the main placental mass, connected by vessels running through the membranes, an abnormality of placental shape rather than of cord insertion site.

## answer_e
False cord knot

## explanation_e
Incorrect. A false knot is a local redundancy or varicosity of umbilical vessels or Wharton's jelly that only looks like a knot, unrelated to where the cord inserts into the placenta.

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-INTRAEMBRYONIC-MESODERM-SOURCE

## title
Major source of intraembryonic mesoderm

## question
The major source of intraembryonic mesoderm is:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Primitive streak

## explanation_a
Correct. Epiblast cells ingress through the primitive streak and spread laterally and cranially between the epiblast and hypoblast to form the bulk of the intraembryonic mesoderm; the primitive node and notochord contribute further mesoderm, but the streak is the major, and first, source. This mesoderm then splits into paraxial, intermediate and lateral plate columns, and is genuinely absent only where the oropharyngeal and cloacal membranes keep ectoderm and endoderm fused directly.

## answer_b
Ectoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Ectoderm is what remains of the epiblast after cells have migrated inward through the streak; it is not itself a source of mesoderm.

## answer_c
Endoderm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Endoderm is formed by epiblast cells displacing the hypoblast, a separate ingression event from the one that generates mesoderm.

## answer_d
Prechordal plate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The prechordal plate is a small area of tightly adherent bilaminar (ectoderm-endoderm) cells cranial to the primitive node that marks the future oropharyngeal membrane; it is one of the mesoderm-free zones, not a mesoderm source.

## answer_e
Neurenteric canal

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The neurenteric canal is a transient passage between the amniotic cavity and yolk sac formed through the primitive pit; it is not a source of mesoderm.

# Item

## id
QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-KIDNEY-INTERMEDIATE-MESODERM-1

## title
Intraembryonic mesoderm part forming the kidneys

## question
The kidneys are derived from which part of the intraembryonic mesoderm?

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Cranial somites

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Cranial somites, like all somites, arise from paraxial mesoderm and form the axial skeleton, dermis and skeletal muscle, not the kidneys.

## answer_b
Caudal somites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Caudal somites share the same paraxial-mesoderm origin and fate as cranial somites; neither region of the somite column forms the urinary system.

## answer_c
Intermediate cell mass

## explanation_c
Correct. The intermediate mesoderm, also called the intermediate cell mass or nephrogenic cord, is the intraembryonic mesoderm column between the paraxial and lateral plate columns, and it gives rise to the urogenital system, including the kidneys and gonads. This three-way subdivision of intraembryonic mesoderm, paraxial, intermediate and lateral plate, is the framework every 'which mesoderm forms X' question in this cluster is testing.

## answer_d
Visceral layer of lateral plate

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The visceral (splanchnic) layer of lateral plate mesoderm contributes to the wall of the gut tube and to the heart, not to the kidneys.
