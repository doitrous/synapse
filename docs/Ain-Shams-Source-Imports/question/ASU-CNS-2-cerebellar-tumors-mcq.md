## title
A highly cellular cerebellar tumour with Homer Wright rosettes, WHO Grade IV and radiosensitive — what is it?

## question
A child presents with a cerebellar tumour. Histological examination reveals a highly cellular tumour with anaplasia, mitoses and Homer Wright rosettes. The tumour is classified as WHO Grade IV and is radiosensitive. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hemangioblastoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hemangioblastoma is a benign (WHO Grade I), slow-growing, highly vascular tumour composed of vacuolated stromal cells and thin-walled vessels — it does not show anaplasia, brisk mitoses or Homer Wright rosettes, and is not WHO Grade IV.

## answer_b
Medulloblastoma

## explanation_b
Correct. This combination — a highly cellular small round blue cell tumour with anaplasia, mitotic activity and Homer Wright rosettes, graded WHO Grade IV yet radiosensitive — is the classic description of medulloblastoma, the most common malignant cerebellar tumour of childhood. It typically arises in the cerebellar vermis and can seed the cerebrospinal fluid, so staging includes imaging of the whole neuraxis.

## answer_c
Pilocytic astrocytoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pilocytic astrocytoma is a benign (WHO Grade I) cerebellar tumour of childhood with Rosenthal fibres and biphasic architecture, not the anaplastic, rosette-forming, WHO Grade IV picture described here.

## answer_d
Ependymoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Ependymoma arises from ependymal cells lining the ventricles and shows perivascular pseudorosettes (and true ependymal rosettes), a different histological pattern from the Homer Wright rosettes of medulloblastoma.

## title
A slow-growing, benign, highly vascular cerebellar tumour with vacuolated cells and thin-walled vessels — what is it?

## question
A patient presents with a slow-growing, benign, highly vascular cerebellar tumour. Histologically, it is composed of large vacuolated cells and thin-walled vessels. This tumour is most likely a:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Medulloblastoma

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Medulloblastoma is a malignant, WHO Grade IV, highly cellular small round blue cell tumour — the opposite of the benign, slow-growing, vacuolated-cell picture described here.

## answer_b
Hemangioblastoma

## explanation_b
Correct. Large, lipid-vacuolated ("foamy") stromal cells amid a dense network of thin-walled capillary-sized vessels is the classic histology of hemangioblastoma, a benign (WHO Grade I), highly vascular cerebellar tumour often associated with von Hippel-Lindau disease. The stromal cells are considered the true neoplastic element, with the rich vasculature a secondary proliferation.

## answer_c
Metastatic clear cell carcinoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Metastatic clear cell (renal cell) carcinoma can mimic hemangioblastoma's vacuolated cells, but it is a malignant metastatic lesion, not the benign, slow-growing tumour described, and typically shows more nuclear atypia and a history of a primary renal tumour.

## answer_d
Pilocytic astrocytoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Pilocytic astrocytoma is benign and can be cystic and vascular, but its histology is defined by biphasic architecture and Rosenthal fibres, not large vacuolated stromal cells with thin-walled vessels.

## title
Which genetic abnormality is most commonly associated with medulloblastoma?

## question
What is the genetic abnormality most commonly associated with the WHO integrated diagnosis of medulloblastoma?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Loss of chromosome 17p and duplication of 17q (isochromosome 17q)

## explanation_a
Correct. Isochromosome 17q — loss of 17p (which carries the TP53 locus) with duplication of 17q — is the single most common cytogenetic abnormality found in medulloblastoma, distinct from the newer molecular-subtype markers (WNT/SHH/Group 3/Group 4) used alongside it in the integrated diagnosis. It is a karyotypic finding that can be identified across multiple molecular subtypes rather than defining one subtype specifically.

## answer_b
EGFR amplification

## explanation_b
Incorrect. EGFR amplification is a hallmark of glioblastoma, a supratentorial adult glioma, not medulloblastoma.

## answer_c
1p/19q co-deletion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 1p/19q co-deletion is the defining molecular marker of oligodendroglioma, an adult supratentorial glioma, not medulloblastoma.

## answer_d
IDH1 mutation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. IDH1 mutation characterises diffuse (lower-grade) adult gliomas and secondary glioblastoma, not the embryonal tumour medulloblastoma.

## title
Nuclear beta-catenin positivity in medulloblastoma is most characteristic of which molecular subtype?

## question
A patient is diagnosed with a cerebellar medulloblastoma. Immunohistochemistry reveals nuclear beta-catenin positivity. This finding is most characteristic of which molecular subtype?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
WNT-activated

## explanation_a
Correct. Nuclear (rather than membranous or cytoplasmic) beta-catenin immunopositivity reflects constitutive activation of the Wnt signalling pathway and is the immunohistochemical hallmark of the WNT-activated molecular subtype of medulloblastoma, which also carries the most favourable prognosis of the four subtypes. This subtype is also enriched for germline or somatic CTNNB1 and APC pathway alterations.

SHH-activated

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The SHH-activated subtype is instead marked by GAB1 and/or YAP1 immunopositivity, reflecting activation of the Sonic Hedgehog pathway, not nuclear beta-catenin.

## answer_c
Group 3

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Group 3 medulloblastoma, often associated with MYC amplification and a poor prognosis, is not defined by nuclear beta-catenin positivity.

## answer_d
Group 4

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Group 4 medulloblastoma, the most common subtype overall, is not defined by nuclear beta-catenin positivity either; it is currently identified largely by exclusion of the other three subtypes plus supporting molecular markers.

The VHL tumour suppressor gene is inactivated in which cerebellar tumour?

## question
The VHL tumour suppressor gene is inactivated in which of the following cerebellar tumours?

## subject

## status

Both histopathologic and molecular/genetic features

## explanation_c
Correct. This dual reporting also guides risk stratification and, increasingly, treatment intensity.

## answer_d
The patient's age and tumour location

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Patient age and tumour location are clinically relevant but are not the defining components of the WHO integrated pathological diagnosis, which is based on histopathology and molecular/genetic features.

## title
What is the clinical significance of medulloblastoma's radiosensitivity?

## question
Medulloblastoma is a highly malignant but radiosensitive tumour. What is the significance of its radiosensitivity?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
It makes the tumour resistant to all forms of therapy.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Radiosensitivity means the opposite of therapy resistance — the tumour's cells are efficiently killed by ionising radiation.

## answer_b
It makes the tumour responsive to radiation therapy, which is a key part of treatment.

## explanation_b
Correct. Because medulloblastoma is markedly radiosensitive despite being a highly malignant, WHO Grade IV tumour, craniospinal radiotherapy (covering both the primary site and the neuroaxis, given its propensity for CSF-borne dissemination) is a cornerstone of adjuvant treatment after maximal safe surgical resection. Chemotherapy is also added in appropriate risk groups, particularly in young children in whom radiotherapy is minimised or delayed.

## answer_c
It means the tumour will spontaneously regress.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Radiosensitivity describes response to radiotherapy, not a tendency toward spontaneous regression without treatment.

## answer_d
It means the tumour is only treatable with surgery.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Surgery alone is not curative for medulloblastoma; radiosensitivity is precisely why adjuvant radiotherapy (and chemotherapy in appropriate risk groups) is added after surgical resection.
