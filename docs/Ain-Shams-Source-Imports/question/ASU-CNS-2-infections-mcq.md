## title
Which organism most commonly causes a spinal epidural abscess in an IV drug user?

## question
A 25-year-old intravenous drug user presents with fever and back pain. MRI reveals an epidural abscess. Which of the following is the most common causative organism for this type of infection?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Streptococcus pneumoniae

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Streptococcus pneumoniae is a leading cause of community-acquired bacterial meningitis, not the typical organism in an IV-drug-user epidural abscess.

## answer_b
Neisseria meningitidis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Neisseria meningitidis causes meningococcal meningitis and septicaemia, not spinal epidural abscess from skin-flora bacteraemia.

## answer_c
Staphylococcus aureus

## explanation_c
Correct. Staphylococcus aureus, seeded from skin flora during non-sterile injection, is the most common cause of spinal epidural abscess in intravenous drug users, presenting with fever, localised back pain and progressive neurological deficit confirmed on MRI. Empirical antibiotic cover for suspected epidural abscess in this population should therefore include anti-staphylococcal activity (e.g. vancomycin) pending cultures.

## answer_d
Mycobacterium tuberculosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mycobacterium tuberculosis can cause a spinal (Pott's) abscess, but this is a chronic, indolent process, not the acute presentation typical of IV-drug-use-associated epidural abscess.

## title
A brain abscess following a tooth infection represents which route of CNS entry?

## question
A patient presents with a brain abscess following a tooth infection. This represents which primary route of entry for organisms into the central nervous system?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Hematogenous

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Haematogenous spread is bloodstream seeding from a distant infected site, not direct contiguous spread from an adjacent dental focus.

## answer_b
Direct Implantation

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Direct implantation refers to organisms introduced by trauma or a surgical/iatrogenic breach, not contiguous spread from an adjacent infected structure.

## answer_c
Local Extension

## explanation_c
Correct. A dental infection tracking directly into adjacent brain tissue, without an intervening bloodstream step, is local extension — the same route by which sinusitis, otitis media and mastoiditis cause brain abscess. Through peripheral nerves

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Peripheral nerve spread describes retrograde axonal transport, as with rabies virus, not contiguous spread from a nearby infected structure.

## title
Which CSF profile is most consistent with acute bacterial meningitis?

## question
A patient presents with acute bacterial meningitis. Which of the following cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) findings is most consistent with this diagnosis?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Normal glucose, elevated lymphocytes, clear appearance

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Normal glucose with a lymphocytic pleocytosis and clear CSF describes viral (aseptic) meningitis, not bacterial meningitis.

## answer_b
Decreased glucose, elevated neutrophils, turbid appearance

## explanation_b
Correct. Decreased glucose (from bacterial and neutrophil consumption), a neutrophil-predominant pleocytosis and turbid/purulent CSF is the classic profile of acute pyogenic bacterial meningitis. A Gram stain and culture of this turbid CSF typically identify the causative organism and guide targeted antibiotic therapy.

Normal glucose, elevated neutrophils, normal protein

Incorrect. Normal glucose with normal protein is inconsistent with acute bacterial meningitis, which characteristically depletes glucose and raises protein.

## answer_d
Decreased glucose, elevated lymphocytes, cobweb formation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Decreased glucose with lymphocytic predominance and cobweb clot formation is the classic profile of tuberculous meningitis, not acute pyogenic bacterial meningitis.

Herpes Simplex Virus type 2 (HSV-2)

## title
In tuberculous meningitis, the subarachnoid exudate is most prominently located where?

## question
In tuberculous meningitis, the subarachnoid space is often filled with a gelatinous or fibrinous exudate. Where is this exudate most prominently located?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Over the cerebral convexities

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Exudate over the cerebral convexities, sparing the base, is more characteristic of acute pyogenic bacterial meningitis than tuberculous meningitis.

## answer_b
At the base of the brain

## explanation_b
Correct. Tuberculous meningitis characteristically produces a thick exudate concentrated at the base of the brain, encasing the basal cisterns, circle of Willis and cranial nerves and predisposing to basal vasculitis and hydrocephalus. This basal predilection explains why cranial nerve palsies, obliterative vasculitis and communicating hydrocephalus are classic complications of tuberculous meningitis.

## answer_c
Within the lateral ventricles

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The exudate of tuberculous meningitis is a subarachnoid, basal process, not primarily an intraventricular one, although secondary hydrocephalus can follow.

## answer_d
Surrounding the spinal cord

## explanation_d
Incorrect. While tuberculous meningitis can extend along the spinal meninges, its most prominent and characteristic location is the base of the brain, not the spinal cord.

## title
A brain abscess spreading from a distant infected site is classified as which route of infection?

## question
A patient presents with a brain abscess. The causative organism is suspected to have spread from a distant site of infection. This mode of infection is classified as:

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Direct Implantation

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Direct implantation describes organisms introduced by trauma or surgery, not bloodstream spread from a distant infected site.

## answer_b
Local Extension

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Local extension describes spread from a contiguous adjacent structure (e.g. sinus, ear, tooth), not from a distant site via the bloodstream.

## answer_c
Hematogenous
