## title
The pudendal (Alcock's) canal in the ischioanal fossa carries the inferior rectal vessels and nerve

## question
Which of the following is a content of the pudendal (Alcock's) canal, formed within the lateral wall of the ischioanal (ischiorectal) fossa?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The inferior rectal artery (and nerve)

## explanation_a
Correct. The pudendal (Alcock's) canal is a fascial tunnel in the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa, carrying the internal pudendal vessels and pudendal nerve toward the perineum. Within its course the inferior rectal (anal) artery and nerve branch off and cross the ischioanal fossa to supply the anal canal and external anal sphincter. This is why the inferior rectal vessels/nerve are classically listed as contents encountered in relation to this canal.

## answer_b
The obturator nerve

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The obturator nerve runs in the pelvis and through the obturator canal in the obturator membrane, not through the pudendal canal in the ischioanal fossa.

## answer_c
The sciatic nerve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The sciatic nerve leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen and runs posterior to the ischioanal fossa in the gluteal region; it is not a content of the pudendal canal.

## answer_d
The superior rectal artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The superior rectal artery is the continuation of the inferior mesenteric artery supplying the upper rectum from above; it does not run through the pudendal canal, which lies below the pelvic floor in the perineum.

## title
The unregressed cranial end of the male paramesonephric duct forms the appendix of the testis

## question
In the male fetus, Sertoli-cell MIH causes regression of most of the paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct, but its unregressed cranial (upper) end persists as which vestigial structure?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Appendix of the testis

## explanation_a
Correct. Müllerian-inhibiting hormone (MIH/AMH) from fetal Sertoli cells causes regression of almost the entire paramesonephric duct in the male. Its cranial-most tip, however, is spared. That unregressed tip persists as a small vestigial appendage attached to the upper pole of the testis, the appendix of the testis (hydatid of Morgagni).

## answer_b
Appendix of the epididymis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The appendix of the epididymis is a mesonephric (Wolffian) duct remnant, not a paramesonephric duct remnant — it arises from an unincorporated cranial mesonephric tubule/duct segment near the head of the epididymis.

## answer_c
Prostatic utricle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The prostatic utricle is the remnant of the fused caudal ends of the paired paramesonephric ducts (the male homologue of the uterus/upper vagina), opening on the seminal colliculus — a caudal, not cranial, paramesonephric remnant.

## answer_d
Paradidymis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The paradidymis is a cluster of vestigial mesonephric tubules near the spermatic cord, not a paramesonephric duct derivative.

## title
Breast cancer in the upper outer quadrant classically spreads first to the anterior (pectoral) axillary nodes

## question
A female patient has a breast carcinoma located in the left upper outer quadrant. Which group of lymph nodes is most likely to be involved first?

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Anterior (pectoral) axillary lymph nodes

## explanation_a
Correct. Roughly three-quarters of breast lymph drains along vessels that follow the lateral thoracic vessels to the anterior (pectoral) group of axillary nodes, which lie along the lower border of pectoralis minor. The upper outer quadrant — the quadrant with the greatest breast tissue volume — drains predominantly through this route, making the pectoral axillary nodes the first-echelon (sentinel) nodes most often involved.

## answer_b
Parasternal (internal thoracic/mammary) lymph nodes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The parasternal (internal thoracic) nodes chiefly drain the medial breast quadrants, not the upper outer quadrant, following lymphatics that accompany the internal thoracic vessels through the intercostal spaces.

## answer_c
Supraclavicular lymph nodes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Supraclavicular nodes are a later echelon, typically involved only after the axillary nodes, once disease has progressed beyond the first-tier nodal groups — they are not the first nodes involved from an upper outer quadrant tumor.

## answer_d
Posterior (subscapular) axillary lymph nodes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The posterior (subscapular) axillary group drains lymph chiefly from the back and posterior shoulder region via the subscapular vessels, not primarily from the breast.

## title
Primitive sex cords become histologically recognizable in the gonadal ridge by about the 7th week

## question
By approximately which week of gestation do primitive (primary) sex cords become histologically recognizable within the developing gonadal (genital) ridge?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
7th week

## explanation_a
Correct. Primordial germ cells arrive in the genital ridges by about the 5th–6th week. The surrounding coelomic epithelium then proliferates to form primary (primitive) sex cords that become histologically identifiable within the indifferent gonad by about the 7th week. From that point, testis- or ovary-determining genes drive further sex-specific differentiation of these cords.

## answer_b
4th week

## explanation_b
Incorrect. At the 4th week the genital (gonadal) ridge itself is only just beginning to form as a thickening of the intermediate mesoderm/coelomic epithelium; it is too early for organized sex cords to have formed.

## answer_c
5th week

## explanation_c
Incorrect. By the 5th week, primordial germ cells are still migrating into the genital ridge; the ridge is still an undifferentiated (indifferent) structure without organized, histologically distinct sex cords.

## answer_d
10th week

## explanation_d
Incorrect. By the 10th week, the gonad has already committed to a testis or ovary phenotype with well-developed structures (seminiferous cords with Sertoli cells, or primary follicles); this is well beyond the initial appearance of primitive sex cords.

## title
Degarelix, a GnRH receptor antagonist, causes an immediate fall in gonadotropin secretion without an initial flare

## question
Regarding degarelix, which of the following statements is true?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
As a GnRH receptor antagonist, it immediately and directly reduces gonadotropin (LH/FSH) secretion, without an initial testosterone flare

## explanation_a
Correct. Degarelix is a GnRH receptor antagonist: it competitively blocks pituitary GnRH receptors immediately, without the initial receptor stimulation that agonists produce, so gonadotropin (LH and FSH) secretion — and downstream testicular testosterone production — falls rapidly from the first dose, with no testosterone flare. This makes it useful in advanced prostate cancer when an agonist-induced flare (which can worsen bone pain or cause spinal cord compression) must be avoided.

## answer_b
It produces an initial testosterone flare before suppression, as GnRH agonists such as leuprolide do

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An initial testosterone flare is characteristic of GnRH agonists (e.g., leuprolide, goserelin), which transiently overstimulate the pituitary before receptor downregulation suppresses gonadotropins; degarelix, as an antagonist, produces no such flare.

## answer_c
It stimulates gonadotropin secretion by mimicking the pulsatile action of natural GnRH

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Degarelix blocks, rather than mimics, GnRH receptor activation; it does not stimulate gonadotropin secretion at any point in its action.

## answer_d
It has no effect on gonadotropin secretion and instead acts as a direct androgen receptor antagonist

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Degarelix acts centrally at the pituitary GnRH receptor to suppress gonadotropin secretion; it is not a peripheral androgen receptor antagonist (that mechanism instead describes drugs such as flutamide or bicalutamide).

## title
Stereocilia lining the ductus (vas) deferens increase luminal surface area and help move sperm along the duct

## question
What is the function of the stereocilia lining the epithelium of the ductus (vas) deferens?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
They are long, non-motile microvilli that increase the epithelial surface area, aiding fluid absorption and the onward movement of spermatozoa along the duct

## explanation_a
Correct. Despite their name, stereocilia are not true motile cilia but long, branching, non-motile microvilli. By greatly increasing the apical surface area of the pseudostratified columnar epithelium lining the epididymis and ductus deferens, they support reabsorption of the bulk of the luminal testicular fluid and, by concentrating and directing the residual luminal flow, are classically credited with assisting the passage of spermatozoa along the duct toward ejaculation.

## answer_b
They beat rhythmically to actively propel sperm forward, similar to the motile cilia of the respiratory tract

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Stereocilia lack the axonemal (9+2 microtubule) structure and motor proteins required for active beating; true motile cilia with that structure are instead found in tissues such as the respiratory epithelium and the uterine tube.

## answer_c
They are the principal secretory source of seminal plasma proteins

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Bulk secretion of seminal plasma proteins is chiefly a function of the seminal vesicles and prostate gland, not the stereocilia of the ductus deferens.

## answer_d
They actively phagocytose and remove defective or degenerating spermatozoa from the lumen

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phagocytosis of defective sperm and residual bodies is a recognized epididymal epithelial function but is carried out by the principal cells generally, not specifically attributed to the stereocilia themselves.

## title
hCG from the syncytiotrophoblast maintains the corpus luteum's progesterone secretion in early pregnancy

## question
Which of the following statements about the corpus luteum of pregnancy is true?

## subject
obs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Human chorionic gonadotropin, secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast, maintains the corpus luteum's progesterone secretion until the placenta takes over steroidogenesis

## explanation_a
Correct. Once implantation occurs, the syncytiotrophoblast begins secreting human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), an LH-like hormone that binds LH/hCG receptors on the corpus luteum, rescuing it from the involution ('luteolysis') it would otherwise undergo and maintaining its progesterone (and estrogen) output. This corpus luteum of pregnancy sustains the endometrium until around 8–10 weeks, by which time the placenta itself has taken over adequate steroidogenesis (the 'luteal-placental shift').

## answer_b
The syncytiotrophoblast itself directly synthesizes progesterone from cholesterol, entirely independent of the corpus luteum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. In early pregnancy the placenta cannot independently complete progesterone synthesis without maternal cholesterol substrate and, before the luteal-placental shift, relies on the corpus luteum (which the syncytiotrophoblast's hCG maintains) rather than synthesizing progesterone entirely on its own from the outset.

## answer_c
The corpus luteum degenerates immediately after fertilization regardless of hCG levels

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Without hCG rescue, the corpus luteum would indeed regress around the time of the expected menses, but hCG from the syncytiotrophoblast actively prevents this regression in a successful pregnancy, maintaining it as the corpus luteum of pregnancy.

## answer_d
Progesterone secreted by the corpus luteum suppresses hCG secretion through negative feedback on the syncytiotrophoblast

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The relationship described is reversed: hCG from the syncytiotrophoblast supports the corpus luteum's progesterone secretion, rather than progesterone suppressing hCG output.

## title
Exclusive breastfeeding suppresses GnRH pulsatility, producing lactational amenorrhea for up to about 6 months

## question
Regarding lactation in an exclusively breastfeeding mother, which of the following statements is true?

## subject
gyn

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Frequent, exclusive breastfeeding elevates prolactin, which suppresses pulsatile hypothalamic GnRH secretion, producing amenorrhea for up to about 6 months postpartum

## explanation_a
Correct. Frequent suckling stimulates sustained prolactin release, and high prolactin levels suppress hypothalamic GnRH pulsatility (partly via increased hypothalamic dopaminergic/opioid tone), which in turn lowers pituitary LH and FSH secretion and blocks ovulation. With frequent, exclusive breastfeeding (including night feeds, with no significant supplementation), this can reliably suppress fertility and menstruation for up to about 6 months postpartum — the basis of the lactational amenorrhea method of contraception.

## answer_b
Normal ovulatory menstrual cycles resume within about 2 weeks of delivery regardless of breastfeeding frequency

## explanation_b
Incorrect. With frequent exclusive breastfeeding, ovulation and menses are typically suppressed for months, not resumed within 2 weeks; return of fertility is much faster in mothers who are not breastfeeding or who breastfeed infrequently/with supplementation.

## answer_c
Prolactin has no meaningful effect on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis during lactation

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Prolactin has a well-established suppressive effect on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis during lactation, which is precisely the mechanism underlying lactational amenorrhea.

## answer_d
Breastfeeding stimulates, rather than suppresses, pulsatile GnRH secretion, accelerating the return of ovulation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The direction of the effect is reversed: prolactin released during frequent breastfeeding suppresses, rather than stimulates, pulsatile GnRH secretion.

Oral contraceptive pill use is a recognized predisposing factor for symptomatic Trichomonas vaginalis infection

## question
Which of the following is a recognized predisposing factor that increases the risk of symptomatic Trichomonas vaginalis vaginitis?

## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Use of oral contraceptive pills, which alter vaginal pH and glycogen content favorably for the organism

## explanation_a
Correct. Trichomonas vaginalis thrives best in an environment with an elevated vaginal pH and an altered glycogen/estrogen milieu. Recognized predisposing factors include use of oral contraceptive pills, pregnancy, and disruption of the normal lactobacillus-dominant flora, for example from antibiotic use. All of these favor the organism's growth and symptomatic infection.

## answer_b
Postmenopausal, atrophic vaginal mucosa specifically

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Trichomoniasis is most common in sexually active reproductive-age women, not specifically linked to postmenopausal atrophic mucosa as its classic predisposing state.

## answer_c
Strict vaginal hygiene practices, including regular douching

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Vaginal douching disrupts protective lactobacillus flora and is a recognized risk factor for various vaginal infections, but it is not the answer being tested here as the paired predisposing factor for T. vaginalis in this item; more importantly, it works by disrupting protective flora rather than the mechanism attributed to OCPs (pH/glycogen alteration).

## answer_d
A low vaginal pH maintained by a healthy, lactobacillus-dominant flora

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A healthy lactobacillus-dominant flora maintaining a low vaginal pH is protective against T. vaginalis and other vaginal pathogens, not a predisposing factor for infection.
