## title
A dural-based mass shows whorled spindle-cell histology. Which tumour is this?

## question
A dural-based intracranial mass, firmly attached to the dura, shows whorled patterns of spindle cells on histology. Which tumour is this?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Meningioma

## explanation_a
Correct. Meningioma is a tumour of arachnoid cap cells that arises attached to the dura mater, classically showing whorled (concentric) patterns of spindle-shaped cells on histology. This combination of firm dural attachment and whorled architecture is its classic gross/microscopic recognition pattern. A subset of meningiomas additionally show calcification of whorl centres into psammoma bodies, a separate secondary feature.

## answer_b
Glioblastoma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Glioblastoma is an intra-axial (within brain parenchyma) tumour showing necrosis and microvascular proliferation, not a dural-based mass with whorled spindle-cell architecture.

## answer_c
Medulloblastoma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Medulloblastoma is a posterior fossa tumour of small, primitive round (embryonal) cells in children, not a dural-based whorled spindle-cell tumour.

## answer_d
Schwannoma

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Schwannoma arises from Schwann cells of a peripheral or cranial nerve (e.g. vestibular schwannoma), showing Antoni A/B patterns, not the dural whorled pattern of meningioma.

## title
What behavioural response follows electrical stimulation of the amygdala?

## question
In experimental animals, electrical stimulation of the amygdala characteristically produces which behavioural response?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Fear and defensive/avoidance behaviour

## explanation_a
Correct. The amygdala is central to processing and generating fear. Its electrical stimulation in experimental animals produces a stereotyped fear/defensive-avoidance response — freezing, autonomic arousal, avoidance behaviour. Bilateral amygdala lesions instead produce the opposite effect, placidity and blunted fear, as in Klüver-Bucy syndrome.

## answer_b
Hyperphagia (excessive eating)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Hyperphagia is classically associated with lesions of the ventromedial hypothalamus (satiety centre), not amygdala stimulation.

## answer_c
Somnolence (sleepiness)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Somnolence is not the characteristic response to amygdala stimulation; the reticular activating system, not the amygdala, is central to arousal/sleep regulation.

## answer_d
Analgesia (reduced pain sensitivity)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Analgesia is characteristic of periaqueductal grey stimulation (descending pain-inhibitory pathway), not amygdala stimulation, whose hallmark effect is fear/defensive behaviour.

## title
What is the recommended empiric antibiotic regimen for suspected bacterial meningitis in an older adult?

## question
A 67-year-old presents with fever, neck stiffness and altered consciousness; bacterial meningitis is suspected. Which empiric antibiotic combination is most appropriate while awaiting CSF culture results?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Vancomycin plus ceftriaxone plus ampicillin

## explanation_a
Correct. Empiric therapy combines vancomycin (covering resistant/penicillin-non-susceptible pneumococcus, including MRSA), ceftriaxone (covering typical meningitis pathogens including N. meningitidis and susceptible pneumococcus), and ampicillin, added specifically for Listeria monocytogenes coverage, a concern in patients over about 50 years old, since Listeria is intrinsically resistant to cephalosporins.

## answer_b
Amoxicillin alone

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Amoxicillin alone would cover Listeria but leaves resistant pneumococcus and most gram-negative meningitis pathogens inadequately treated; it is not sufficient empiric monotherapy.

## answer_c
Metronidazole plus gentamicin

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Metronidazole plus gentamicin targets anaerobes and some gram-negatives but does not reliably cover the typical bacterial meningitis pathogens (pneumococcus, meningococcus, Listeria) and is not standard empiric meningitis therapy.

## answer_d
Doxycycline plus azithromycin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Doxycycline plus azithromycin targets atypical organisms and is not standard empiric coverage for acute bacterial meningitis.
