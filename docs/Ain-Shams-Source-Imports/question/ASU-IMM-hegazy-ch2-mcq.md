Bacteria are killed by:

## question
Bacteria are killed by:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Phagocytosis & complement

## explanation_a
Correct. Bacteria are cleared chiefly by phagocytosis, meaning engulfment and intracellular killing by neutrophils and macrophages. Complement contributes alongside it, either opsonising bacteria to make them easier to phagocytose or lysing them directly via the membrane attack complex. Together, these are the two central innate effector mechanisms against bacterial infection.

## answer_b
Histamine released by mast cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Histamine released by mast cells drives vascular permeability and the acute allergic/inflammatory response; it is not itself a bactericidal mechanism.

## answer_c
T cells

## explanation_c
Incorrect. T cells are central to cell-mediated immunity against viruses, intracellular pathogens and tumours, not the principal mechanism killing extracellular bacteria.

## answer_d
C reactive protein

## explanation_d
Incorrect. C-reactive protein is an acute-phase opsonin that helps flag pathogens for phagocytosis; it assists but does not itself kill bacteria the way phagocytosis and complement do.

## question
Phagocytosis:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Can be stimulated by Ag binding to complement or Antibody

## explanation_a
Correct. Phagocytosis is enhanced, or opsonised, when the target antigen is bound by complement (C3b) or antibody. Both of those opsonins are recognised by dedicated receptors on the phagocyte surface, and that recognition is what stimulates uptake. This opsonisation step is a central trigger for efficient phagocytosis, far more efficient than uptake of an unopsonised particle.

## answer_b
Is an Ag specific process

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Phagocytosis is not an antigen-specific process; phagocytes recognise broad, shared patterns (PAMPs) and opsonins, not a single specific epitope the way a B- or T-cell receptor does.

## answer_c
Must be preceded by Ag processing

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Antigen processing (breaking a protein into peptides for MHC loading) happens as a consequence of phagocytic uptake in an antigen-presenting cell, not as a precondition before phagocytosis can occur.

## answer_d
Clear the body of virus infected cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phagocytosis clears extracellular microbes and debris; clearing already virally infected cells is chiefly the job of cytotoxic T cells and NK cells, not phagocytosis.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Adhesion molecules mediate the neutrophil's rolling and firm adhesion to endothelium en route to the infection site; they are not how the neutrophil recognises its microbial target once there.

## answer_b
Cytokine receptors

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Cytokine receptors let the neutrophil respond to chemotactic and activating signals, but are not the receptors that directly recognise the microbial target.

This is the same general PRR-PAMP recognition mechanism innate cells use throughout the body, in contrast to a clonal, antigen-specific receptor.

Non of the above

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Since TLRs genuinely are the receptor macrophages use to bind LPS, 'none of the above' is wrong.

Target recognition

## explanation_e
Incorrect. While MBL does recognise mannose-rich microbial surfaces, its functional contribution tested here is enhancing phagocytosis through that recognition, not 'target recognition' as an isolated function.

D

## answer_a
They utilize already activated antigen presenting cells

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Innate mechanisms do not require antigen-presenting cells to already be activated; they are the first line of defence acting before any such activation.

## answer_b
They have memory cells

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Memory cells are a hallmark of adaptive, not innate, immunity — innate immunity does not form memory.

## answer_c
They are highly specific for the invading pathogens that avoid PAMP receptor recognition

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Innate immunity is broadly, not highly, specific; it recognises shared PAMPs via pattern-recognition receptors, which is the opposite of avoiding PAMP-receptor recognition.

## answer_d
They provide immediate, continuous protection in the absence of a specific immune response

## explanation_d
Correct. Innate host defence mechanisms are critical because they provide immediate, continuous protection. That protection covers the time before a specific adaptive immune response has had the days it typically needs to develop. Without it, the body would otherwise be left defenceless during the earliest, most vulnerable phase of an infection.

## answer_e
They can always eradicate infection even in the absence of adaptive immune response

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Innate immunity alone cannot always eradicate infection; many pathogens require the specificity and power of the adaptive response for full clearance, which is exactly why the two systems work together.
