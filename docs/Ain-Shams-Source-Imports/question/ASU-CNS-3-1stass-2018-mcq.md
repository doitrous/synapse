## title
Which structure is NOT part of the basal ganglia?

## question
Which of the following deep grey-matter structures is NOT one of the basal ganglia?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Tuber cinereum

## explanation_a
Correct. The tuber cinereum is part of the hypothalamus, the median-eminence region of the floor of the third ventricle, from which the infundibulum (pituitary stalk) descends. It is functionally and anatomically distinct from the basal ganglia, despite its similarly deep location. Naming it among the basal ganglia is a classic distractor that exploits this superficial similarity in position.

## answer_b
Caudate nucleus

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The caudate nucleus, together with the putamen, forms the neostriatum, one of the basal ganglia.

## answer_c
Putamen

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The putamen is part of the neostriatum and is one of the basal ganglia.

## answer_d
Globus pallidus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The globus pallidus (paleostriatum) is one of the basal ganglia.

QST-ASUCNS31STASS2018-BRAIN-TUMOUR-HEADACHE

## title
Through which mechanism does a brain tumour classically cause headache?

## question
A patient with a newly diagnosed brain tumour reports headache that is worse on waking and aggravated by coughing or straining. Through which mechanism does the tumour classically produce this headache?

## subject
neuro

## status
Draft

## source
past-paper

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Raised intracranial pressure and traction on pain-sensitive intracranial structures

## explanation_a
Correct. A brain tumour classically causes headache through intracranial mechanisms — mass effect raising intracranial pressure, obstruction of cerebrospinal fluid flow (hydrocephalus), and traction or distortion of pain-sensitive intracranial structures such as the dura mater, large blood vessels and venous sinuses. This is why the headache is classically worse in the morning or on waking, and aggravated by coughing, straining or bending, reflecting further transient rises in intracranial pressure.

## answer_b
Direct pain sensation from the brain parenchyma itself

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Brain parenchyma itself is insensitive to pain; the headache arises from traction on the surrounding pain-sensitive dura and vessels, not from the tumour tissue itself.

## answer_c
Referred pain from pain-sensitive extracranial structures such as the scalp and sinuses

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Referred pain from extracranial structures is the mechanism of extracranial headache (e.g. from otitis media or sinus disease), the mechanistic counterpart to, not the cause of, this intracranial tumour's headache.

## answer_d
Contraction of the scalp muscles

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Scalp muscle contraction is implicated in tension-type headache, an extracranial mechanism unrelated to the intracranial mass effect of a brain tumour.
