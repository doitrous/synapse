# Item

QST-ASUENDO2-ANAT-A01

## title
Nerve accompanying the superior thyroid artery

## question
Which nerve travels closely applied to the superior thyroid artery on its course to the upper pole of the thyroid gland, and is therefore at risk during high ligation of the superior pedicle in thyroidectomy?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
External laryngeal nerve

## explanation_a
Correct. The external (motor) branch of the superior laryngeal nerve descends closely applied to the superior thyroid artery as it runs to the upper pole of the thyroid gland, before turning medially to supply the cricothyroid muscle. Because the nerve and artery run together over this last stretch, surgeons ligate the superior pedicle flush against the gland capsule, rather than high and away from it, specifically to avoid injuring this nerve. Injury to the external laryngeal nerve causes voice fatigue and loss of the upper vocal range, not the hoarseness typical of recurrent laryngeal nerve injury.

## answer_b
Recurrent laryngeal nerve

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The recurrent laryngeal nerve runs with the inferior thyroid artery and in the tracheoesophageal groove, not with the superior thyroid artery; it is at risk near the lower pole and posterior aspect of the gland, and its injury causes vocal cord paralysis and hoarseness.

## answer_c
Internal laryngeal nerve

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The internal laryngeal nerve is the sensory branch of the superior laryngeal nerve; it pierces the thyrohyoid membrane together with the superior laryngeal artery, above the level of the superior thyroid artery's course to the gland.

## answer_d
Vagus nerve

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The vagus nerve runs within the carotid sheath, posterior to the common/internal carotid artery and internal jugular vein, and does not directly accompany the superior thyroid artery to the gland.

## title
Anterior boundary of the pituitary fossa

## question
The pituitary (hypophyseal) fossa on the body of the sphenoid bone is bounded anteriorly by which structure?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Tuberculum sellae

## explanation_a
Correct. The tuberculum sellae is the low bony ridge on the body of the sphenoid immediately in front of the hypophyseal fossa, forming its anterior boundary and separating it from the optic groove/chiasmatic sulcus that lies further anteriorly. Radiologists and pituitary surgeons use this landmark to describe how far a pituitary tumour extends anteriorly.

Dorsum sellae

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The dorsum sellae is the bony plate that forms the posterior boundary of the pituitary fossa, not the anterior one.

## answer_c
Optic chiasma

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The optic chiasma lies superior to the tuberculum sellae/diaphragma sellae, above and anterior to the pituitary gland, not as its immediate anterior bony wall.

## answer_d
Cavernous sinus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cavernous sinuses lie lateral to the pituitary fossa on each side, not anteriorly.

## title
Embryological origin of the infundibular process

## question
The infundibular process, part of the neurohypophysis (posterior pituitary), develops from which embryonic structure?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
A downgrowth of the floor of the diencephalon

## explanation_a
Correct. The infundibular process and the rest of the neurohypophysis develop from a neuroectodermal downgrowth of the floor of the diencephalon (the future hypothalamus). This downgrowth meets the separate upgrowth of Rathke's pouch from the oral cavity, and the two primordia fuse to form the definitive pituitary gland while keeping distinct cell populations, which is why the neurohypophysis remains neural tissue continuous with the hypothalamus rather than true glandular epithelium.

## answer_b
Rathke's pouch

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Rathke's pouch gives rise to the adenohypophysis (anterior pituitary lobe), not the infundibular process/neurohypophysis.

## answer_c
Oral (surface) ectoderm directly

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Oral (surface) ectoderm gives rise to Rathke's pouch and, through it, the adenohypophysis; the infundibular process instead arises from neuroectoderm of the diencephalon floor.

## answer_d
The first pharyngeal pouch

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The first pharyngeal pouch gives rise to middle ear and eustachian tube structures, unrelated to pituitary gland embryology.

## title
Germ layer origin of the pituitary gland

## question
The pituitary gland as a whole (both anterior and posterior lobes) develops from which embryonic germ layer?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Ectoderm

## explanation_a
Correct. Both lobes of the pituitary gland are ectodermal in origin, though from two different ectodermal sources: the adenohypophysis (anterior lobe) arises from oral (surface) ectoderm via Rathke's pouch, while the neurohypophysis (posterior lobe) arises from neuroectoderm via a downgrowth of the diencephalon floor. Because no part of the gland is mesodermal or endodermal, the whole-organ classification given in an exam is simply "ectoderm", even though the two lobes are separate primordia that only later fuse.

## answer_b
Mesoderm

## explanation_b
Incorrect. No part of the pituitary gland is mesodermal; the connective-tissue capsule around the gland is mesodermal, but the glandular and neural tissue itself is not.

## answer_c
Endoderm

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The pituitary gland has no endodermal contribution; endoderm gives rise to the gut tube and its derivative glands (thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, liver, pancreas), not the pituitary.

## answer_d
Neural crest

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Neural crest contributes to structures such as the adrenal medulla and cranial ganglia, not the pituitary gland, whose posterior lobe instead arises from a direct downgrowth of neural-tube-adjacent diencephalon floor.

## title
Vertical level of the thyroid isthmus

## question
The isthmus of the thyroid gland lies anterior to the trachea at the level of which tracheal rings?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Second to fourth tracheal rings

## explanation_a
Correct. The thyroid isthmus, connecting the lower parts of the two lateral lobes, overlies the second to fourth tracheal rings, just below the cricoid cartilage. This level is the surgical landmark for elective tracheostomy, which is classically performed through or just below these rings after retracting or dividing the isthmus.

## answer_b
First tracheal ring only

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The first tracheal ring lies above the isthmus, closer to the cricoid cartilage, not at the isthmus's own level.

## answer_c
Fifth and sixth tracheal rings

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The fifth and sixth tracheal rings lie well below the isthmus, at a level closer to the thoracic inlet.

## answer_d
The cricoid cartilage

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The cricoid cartilage lies above the level of the thyroid isthmus, not at the same level; the isthmus sits on the trachea just below it.

## title
Derivative of the anterior wall of Rathke's pouch

## question
In the adult, the anterior wall of Rathke's pouch thickens and gives rise to which structure?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Pars distalis

## explanation_a
Correct. Rathke's pouch gives rise to the entire adenohypophysis, and its anterior wall specifically proliferates and thickens to form the pars distalis, the largest and functionally dominant hormone-secreting part of the anterior pituitary lobe. Pituitary (adenohypophyseal) adenomas most often arise here, in the pars distalis.

## answer_b
Pars intermedia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The pars intermedia develops from the thin posterior wall of Rathke's pouch, not the thickened anterior wall.

## answer_c
Pars nervosa

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The pars nervosa is part of the neurohypophysis, derived from the diencephalon-floor downgrowth, not from Rathke's pouch at all.

## answer_d
Pars tuberalis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The pars tuberalis develops from a further outgrowth of Rathke's pouch tissue around the infundibular stalk, not from the anterior wall proliferation that forms the pars distalis.

The inferior border of the thyroid isthmus is related to which of the following?

Recurrent laryngeal nerves

Incorrect. The recurrent laryngeal nerves run posterior to the lateral lobes in the tracheoesophageal groove, not at the inferior border of the isthmus.

## title
Embryological origin of the superior parathyroid gland

## question
The superior parathyroid glands develop from which pharyngeal pouch?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Fourth pharyngeal pouch

## explanation_a
Correct. The superior parathyroid glands develop from the dorsal part of the fourth pharyngeal pouch, alongside the ultimobranchial body, and descend only a short and fairly constant distance with the developing thyroid gland. This short, consistent descent is why the superior parathyroid glands are found more reliably at their expected position (near the mid-posterior border of the thyroid lobe) during surgery than the inferior glands.

## answer_b
Third pharyngeal pouch

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The third pharyngeal pouch gives rise to the inferior parathyroid glands (and the thymus), which undergo a longer and more variable descent than the superior glands.

## answer_c
Second pharyngeal pouch

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The second pharyngeal pouch contributes to the palatine tonsil, not the parathyroid glands.

## answer_d
Sixth pharyngeal arch

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The sixth pharyngeal arch contributes to the intrinsic laryngeal cartilages and musculature, not the parathyroid glands, which arise from pharyngeal pouches rather than arches.
