## title
Which of the following is NOT a content of the deep perineal pouch in the male?

## question
Which of the following is NOT a content of the deep perineal pouch in the male?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Bulb of the penis

## explanation_a
Correct. The bulb of the penis lies in the superficial perineal pouch, attached to the inferior surface of the perineal membrane and covered by bulbospongiosus; it is not a content of the deep perineal pouch, which lies between the two fascial layers of the urogenital diaphragm above the perineal membrane. This is a common source of confusion because the bulb sits immediately below the deep pouch, on the other side of the perineal membrane.

## answer_b
Membranous urethra

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The membranous urethra pierces the deep perineal pouch as it passes from the prostate to the bulb of the penis, surrounded by the external urethral sphincter.

## answer_c
Bulbourethral (Cowper) glands

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The paired bulbourethral (Cowper) glands lie within the substance of the deep perineal pouch, embedded in the external urethral sphincter, with ducts that cross the perineal membrane to open into the bulbous urethra.

## answer_d
External urethral sphincter (sphincter urethrae)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The external urethral sphincter (sphincter urethrae) is a genuine skeletal-muscle content of the deep perineal pouch, encircling the membranous urethra.

Incorrect. The lateral surfaces of the prostate are related to levator ani, not to the urethra course.

Which lobe of the prostate lies posterior to the upper (proximal) part of the prostatic urethra, between the urethra and the ejaculatory ducts?

## question
Which lobe of the prostate lies posterior to the upper (proximal) part of the prostatic urethra, between the urethra and the ejaculatory ducts?

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Median (middle) lobe

## explanation_a
Correct. The median (middle) lobe is a wedge of glandular tissue that lies between the urethra anteriorly and the two ejaculatory ducts posteriorly, immediately below the bladder neck; its enlargement in benign prostatic hyperplasia can project into the bladder as a "ball valve" and obstruct the internal urethral orifice. This wedge is the site of the classic bladder-neck "ball-valve" obstruction seen when the median lobe enlarges in benign prostatic hyperplasia.

## answer_b
Posterior lobe

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The posterior lobe lies posterior to the urethra as a whole and below the ejaculatory ducts, not sandwiched between the upper urethra and the ducts.

## answer_c
Lateral lobes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The two lateral lobes flank the urethra on either side and form the bulk of the gland; they are not the wedge specifically between urethra and ejaculatory ducts.

## answer_d
Anterior lobe (isthmus)

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The anterior lobe (isthmus) is a small fibromuscular band anterior to the urethra with little or no glandular tissue.

The lumbosacral trunk, which descends into the pelvis to join the sacral plexus, is formed by the ventral rami of which spinal nerves?

## subject

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
L4 and L5

## explanation_a
Correct. The lumbosacral trunk is formed by the smaller, descending part of the L4 ventral ramus joining the whole L5 ventral ramus; it crosses the pelvic brim anterior to the sacroiliac joint to join S1 and contribute to the sacral plexus, linking the lumbar and sacral plexuses functionally. This partial contribution explains why the lumbosacral trunk is often drawn as the structural bridge linking the two plexuses on diagrams.

L2 and L3

## explanation_b
Incorrect. L2 and L3 ventral rami contribute to the lumbar plexus proper (e.g., obturator and femoral nerves), not the lumbosacral trunk.

## answer_c
L3 and L4

## explanation_c
Incorrect. L3 contributes to the lumbar plexus, not the lumbosacral trunk; only part of L4 descends to join L5.

## answer_d
S1 and S2

## explanation_d
Incorrect. S1 and S2 are sacral plexus roots that the lumbosacral trunk joins, not the rami that form the trunk itself.

Obturator nerve

Raloxifene, a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), is used clinically for which of the following?

## question
Raloxifene, a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), is used clinically for which of the following?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Prevention and treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis

## explanation_a
Correct. Raloxifene acts as an estrogen-receptor agonist on bone (reducing resorption and increasing density, so is approved for prevention and treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis) while acting as an antagonist on breast and endometrial tissue, giving it a tissue-selective SERM profile distinct from either estrogen or tamoxifen. This tissue-selective profile is why raloxifene is sometimes described as a "designer estrogen" that captures bone benefit while avoiding breast and endometrial stimulation.

## answer_b
Treatment of menopausal hot flashes

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Raloxifene does not relieve, and can even worsen, vasomotor symptoms such as hot flashes, unlike hormone replacement therapy.

## answer_c
Endometrial stimulation alongside estrogen replacement therapy

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Raloxifene is an estrogen-receptor antagonist in the endometrium (unlike tamoxifen), so it does not stimulate endometrial proliferation; this is part of why it avoids tamoxifen's endometrial cancer risk.

## answer_d
Treatment of active, metastatic breast cancer

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Raloxifene is used to reduce the risk of invasive breast cancer in high-risk women, not to treat established active or metastatic breast cancer, for which tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors are used instead.

## title
Tadalafil is contraindicated in patients taking which class of drugs, because of the risk of severe, potentially life-threatening hypotension?

## question
Tadalafil is contraindicated in patients taking which class of drugs, because of the risk of severe, potentially life-threatening hypotension?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Nitrates

## explanation_a
Correct. Both nitrates and PDE5 inhibitors such as tadalafil raise intracellular cGMP in vascular smooth muscle (nitrates by donating nitric oxide, tadalafil by preventing cGMP breakdown); co-administration causes an exaggerated, synergistic fall in blood pressure that can be severe and life-threatening, so concurrent use is contraindicated. Patients are specifically counselled to disclose PDE5 inhibitor use to any physician considering nitrate therapy (e.g., for chest pain), since the interaction can be fatal even with short-acting nitrates taken hours apart.

## answer_b
Beta-blockers

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Beta-blockers are not absolutely contraindicated with tadalafil, though additional blood-pressure lowering should be monitored; the mechanism-specific, dangerous interaction is with nitrates.

## answer_c
Thiazide diuretics

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Thiazide diuretics can contribute to hypotension in general but do not share the specific cGMP-mediated synergistic mechanism that makes the nitrate interaction dangerous.

## answer_d
ACE inhibitors

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ACE inhibitors are used cautiously with PDE5 inhibitors but are not the classic absolute contraindication taught for this drug class.

## title
Which adverse effect of combined oral contraceptives (OCPs) is attributed to the estrogen component rather than the progestin?

## question
Which adverse effect of combined oral contraceptives (OCPs) is attributed to the estrogen component rather than the progestin?

## subject
pharm

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
A small increase in breast cancer risk

## explanation_a
Correct. A small increase in breast (and cervical) cancer risk associated with combined OCP use is attributed mainly to the estrogenic component's proliferative effect on hormone-sensitive tissue, in contrast with several other listed adverse effects, which are more closely tied to the progestin component of older formulations. Because this risk is small and the absolute numbers are debated, clinicians weigh it against OCPs' well-established reduction in ovarian and endometrial cancer risk when counselling patients.

## answer_b
Acne and hirsutism

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Acne and hirsutism are androgenic side effects driven by progestins with androgenic activity (e.g., older second-generation progestins), not by the estrogen component.

## answer_c
Weight gain and fluid retention

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Weight gain and fluid retention with older, higher-dose formulations were mainly attributed to the progestin's mineralocorticoid-like/androgenic activity, not the estrogen.

## answer_d
Depression and mood changes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Mood changes and depression have classically been attributed more to the progestin component of combined OCPs.
