## question
A bottle-fed 1-month-old boy is exposed to his sister who has chicken pox. He doesn't develop signs of the disease. His mother had chicken pox 5 years ago. Which class of immunoglobulins protected the boy:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
IgA

## explanation_a
Incorrect. IgA protects mucosal surfaces and is transferred through breast milk, but this baby is bottle-fed, so maternal IgA protection is not in play here.

## answer_b
IgD

## explanation_b
Incorrect. IgD functions chiefly as a B-cell surface receptor and does not cross the placenta to protect a newborn.

## answer_c
IgE

## explanation_c
Incorrect. IgE mediates allergic and antiparasitic responses; it is not the placentally transferred protective antibody here.

## answer_d
IgG

## explanation_d
Correct. IgG is the only antibody isotype actively transported across the placenta, so a baby is born with maternal IgG reflecting the mother's own immune history. Here, that means immunity to varicella carried over from her chickenpox 5 years earlier. This protects the bottle-fed boy even though he receives no protective antibody through milk, since he is not breastfed.

## answer_e
IgM

## explanation_e
Incorrect. IgM does not cross the placenta at all, and this baby is bottle-fed, so no maternal IgM protection reaches him by either route.

## question

## subject

## status

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. CD56 is an NK-cell identification marker, not the receptor responsible for recognising antibody-coated targets.

## answer_b
Fas receptor

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The Fas receptor is engaged by Fas ligand in a separate, antibody-independent killing pathway, not this antibody-dependent one.

## answer_c
Fc-gamma receptor

## explanation_c
Correct. Lysis of an antibody-coated tumour cell (ADCC) requires the NK cell's Fc-gamma receptor to bind the Fc portion of the IgG already coating the target, which then triggers the NK cell to kill it. ## explanation_d
Incorrect. Killer-inhibitory receptors restrain NK killing when they engage self MHC class I; they are not what enables antibody-dependent lysis.

## answer_e
MHC class I

## explanation_e
Incorrect. MHC class I is the ligand for killer-inhibitory receptors on the target cell side, not an NK-cell surface marker required for ADCC.

## question
Bacterial capsules:

## subject
imm

## status

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Activate B cells directly without T-cell help

## explanation_a
Correct. Bacterial capsular polysaccharides are classic thymus-independent (TI) antigens: their repetitive structure can directly cross-link enough B-cell receptors to activate the B cell without requiring T-cell help. Because this pathway bypasses T-cell help, class switching is limited and true immunological memory does not form the way it would for a thymus-dependent antigen.

## answer_b
Induce production of IgG

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Without T-cell help, class switching is limited, so a thymus-independent response like this is dominated by IgM rather than IgG.

## answer_c
On re-exposure to them, the immune response will be faster & stronger

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Because thymus-independent responses generate little to no memory, re-exposure to a bacterial capsule does not produce the faster, stronger response characteristic of a genuine memory (anamnestic) reaction.

## answer_d
Are formed of multiple non-repeating epitopes

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Bacterial capsular antigens are characteristically formed of multiple REPEATING epitopes (which is exactly what allows the repetitive BCR cross-linking), not non-repeating ones.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The constant regions determine isotype and effector function, not antigen-binding affinity.

The variable regions of the immunoglobulin molecule

The J-chain

## question
Comparing BCR to TCR, which statement is true:

## subject
imm

## status
Draft

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The B & T cell receptors become released into the surroundings

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Neither the BCR nor the membrane-bound TCR is released into the surroundings as a receptor; only the secreted antibody form (from BCR) circulates freely.

## answer_b
In contrast to BCR, TCR has 2 antigen-binding sites

## explanation_b
Incorrect. It is the BCR, not the TCR, that has two antigen-binding sites (in its typical Y-shaped monomeric form); the TCR has a single antigen-binding site.

## answer_c
Both require signal transducing molecules

## explanation_c
Correct. Both receptors need dedicated accessory signal-transducing molecules, since neither receptor's antigen-binding chains alone carry a cytoplasmic tail long enough to transduce a signal: the BCR signals through Ig-alpha/Ig-beta, and the TCR signals through the CD3 complex. Neither receptor's own antigen-recognition chains carry a cytoplasmic tail long enough to signal without this accessory partner.

## answer_d
Both molecules bind to MHC molecule

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Only the TCR requires MHC-restricted presentation; the BCR recognises native antigen directly, without needing MHC.

## answer_e
They bind to antigen non-specifically

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Both receptors bind antigen highly specifically, not non-specifically; specificity is a defining feature of both.

## question
Which of the following isn't involved in killing virally infected cells (VICs):

## subject
imm

## status

## owner
Dr. Omar

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
ADCC

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS involved). ADCC directly lyses an antibody-coated infected cell via an Fc-receptor-bearing effector cell.

## answer_b
Opsonization

## explanation_b
Correct. Opsonization enhances phagocytosis of free, extracellular particles or pathogens coated by antibody or complement. It is a mechanism suited to clearing pathogens outside cells, not to killing an already-infected host cell, which instead requires ADCC, CTLs or NK cells.

## answer_c
CTLs

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this IS involved). Cytotoxic T lymphocytes directly kill virally infected cells presenting foreign antigen on MHC class I.

## answer_d
NK cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this IS involved). NK cells directly kill virally infected cells, especially those that have lost MHC class I expression.
