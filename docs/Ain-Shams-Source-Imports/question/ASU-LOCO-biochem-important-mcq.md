## title
AGAT-deficiency vignette -- which reaction does AGAT catalyse

## question
A child had moderate developmental delay in infancy. He was mentally retarded and fatigued easily. His serum and urine creatinine were low, and fasting blood sugar was normal. However, his gene analysis identified a homozygous missense mutation in the AGAT enzyme, which catalyzes the following:

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The transfer of methyl group from SAM to guanidoacetate

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Transferring a methyl group from SAM to guanidoacetate is GAMT's reaction, the second step of creatine synthesis (carried out in the liver), not AGAT's.

## answer_b
The reaction between glycine and arginine in the kidney

## explanation_b
Correct. AGAT (arginine:glycine amidinotransferase) catalyses the first step of creatine synthesis in the kidney: arginine reacts with glycine to form guanidoacetate (and ornithine). A homozygous AGAT missense mutation blocks this first step, so creatine can never be made -- explaining this child's low serum and urine creatinine (creatinine is creatine's breakdown product, so no creatine means no creatinine), developmental delay and fatigue (creatine phosphate normally buffers ATP in brain and muscle), and normal fasting blood sugar (creatine synthesis does not touch glucose metabolism, distinguishing this from a glycogen-storage disease).

## answer_c
The conversion of creatine to creatine phosphate

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Creatine is converted to creatine phosphate by creatine kinase, a separate reaction from creatine's own synthesis; AGAT acts upstream of creatine even existing.

## answer_d
The conversion of creatine phosphate to creatinine

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Creatine phosphate spontaneously loses phosphate to form creatinine (a non-enzymatic process, unrelated to AGAT); this is creatine's breakdown, not its synthesis.

## answer_e
The reaction between arginine and methionine in the liver

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Arginine and methionine do not react directly in the liver; GAMT (the liver enzyme) transfers a methyl group from SAM (S-adenosylmethionine, methionine's activated derivative) to guanidoacetate, not to arginine, and this is GAMT's reaction, not AGAT's.

## title
Vitamin D activation pathway -- which statement is correct

## question
Vitamin D activation runs as follows: 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin is converted by UV light to cholecalciferol (vitamin D3). In the liver, an enzyme labelled '1' converts cholecalciferol to a hydroxylated intermediate. In the kidney, an enzyme labelled '2' converts that intermediate to the final product, labelled '3', the active form of vitamin D. Which of the following statements about this pathway is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
1 is the enzyme 1-alpha-hydroxylase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Enzyme 1 acts in the liver, and the liver step of vitamin D activation is 25-hydroxylase, not 1-alpha-hydroxylase; 1-alpha-hydroxylase is the kidney enzyme (labelled 2 in this pathway).

## answer_b
2 is the enzyme 25-hydroxylase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Enzyme 2 acts in the kidney, and the kidney step is 1-alpha-hydroxylase, not 25-hydroxylase; 25-hydroxylase is the liver enzyme (labelled 1 in this pathway).

## answer_c
3 is 25-OH-D3

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 25-OH-D3 (calcidiol) is the intermediate produced by the liver step, not the final product labelled 3; 3 is the pathway's end product after the kidney step acts on 25-OH-D3.

## answer_d
1 is the enzyme 24-hydroxylase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 24-hydroxylase produces an inactive metabolite (24,25-dihydroxy-D3) as a side branch that limits vitamin D activity; it is not the liver enzyme in this main activation sequence, which is 25-hydroxylase.

## answer_e
3 is 1,25-dihydroxy-D3

## explanation_e
Correct. The kidney's 1-alpha-hydroxylase adds a second hydroxyl group to 25-OH-D3, producing 1,25-dihydroxy-D3 (calcitriol) -- the active, hormonally functional form of vitamin D, which is what label 3 in this pathway represents. Calcitriol then acts on intestine, bone and kidney to raise serum calcium, completing the pathway from skin synthesis to hormone action. This two-step hydroxylation sequence, liver then kidney, is the detail this item is testing.

## title
Which enzyme-reaction pair is correct (purine salvage/degradation)

## question
Which of the following pair of the enzyme and its catalyzed reaction is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
HGPRT (guanosine → guanine + ribose 1-P)

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Guanosine to guanine plus ribose-1-phosphate is purine nucleoside phosphorylase's (PNP's) reaction, not HGPRT's; HGPRT instead salvages the free base hypoxanthine or guanine directly to a nucleotide (e.g. hypoxanthine to IMP).

## answer_b
PNP (Adenosine → inosine)

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Adenosine to inosine is adenosine deaminase's (ADA's) reaction, not PNP's; PNP instead cleaves a nucleoside to its base plus ribose-1-phosphate (e.g. guanosine to guanine plus ribose-1-phosphate).

## answer_c
5' nucleotidase (hypoxanthine → IMP)

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hypoxanthine to IMP is HGPRT's salvage reaction, not 5'-nucleotidase's; 5'-nucleotidase instead dephosphorylates a nucleotide to its nucleoside.

## answer_d
Guanase (guanine → xanthine)

## explanation_d
Correct. Guanase (guanine deaminase) hydrolytically deaminates guanine to xanthine, the guanine-derived branch's entry step into the shared xanthine-to-uric-acid pathway -- the one pairing among these five that is genuinely correct as printed. Xanthine then goes on to be oxidised to uric acid by xanthine oxidase, the same terminal step the adenine-derived branch also feeds into. Getting this pairing right means tracking guanase's substrate and product exactly, since every other option here swaps in a plausible-sounding but wrong enzyme for the same reaction.

## answer_e
Adenosine deaminase (nucleotide → nucleoside)

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Nucleotide to nucleoside is 5'-nucleotidase's reaction, not adenosine deaminase's; ADA instead deaminates the nucleoside adenosine to inosine.

## title
Which defective-enzyme-to-disease pair is correct

## question
Which of the following pair of the defective enzyme and its related disease is correct?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Mild immune deficiency syndrome - Guanase

## explanation_a
Incorrect. This curriculum's mild, T-cell-only immunodeficiency is caused by purine nucleoside phosphorylase (PNP) deficiency, not guanase deficiency; guanase deficiency is not a recognised immunodeficiency disease in this pairing.

## answer_b
Primary hyperuricemia - 1 hydroxylase

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Primary hyperuricemia (gout) is a purine-pathway disorder, most classically tied to overactivity of purine synthesis or a partial HGPRT deficiency; 1-alpha-hydroxylase is a vitamin D-activating kidney enzyme, unrelated to uric acid metabolism.

## answer_c
Severe combined immune deficiency - Adenosine deaminase

## explanation_c
Correct. Adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency lets deoxyadenosine accumulate to levels toxic to both T and B lymphocytes, producing severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) -- the one genuinely correct enzyme-disease pairing among these five. This combined T- and B-cell loss is what distinguishes ADA deficiency from PNP deficiency, which spares B cells and produces a milder, T-cell-only picture. Recognising which lymphocyte lineages are lost is exactly how this pairing is confirmed against the other four options.

## answer_d
Vitamin D resistant Rickets - Hypoxanthine phosphoribosyl transferase

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Vitamin D-resistant rickets from a 1-alpha-hydroxylase defect is a vitamin D-pathway disease; hypoxanthine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT) is a purine-salvage enzyme, and its deficiency instead causes Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, not vitamin D-resistant rickets.

## answer_e
Lesch Nyhan syndrome - Purine nucleoside phosphorylase

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is caused by HGPRT deficiency, not purine nucleoside phosphorylase (PNP) deficiency; PNP deficiency instead causes a T-cell-only immunodeficiency, distinct from Lesch-Nyhan's triad of hyperuricaemia, choreoathetosis and self-mutilation.
