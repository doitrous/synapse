## title
Staining property of thyroid follicular colloid

## question
Which of the following is true about thyroid follicles?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The lumen colloid stains positively with PAS (periodic acid-Schiff)

## explanation_a
Correct. The colloid filling the lumen of a thyroid follicle is rich in the glycoprotein thyroglobulin, which reacts positively with the periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) stain. PAS positivity is a standard histological feature used to identify colloid on light microscopy. This is distinct from the amount of colloid present, which varies with the follicle's activity state.

## answer_b
The follicular cell cytoplasm, not the colloid, stains positively with PAS

## explanation_b
Incorrect. It is the colloid, not the follicular cell cytoplasm, that is characteristically PAS-positive because of its high glycoprotein content.

## answer_c
The colloid is PAS-negative but stains positively with Congo red

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Colloid is PAS-positive, not PAS-negative; Congo red staining is used to detect amyloid, not thyroid colloid.

## answer_d
The follicular basement membrane is the only PAS-positive structure in the follicle

## explanation_d
Incorrect. While basement membranes can be PAS-positive, colloid is also, and characteristically, PAS-positive — the basement membrane is not the only PAS-positive structure.

## title
Ultrastructural characteristic of steroid-secreting cells

## question
Which of the following is a characteristic of steroid-secreting cells?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum and cytoplasmic lipid droplets

## explanation_a
Correct. Steroid hormone-secreting cells share a characteristic ultrastructure: abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum (site of enzymatic cholesterol-to-steroid conversion), mitochondria with tubular/vesicular cristae, and cytoplasmic lipid droplets storing cholesterol precursor. This combination, rather than any single feature alone, identifies a cell as steroidogenic on electron microscopy.

## answer_b
Abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum and dense secretory granules

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum and dense secretory granules describe a peptide/protein-hormone-secreting cell profile, not a steroid-secreting cell.

## answer_c
Numerous lysosomes with minimal endoplasmic reticulum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Steroid-secreting cells are not characterised by prominent lysosomes with minimal ER — the opposite is true, with abundant smooth ER being a defining feature.

## answer_d
Abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum and mucous-type granules

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Rough ER and mucous granules describe a mucus-secreting cell profile, unrelated to steroidogenic ultrastructure.

## title
Vascular connection between hypothalamus and adenohypophysis

## question
What is true concerning the connection between the hypothalamus and the pituitary gland?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The adenohypophysis is connected to the hypothalamus by the hypophyseal portal venous system

## explanation_a
Correct. Hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones enter a primary capillary plexus in the median eminence, drain via the hypophyseal portal veins, and reach a secondary capillary plexus in the anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis) to act on its cells. This vascular route is the adenohypophysis's connection to the hypothalamus.

## answer_b
The adenohypophysis is connected to the hypothalamus by a direct axonal (neural) tract

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A direct axonal (neural) tract describes the neurohypophysis's connection to the hypothalamus (the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract), not the adenohypophysis's.

## answer_c
The neurohypophysis is connected to the hypothalamus by a portal venous system

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The portal venous system connects the hypothalamus to the adenohypophysis, not the neurohypophysis, which has a direct neural connection instead.

## answer_d
Neither lobe of the pituitary has any vascular or neural connection to the hypothalamus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Both pituitary lobes are connected to the hypothalamus — the adenohypophysis vascularly (portal system) and the neurohypophysis neurally (direct axon tract).

## title
Mechanism of thyroid hormone release by follicular cells

## question
Which of the following is true regarding thyroid follicular cells?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
They release T3 and T4 after lysosomal proteolytic degradation of thyroglobulin

## explanation_a
Correct. Thyroid follicular cells synthesise thyroglobulin and iodinate it within the follicular colloid. Under TSH stimulation, they endocytose iodinated thyroglobulin back into the cell. Lysosomal proteolytic enzymes then degrade it, releasing free T3 and T4 into the circulation.

## answer_b
They release T3 and T4 directly, without any thyroglobulin involvement

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Thyroglobulin is central to the pathway — T3 and T4 are generated from iodinated thyroglobulin, not released directly without it.

## answer_c
They store T3 and T4 permanently and never release them

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Follicular cells do release stored hormone (as T3/T4) under TSH stimulation; they do not store it permanently without release.

## answer_d
They release free iodide without ever forming thyroglobulin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Iodide is actively taken up, oxidised, and organified onto thyroglobulin as part of hormone synthesis — thyroglobulin formation is a required step, not bypassed.

## title
Staining property of parathyroid oxyphil cells

## question
Which of the following is true regarding parathyroid oxyphil cells?

## subject
endo

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Deeply acidophilic (eosinophilic) staining

## explanation_a
Correct. Oxyphil cells are packed with numerous mitochondria. This mitochondrial content is what gives them their characteristic deeply acidophilic (eosinophilic) cytoplasmic staining on light microscopy.

## answer_b
Deeply basophilic staining

Incorrect. Oxyphil cells stain acidophilic (eosinophilic), not basophilic.

PAS-positive staining

Incorrect. PAS-positive staining is a feature of glycoprotein-rich structures such as thyroid colloid, not the mitochondria-driven acidophilia of oxyphil cells.
