## title
Upper limit for abnormal sperm forms

## question
The abnormal forms of sperms should not exceed ......... of the total count:

## subject
androl

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
E

## answer_a
2%

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 2% is far below the accepted upper limit; a semen sample this clean of abnormal forms is unremarkable, not a threshold value.

## answer_b
5%

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 5% likewise sits well under the accepted ceiling for abnormal sperm morphology.

## answer_c
10%

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 10% is still below the conventional cut-off used in this bank.

## answer_d
15%

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 15% is closer to the true figure but is not the value this bank's key marks.

## answer_e
20%

## explanation_e
Correct. A normal semen sample may contain a substantial minority of morphologically abnormal sperm — forms with head, neck or tail defects — without this being considered pathological, and the accepted upper limit taught here is 20% of the total count. Once the proportion of abnormal forms rises above this figure (teratospermia), fertilising capacity is considered impaired even if the sperm count and motility are otherwise normal. This is one of the four classic semen-parameter thresholds tested alongside sperm count, motility and viability.

## title
Duration of progesterone secretion by the corpus luteum of pregnancy

## question
Corpus luteum of pregnancy continues to secrete progesterone for:

## subject
gyn

## status

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer

## answer_a
9 days

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 9 days is well short of the corpus luteum of pregnancy's active lifespan; it describes roughly the time from ovulation to the point implantation is complete, not the corpus luteum's own endocrine lifespan.

## answer_b
15 days

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 15 days is close to the lifespan of the corpus luteum of menstruation (which regresses after about 12-14 days if fertilisation fails), not the corpus luteum of pregnancy, which is rescued and persists far longer.

## answer_c
20 days

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 20 days is still well short of the corpus luteum of pregnancy's true active period.

2-3 months

## explanation_d
Incorrect. 2-3 months undershoots the figure this bank's key marks, though it is in the right general range.

## answer_e
3-4 months

## explanation_e
Correct. Once implantation rescues the corpus luteum from regression, human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) from the syncytiotrophoblast keeps it secreting progesterone for roughly 3-4 months (to about the end of the first trimester). After that point the placenta itself has taken over as the dominant source of progesterone, and the corpus luteum of pregnancy is no longer clinically necessary to maintain the pregnancy — this handover is why removing the corpus luteum in the first trimester can cause miscarriage but doing so later usually does not.

## answer_a
The follicle matures

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Follicle maturation is a follicular-phase event, driven by rising FSH before ovulation; by the secretory phase the dominant follicle has already ovulated and become the corpus luteum.

## answer_b
Glands constrict

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Under progesterone's influence, the endometrial glands become coiled and dilated, actively secreting glycogen-rich fluid, not constricted.

## answer_c
Arteries become tortuous

## explanation_c
Correct. Progesterone from the corpus luteum drives the spiral arteries of the endometrium to elongate and coil into a markedly tortuous pattern during the secretory phase, alongside glandular coiling and secretion, preparing the endometrium to support a possible implanting blastocyst. This same coiled architecture is what allows the spiral arteries to constrict sharply if fertilisation fails, precipitating the ischaemia that triggers menstrual shedding.

## answer_d
Level of progesterone drops

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Progesterone rises through most of the secretory phase, sustained by the corpus luteum, only dropping late in the phase if fertilisation does not occur and the corpus luteum regresses.

## answer_e
Endometrium is thin

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The endometrium is at its thickest, most vascular and most glandular during the secretory phase, not thin -- a thin endometrium instead describes the early proliferative phase, just after menstrual shedding.
