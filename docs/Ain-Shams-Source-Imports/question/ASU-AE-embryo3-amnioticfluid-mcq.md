## title
Normal amniotic fluid volume at birth

## question
The normal volume of amniotic fluid at birth is:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
100 mL

## explanation_a
Incorrect. 100 mL is far below the normal term range and would in fact sit deep in the oligohydramnios range (conventionally under 400 mL) -- too little to be the normal figure the question asks for.

## answer_b
200 mL

## explanation_b
Incorrect. 200 mL is likewise well under the roughly 800-1500 mL normal term range and would be classed as abnormally reduced, not normal.

## answer_c
500 mL

## explanation_c
Incorrect. 500 mL is closer to the normal range but still sits below it, near the lower edge that starts to raise concern for reduced fluid rather than representing the typical term value.

## answer_d
1000 mL

## explanation_d
Correct. Amniotic fluid volume rises through pregnancy to roughly 800-1500 mL at term, and the printed answer key here rounds this to the commonly quoted teaching figure of about 1000 mL (1 litre). From mid-pregnancy onward most of the fluid is fetal urine, with contributions also from fetal lung fluid, balanced by fetal swallowing -- a balance whose disruption is exactly what the oligohydramnios (too little, often from reduced fetal urine output, for example in renal agenesis) and polyhydramnios (too much, often from reduced fetal swallowing, for example in esophageal or other GI atresia) questions elsewhere in this same bank test.

## answer_e
1500 mL

## explanation_e
Incorrect. 1500 mL sits at the upper edge of, or just past, the normal term range and is closer to a volume that would be flagged as excessive (polyhydramnios) than to the typical value this question asks for.

QST-DEV-ASU-AE-EMBRYO3-OLIGOHYDRAMNIOS-RENAL-AGENESIS

## title
Oligohydramnios and renal agenesis

## question
Oligohydramnios could indicate the presence of:

## subject
dev

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Diabetes in the mother

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Maternal diabetes is classically associated with polyhydramnios (excess amniotic fluid), driven by fetal hyperglycaemia and osmotic polyuria increasing fetal urine output -- the opposite direction of change from oligohydramnios.

## answer_b
Knots in the umbilical cord

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Knots (true or false) in the umbilical cord are a structural finding along the cord itself and are not a recognised cause of reduced amniotic fluid volume.

## answer_c
Renal agenesis

## explanation_c
Correct. From mid-pregnancy onward, fetal urine is the dominant source of amniotic fluid. Bilateral renal agenesis leaves the fetus unable to produce urine, so amniotic fluid volume falls sharply -- the classic mechanistic link (reduced fetal urine output) behind oligohydramnios, and the fetal renal/urinary-outflow explanation this concept's own definition names directly.

## answer_d
A large fetus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Fetal macrosomia (a large fetus) is more often associated with maternal diabetes and, through the same mechanism as option A, with polyhydramnios rather than oligohydramnios.

## answer_e
Esophageal atresia

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Esophageal atresia prevents the fetus from swallowing amniotic fluid, so fluid accumulates rather than depletes -- this is a cause of polyhydramnios, not oligohydramnios (the opposite pairing is tested directly at embryo3-q032).
