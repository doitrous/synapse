## title
Term for change of one epithelium type to another

## question
Change of type of epithelium into another type is called :

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Hyperplasia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hyperplasia is an increase in cell number of the same type, not a change from one epithelial type to another.

## answer_b
Metaplasia

## explanation_b
Correct. Metaplasia is defined as a reversible change of one epithelial type into another — the cell population itself is replaced by a different, better-adapted epithelial type, without the epithelium becoming a tumour. This is the specific term the bank is testing whenever a question describes an epithelium changing its type, as opposed to changing in number (hyperplasia), degree of disorder (dysplasia) or becoming neoplastic.

## answer_c
Increased mitosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Increased mitosis alone describes more cell division, not a change of epithelial type.

## answer_d
Tumor formation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tumour formation is neoplasia, an abnormal increase in the growth of cells, a different and more serious process than the reversible type-change of metaplasia.

## title
Characteristic of the terminal web

## question
Which of the following is a characteristic of the terminal web of the epithelial cells?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
It is located at the basal cytoplasm of the cell.

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The terminal web lies in the apical cytoplasm, just beneath the free surface, not the basal cytoplasm.

## answer_b
It is associated with the axoneme of the cilia.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The axoneme is the microtubular core of the cilium itself; the terminal web is a separate actin/intermediate-filament network, not part of the axoneme.

## answer_c
It is associated with the basal body of the cilia,

## explanation_c
Incorrect. While the terminal web does anchor the basal bodies of cilia, this option names the basal body rather than the terminal web's own composition, which is the specific feature this question tests.

## answer_d
It is formed of actin and intermediate filaments

## explanation_d
Correct. The terminal web is a dense network of cytoskeletal filaments — actin and intermediate filaments — lying in the apical cytoplasm of an absorptive or ciliated epithelial cell. Microvillus actin cores insert into it and it anchors the basal bodies of cilia, giving the apical cytoplasm structural support distinct from the rest of the cell. This actin-plus-intermediate-filament composition is precisely the characteristic being tested.

## answer_e
It is associated with mitochondria.

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Mitochondria are not a structural component of the terminal web; the terminal web is a cytoskeletal filament network, not an organelle association.

A dense network of cytoskeletal filaments in the apical cytoplasm of the epithelial cells is called....

Centrosome

## explanation_e
Incorrect. The centrosome is the microtubule organising centre near the nucleus, not the apical cytoplasmic filament network being described.

## subject

Correct — this is the false statement. Epithelium does not have a constant thickness: thickness varies by epithelial type and body site, from a single layer in simple epithelium to many layers in stratified epithelium, so "constant thickness" is the one listed property that does not describe epithelium as a tissue class. Recognising thickness as the variable feature, alongside the genuinely constant ones listed in the other options, is exactly what this "except" question is testing.

They have a strong lateral adhesions.

## explanation_b
Incorrect (i.e. a true statement). Epithelium genuinely has strong lateral adhesions between its cells, provided by the lateral junctions (tight junction, adherens junctions, desmosomes and gap junctions).

They do not contain blood vesseles.

## explanation_c
Incorrect (i.e. a true statement). Epithelium genuinely does not contain blood vessels — it is avascular and nourished by diffusion from the underlying connective tissue.

## answer_d
They have polarity.

## explanation_d
Incorrect (i.e. a true statement). Epithelium genuinely does show polarity, the structural and functional difference between its apical and basal surfaces.

## title
Junction that completely prevents intercellular passage

## question
Which type of the following structure completely prevents passage of substances between adjacent cells?

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Hemidesmosomes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Hemidesmosomes are basal specialisations that fix the epithelium to the basement membrane; they do not seal the space between adjacent lateral cell surfaces.

## answer_b
Gap junction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The gap junction is, on the contrary, the one lateral junction that specifically allows material (ions and small molecules) to pass between adjacent cells — the opposite of what this question asks for.

## answer_c
Tight junction

## explanation_c
Correct. The tight (zonula occludens) junction is the most apical of the lateral junctions: adjacent plasma membranes fuse at points through transmembrane proteins, obliterating the intercellular space at those points and encircling the apex of the cell like a belt. This seals the paracellular route completely, restricting passage of substances between adjacent cells and forcing anything crossing the epithelium to go through the cells rather than between them — precisely the barrier function this question is testing.

## answer_d
Adhering junction

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Adhering (zonula/macula adherens) junctions provide mechanical adhesion, not a seal against paracellular passage; they leave a wide intercellular space rather than obliterating it.

## answer_e
Lateral interdigitations

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Lateral interdigitations increase the surface area of contact between adjacent lateral membranes for mechanical interlocking; they do not seal off intercellular passage the way the tight junction does.

Where connexins are found

Connexins are transmembrane protein present in:

## subject

## status

## title
Protein of the prickle-cell dense plaque

## question
The dense plaque in the cytoplasmic aspects of the adjacent cells of the prickle cell layer in the epidermis of the skin is associated with...

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Actin

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Actin filaments anchor into the zonula adherens attachment site, not the desmosomal dense plaque of the prickle-cell layer.

## answer_b
Claudin

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Claudin is a tight-junction (zonula occludens) transmembrane protein, not the intermediate filament anchored at the desmosomal plaque.

## answer_c
Cytokeratin = tonofilaments

## explanation_c
Correct. The prickle-cell layer of the epidermis is named for its numerous desmosomes (macula adherens), whose thickened cytoplasmic attachment plaque anchors bundles of intermediate filaments — in epithelium, cytokeratin, also called tonofilaments. This cytokeratin/tonofilament association with the desmosomal dense plaque is exactly the fact this question is testing.

## answer_d
Vimentin

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Vimentin is the intermediate filament of connective tissue and muscle cells, not the epithelial (epidermal) intermediate filament anchored at desmosomes.

## answer_e
Laminin
