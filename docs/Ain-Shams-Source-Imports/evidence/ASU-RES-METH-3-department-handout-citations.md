▪ Hypothesis testing is a decision-making process for evaluating a suggestion about a
population. Our role is to test this suggestion to determine if it is right or wrong.
▪ This decision is taken after performing specific statistical tests that measure the
probability of the results of our research in case the null hypothesis is true.
▪ The purpose of hypothesis testing is to determine whether there is enough statistical
evidence in favour of our hypothesis i.e., to assess the plausibility of a hypothesis by
using sample data.

▪ We use the test statistic to determine this probability, which is the p-value (sometimes
called the α level). P-value indicates the strength of evidence against the null hypothesis
in term of probability. (i.e., saying there is something happening when, in fact, there is
not).
▪ If the probability (p-value) is very small, then it would be very unlikely to get data like
the obtained if H0 is true. Therefore, the probability of getting the observed data in our
sample is evidence against the H0 and we should reject it.
▪ On the other hand, if the probability is not very small, then it would be very likely to
observe similar data to what we would obtain if H0 is true. Therefore, the data observed in
our sample does not provide sufficient evidence against the H0 and we fail to reject it or
we accept the H0.

## support_span
▪ There is a possibility of committing two types of error: Type I Error if the null
hypothesis is rejected while actually it is true; the probability of that error is called α
(Greek alpha), e.g., we conclude that diabetic patients are more hypertensive than non-
diabetics while in fact in the population this difference is not present. The conclusion of a
difference when there is none is similar to the probability of a false-positive result of a
clinical test.
▪ This error always happens in any research despite the researcher’s will. So, the
probability of type I error or α is predetermined by scientists to be 0.05 or less. This
means that if the collected evidence suggests a probability of type I error is 0.05 or less,
we should act (make decisions) as if we are sure that the null hypothesis is false.
▪ The second type of error is Type II Error, this is the error committed when we do not
reject the null hypothesis while it is false; the probability of that error is called ß (Greek
beta).
