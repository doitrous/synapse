## label
The dorsal root of a spinal nerve is purely sensory and the ventral root is purely motor

## canonical_key
spinalnerve.roots.dorsal-sensory-ventral-motor

## definition
Each spinal nerve forms from the union of two roots as it leaves the spinal cord. The dorsal (posterior) root carries only afferent, sensory fibres, and its cell bodies sit in the dorsal root ganglion just proximal to the union. The ventral (anterior) root carries only efferent, motor fibres, arising from motor neurons in the ventral grey horn of the spinal cord. Once the two roots join to form the mixed spinal nerve trunk, this pure sensory/motor separation is lost, so it is only at root level, not distal to it, that the nerve is functionally "pure".

## explicit_objective
State that the dorsal root of a spinal nerve is purely sensory and the ventral root is purely motor, and that this separation is lost once the roots unite into the mixed spinal nerve trunk.

Preganglionic sympathetic neurons have their cell bodies in the lateral horn of the thoracolumbar spinal cord. Their axons leave the cord in the ventral root together with the somatic motor fibres, travel a short distance in the mixed spinal nerve trunk, and then branch off via the white ramus communicans to reach the sympathetic chain ganglion. This is why a question asking which structure "contains preganglionic sympathetic fibres" at the level of the spinal nerve itself points to the ventral root rather than the dorsal root or dorsal root ganglion, both of which are purely sensory pathways.

## label
Nerve plexuses form from the ventral rami of spinal nerves, and a ganglion is a group of nerve cell bodies outside the CNS

## canonical_key
spinalnerve.ganglia-and-plexus.ventral-rami-origin

## definition
A ganglion is a group of nerve cell bodies located outside the central nervous system, in contrast to a nucleus, the equivalent grouping inside the CNS. The great somatic nerve plexuses -- cervical, brachial, lumbar and sacral -- are all formed by the ventral rami of spinal nerves, not the dorsal rami, which instead supply the skin and muscles of the back segmentally without joining a plexus. The ventral rami themselves are mixed nerves, carrying both motor and sensory fibres, which is what allows a single plexus branch to supply both movement and sensation to its target region.

State that nerve plexuses are formed by the ventral rami of spinal nerves, and that a ganglion is a group of nerve cell bodies outside the CNS.

## canonical_key

The nervous system uses paired terms depending on whether a structure lies inside or outside the central nervous system. A group of nerve cell bodies is called a nucleus within the CNS, and a ganglion within the peripheral nervous system. ## canonical_key

The meninges form three layers around the brain and spinal cord: the outer dura mater, the middle arachnoid mater, and the inner pia mater, which closely invests the neural tissue itself. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) occupies the subarachnoid space, between the arachnoid mater and the pia mater, not the potential space between the dura and arachnoid (the subdural space) or the epidural space external to the dura. This is the space CSF actually fills, and it is what a lumbar puncture needle must reach in order to sample CSF.
