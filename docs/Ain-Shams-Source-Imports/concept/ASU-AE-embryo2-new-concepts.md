## label
Placenta praevia is implantation of the blastocyst in the lower uterine segment

## canonical_key
placenta.praevia.lower-uterine-segment-implantation

## definition
Placenta praevia occurs when the blastocyst implants in the lower part of the uterus, so that the developing placenta lies over or near the internal cervical os. This is a site abnormality, distinct from depth-of-invasion abnormalities such as placenta accreta/percreta or shape/number abnormalities such as placenta membranacea/succenturiata. Because it places the placenta in the path the fetus must pass through at delivery, placenta praevia is a recognised cause of antepartum haemorrhage and can obstruct a vaginal delivery.

## explicit_objective
State that placenta praevia results from implantation in the lower uterine segment, and distinguish it by name from other placental site/attachment abnormalities.

Cortical granule release blocks polyspermy after the first sperm fuses with the oocyte

## canonical_key
fertilisation.polyspermy-block.cortical-granule-release

## definition
When the first sperm fuses with the oocyte membrane, cortical granule release alters the composition of the oocyte's surrounding coat, producing a barrier that prevents any further sperm from penetrating and fertilising the same oocyte. This barrier is commonly taught as the zona reaction; a local terminology variant labels it the vitelline (membrane) block. Either name describes the same protective mechanism against polyspermy, a fertilisation by more than one sperm that would produce a non-viable, triploid conceptus.

Hypoblast cells lining it spread to form the exocoelomic (Heuser's) membrane, and together with the hypoblast this lining converts the blastocoele into the primary yolk sac. ## canonical_key

## definition
The cell released at ovulation and penetrated by the sperm is the secondary oocyte, still arrested at metaphase of the second meiotic division.
