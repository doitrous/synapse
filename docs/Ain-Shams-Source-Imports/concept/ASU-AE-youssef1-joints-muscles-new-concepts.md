## label
The temporomandibular joint contains a fibrocartilaginous articular disc

## canonical_key
temporomandibular-joint.articular-disc

## definition
The temporomandibular joint (TMJ), between the mandibular condyle and the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone, contains a fibrocartilaginous articular disc that divides its cavity into separate upper and lower synovial compartments. The disc lets the joint combine two different kinds of movement in series: a hinge-like rotation of the condyle against the underside of the disc in the lower compartment, and a gliding (translation) of the disc-condyle unit against the temporal bone in the upper compartment. This two-compartment, disc-divided arrangement is what allows the mandible to both open/close and protrude/retract, and it is the feature that marks the TMJ out from joints such as the superior radioulnar, ankle, metacarpophalangeal or carpometacarpal-of-thumb joints, none of which contain an intra-articular disc of this kind.

## explicit_objective
State that the temporomandibular joint contains a fibrocartilaginous articular disc dividing it into upper and lower compartments.

## label
Temporalis is a triangular (fan-shaped) muscle covering the temporal fossa

## canonical_key
muscle.temporalis-triangular-fan-shape

## definition
Muscles are classified by shape as well as by fibre direction, and the triangular (fan-shaped) form is one such shape class: a broad, curved origin narrowing to a single point of insertion, like a fan closing to its pivot. Temporalis is a worked example -- it arises broadly from the temporal fossa and the temporal fascia and converges, fan-like, to insert by a narrow tendon onto the coronoid process and the anterior border of the ramus of the mandible. This is a shape-based classification (parallel-sided, triangular, circular and so on), distinct from the fibre-direction classification of pennate muscles (unipennate, bipennate, multipennate) and from the fibre-arrangement classification of parallel/strap muscles such as sartorius.
