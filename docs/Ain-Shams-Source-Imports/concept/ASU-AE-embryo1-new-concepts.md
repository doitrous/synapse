Necrospermia is death of the sperm present in the ejaculate

## canonical_key
semen-terminology.necrospermia.dead-sperm

## definition
Necrospermia is a semen abnormality in which the spermatozoa present in the ejaculate are dead (non-viable). It is distinct from asthenospermia, in which sperm are alive but poorly motile, and from azoospermia, in which no sperm are present at all. The sperm count itself can be entirely normal in necrospermia, since the defect lies in viability rather than in numbers, so it is diagnosed by a vitality stain (such as eosin exclusion), not by counting alone.

## explicit_objective

## definition
The cell released at ovulation is a secondary oocyte, arrested in metaphase of the second meiotic division, not a "mature ovum" as the popular term "egg" loosely implies. It only completes meiosis II, extrudes the second polar body, and becomes a true mature (fertilised) ovum if a sperm penetrates it; if fertilisation never occurs, the secondary oocyte degenerates within about 24 hours, still arrested at metaphase II. The primary oocyte that preceded it has already completed the first meiotic division just before ovulation, which is what converts it into the secondary oocyte that is actually discharged.

The corpus luteum of menstruation regresses after 12-14 days but the corpus luteum of pregnancy persists to about the 4th month

## canonical_key
corpus-luteum.fate.menstruation-vs-pregnancy-duration

## definition
If fertilisation does not occur, the corpus luteum of menstruation has a fixed functional lifespan of about 12-14 days from ovulation, after which it begins to regress into the fibrous corpus albicans; its falling progesterone output is what triggers endometrial shedding roughly 14 days after ovulation. If implantation does occur, human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) from the syncytiotrophoblast rescues the corpus luteum from this regression, and the resulting corpus luteum of pregnancy continues secreting progesterone for about 3-4 months. By about the 4th month the placenta has become fully capable of producing enough progesterone on its own, so progesterone during pregnancy comes from these two sequential sources -- the corpus luteum for roughly the first trimester, then the placenta for the remainder.

## canonical_key
germ-cells.diploid.primary-spermatocyte-and-primary-oocyte

## definition
The primary spermatocyte and the primary oocyte are each the last diploid cell in their respective germ lines, still carrying 46 chromosomes, because both exist before the first meiotic division reduces the chromosome number. The primary spermatocyte forms by growth of a spermatogonium and divides within days; the primary oocyte, by contrast, is arrested in prophase of the first meiotic division for years, from before birth until it resumes and completes that division just before ovulation. Every cell downstream of either one -- the secondary spermatocyte and secondary oocyte, and everything produced from them -- is already haploid.

## definition
Spermiogenesis is the purely structural remodelling of the haploid spermatid into a mature sperm, without any further cell division. Proacrosomal granules from the Golgi apparatus coalesce into the acrosomal vesicle, which spreads over the anterior half of the condensing nucleus to form the acrosomal cap; the nucleus itself condenses markedly to fit the streamlined head. Mitochondria migrate to and wrap helically around the proximal tail, forming the mitochondrial sheath of the middle piece, while the centrioles form the axial filament (axoneme) that runs the length of the tail. ## label
Combined contraceptive pills act by suppressing follicle maturation

## canonical_key
contraceptive-pills.mechanism.suppress-follicle-maturation

## definition
Combined oral contraceptive pills supply exogenous estrogen and progestogen that, through negative feedback on the hypothalamic-pituitary axis, suppress pituitary FSH and LH release. Without an adequate FSH signal, the ovarian follicles fail to mature and no dominant follicle is selected that cycle, so the LH surge that would otherwise trigger ovulation never happens. Suppressing follicle maturation -- and the ovulation that would follow it -- is therefore the pill's primary contraceptive mechanism, distinct from spermicidal, embryocidal, or menstruation-suppressing actions.

The follicular phase of the ovarian cycle is controlled by pituitary FSH

ovarian-cycle.follicular-phase.pituitary-fsh-control

## definition
Pituitary follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) rises early in the ovarian cycle and drives the recruitment and growth of a cohort of ovarian follicles, one of which becomes dominant, during the follicular phase. Rising estrogen from the maturing dominant follicle then feeds back on the hypothalamic-pituitary axis to trigger the mid-cycle LH surge, which ends the follicular phase at ovulation. Progesterone and the corpus luteum, by contrast, are the defining features of the luteal (post-ovulatory) phase, not the follicular phase this concept addresses.

## label
The spiral arteries become tortuous during the secretory (progestational) phase of the menstrual cycle

## canonical_key
menstrual-cycle.secretory-phase.spiral-arteries-tortuous

## definition
Progesterone from the corpus luteum drives the endometrium's spiral arteries to elongate and coil into a markedly tortuous pattern during the secretory (progestational) phase, alongside glandular coiling and active glycogen-rich secretion, preparing the endometrium to support a possible implanting blastocyst. The endometrium is at its thickest, most vascular and most glandular during this phase, not thin as in the early proliferative phase just after menstrual shedding. This same coiled architecture is what allows the spiral arteries to constrict sharply if fertilisation fails and the corpus luteum regresses, precipitating the ischaemia that triggers menstrual shedding.
