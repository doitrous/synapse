## id

## canonical_key
multiple-sclerosis.presentation.relapsing-attacks-plaques

## definition
Multiple sclerosis is a chronic autoimmune, demyelinating disease of the central nervous system. Its classic clinical course (relapsing-remitting MS, the commonest subtype) is one of discrete attacks of neurological dysfunction — commonly motor weakness and sensory disturbance/paraesthesia, but potentially involving any CNS pathway (optic neuritis, cerebellar signs, bladder dysfunction) — followed by partial or complete recovery. These attacks correlate anatomically with demyelinating plaques that are disseminated in time and space, visualised on MRI as characteristic periventricular and other white-matter lesions, which underpins the clinical/radiological diagnostic criteria for the disease.

## definition
The pharyngeal (branchial) pouches are paired embryonic endodermal outpocketings that give rise to distinct adult structures. The fourth pharyngeal pouch gives rise to the superior parathyroid glands (along with the ultimobranchial body, which contributes calcitonin-secreting C cells to the thyroid). isoflurane.adverse-effect.coronary-steal-ischemic-heart-disease

## definition
Isoflurane is a volatile (inhalational) general anaesthetic. Its most clinically important cardiovascular property is coronary vasodilation, which in patients with existing coronary artery disease can produce "coronary steal": blood is diverted away from already-narrowed, poorly-autoregulating stenotic vessels toward normal vessels that dilate more readily, worsening perfusion to the ischaemic myocardial territory. For this reason isoflurane is used with particular caution, or avoided, in patients with significant ischaemic heart disease, in favour of anaesthetics with a more neutral coronary vascular profile.

## label
Long-term haloperidol therapy causes tardive dyskinesia, presenting with orofacial involuntary movements

## id
CON-NEU-01EAA0B40E344E

## canonical_key
haloperidol.adverse-effect.tardive-dyskinesia

## definition
