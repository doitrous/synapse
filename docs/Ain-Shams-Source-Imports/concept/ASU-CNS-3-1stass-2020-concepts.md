## label
Complete spinal cord transection produces bilateral paraplegia with total loss of all sensory modalities below the lesion

## id
CON-NEU-C40A60A85AB820

## canonical_key
spinalcordtransection.complete.bilateral-paraplegia-total-sensory-loss

## definition
Complete transection of the spinal cord severs every ascending and descending pathway bilaterally at that level, so below the lesion there is total loss of all sensory modalities (touch, pain, temperature, proprioception, vibration) together with bilateral paraplegia (or tetraplegia, if cervical) from loss of all descending motor control. This distinguishes complete transection from partial cord syndromes such as Brown-Séquard syndrome (hemisection, dissociated ipsilateral/contralateral losses) or anterior cord syndrome (bilateral pain/temperature loss with dorsal columns spared), which selectively spare specific tracts because only part of the cord cross-section is damaged.

## explicit_objective
State that complete spinal cord transection causes bilateral paraplegia with total (all-modality) sensory loss below the lesion, unlike partial cord syndromes.

## label
The fourth ventricle is the cavity of the rhombencephalon (hindbrain)

## id
CON-NEU-371754CC16510D

## canonical_key
fourthventricle.embryology.rhombencephalon-derivation

## definition
Each embryonic brain vesicle encloses part of the ventricular system: the paired lateral ventricles are the cavities of the telencephalon, the third ventricle is the cavity of the diencephalon, the cerebral aqueduct is the cavity of the mesencephalon, and the fourth ventricle is the cavity of the rhombencephalon (hindbrain, comprising the developing pons/cerebellum and medulla). This embryological derivation is why the fourth ventricle lies between the pons/cerebellum and the medulla in the adult.

## explicit_objective
State that the fourth ventricle develops as the cavity of the rhombencephalon (hindbrain).

## label
The lateral spinothalamic (spinal lemniscus) pathway continues as the neospinothalamic tract, carrying fast, discriminative pain

## id
CON-NEU-F94F15D61C579B

## canonical_key
spinallemniscus.continuation.neospinothalamic-tract

## definition
Second-order fibres of the lateral spinothalamic tract decussate near their level of entry and ascend as the spinal lemniscus, which continues rostrally as the neospinothalamic tract — a fast, few-synapse pathway carrying sharp, well-localised (fast) pain directly to the ventral posterolateral thalamic nucleus and on to the somatosensory cortex. This is distinct from the paleospinothalamic pathway, a slower, multisynaptic route through the reticular formation and intralaminar thalamic nuclei that carries poorly localised, dull (slow) pain.

## explicit_objective
Identify the neospinothalamic tract as the rostral continuation of the spinal lemniscus (lateral spinothalamic pathway), carrying fast pain.

## label
The common facial vein drains directly into the internal jugular vein

## id
CON-NEU-C04FD05E0EEF6C

## canonical_key
commonfacialvein.drainage.internal-jugular-vein

## definition
The common facial vein is formed by the union of the (anterior) facial vein and the retromandibular vein's anterior division, in the upper part of the neck. It crosses superficial to the carotid sheath and drains directly into the internal jugular vein. This is distinct from other named tributaries (e.g. the occipital vein, which more commonly joins the internal jugular vein separately or via the posterior auricular vein), giving the internal jugular vein multiple, individually named venous inputs along its course.
