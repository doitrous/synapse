## label
The tuber cinereum is a hypothalamic structure, not a component of the basal ganglia

## id
CON-NEU-D9221DED57BA4E

## canonical_key
basalganglia.exclusion.tuber-cinereum-is-hypothalamic

## definition
The tuber cinereum is part of the hypothalamus — the median-eminence region of the floor of the third ventricle, between the optic chiasm and the mammillary bodies, from which the infundibulum (pituitary stalk) descends — and is functionally and anatomically distinct from the basal ganglia. The basal ganglia proper comprise the caudate nucleus and putamen (together the neostriatum), the globus pallidus (paleostriatum), the subthalamic nucleus and the substantia nigra; the tuber cinereum plays no part in this motor-control system, and naming it among the basal ganglia is a classic distractor exploiting its similarly deep, grey-matter location.

## explicit_objective
State that the tuber cinereum is a hypothalamic structure and is not one of the basal ganglia.

## label
A brain tumour causes headache chiefly through intracranial mechanisms such as raised intracranial pressure and traction on pain-sensitive structures

## id
CON-NEU-747B1672BEE9DF

## canonical_key
headache.mechanism.intracranial-cause-brain-tumour

## definition
A brain tumour classically causes headache through intracranial mechanisms — mass effect raising intracranial pressure, obstruction of cerebrospinal fluid flow (causing hydrocephalus), and traction or distortion of pain-sensitive intracranial structures such as the dura mater, large blood vessels and venous sinuses — none of which is itself pain-sensitive at the level of brain tissue. This intracranial route is the mechanistic counterpart to extracranial headache, which instead arises from pain-sensitive structures outside the cranial cavity such as the scalp, sinuses and cervical spine; a tumour-related headache is classically worse in the morning or on waking, and aggravated by coughing, straining or bending, reflecting further transient rises in intracranial pressure.

## explicit_objective
State that a brain tumour causes headache through intracranial mechanisms — raised intracranial pressure, hydrocephalus and traction on pain-sensitive intracranial structures — as opposed to an extracranial cause.
