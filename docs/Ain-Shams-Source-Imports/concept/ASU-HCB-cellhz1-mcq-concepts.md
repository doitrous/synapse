The Golgi apparatus sorts, glycosylates and modifies protein but plays no part in glycogenesis

## canonical_key
golgi.functions.overview-excludes-glycogenesis

## definition
The Golgi apparatus receives newly made protein from the endoplasmic reticulum and carries out three jobs on it before packaging it for its destination: sorting proteins to the correct address (lysosome, secretory vesicle or plasma membrane), completing and modifying their carbohydrate side chains (glycosylation), and processing precursor molecules such as prohormones and proproteins into their active, mature form. Glycogen synthesis (glycogenesis) is a separate cytosolic pathway carried out by soluble enzymes such as glycogen synthase and branching enzyme; it is not a Golgi function and is not one of the products the Golgi packages.

## explicit_objective

## pitfalls
Counting the shaft's microtubules as 27, the centriole/basal-body number. The shaft's 9+2 axoneme has 9 doublets (18 microtubules) plus 2 central singlets, 20 microtubules in all — one fewer pair than the 9 triplets (27 microtubules) of the centriole/basal body it grows from, because the central singlet pair is unique to the motile axoneme.
