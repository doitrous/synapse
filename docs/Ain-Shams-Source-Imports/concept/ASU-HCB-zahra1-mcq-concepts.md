## label
Heterochromatin is highly coiled, electron-dense, peripherally distributed chromatin that is transcriptionally inactive

## canonical_key
cell.chromatin.heterochromatin-properties

## definition
Heterochromatin is the tightly (highly) coiled, condensed form of chromatin. Because it is so densely packed it stains darkly with basic dyes on light microscopy and appears electron-dense on EM, and it is typically distributed at the nuclear periphery against the inner nuclear membrane as well as around the nucleolus. This tight packing keeps its DNA inaccessible to the transcription machinery, so heterochromatin is transcriptionally inactive — it is euchromatin, the loosely extended form, that directs active protein synthesis.

## explicit_objective
State that heterochromatin is highly coiled and electron-dense, and that it is transcriptionally inactive (not the form that directs protein synthesis).

## canonical_key
cell.chromatin.euchromatin-properties

## definition
Euchromatin is the loosely extended (uncoiled) form of chromatin, in contrast with the tightly coiled heterochromatin. Because its DNA is decondensed and accessible, euchromatin is the transcriptionally active form that directs protein synthesis. This same loose packing means euchromatin does not stain as a distinct dark clump the way heterochromatin does, so on a routine light-microscopic preparation it is not resolved as a separate, visible structure — it simply contributes to the pale, finely stippled background of the nucleoplasm.
