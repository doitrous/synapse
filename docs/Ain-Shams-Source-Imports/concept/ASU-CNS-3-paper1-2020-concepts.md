## label
Propranolol is used to treat essential tremor, including in elderly patients

## id
CON-NEU-A4AB4DBFFEDCF6

## canonical_key
propranolol.use.essential-tremor

## definition
Essential tremor is a common movement disorder causing a fine, bilateral action/postural tremor of the hands, distinct from the resting tremor of Parkinson disease. Propranolol, a non-selective beta-adrenergic antagonist, is first-line pharmacological therapy: blocking peripheral beta-2 receptors in skeletal muscle reduces the tremor's amplitude. It is used across age groups, including elderly patients, though beta-blockade requires caution in comorbidities such as bradycardia, asthma or decompensated heart failure.

## label
Anterior spinal artery occlusion at the spinal cord level causes bilateral flaccid paraplegia below the lesion with bilateral loss of pain and temperature sensation, sparing the dorsal columns

## id
CON-NEU-725CFCD1D52BEA

## canonical_key
anterior-spinal-artery-syndrome.spinal-cord.bilateral-paraplegia-pain-loss

## definition
The anterior spinal artery supplies the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord (the corticospinal, spinothalamic tracts and anterior horns), while the paired posterior spinal arteries supply the dorsal columns. Occlusion of the anterior spinal artery at the spinal cord level (anterior spinal artery/anterior cord syndrome) therefore causes bilateral motor weakness (flaccid initially, becoming spastic paraplegia below the lesion) and bilateral loss of pain and temperature sensation below the lesion, while proprioception and vibration sense (carried by the dorsal columns, supplied by the posterior spinal arteries) are classically spared. This spinal cord-level syndrome is distinct from medial medullary syndrome, in which anterior spinal artery territory occlusion at the medulla produces the different, more localised picture of ipsilateral tongue weakness with contralateral limb weakness.

Naloxone, a short-acting opioid receptor antagonist, is used to reverse fentanyl (opioid) intoxication

## id
CON-NEU-ADE33D9DEAF8E7

## canonical_key
naloxone.use.fentanyl-intoxication-reversal

## definition
Naloxone is a competitive antagonist at the mu-opioid receptor, given intravenously or intramuscularly to acutely reverse opioid intoxication/overdose, restoring respiratory drive and consciousness within minutes. Because it is short-acting relative to many opioids (including the potent, high-affinity opioid fentanyl), repeated doses or an infusion may be needed to prevent re-sedation as naloxone wears off before the opioid does. This acute reversal role is distinct from naltrexone, a related but long-acting oral antagonist used for relapse prevention after detoxification, not for acute overdose.

## label
The cochlear nucleus is the origin of the trapezoid body

## id
CON-NEU-EFFD1F2F22CB53

## canonical_key
cochlear-nucleus.function.trapezoid-body-origin

## definition
The trapezoid body is a bundle of decussating auditory fibres in the ventral pons, part of the ascending central auditory pathway. Its fibres arise from the (ventral) cochlear nucleus, the first central relay station for auditory information after the cochlear nerve; many of these fibres cross the midline within the trapezoid body to reach the contralateral superior olivary nucleus and, ultimately, the inferior colliculus, underpinning the bilateral, partially crossed representation of sound needed for binaural hearing and sound localisation.
