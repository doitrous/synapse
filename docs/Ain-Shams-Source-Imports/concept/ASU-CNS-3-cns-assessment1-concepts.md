## label
Huntington disease is caused by a CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion in the huntingtin gene, and its microscopic feature is atrophy of the caudate nucleus with gliosis

## id
CON-NEU-1C781DB1CB6B9D

## canonical_key
huntington-disease.pathology.cag-expansion-caudate-atrophy

## definition
Huntington disease is an autosomal-dominant neurodegenerative disorder caused by an expanded CAG trinucleotide repeat in the huntingtin (HTT) gene on chromosome 4, producing a mutant huntingtin protein with an abnormally long polyglutamine tract that is toxic to neurons; longer repeat expansions correlate with earlier onset and a phenomenon of anticipation across generations. The disease's characteristic microscopic/macroscopic pathological feature is severe atrophy of the striatum, especially the caudate nucleus (with accompanying gliosis), reflecting selective early neuronal loss there, which underlies the disease's classic triad of chorea, cognitive decline and psychiatric disturbance.

## explicit_objective
State that Huntington disease is caused by a CAG repeat expansion in the huntingtin gene, with caudate nucleus atrophy as its microscopic feature.

## label
Genioglossus is supplied by contralateral corticobulbar (upper motor neuron) fibres only, unlike most other tongue muscles which receive bilateral supply

## id
CON-NEU-D896D3ABBD166E

## canonical_key
genioglossus.innervation.contralateral-corticobulbar-only

## definition
Most muscles supplied by cranial nerve motor nuclei receive bilateral corticobulbar (upper motor neuron) input, so a unilateral upper motor neuron lesion produces little clinical deficit in those muscles. Genioglossus is an important exception: it receives corticobulbar input from the contralateral cerebral hemisphere only. Consequently, a unilateral upper motor neuron lesion (e.g. a stroke affecting the corticobulbar tract) causes the protruded tongue to deviate toward the side of the lesion (weak genioglossus fails to push the tongue to the opposite, unaffected side) — the basis of the classic "tongue deviates toward the lesion in a UMN lesion, away from the lesion in an LMN (hypoglossal nerve) lesion" clinical rule.

Glutamate is the principal excitatory neurotransmitter of the central nervous system

## id
CON-NEU-3F5B371AFE55C5

## canonical_key
glutamate.function.principal-excitatory-neurotransmitter

## definition
Glutamate is an amino acid that also functions as the principal excitatory neurotransmitter throughout the central nervous system, acting on ionotropic receptors (NMDA, AMPA, kainate) and metabotropic glutamate receptors to mediate fast excitatory synaptic transmission at the great majority of excitatory synapses in the brain and spinal cord. This complements GABA, the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter, and the balance between glutamatergic excitation and GABAergic inhibition underlies normal CNS signalling; excess glutamatergic activity is also implicated in excitotoxic neuronal injury (for example in ischaemia and some neurodegenerative processes).

## definition
Rexed laminae are the ten histologically-defined layers of spinal cord grey matter, numbered I (most dorsal) to X. This lamina II location is present in the dorsal horn at all spinal cord levels, including the cervical segments.

## label
In the Pacinian corpuscle, the sensory nerve fibre runs axially through the centre of the receptor, distinguishing its structure from the Meissner corpuscle

## id
CON-NEU-1DA068411DB5F6

## canonical_key
pacinian-corpuscle.structure.axial-nerve-fiber

## definition
The Pacinian corpuscle is a large, encapsulated mechanoreceptor with a characteristic "onion-skin" arrangement of concentric lamellae, chiefly responsible for detecting vibration and deep pressure; the Meissner corpuscle is a smaller, more superficial encapsulated receptor for light touch/flutter, with a different internal architecture in which flattened lamellar cells interdigitate around the nerve terminal in a more coiled/branching pattern. A defining structural feature of the Pacinian corpuscle is that its single sensory (afferent) nerve fibre runs axially, straight through the centre of the corpuscle's concentric lamellae, a histological distinction from the Meissner corpuscle's terminal, which does not show this same simple axial course.

## explicit_objective
