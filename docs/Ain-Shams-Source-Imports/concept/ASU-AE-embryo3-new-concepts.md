## label
Endoderm forms the epithelial lining of the gut tube and its respiratory outgrowth, including the bronchial tree

## canonical_key
endoderm.gut-and-respiratory-epithelial-lining

## definition
The endoderm is the innermost germ layer and gives rise to the epithelial lining of the entire primitive gut tube, from the pharynx to the anal canal. The respiratory system arises as a ventral outgrowth (the laryngotracheal diverticulum) of the foregut, so the epithelium lining the pharynx, trachea, bronchial tree and alveoli is likewise endodermal, even though the surrounding cartilage, smooth muscle and connective tissue of these same structures are mesodermal. Because respiratory and gut epithelium share this single embryological source, exam questions naming either the lining of the respiratory passages or the bronchial tree as endodermal are testing the same underlying fact as questions about the gut's own epithelium.

## explicit_objective
State that the epithelial lining of the gut tube and its respiratory outgrowth (including the bronchial tree) is endodermal in origin.

The kidneys develop from the intermediate mesoderm (intermediate cell mass), the intraembryonic mesoderm subdivision between the paraxial and lateral plate columns

## canonical_key
intraembryonic-mesoderm.intermediate-cell-mass-forms-kidneys

## definition
Intraembryonic mesoderm on each side of the notochord differentiates into three longitudinal columns: paraxial mesoderm (forming somites), intermediate mesoderm (also called the intermediate cell mass or nephrogenic cord), and lateral plate mesoderm. A question naming intermediate cell mass or intermediate mesoderm as the source of the kidneys is testing this three-way subdivision of intraembryonic mesoderm.

## label
Lateral folding of the trilaminar disc rolls its edges ventrally to enclose an elongated gut tube

## canonical_key
embryonic-folding.lateral-folding-forms-gut-tube

## definition
As the flat trilaminar embryonic disc grows, its lateral margins fold ventrally toward the midline, in addition to the cephalocaudal folding that occurs along the disc's long axis. This lateral folding carries the two lateral halves of the endoderm-lined yolk sac roof together and pinches them into a single, continuous, tubular gut, converting what was a flat sheet of endoderm into an elongated internal tube running the length of the embryo. Cephalocaudal folding, by contrast, is what carries the septum transversum, heart and pericardial cavity onto the future ventral surface while the notochord stays fixed dorsally; the two folding movements act together but produce different specific results, which is what distinguishes exam items asking about lateral folding from ones asking about folding in general.

The allantois is a diverticulum of the hindgut that projects into the connecting stalk

## canonical_key
allantois.hindgut-diverticulum-within-connecting-stalk

## definition
The allantois arises early in development as a small, finger-like diverticulum (outpouching) from the caudal wall of the yolk sac, in continuity with the hindgut, and it extends from there into the connecting stalk that will become the umbilical cord. Because it projects into the connecting stalk, the allantois is anatomically located within that structure from early development onward, and its blood vessels go on to form the umbilical arteries and vein. In humans the allantois itself remains small and largely functionless, later contributing fibrous tissue (the urachus) that runs from the bladder to the umbilicus, but its early position within the connecting stalk is the fact most often tested.

## label
The full-term umbilical cord normally contains two umbilical arteries and one umbilical vein (the right umbilical vein degenerates), not the ductus venosus itself

## canonical_key
umbilical-cord.vessel-count-and-ductus-venosus-location

## definition
Early in development the umbilical cord contains a pair of umbilical arteries and a pair of umbilical veins, but the right umbilical vein normally degenerates during the embryonic period, so the full-term umbilical cord contains two umbilical arteries and only the single, persisting left umbilical vein. The ductus venosus is a separate vessel that the left umbilical vein drains into once it has entered the fetal abdomen; it runs through the fetal liver, not through the umbilical cord itself, so it is not one of the cord's own contents. The cord's other normal components at term include Wharton's jelly, the mucous connective tissue that gives the cord its bulk and protects the vessels, and remnants of the allantois and vitelline duct, all surrounded by an amniotic covering.

Oligohydramnios classically points to fetal renal agenesis, because fetal urine is the major late-pregnancy source of amniotic fluid

## canonical_key
oligohydramnios.renal-agenesis-as-classic-cause

## definition
From the second trimester onward, fetal urine production becomes the dominant source of amniotic fluid, so any fetal condition that abolishes urine output severely reduces the fluid volume. Bilateral renal agenesis, in which the kidneys fail to form, is the classic cause taught for oligohydramnios, an abnormally reduced amniotic fluid volume, and severe oligohydramnios from this cause is itself dangerous, since it also compresses the fetus and can restrict lung development (pulmonary hypoplasia). This contrasts with polyhydramnios, whose classic causes instead interfere with the fetus's ability to swallow amniotic fluid rather than with amniotic fluid production.

State that fetal renal agenesis is the classic cause of oligohydramnios, because fetal urine is the main source of amniotic fluid.

## canonical_key
neurenteric-canal.amniotic-cavity-to-yolk-sac-connection

## definition
The primitive pit, a small depression in the primitive node, deepens and extends ventrally until it briefly connects the amniotic cavity above the embryonic disc with the yolk sac cavity below it; this transient passage is called the neurenteric canal. It exists only briefly during the third week and normally closes again as the notochordal process solidifies, but its persistence is one embryological explanation offered for certain rare enteric or spinal anomalies. Because the canal runs specifically between the amniotic cavity and the yolk sac and not any other pair of cavities, it is the standard answer whenever a question describes a temporary connection between the amniotic cavity and the yolk sac cavity or asks what the neurenteric canal itself connects.
