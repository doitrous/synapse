Parkinson disease is characterised histologically by intracytoplasmic Lewy bodies and loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra

## definition
Parkinson disease is a neurodegenerative disorder characterised histologically by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and by intracytoplasmic Lewy bodies — eosinophilic inclusions composed principally of aggregated, misfolded alpha-synuclein protein — within surviving neurons. This nigrostriatal dopaminergic loss underlies the disease's cardinal motor features (bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity and postural instability) and is the physiological target of dopamine-replacement therapies such as levodopa.

## pitfalls
Confusing Parkinson disease's substantia nigra Lewy bodies (alpha-synuclein) with Huntington disease's caudate atrophy or Alzheimer disease's amyloid plaques and tau tangles — each neurodegenerative disease has its own distinct histological signature, not interchangeable between diseases.
