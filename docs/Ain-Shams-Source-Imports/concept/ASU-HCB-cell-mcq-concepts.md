## label
Constitutive secretion (e.g. procollagen) is continuous and unstored; regulated secretion is stored in granules and released on a signal

## canonical_key
secretion.pathway.constitutive-vs-regulated

## definition
The constitutive pathway of protein secretion releases its product continuously as it is made, with no storage step and no external trigger — procollagen for bone and connective-tissue formation is the standard example. The regulated pathway packages its product into secretory granules that are stored in the cytoplasm and released only on a specific signal, such as insulin release triggered by a rise in blood glucose.

## explicit_objective
Distinguish the constitutive from the regulated pathway of protein secretion and name procollagen as a constitutive example.

## label
Tubular mitochondrial cristae on EM mark a steroid-secreting cell such as the adrenal cortex

## canonical_key
mitochondria.tubular-cristae.steroidogenic-cell-marker

## definition
A steroid-secreting cell needs a very large area of inner mitochondrial membrane to carry the enzymes of steroidogenesis (cholesterol side-chain cleavage and hydroxylation), and it achieves that by folding its cristae as tubules rather than as flat shelves. Tubular cristae, together with abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum, are the electron-microscopy marker of a steroidogenic cell such as an adrenal cortical cell.

Identify tubular mitochondrial cristae as an EM marker of a steroid-secreting cell.
