## label
Kinetic (intention) tremor results from a spinocerebellar lesion

## id
CON-NEU-AE9CD2F605A1B3

## canonical_key
kinetictremor.cause.spinocerebellar-lesion

## definition
Kinetic (intention) tremor is a tremor that appears or worsens during a targeted, voluntary movement — classically demonstrated as past-pointing and increasing oscillation on the finger-nose-finger and heel-shin tests as the target is approached. It results from a lesion of the cerebellum or its spinocerebellar connections, which disrupts the real-time error correction the cerebellum normally provides during a movement, rather than from a basal ganglia or peripheral nerve lesion.

## explicit_objective
Attribute kinetic (intention) tremor, worsening as a target is approached, to a spinocerebellar lesion.

## label
Spinal cord hemisection (Brown-Séquard syndrome) causes ipsilateral dorsal column loss and contralateral pain/temperature loss below the lesion

## id
CON-NEU-53B558B50820FB

## canonical_key
brownsequardsyndrome.hemisection.ipsilateral-dorsal-column-contralateral-pain-temperature-loss

## definition
Brown-Séquard syndrome follows hemisection (lateral half-section) of the spinal cord. Because the dorsal columns (fine touch, vibration, proprioception) ascend uncrossed until the medulla, hemisection causes ipsilateral loss of these modalities below the level of the lesion. Because the lateral spinothalamic tract carries fibres that have already crossed in the cord one to two segments below their entry, hemisection instead causes contralateral loss of pain and temperature sensation below the lesion — the two sensory losses lie on opposite sides of the body.

## explicit_objective
State that spinal cord hemisection produces ipsilateral dorsal column loss together with contralateral pain and temperature loss below the level of the lesion.

## label
A lesion of the primary motor cortex (area 4) causes contralateral (crossed) hemiplegia with loss of fine skilled movement

## id
CON-NEU-7DF3D20F6D51DF

## canonical_key
area4lesion.effect.contralateral-hemiplegia-and-loss-of-fine-skilled-movement

## definition
Because the corticospinal tract, which originates chiefly from the primary motor cortex (Brodmann area 4), decussates at the pyramidal decussation in the lower medulla, a destructive lesion of area 4 on one side produces hemiplegia (or hemiparesis) of the contralateral (crossed) side of the body. Fine, discrete, skilled voluntary movements — most severely affecting the distal limb and hand — are lost first and most completely, because these movements depend most heavily on direct corticospinal (particularly corticomotoneuronal) control, unlike gross axial and proximal movements, which are partly preserved through other descending pathways.

## explicit_objective
State that a lesion of area 4 produces contralateral hemiplegia with the most severe loss affecting fine skilled (especially distal/hand) movement.

## label
The gate control theory explains why rubbing a painful area relieves pain

## id
CON-NEU-EC01B119738618

## canonical_key
gatecontroltheory.mechanism.rubbing-relieves-pain

## definition
The gate control theory (Melzack and Wall) proposes that a 'gate' in the dorsal horn of the spinal cord, formed by inhibitory interneurons in the substantia gelatinosa, regulates the passage of nociceptive signals toward higher centres. Large-diameter, fast-conducting A-beta touch/pressure afferents activate these inhibitory interneurons, closing the gate and suppressing transmission of slower, small-diameter A-delta/C-fibre pain signals arriving from the same or a nearby segment — which is why rubbing or massaging a painful area (activating A-beta afferents) provides real, physiologically grounded pain relief.

## explicit_objective
Explain, via the gate control theory, why activating large touch afferents by rubbing a painful area reduces pain transmission.

## label
The muscle spindle, not the Golgi tendon organ, is chiefly responsible for regulating muscle tone

## id
CON-NEU-2497888994D779

## canonical_key
musclespindle.function.tone-regulation-vs-golgi-tendon-organ-tension

## definition
Muscle tone — the continuous, low-level resistance of a resting muscle to passive stretch — is set primarily by the muscle spindle's stretch (myotatic) reflex: spindle afferents signal muscle length and rate of stretch, driving reflex alpha-motor-neuron activity that maintains tone and resists over-lengthening. The Golgi tendon organ, by contrast, is a tension (not length) receptor located at the musculotendinous junction, and its role is autogenic inhibition — protecting the muscle from excessive tension rather than setting resting tone.

## explicit_objective
Distinguish the muscle spindle (length, tone regulation via the stretch reflex) from the Golgi tendon organ (tension, protective autogenic inhibition).

## definition
