## id
CON-NEU-54875A0284C08C

## canonical_key
temporomandibularjoint.lateralligament.prevents-posterior-dislocation

## definition
The temporomandibular joint's lateral (temporomandibular) ligament runs obliquely downward and backward from the articular tubercle of the temporal bone to the neck of the mandibular condyle, on the lateral aspect of the joint capsule. By checking excessive posterior and lateral movement of the condyle, this ligament is the principal structure preventing posterior dislocation of the mandible — for example, from a direct blow to the chin — protecting the external acoustic meatus, which lies immediately behind the joint.

## explicit_objective
Identify the lateral (temporomandibular) ligament of the TMJ as the structure preventing posterior dislocation of the mandibular condyle.

## label
The intralaminar thalamic nuclei are the principal thalamic relay of the paleospinothalamic (slow, diffuse) pain pathway

## id
CON-NEU-CA7EB9421346C7

## canonical_key
intralaminarnuclei.relay.paleospinothalamic-slow-pain-pathway

## definition
The paleospinothalamic tract, carrying slow, poorly-localised, dull/aching pain via small unmyelinated C fibres, relays polysynaptically through the reticular formation before terminating diffusely in the intralaminar nuclei of the thalamus — in contrast to the neospinothalamic tract, which carries fast, sharp, well-localised pain via A-delta fibres directly to the ventral posterolateral nucleus. The intralaminar nuclei then project widely and diffusely to the cerebral cortex and limbic structures, which accounts for the poorly localised, affect-laden quality of the pain this pathway conveys.

## explicit_objective
Attribute the paleospinothalamic (slow pain) pathway's thalamic relay to the intralaminar nuclei, contrasting with the neospinothalamic pathway's relay in the ventral posterolateral nucleus.

## label
Visceral pain from an inflamed organ such as the stomach can trigger reflex rigidity of the overlying abdominal wall muscles

## id
CON-NEU-03C7AC01585098

## canonical_key
visceralpain.viscerosomaticreflex.overlying-abdominal-muscle-rigidity

## definition
Because visceral and somatic (body-wall) afferents from the same embryonic segment converge on shared second-order neurons in the spinal cord dorsal horn, sustained noxious input from an inflamed viscus — for example, the stomach in chronic gastritis — can spread reflexively to somatic efferents supplying the overlying abdominal wall muscles, producing involuntary reflex rigidity (guarding) of that wall. This viscerosomatic reflex is a protective response and is a classic clinical sign used to localise visceral pathology by the segmental distribution of the abdominal wall it affects.

## explicit_objective
Attribute reflex rigidity of the overlying abdominal wall muscles, in conditions such as chronic gastritis, to a viscerosomatic reflex arising from convergent visceral and somatic afferent input at the same spinal segment.

## id

## canonical_key
hypoglossalnucleus.position.most-medial-medullary-motor-nucleus

## definition
In the medulla, the somatic motor nuclei are arranged in a mediolateral column reflecting their embryological cell-column origin, with the general somatic efferent (GSE) column lying most medially. The hypoglossal nucleus, a GSE nucleus supplying the tongue musculature, therefore lies closest to the midline of any motor nucleus in the medulla, underlying the hypoglossal trigone of the floor of the fourth ventricle, medial to the more laterally placed special visceral efferent (e.g. nucleus ambiguus) and general visceral efferent (e.g. dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus) columns.

Localise the hypoglossal nucleus as the most medial motor nucleus in the medulla, reflecting its general somatic efferent (GSE) column identity.

## id

## canonical_key
naltrexone.mechanism.opioid-antagonist-relapse-prevention

## definition
Naltrexone is a competitive antagonist at opioid receptors (chiefly the mu receptor); by blocking these receptors, it prevents exogenous opioids from producing their euphoric/reinforcing effect and, in alcohol use disorder, reduces alcohol's reward via the same opioid-receptor-mediated pathway. Because it removes the pharmacological reward that drives continued use without itself being an agonist, naltrexone (given orally or as a long-acting injectable) is used as maintenance therapy to help prevent relapse in both opioid and alcohol addiction, once the patient is opioid-free.

## explicit_objective
Attribute naltrexone's relapse-prevention effect in opioid and alcohol addiction to competitive opioid receptor antagonism, which blocks the reinforcing effect of continued use.

Carbidopa, a peripheral dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor, reduces the dose of L-dopa needed to treat Parkinson disease

## id
CON-NEU-24679C278E7E0B

## canonical_key
carbidopa.mechanism.peripheral-dopadecarboxylase-inhibition-reduces-ldopa-dose

## definition
Carbidopa inhibits aromatic L-amino acid (dopa) decarboxylase in peripheral tissues but, because it does not cross the blood-brain barrier, spares the same enzyme within the central nervous system. Co-administered with L-dopa, it prevents the peripheral conversion of L-dopa to dopamine — which would otherwise cause peripheral side effects (nausea, hypotension) without benefiting the brain — so that a much larger proportion of an oral L-dopa dose survives to cross the blood-brain barrier and be converted to dopamine centrally, allowing the total L-dopa dose needed for benefit to be markedly reduced.

## explicit_objective
Attribute carbidopa's L-dopa-dose-sparing effect in Parkinson disease treatment to peripheral (not central) dopa-decarboxylase inhibition.
