## label
A highly cellular cerebellar tumour with anaplasia, mitoses and Homer Wright rosettes, WHO Grade IV and radiosensitive, is medulloblastoma

## id
CON-NEU-06A7267A30C7E2

## canonical_key
medulloblastoma.diagnosis.homer-wright-rosettes-anaplasia-who-grade-iv-radiosensitive

## definition
Medulloblastoma is the classic embryonal cerebellar tumour of childhood, histologically highly cellular with sheets of small round blue cells showing anaplasia, brisk mitotic activity and Homer Wright rosettes (tumour cells radially arranged around a central neuropil core). It is graded WHO Grade IV, the highest malignancy grade, yet is characteristically radiosensitive, a property exploited in adjuvant treatment after surgical resection.

## explicit_objective
Identify medulloblastoma from the combination of Homer Wright rosettes, anaplasia/mitoses, WHO Grade IV grading and radiosensitivity.

Hemangioblastoma is histologically composed of large vacuolated stromal cells and numerous thin-walled capillary channels

## id
CON-NEU-FA7C7CBC44EE37

## canonical_key
hemangioblastoma.histology.vacuolated-stromal-cells-and-thin-walled-capillaries

## definition
Hemangioblastoma, a slow-growing benign cerebellar tumour (WHO Grade I) often associated with von Hippel-Lindau disease, is composed microscopically of two components: large, lipid-vacuolated ("foamy") stromal cells and a dense network of thin-walled, delicate capillary-sized blood vessels, giving the tumour its highly vascular character. The stromal cells are thought to be the true neoplastic element, with the vasculature a secondary, reactive proliferation.

## id

## canonical_key

## definition
The single most common cytogenetic abnormality in medulloblastoma is isochromosome 17q, in which the short arm of chromosome 17 (17p, containing the TP53 tumour-suppressor locus) is lost and replaced by a duplicated long arm (17q). This karyotypic finding is distinct from the molecular-subtype markers (for example WNT-pathway beta-catenin activation or SHH-pathway alterations) used in the newer WHO integrated classification, and remains a recognisable hallmark of the tumour as a whole rather than of any one subtype.

## id

## canonical_key
hemangioblastoma.genetics.vhl-tumour-suppressor-gene-inactivation

## definition
The VHL tumour suppressor gene, located on chromosome 3p, is inactivated (by mutation or deletion, per Knudson's two-hit model) in hemangioblastoma, whether the tumour arises sporadically or as part of von Hippel-Lindau disease. Loss of functional VHL protein leads to stabilisation of hypoxia-inducible factors and upregulation of angiogenic factors such as VEGF, which explains the tumour's characteristic high vascularity.

## label
Clear cell change is not a recognised morphological variant of medulloblastoma; the classic, desmoplastic/nodular, large cell/anaplastic and extensively nodular variants are

## id
CON-NEU-ED3651C7285B79

## canonical_key
medulloblastoma.variants.clear-cell-not-a-recognised-variant

## definition
The WHO classification recognises several histological variants of medulloblastoma: classic, desmoplastic/nodular, medulloblastoma with extensive nodularity, and large cell/anaplastic, each with distinct morphology and, in some cases, prognostic or molecular-subtype associations (desmoplastic/nodular variants, for instance, are enriched for the SHH molecular subtype). "Clear cell" is not among these recognised variants; clear-cell morphology in a cerebellar tumour instead should prompt consideration of other entities such as clear cell ependymoma or metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma.

State that clear cell is not a recognised morphological variant of medulloblastoma.

The WHO integrated diagnosis of medulloblastoma requires both histopathologic and molecular/genetic features, not either alone

## id
CON-NEU-714607A64C4F48

## canonical_key
medulloblastoma.who-classification.integrated-diagnosis-histology-plus-molecular

## definition
Since the WHO 2016/2021 CNS tumour classification updates, medulloblastoma is no longer diagnosed on histology alone. The integrated diagnosis combines the traditional histological variant (classic, desmoplastic/nodular, extensive nodularity, or large cell/anaplastic) with the molecular subtype (WNT-activated, SHH-activated, Group 3 or Group 4) determined by immunohistochemistry and/or molecular testing, because this integration reflects underlying biology and prognosis better than either axis alone.

## label
Medulloblastoma's radiosensitivity, despite its high WHO grade, makes radiotherapy a key part of its treatment

## id
CON-NEU-286514CC9CAC93

## canonical_key
medulloblastoma.treatment.radiosensitivity-supports-radiotherapy

## definition
Although medulloblastoma is a highly malignant, WHO Grade IV tumour, it is also markedly radiosensitive, meaning its cells are efficiently killed by ionising radiation. This radiosensitivity is clinically important because it makes craniospinal radiotherapy (targeting both the primary site and the neuroaxis, given the tumour's propensity for CSF-borne dissemination) a cornerstone of adjuvant treatment after maximal safe surgical resection, alongside chemotherapy in appropriate risk groups.

## explicit_objective
State that medulloblastoma's radiosensitivity makes radiotherapy a key part of its treatment, despite its high WHO grade.
