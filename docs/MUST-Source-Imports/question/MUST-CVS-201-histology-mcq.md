## title
Cause of enlarged, tortuous superficial leg veins

## question
A patient presents with enlarged, tortuous superficial veins in the leg. This is most likely due to:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Atherosclerosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Atherosclerosis is an intimal disease of large and medium arteries, not veins, and it does not produce visible, tortuous superficial dilation.

## answer_b
Varicose veins

## explanation_b
Correct. A varicose vein is a superficial vein permanently dilated and tortuous, most often in the leg, from sustained raised venous pressure combined with valve incompetence. Once a valve fails, blood refluxes and pools in the segment below it, progressively distending and lengthening the wall into the visible, palpable tortuous loops described here. Prolonged standing, pregnancy, obesity and a family history of weak vein walls are the usual predisposing factors.

## answer_c
Arteritis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Arteritis is inflammation of an artery wall, not a chronic dilation of superficial veins.

## answer_d
Phlebosclerosis

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Phlebosclerosis is fibrous thickening/hardening of a vein wall, not the dilated, tortuous appearance described here.

## title
Stain for visualising elastic laminae in arteries

## question
Which stain is used to clearly visualize elastic laminae in arteries?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
H&E

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Ordinary haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) shows general tissue architecture but does not selectively highlight elastic fibres, so the elastic laminae are not clearly resolved.

## answer_b
Orcein

## explanation_b
Correct. Orcein is the special stain used to visualise elastic laminae in the arterial wall, because routine H&E does not resolve them clearly. Orcein selectively colours elastic fibres, so the internal and external elastic laminae and the concentric elastic membranes of the tunica media stand out sharply from the surrounding collagen and smooth muscle. This is exactly the basis for counting elastic laminae to distinguish an elastic from a muscular artery.

## answer_c
PAS

## explanation_c
Incorrect. PAS (periodic acid-Schiff) highlights glycogen and basement-membrane carbohydrates, not elastic fibres.

## answer_d
Masson's trichrome

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Masson's trichrome differentiates collagen from muscle (typically staining collagen blue/green and muscle red), not elastic fibres specifically.

## title
Functional role of pericytes in capillaries

## question
What is the role of pericytes in capillaries?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Phagocytosis

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Phagocytosis in the capillary/sinusoid wall is a macrophage function, not a pericyte one.

## answer_b
Contractility and repair

## explanation_b
Correct. Pericytes are contractile, mesenchymal-derived cells that wrap the outer surface of capillary and postcapillary-venule endothelium, sharing its basal lamina. Their contractile filaments let them modulate capillary calibre and blood flow, and, as an undifferentiated reserve cell population, they can also proliferate and differentiate to help repair damaged small vessels. This dual contractile-and-reparative role is why pericytes are sometimes described as the capillary's own mural cell.

## answer_c
Hormone secretion

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Hormone secretion is not a pericyte function; endocrine cells and, in the vessel wall itself, juxtaglomerular-type cells serve that role elsewhere.

## answer_d
Elastic support

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Elastic support in a vessel wall comes from elastic fibres and laminae, not from pericytes, which are contractile rather than structural-elastic cells.

## title
Risk factor for deep vein thrombosis after bed rest

## question
A patient with long bed rest after surgery develops deep vein thrombosis. Which condition is a risk factor?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Varicose veins

## explanation_a
Correct. Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) follows Virchow's triad: venous stasis, hypercoagulability and endothelial injury. Varicose veins are themselves a stasis-arm risk factor for DVT, because the pooled, sluggish blood in a dilated, valve-incompetent vein favours clot formation, the same mechanism by which post-surgical bed rest raises DVT risk. This is why both bed rest and pre-existing varicose veins can be tested as separate DVT risk factors without contradiction.

## answer_b
Arteriosclerosis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Arteriosclerosis is arterial-wall thickening/hardening; it is not part of Virchow's triad for venous thrombosis.

## answer_c
Frostbite

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Frostbite is cold-induced tissue injury, not a recognised risk factor for deep vein thrombosis.

## answer_d
Hypertension

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Hypertension is a risk factor for arterial disease (atherosclerosis, stroke), not specifically for deep venous thrombosis.

## title
Diagnosis for a painful, swollen, clotted deep leg vein

## question
A patient presents with leg pain, swelling, and redness. Ultrasound reveals a blood clot in a deep leg vein with inflammation. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Varicose veins

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Varicose veins are chronically dilated, tortuous superficial veins from valve incompetence; they do not on their own produce an ultrasound-confirmed deep-vein clot with inflammation.

## answer_b
Thrombophlebitis

## explanation_b
Correct. Thrombophlebitis is a deep vein thrombosis whose thrombosed segment is also inflamed, presenting with leg pain, swelling, warmth and redness over a palpable venous cord — exactly the picture confirmed here by ultrasound. The inflammation is what distinguishes thrombophlebitis from a silent, asymptomatic DVT that carries a clot without these local signs.

## answer_c
Atherosclerosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Atherosclerosis is an arterial intimal disease; it does not present as an inflamed deep venous clot on ultrasound.

## answer_d
Aneurysm

## explanation_d
Incorrect. An aneurysm is an abnormal arterial dilation, not a venous clot with inflammation.
