## title
Gout management — withdrawal of thiazide

## question
For the same patient once the acute gout attack is treated, what change to his existing drug therapy is most appropriate?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Start allopurinol immediately

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Allopurinol is chronic urate-lowering therapy, but it is deliberately delayed until several weeks after the attack has settled, not started immediately.

## answer_b
Withdraw the thiazide diuretic

## explanation_b
Correct. General management of gout removes the precipitating cause — in this case, the thiazide diuretic, which raises serum urate by reducing its renal excretion. Withdrawing it (and substituting an alternative antihypertensive if needed) is the specific, appropriate step for a patient whose gout is linked to this known drug trigger.

## answer_c
Add a loop diuretic instead

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Loop diuretics can also raise urate and are not a safe substitute chosen specifically to help this patient's gout.

## answer_d
Continue the thiazide diuretic unchanged

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Continuing the precipitating drug unchanged would leave the patient at ongoing risk of further gout attacks.

## title
Seronegative arthritis — defining serology

## question
The seronegative arthritides (ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthropathy, IBD-associated arthritis) are characterised by:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
A positive rheumatoid factor

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A positive rheumatoid factor is the defining serology of rheumatoid arthritis, the seropositive counterpart this group is contrasted against.

## answer_b
A negative rheumatoid factor

## explanation_b
Correct. The seronegative spondyloarthropathies — ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthropathy and IBD-associated arthritis — are, by definition, rheumatoid-factor negative, distinguishing them serologically from rheumatoid arthritis. They instead share a strong HLA-B27 association.

## answer_c
A positive anti-CCP antibody

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Anti-CCP antibody positivity is specific to rheumatoid arthritis, not to the seronegative arthritides.

## answer_d
A positive ANA

## explanation_d
Incorrect. ANA positivity is more relevant to connective tissue diseases such as SLE, not to the seronegative spondyloarthropathies as a defining feature.
