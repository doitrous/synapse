## title
Vessel-wall layer containing the endothelium

## question
Which layer of a blood vessel contains the endothelium?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Tunica adventitia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The adventitia is the outermost connective-tissue coat; it carries no endothelium.

## answer_b
Tunica media

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The media is the middle, mostly smooth-muscle coat, not the endothelial one.

## answer_c
Tunica intima

## explanation_c
Correct. The tunica intima is the innermost layer of a blood vessel and is in direct contact with the blood. It consists of an endothelium — a simple squamous epithelium on its own basal lamina — providing a smooth surface for flow, supported by a subendothelial layer of loose connective tissue. In arteries an internal elastic lamina also sits within this layer.

## answer_d
Vasa vasorum

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Vasa vasorum are small nutrient vessels within the adventitia, not the endothelial lining.

## title
Layers separated by the internal elastic lamina

## question
The internal elastic lamina separates which two layers?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Tunica intima and tunica media

## explanation_a
Correct. The internal elastic lamina is a condensed, fenestrated band of elastic fibres that lies at the boundary between the tunica intima and the tunica media in arteries, preventing complete occlusion of the lumen while allowing nutrients to diffuse through its fenestrations. This is why an artery's intima is easy to identify histologically even before the media's smooth muscle is examined.

## answer_b
Tunica media and tunica adventitia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. It is the external elastic lamina, not the internal one, that lies between the media and the adventitia.

## answer_c
Tunica adventitia and vasa vasorum

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Vasa vasorum sit within the adventitia itself; the internal elastic lamina is not positioned there.

## answer_d
Subendothelium and endothelium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The subendothelium and endothelium are both part of the tunica intima, on the luminal side of the internal elastic lamina, not separated by it.

Structure preventing complete occlusion in arteries

## question
What structure is absent in veins but present in arteries to prevent complete occlusion?

## subject
cvs

## status

B

## explanation_a

Correct. The internal elastic lamina, a condensed fenestrated band of elastic fibres at the intima-media boundary, is a feature of arteries and is what prevents complete occlusion of the arterial lumen; ordinary veins lack a well-developed internal elastic lamina. ## title
Vessel-wall layer containing vasa vasorum

## question
Which layer of blood vessels contains vasa vasorum?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

C

Tunica intima

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The intima is nourished by direct diffusion from the lumen, not by vasa vasorum.

Tunica media

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The inner media is also nourished by luminal diffusion; vasa vasorum do not run within the media itself.

Tunica adventitia

Endothelium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The endothelium is a single cell layer lining the lumen; it does not contain vessels of its own.

## title
Vessel-wall layer containing nervi vasorum

## question
In which layer of the blood vessel would you find nervi vasorum?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Tunica intima

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The intima has no autonomic nerve supply of its own; it is regulated indirectly via the media's smooth muscle.

## answer_b
Tunica media

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The media contains the smooth muscle that is innervated, but the nerve fibres themselves (nervi vasorum) run in the adventitia before reaching the media.

Because nervi vasorum act on the smooth muscle of the media, damage to the adventitia can itself disturb a vessel's autonomic tone.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The subendothelium is part of the tunica intima, well away from the adventitial nerve supply.

Primary component of the arterial subendothelial layer

What is the primary component of the subendothelial layer in arteries?

What is the function of the external elastic lamina?

## title
Artery type with 40-60 concentric elastic laminae

## question
Which type of artery is characterized by having 40–60 distinct concentric elastic laminae in the tunica media?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A muscular artery's media is almost entirely circular smooth muscle with elastic fibres between the cells, not dozens of concentric elastic membranes.

## explanation_b
Incorrect. An arteriole's media has only one or two thin layers of smooth muscle, the opposite of a media built from many elastic laminae.

Correct. Large elastic (conducting) arteries such as the aorta have a thick media built from roughly 40 to 70 circularly arranged, fenestrated elastic membranes, increasing in number with age, with only some smooth muscle between them. Their number increases with age, one of the few histological features of an artery that changes across the lifespan.

Metarteriole

## explanation_d
Incorrect. A metarteriole is the arteriole's own terminal segment, an even smaller vessel with a precapillary sphincter, not an elastic-laminae-rich artery.

Artery with a well-developed IEL but no EEL

## question
Which artery has a well-developed internal elastic lamina but no external elastic lamina?

## subject
cvs

Femoral artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The femoral artery is a typical muscular artery and, like most muscular arteries, carries both a prominent internal elastic lamina and a recognisable external one.

## title
Vessel wall made only of endothelium and pericytes

## question
Which vessel has a wall composed only of endothelium and pericytes?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Muscular artery

## explanation_a
Incorrect. A muscular artery has a full three-tunic wall (intima, media, adventitia), not just endothelium and pericytes.

## answer_b
Capillaries

## explanation_b
Correct. A capillary's wall is reduced to its simplest form: an endothelial tube on its basal lamina, with pericytes as its only other, contractile cell type wrapped around the outside — no separate media or adventitia the way a true artery or vein has. This minimal wall is exactly what makes a capillary the circulation's exchange vessel rather than a resistance or capacitance one.

## answer_c
Elastic artery

## explanation_c
Incorrect. An elastic artery has a thick, multi-laminar three-tunic wall, far more than endothelium and pericytes alone.

## answer_d
Arteriole

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Even a reduced-form arteriole still has all three tunics, including a media with one or two smooth-muscle layers, not just endothelium and pericytes.

## title
Phagocytic cell type in sinusoid walls

## question
Which cell type is found in the walls of sinusoids for phagocytosis?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Fibroblasts

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Fibroblasts are connective-tissue cells that make matrix; phagocytosis is not their role.

## answer_b
Macrophages

## explanation_b
Correct. Macrophages associated with the sinusoid wall (for example Kupffer cells lining liver sinusoids) sit interspersed among or adjacent to the sinusoidal endothelium, exploiting the vessel's wide intercellular gaps and discontinuous basal lamina to phagocytose particulate matter, old cells and debris directly from the passing blood. Their presence is part of the same reticuloendothelial-system role that sinusoidal organs like the liver and spleen carry out throughout the body.

## answer_c
Pericytes

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Pericytes are contractile, reparative mural cells, not the phagocytic cell of the sinusoid wall.

## answer_d
Endothelial cells

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sinusoidal endothelial cells themselves have some phagocytic capacity in certain organs, but the cell type specifically credited with phagocytosis in this context is the macrophage, not the endothelial cell as a class.
