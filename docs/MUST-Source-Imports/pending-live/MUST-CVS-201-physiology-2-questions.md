## title
Atrial receptor stimulation by high venous pressure (Bainbridge reflex)

## question
Stimulation of atrial receptors (volume receptors) by high central venous pressure leads to:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Vasoconstriction and bradycardia

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Vasoconstriction and bradycardia describe roughly the opposite of the atrial-receptor response to a high venous pressure.

## answer_b
Vasoconstriction and tachycardia

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Vasoconstriction is not the response of this reflex; the atrial-stretch reflex is a vasodilator, heart-rate-raising response.

## answer_c
Vasodilation and tachycardia

## explanation_c
Correct. When high central venous pressure distends the right atrium, stretch of receptors in its wall (including the Bainbridge reflex acting on the SA node) raises heart rate and produces vasodilation, together increasing cardiac output to help the heart cope with the extra venous inflow rather than let blood dam up behind it. This is a distinct, atrial-stretch-specific mechanism, not an extension of the pressure-lowering arterial baroreceptor reflex. The higher heart rate paired with the extra venous return also raises stroke volume.

## answer_d
Vasodilation and bradycardia

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Bradycardia is the opposite of what atrial stretch produces; the Bainbridge reflex raises, not lowers, heart rate.

## title
Edema mechanism with low plasma albumin

## question
A patient presents with generalized edema. Investigations reveal low plasma albumin levels. Which of the following mechanisms is the primary cause of this patient's edema?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Raised capillary hydrostatic pressure is a separate mechanism of oedema (as in venous obstruction or heart failure), not the one driven by low plasma albumin.

## answer_b
Lymphatic obstruction

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Lymphatic obstruction produces a distinct, high-protein, non-pitting pattern of oedema (as in elephantiasis), not the mechanism linked to low plasma albumin.

## answer_c
Decreased plasma colloidal osmotic pressure

## explanation_c
Correct. Low plasma albumin lowers plasma colloidal (oncotic) osmotic pressure, the force that normally holds fluid in the capillaries and draws it back in at the venous end. With this osmotic pull weakened, filtration at the capillary wall outpaces reabsorption and fluid accumulates in the interstitium as generalised oedema — the mechanism behind hypoalbuminaemic states such as nutritional deficiency, liver cirrhosis and nephrotic syndrome.

## answer_d
Increased capillary permeability

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Increased capillary permeability is the mechanism behind inflammatory oedema (from mediators such as histamine or kinins), not the mechanism specifically linked to low plasma albumin.

## title
Precapillary sphincter relaxation trigger

## question
The precapillary sphincters at the beginning of capillaries relax primarily in response to:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Increased sympathetic tone

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Increased sympathetic tone constricts, rather than relaxes, precapillary sphincters and arterioles.

## answer_b
Accumulation of metabolites

## explanation_b
Correct. Precapillary sphincters relax primarily in response to the local accumulation of vasodilator metabolites (such as CO2, H+ and adenosine) and hypoxia that build up when tissue blood flow falls short of metabolic demand. This is the metabolic mechanism of local blood-flow autoregulation and active hyperaemia: a fall in flow causes metabolite accumulation, which dilates the arterioles and precapillary sphincters, restoring flow toward the tissue's needs.

Decreased interstitial fluid osmotic pressure

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Interstitial fluid osmotic pressure is a minor term in the Starling balance for fluid exchange, not the trigger for precapillary sphincter tone.

## answer_d
Increased plasma colloidal osmotic pressure

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Plasma colloidal osmotic pressure governs fluid movement across the capillary wall, not the smooth-muscle tone of the precapillary sphincter itself.

## title
General causes of oedema

## question
Edema can be caused by:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_a
Correct. Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure is one of the four classic mechanisms of oedema. It arises from arteriolar dilation, venular constriction, raised venous pressure (from gravity, incompetent valves, venous obstruction or heart failure), or a raised total extracellular fluid volume. Any of these tips capillary filtration ahead of reabsorption and lymphatic drainage capacity, so fluid accumulates in the interstitium.

## answer_b
Increased concentration of plasma proteins

## explanation_b
Incorrect. A raised, not increased, plasma protein concentration would raise colloidal osmotic pressure and oppose filtration; oedema follows from decreased, not increased, plasma protein concentration.

## answer_c
Decreased capillary hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Decreased capillary hydrostatic pressure would favour reabsorption over filtration, the opposite of an oedema-producing change.

## answer_d
Decreased capillary permeability

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Decreased, not increased, capillary permeability would reduce fluid movement out of the vessel; increased permeability, not decreased, is an oedema-producing mechanism.

## title
Force absorbing fluid at the capillary venous end

## question
What is the primary force responsible for the absorption of fluid into the capillaries at the venous end?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Hydrostatic capillary pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Capillary hydrostatic pressure favours filtration (fluid moving out), not absorption; it falls along the capillary but never itself pulls fluid in.

## answer_b
Interstitial fluid osmotic pressure

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Interstitial fluid osmotic pressure is a minor term in the Starling balance and, where it acts, favours filtration rather than absorption.

## answer_c
Colloidal osmotic pressure of plasma proteins

## explanation_c
Correct. The colloidal (oncotic) osmotic pressure of plasma proteins, largely albumin, is the force that draws fluid back into the capillary. At the venous end, where capillary hydrostatic pressure has fallen well below its arteriolar-end value, this steady osmotic pull dominates the Starling balance and produces net absorption of fluid into the vessel.

## answer_d
Interstitial fluid hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Interstitial fluid hydrostatic pressure favours absorption only when it exceeds capillary pressure, which is not the primary mechanism this source names for venous-end absorption; the dominant force is plasma colloidal osmotic pressure.

## title
Force driving filtration at the capillary arterial end

## question
Filtration at the arterial end of capillary occurs mainly due to:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Hydrostatic pressure in capillaries

## explanation_a
Correct. At the arterial end of the capillary, capillary hydrostatic pressure is at its highest and exceeds the opposing plasma colloidal osmotic pressure, so the net Starling balance favours filtration — fluid moving out of the capillary into the interstitium. This hydrostatic pressure falls progressively toward the venous end, where the balance reverses toward absorption.

## answer_b
Hydrostatic pressure in interstitium

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Interstitial hydrostatic pressure opposes filtration (it pushes fluid back toward the capillary), and is a minor term compared with capillary hydrostatic pressure in driving arterial-end filtration.

## answer_c
Oncotic pressure in capillaries

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Capillary oncotic (colloidal osmotic) pressure opposes filtration by pulling fluid inward; it is not the force driving fluid out at the arterial end.

## answer_d
Oncotic pressure in interstitium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Interstitial oncotic pressure is usually very small and, where present, favours filtration only marginally; it is not the main driver named here, which is capillary hydrostatic pressure.

## title
Factors decreasing capillary filtration rate

## question
Which of the following tends to decrease capillary filtration rate?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure raises, not lowers, the outward filtration force and so increases filtration rate.

## answer_b
Decreased plasma colloid osmotic pressure

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Decreased plasma colloid osmotic pressure weakens the inward-pulling force that opposes filtration, so it increases, not decreases, net filtration.

## answer_c
Increased interstitial colloid osmotic pressure

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Increased interstitial colloid osmotic pressure would favour outward filtration further (drawing fluid into the interstitium), increasing rather than decreasing filtration rate.

## answer_d
Decreased capillary water permeability

## explanation_d
Correct. Capillary filtration rate depends on the capillary filtration coefficient — the product of the wall's water permeability and the surface area available for exchange — as well as the Starling pressure gradient. A fall in capillary water permeability lowers this coefficient directly, decreasing filtration rate independent of any change in the hydrostatic or osmotic pressures themselves.

## title
Mechanism of electrolyte exchange across capillaries

## question
Which of the following mechanisms is most important for the exchange of electrolytes across capillaries?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Diffusion

## explanation_a
Correct. Diffusion, driven by the concentration gradient and set by capillary permeability and molecular size, is quantitatively the most important mechanism of capillary exchange for small solutes such as electrolytes. Its rate depends on capillary permeability type, which increases across the sequence continuous (lowest) to fenestrated to discontinuous (highest) capillaries.

## answer_b
Bulk flow

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Bulk flow (the Starling-forces-driven filtration/reabsorption of fluid) moves water and small dissolved solutes together but is not the principal mechanism for electrolyte exchange specifically, which is dominated by diffusion.

## answer_c
Osmosis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Osmosis describes water movement driven by an osmotic gradient, not the primary mechanism by which electrolytes themselves cross the capillary wall.

## answer_d
Vesicular transport

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Vesicular transport carries large, lipid-insoluble molecules such as proteins across the endothelium, not small electrolytes, whose exchange is dominated by diffusion instead.

## title
What vesicular transport carries across capillaries

## question
Vesicular transport across the capillary wall is primarily concerned with the movement of:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Water and electrolytes

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Water and electrolytes cross mainly by diffusion (and bulk flow for water), not by vesicular transport.

## answer_b
Crystalloids

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Small crystalloid solutes diffuse readily through capillary pores; they do not require the vesicular route.

## answer_c
Large molecules like proteins

## explanation_c
Correct. Vesicular transport is a separate mechanism from diffusion that carries large, lipid-insoluble molecules — such as proteins, antibodies, cytokines and protein-bound hormones — across endothelial cells in membrane-bound vesicles. It supplies tissues with high-molecular-weight molecules that are too large to pass through ordinary capillary pores by diffusion.

## answer_d
Dissolved gases

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dissolved gases (O2, CO2) are small, lipid-soluble molecules that diffuse freely across the endothelium; they do not require vesicular transport.

## title
Pathophysiology of elephantiasis edema

## question
A patient with elephantiasis has massive edema in one leg. The underlying pathophysiology is:

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure produces a pitting, low-protein pattern of oedema (as in venous obstruction or heart failure), not the pattern seen in elephantiasis.

## answer_b
Decreased plasma colloidal osmotic pressure

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Decreased plasma colloidal osmotic pressure is the hypoalbuminaemic mechanism of oedema, unrelated to the lymphatic disease underlying elephantiasis.

## answer_c
Lymphatic obstruction

## explanation_c
Correct. Elephantiasis is the classic example of oedema from inadequate lymph flow. Chronic lymphatic obstruction, in this disease from filarial infection, causes oedema fluid with a high protein content rather than the low-protein fluid of filtration-driven oedema. In longstanding disease this produces non-pitting oedema with inflammation and fibrosis of the interstitial tissue, the massive, brawny swelling characteristic of elephantiasis.

## answer_d
Salt and water retention

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Generalised salt and water retention (as in renal or heart failure) produces a different, more generalised oedema pattern, not the localised, fibrotic, lymphatic-obstruction pattern of elephantiasis.

## title
Force decreasing from the capillary arterial to venous end

## question
Which force, tending to move fluid out of the capillary, decreases significantly from the arterial end (35 mmHg) to the venous end (15 mmHg)?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Interstitial fluid osmotic pressure

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Interstitial fluid osmotic pressure is usually very small and roughly constant along the capillary's length; it is not the outward force that falls sharply from arterial to venous end.

## answer_b
Colloidal osmotic pressure of plasma

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Colloidal osmotic pressure of plasma proteins is an inward-pulling (absorptive) force, not an outward-filtering one, and it stays comparatively steady along the capillary.

## answer_c
Hydrostatic capillary pressure

## explanation_c
Correct. Capillary hydrostatic pressure is the outward-filtering force that falls markedly along the length of the capillary — from about 35 mmHg at the arterial end to about 15 mmHg at the venous end — because of the resistance the blood encounters flowing through the vessel. This fall is what shifts the Starling balance from net filtration at the arterial end to net absorption at the venous end.

## answer_d
Interstitial fluid hydrostatic pressure

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Interstitial fluid hydrostatic pressure is an inward (absorptive) force, and is comparatively small and steady, not the outward force that falls from 35 to 15 mmHg along the capillary.

## title
Edema from increased capillary hydrostatic pressure

## question
Which of the following is a cause of edema related to increased capillary hydrostatic pressure?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Proteinuria

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Proteinuria causes oedema by lowering plasma colloidal osmotic pressure (as in nephrotic syndrome), not by raising capillary hydrostatic pressure.

## answer_b
Right-sided heart failure

## explanation_b
Correct. Right-sided heart failure raises systemic venous pressure as blood backs up behind the failing right ventricle. This raised venous pressure is transmitted upstream to raise systemic capillary hydrostatic pressure, producing the dependent, pitting peripheral oedema characteristic of right heart failure. It is one of the named causes of the raised-filtration-pressure mechanism of oedema, alongside venous obstruction and incompetent venous valves.

## answer_c
Filariasis

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Filariasis causes oedema through lymphatic obstruction (as in elephantiasis), not through raised capillary hydrostatic pressure.

## answer_d
Left-sided heart failure

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Left-sided heart failure raises pulmonary venous and capillary pressure, producing pulmonary oedema, rather than the systemic capillary hydrostatic-pressure rise this question asks about.
