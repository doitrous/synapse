## title
Artery in the anterior interventricular groove causing anterior-arm-radiating chest pain

## question
A 58-year-old male presents with chest pain radiating to his left arm. Angiography reveals a blockage in the artery running in the anterior interventricular groove. Which artery is most likely affected?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Right coronary artery

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The right coronary artery runs in the coronary (atrioventricular) sulcus, not the anterior interventricular groove.

## answer_b
Circumflex artery

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The circumflex artery runs in the coronary sulcus on the posterior/left surface of the heart, not the anterior interventricular groove.

## answer_c
Anterior interventricular branch

## explanation_c
Correct. The anterior interventricular branch (the left anterior descending artery) is the branch of the left coronary artery that runs in the anterior interventricular groove, and it is the most commonly occluded coronary vessel in clinical practice. It supplies the anterior wall of the left ventricle and the anterior two-thirds of the interventricular septum, so an angiographic blockage there fits classic anterior-territory ischaemia with pain radiating down the left arm. Because this territory is so large, occlusion here is often nicknamed the "widow-maker" lesion.

## answer_d
Right marginal artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The right marginal artery is a branch of the right coronary artery running along the sharp right border of the heart, not the anterior interventricular groove.

Vessel accompanying the anterior interventricular artery

## question
Which vessel accompanies the anterior interventricular artery in the anterior interventricular groove?

## subject
cvs

## status

## owner

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The coronary sinus lies in the posterior part of the coronary (atrioventricular) sulcus, not in the anterior interventricular groove.

## title
Branch of the left coronary artery along the rounded left margin

## question
Which branch of the left coronary artery runs along the rounded left margin of the heart?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Anterior interventricular branch

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The anterior interventricular branch runs in the anterior interventricular groove, not along the rounded left (obtuse) margin.

## answer_b
Left marginal branch

## explanation_b
Correct. The left marginal branch is given off by the circumflex artery — itself a branch of the left coronary artery — and runs along the rounded, obtuse left margin of the heart, supplying the left ventricle's lateral wall as it goes. Its course along this specific margin is exactly what distinguishes it from the anterior interventricular and septal branches, which run in the grooves rather than along the free margin. It is the left-sided counterpart of the right marginal branch given off by the right coronary artery along the heart's sharp right border.

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Septal branches are perforating branches that dive into the interventricular septum; they do not run along the heart's margin.

## answer_d
Conus artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The conus (infundibular) artery supplies the pulmonary conus/infundibulum near the origin of the pulmonary trunk, not the left margin.

## title
Vein opening directly into the right atrium

## question
Which of the following veins opens directly into the right atrium?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The great cardiac vein is a tributary of the coronary sinus; it does not open directly into the right atrium.

## answer_b
Middle cardiac vein

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The middle cardiac vein is also a tributary of the coronary sinus, reaching the right atrium indirectly through it.

## answer_c
Anterior cardiac veins

## explanation_c
Correct. The anterior cardiac veins, draining the anterior surface of the right ventricle, are the exception among the cardiac veins: they cross the coronary sulcus and open directly into the right atrium, bypassing the coronary sinus altogether. The distinction is a favourite exam point precisely because it is the exception to the "everything drains via the coronary sinus" rule.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The posterior vein of the left ventricle is a tributary of the coronary sinus, not a direct right-atrial opening.

## title
Territory NOT supplied by the left coronary artery

## question
Which of the following is NOT supplied by the left coronary artery?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Left ventricle

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The left ventricle (most of it) is classic left coronary artery territory, so this IS supplied by it — not the exception being asked for.

## answer_b
Anterior 2/3 of interventricular septum

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The anterior two-thirds of the interventricular septum is supplied by septal perforators from the anterior interventricular (left anterior descending) branch of the left coronary artery, so this IS supplied by it.

## explanation_c
Correct. The posterior one-third of the interventricular septum is supplied by the posterior interventricular artery, which is a branch of the right coronary artery in the majority of hearts (right-dominant circulation), not the left coronary artery. This division — left coronary artery for the anterior two-thirds, right coronary artery for the posterior one-third — is the standard teaching point for septal blood supply and is exactly why it is tested as the "NOT supplied" exception here. Recognising this split also explains why an inferior/posterior myocardial infarction (right coronary artery territory) can produce different conduction complications than an anterior one.

## answer_d
Left atrium

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The left atrium is supplied by the left coronary artery (via its circumflex branch), so this IS supplied by it.

The circumflex artery is a continuation of:

## subject

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The circumflex artery is not a branch or continuation of the right coronary artery.

At its origin, the left coronary artery runs a very short main stem before dividing into its two terminal branches: the anterior interventricular (left anterior descending) artery and the circumflex artery. ## title
Mechanism compensating for gradual coronary obstruction

## question
In a case of gradual coronary obstruction, which mechanism may help overcome ischemia?

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Venae cordis minimi

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Venae cordis minimi (Thebesian veins) are tiny venous channels draining directly into the cardiac chambers; they play no role in compensating for arterial obstruction.

## answer_b
Anastomosis between coronaries

## explanation_b
Correct. Anastomotic connections exist between branches of the right and left coronary arteries (and between branches of the same artery), and when an obstruction develops gradually rather than suddenly, these collateral channels have time to enlarge and take over perfusion of the threatened territory. This is why slowly progressive coronary disease can sometimes be tolerated with less myocardial damage than a sudden occlusion of the same vessel, which gives collaterals no time to develop. The principle underlies why chronic stable angina and acute myocardial infarction can have such different clinical courses despite similar degrees of luminal narrowing.

## answer_c
Direct venous drainage

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Venous drainage carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart; it cannot supply oxygenated blood to an ischaemic territory.

## answer_d
Sympathetic stimulation

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Sympathetic stimulation increases heart rate and contractility, raising myocardial oxygen demand — it does not improve collateral supply and would worsen, not relieve, ischaemia.
