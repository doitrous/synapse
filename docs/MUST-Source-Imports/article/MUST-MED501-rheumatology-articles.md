## title
Rheumatoid arthritis — serology, deformity, prognosis and drug therapy

## subject
msk

## topic
Musculoskeletal system

## summary
Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic symmetrical inflammatory synovitis. Its serology (rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP), its two named deformity mechanisms, its recognised variants, its poor-prognosis markers, and the split between symptom-control NSAIDs and disease-modifying therapy (DMARDs, biologics) are each tested from a different angle in the MUST-MED501 solved bank.

## sections
### Definition
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic, symmetrical, peripheral inflammatory synovitis. The ACR 1987 classification criteria are morning stiffness ≥1 hour, swelling of ≥3 joint areas, swelling of a PIP/MCP/wrist joint, subcutaneous nodules, a positive rheumatoid factor, symmetrical joint swelling, and hand/wrist radiographic erosion or peri-articular osteopenia — 4 or more of the seven, the first four lasting over 6 weeks, classifies RA.

### Mechanism
Serology: rheumatoid factor is used for follow-up and prognosis but is not RA-specific — it is also false-positive in SLE, scleroderma, polyarteritis nodosa, hepatitis, subacute bacterial endocarditis, and in relatives of RA patients. Anti-CCP (cyclic citrullinated peptide) antibody is specific for RA, unlike rheumatoid factor. Deformity: the characteristic hand deformities include ulnar deviation of the fingers and radial deviation of the wrist. Two named PIP/DIP deformity patterns occur: boutonniere (buttonhole) deformity is PIP flexion with DIP hyperextension, from central-slip extensor-tendon rupture; swan-neck deformity is the reverse pattern. Anaemia: RA anaemia can be normocytic (chronic-disease marrow suppression — the default, uncomplicated pattern), microcytic (NSAID-induced GI bleeding), megaloblastic (methotrexate-induced folate deficiency), or haemolytic (hypersplenism in Felty syndrome) — the mechanism named must match the case's own clinical detail. Drug mechanism: anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs, COX-2 inhibitors, small-dose steroids) control symptoms by inhibiting prostaglandin synthesis but do not modify the disease course, so they are not DMARDs. Disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (gold, D-penicillamine, sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide, methotrexate) act on the underlying disease. Biological response modifiers act faster still by targeting specific inflammatory cytokines directly: infliximab is a TNF-alpha inhibitor; kineret is an IL-1 receptor antagonist.

### Key determinants
Poor prognostic factors are female sex, a rapid/aggressive disease course, a strongly positive rheumatoid factor, anaemia within the first 3 months, and HLA-DR4 carriage. The recognised variants of RA are Felty syndrome (splenomegaly, pancytopenia, lymphadenopathy, vasculitis), Caplan syndrome (pneumoconiosis plus RA), and the juvenile forms (oligoarticular, polyarticular, Still's disease). RA has no congenital variant. Methotrexate is first-line among the disease-modifying drugs.

### Clinical significance
Anti-CCP's specificity (versus rheumatoid factor's non-specificity) makes it the better confirmatory serology test. Recognising which anaemia mechanism is in play changes management (stopping an NSAID or methotrexate versus treating the underlying inflammation). Knowing which listed feature is a true RA variant, and which drug class genuinely modifies disease course, are both recurrent exam angles on the same core disease.

### Common misconceptions
Treating rheumatoid factor as RA-specific instead of anti-CCP. Reversing the boutonniere/swan-neck joint pattern. Calling an NSAID a DMARD because it is used to treat RA. Assuming any RA anaemia is chronic-disease type by default.

## hold_these
Rheumatoid factor is non-specific (false-positive in SLE, scleroderma, PAN, hepatitis, SBE, RA relatives); anti-CCP is the specific RA antibody.
Boutonniere deformity is PIP flexion + DIP hyperextension; swan-neck is the reverse.
RA's true variants are Felty, Caplan and the juvenile forms — never congenital.
NSAIDs treat RA symptoms; only DMARDs and biologics modify the disease course.

## lose_the_mark
Treating rheumatoid factor as RA-specific instead of anti-CCP.
Reversing the boutonniere/swan-neck joint pattern.
Calling an NSAID a DMARD because it is used to treat RA.

# Item

## id
ART-MSK-MUST-MED501-GOUT-AND-PSEUDOGOUT

## title
Gout and pseudogout — presentation, investigation and drug timing

## subject
msk

## topic
Musculoskeletal system

## summary
Gout and pseudogout are the two crystal arthropathies the MUST-MED501 bank draws on repeatedly: gout's risk factors and precipitants, its two-tier investigation (crystal microscopy vs. serum urate), the timing rule that keeps allopurinol out of the acute attack, and pseudogout's calcium-pyrophosphate crystal as the discriminator from gout's urate.

## sections
### Definition
Gout is a disturbance of purine metabolism causing hyperuricaemia with deposition of monosodium urate crystals in the tissues. Pseudogout (chondrocalcinosis) is deposition of calcium pyrophosphate crystals in joints — a different crystal, a different disease.

### Mechanism
Gout is roughly ten times more common in men, classically presenting with sudden onset of a hot, painful, swollen joint (most often the first metatarsophalangeal joint), precipitated by excess meat/purine intake, alcohol, infection, exercise, trauma, or a thiazide diuretic. Joint aspiration examined by polarizing-light microscopy shows needle-shaped, negatively birefringent urate crystals in gout, versus rhomboid-shaped calcium pyrophosphate crystals in pseudogout. General measures remove the precipitating cause, including stopping a thiazide diuretic. The acute attack is treated with colchicine, NSAIDs or corticosteroids — not with a urate-lowering drug. Allopurinol (a xanthine oxidase inhibitor) is chronic/maintenance therapy: starting it mid-attack can precipitate or worsen acute gouty arthritis, so it is begun only about four weeks after the last attack, with colchicine cover.

### Key determinants
Serum uric acid is raised in gout (normal roughly 2-7 mg%) and is a supportive, not gold-standard, investigation next to crystal microscopy. Precipitants to remove: thiazide diuretics, excess alcohol, high meat/purine intake, infection, exercise, trauma. Pseudogout's calcium-pyrophosphate crystal (rhomboid) is the discriminator from gout's urate (needle-shaped).

### Clinical significance
Getting the investigation hierarchy right (crystal microscopy over uric acid) and the drug-timing rule right (allopurinol only after the attack settles) are the two angles this bank tests repeatedly, because both are places where a plausible-sounding but wrong answer is the trap.

### Common misconceptions
Naming allopurinol as acute-attack treatment. Naming serum uric acid as more diagnostic than joint-fluid crystal microscopy.

## hold_these
Acute gout: male, thiazide/purine-rich diet/alcohol as precipitants, sudden hot swollen joint (classically the great toe).
Crystal microscopy (needle-shaped, negatively birefringent = gout; rhomboid = pseudogout) is the gold-standard investigation; serum uric acid is supportive.
Acute management removes the precipitant and treats inflammation; allopurinol is started only after the attack has settled, never during it.

## lose_the_mark
Naming allopurinol as acute-attack treatment.
Naming serum uric acid as more diagnostic than joint-fluid crystal microscopy.

ART-MSK-MUST-MED501-SERONEGATIVE-SPONDYLOARTHROPATHIES

## title
Seronegative spondyloarthropathies — ankylosing spondylitis, HLA-B27 and psoriatic arthritis

## subject
msk

## topic
Musculoskeletal system

## summary
Ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis (Reiter's), psoriatic arthropathy and IBD-associated arthritis form the HLA-B27-linked "seronegative" family — rheumatoid-factor-negative, axial/enthesitis-predominant diseases that are frequently confused with each other and with unrelated connective-tissue disease on exam.

## sections
### Definition
Ankylosing spondylitis (AS) is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the axial skeleton and sacroiliac joints. It belongs, with reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthropathy and IBD-associated arthritis, to the seronegative spondyloarthropathies — all HLA-B27-associated (95% in AS, 80% in reactive arthritis), rheumatoid-factor-negative.

### Mechanism
AS typically presents in men aged 15-40 (3:1) with insidious low back pain and morning stiffness that improves with activity and worsens with rest — the opposite pattern to mechanical back pain. Psoriatic arthropathy has five patterns; asymmetrical oligoarticular disease is commonest (>50%), but a distinct pattern predominantly involves the distal interphalangeal (DIP) joints (~10%), associated with nail ridging and hyperkeratosis — unlike rheumatoid arthritis, which spares the DIP joints.

### Key determinants
Behçet syndrome is seronegative and inflammatory but is linked to HLA-B51, not B27, and is not part of this family. Reiter's syndrome/reactive arthritis is a member of this seronegative family, not a feature or association of scleroderma (systemic sclerosis), which is an unrelated connective-tissue disease with its own distinct manifestations (Raynaud's, skin fibrosis, oesophageal dysmotility).

### Clinical significance
The movement-response direction (inflammatory back pain improves with activity) is the single discriminator most likely to be tested against mechanical back pain, and the family membership (who is HLA-B27-linked, who is not; who is seronegative-arthritis, who is unrelated connective-tissue disease) is the other recurrent angle.

### Common misconceptions
Naming Behçet syndrome as HLA-B27-associated. Filing Reiter's syndrome under scleroderma instead of the seronegative-arthritis family.

## hold_these
AS: young male, inflammatory (activity-improved) back pain, HLA-B27 in ~95%.
The HLA-B27 family: ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis (Reiter's), psoriatic arthropathy, IBD-associated arthritis — not Behçet syndrome (HLA-B51).
Psoriatic DIP-predominant pattern + nail changes; RA characteristically spares the DIP joint.

## lose_the_mark
Naming Behçet syndrome as HLA-B27-associated.
Filing Reiter's syndrome under scleroderma instead of the seronegative-arthritis family.

## id
ART-MSK-MUST-MED501-OA-SEPTIC-CTD

## title
Osteoarthritis, septic arthritis and connective tissue disease — the exam's exclusion questions

## subject
msk

## topic
Musculoskeletal system

## summary
A cluster of MUST-MED501 items each turn on naming the one thing that does NOT belong: Bouchard's vs. Heberden's nodes, blood transfusion in septic-arthritis management, and beta-blockers in Raynaud's phenomenon — plus osteoarthritis's own epidemiology and CREST syndrome's comparatively good prognosis.

## sections
### Definition
Osteoarthritis is a degenerative (not inflammatory) joint disease; septic arthritis is a bacterial joint infection needing urgent drainage and antibiotics; limited scleroderma (CREST syndrome) is calcinosis, Raynaud's phenomenon, oesophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly and telangiectasia — the better-prognosis, skin-and-peripheral-vessel-predominant form of systemic sclerosis.

### Mechanism
Osteoarthritis is the most common form of arthritis, degenerative rather than inflammatory, presenting from about 45 and usually over 60. Its nodal generalised form is markedly more common in postmenopausal women. Bouchard's nodes are the PIP-joint counterpart of Heberden's nodes at the DIP joint. A new acute monoarthritis needs joint aspiration first, primarily to exclude septic arthritis — the differential that cannot wait — before a crystal or degenerative diagnosis is assumed. Septic arthritis management is joint drainage/aspiration plus systemic antibiotics; blood transfusion has no role in treating the joint infection itself. Limited scleroderma (CREST syndrome — calcinosis, Raynaud's, oesophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia) carries a better prognosis than diffuse systemic sclerosis, at roughly 70% ten-year survival, because it specifically spares the severe early internal-organ involvement of diffuse disease. Raynaud's phenomenon is treated with vasodilators (nifedipine); beta-blockers are avoided because their vasoconstrictor action can precipitate or worsen attacks.

### Key determinants
Heberden's = DIP, Bouchard's = PIP. Acute monoarthritis: aspirate first, to exclude septic arthritis. Septic arthritis management is drainage + antibiotics, not blood transfusion. CREST/limited scleroderma: ~70% ten-year survival, better than diffuse disease. Raynaud's: avoid beta-blockers, use vasodilators.

### Clinical significance
Each of these facts is tested as an exclusion question — which listed option is NOT part of management, NOT the right eponym, NOT the right drug choice — so the objective is recognising the one wrong option as much as knowing the right one.

### Common misconceptions
Swapping Heberden's and Bouchard's. Choosing an imaging study over aspiration in a new acute monoarthritis. Treating beta-blockers as a neutral antihypertensive choice in a Raynaud's patient.

## hold_these
Heberden's = DIP, Bouchard's = PIP.
Acute monoarthritis: aspirate first, to exclude septic arthritis.
Septic arthritis management is drainage + antibiotics, not blood transfusion.
CREST/limited scleroderma: ~70% ten-year survival, better than diffuse disease.
Raynaud's: avoid beta-blockers, use vasodilators.

## lose_the_mark
Swapping Heberden's and Bouchard's.
Choosing an imaging study over aspiration in a new acute monoarthritis.
Treating beta-blockers as a neutral antihypertensive choice in a Raynaud's patient.
