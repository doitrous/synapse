## summary
How cells respond to injury, how the body responds to them, and how that sequence ends in scar or in cancer. The map for the nine articles under it.
## sections
### Definition
General pathology is the study of what goes wrong at the level of cells and tissues, independent of which organ it happens in. It is what makes the systems that follow intelligible: the same handful of processes recur in every one of them.

The topic runs in one sequence — injury, death, response, repair, and the failure of control that is neoplasia — and each article below takes one step of it.
### Mechanism
A cell meeting a stress first adapts. Adaptation successfully maintains homeostasis by reversible responses that can alter cell function and structure. Past what adaptation can accommodate the cell is injured, and past that it dies.

Death comes in two forms. Necrosis is often coined as accidental death, as it is generally seen as not controlled. Apoptosis is the regulated alternative, and the contrast between them runs through the rest of pathology.

Death provokes a response. Acute inflammation initiates following a specific injury, triggering the release of soluble mediators. Where the stimulus persists it becomes chronic: chronic inflammation is also referred to as slow, long-term inflammation lasting for prolonged periods.
### Key determinants
Two things decide where a tissue ends up. Whether the injurious stimulus is removed, and whether the tissue can regenerate. If both are favourable the tissue returns to normal; if either fails, the outcome is scar or persistent inflammation.

The repair process is the same either way. The proliferative phase is characterised by the formation of granulation tissue, reepithelialisation, and neovascularisation — what differs is whether that tissue is replaced by the original cell type or left as fibrous scar.
### Clinical significance
Neoplasia is the failure of the controls that govern all of the above. Cancer is a disease in which some of the body’s cells grow uncontrollably and spread to other parts of the body. The process by which cancer cells spread to other parts of the body is called metastasis.

Two measurements follow from that, and they answer different questions. Tumour grade describes how normal or abnormal cancer cells look under a microscope. Stage refers to the extent of the cancer, such as how large the tumour is and if it has spread.
### Common misconceptions
Reading general pathology as a list of nine separate topics. It is one sequence, and an article read out of order will seem arbitrary — inflammation makes little sense before cell death, and repair makes none before inflammation.

## summary
Two questions in opposite directions — what the body does to the drug, and what the drug does to the body — and the margin between benefit and harm that both feed into.
## sections
### Definition
General pharmacology asks two questions about every drug, and keeping them apart is most of the subject. Pharmacokinetics is the study of how the body interacts with administered substances for the entire duration of exposure. Pharmacodynamics studies a drug's molecular, biochemical, and physiologic effects or actions.

Stated plainly, it is this. Pharmacodynamics studies the action of the drug on the organism, and pharmacokinetics studies the actions of the organism on the drug.
### Mechanism
Pharmacokinetics generally examines four main parameters: absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion. Together they determine how much drug reaches the site of action and for how long it stays there.

Pharmacodynamics takes over at that point. Given a concentration at the target, it asks what effect follows — which receptor is occupied, what happens downstream, and how the effect changes as concentration rises.
### Key determinants
The two meet at the dose. A dose is chosen to put a concentration inside a range, and that range is defined from both sides: The therapeutic index is a quantitative measurement of the relative safety of a drug. Between the minimally effective dose and the toxic dose lies the therapeutic window.

When a patient's handling of a drug changes — through organ impairment, age, or another drug — it is almost always the kinetics that moved, and the window that was crossed as a result.
### Clinical significance
Two of the commonest clinical problems in prescribing follow directly. An adverse drug reaction is a response to a drug which is noxious and unintended. And drug interactions are reactions between a drug and other drugs, foods, beverages, supplements.

Both are usually explicable in the terms above: an interaction that changes metabolism changes the kinetics, and an adverse reaction is often the pharmacodynamics of a concentration that went outside the window.
### Common misconceptions
Treating pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics as two unrelated syllabus topics. They are two halves of one question about dose, and neither predicts an effect on its own.
## hold_these
Pharmacokinetics is the study of how the body interacts with administered substances for the entire duration of exposure.
Pharmacodynamics studies the action of the drug on the organism, and pharmacokinetics studies the actions of the organism on the drug.
Pharmacokinetics generally examines four main parameters: absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion.
The therapeutic index is a quantitative measurement of the relative safety of a drug.
An adverse drug reaction is a response to a drug which is noxious and unintended.
## lose_the_mark
Treating kinetics and dynamics as unrelated topics. They are two halves of one question about dose.
Assuming a changed effect means changed pharmacodynamics. Far more often the kinetics moved and the concentration left the window.

## summary
Four kinds of organism, one chain from arrival to disease, and the laboratory and decontamination methods that break it. The map for the eleven articles under it.
## sections
### Definition
General microbiology covers the organisms that cause human disease and the methods used to find and control them. It is organised in three parts, and the eleven articles beneath this one follow that order: what the organisms are, how they cause disease, and how they are detected and stopped.
### Mechanism
The organisms divide into four groups by what kind of cell they are. Bacteria are organisms that have only one cell. Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites containing either an RNA or a DNA genome surrounded by a protein coat. Fungi are eukaryotic microorganisms, some of which cause superficial, cutaneous, subcutaneous or systemic disease. A parasite is a pathogen that simultaneously injures and derives sustenance from its host.

That division is not taxonomy for its own sake. It predicts which drugs can work, because every antimicrobial exploits something the organism has and the patient does not.
### Key determinants
Causing disease takes two further steps beyond arriving. Colonization is the presence of an organism on a non-sterile body surface, such as skin or mucosa, in the absence of disease. Presence alone is therefore not disease. What converts it is the organism's capacity to do harm. Virulence is the measure of the pathogenicity of an organism.

Before either, the organism has to arrive. Direct contact transmission occurs when microorganisms pass from one person to another without a contaminated intermediate, and five other routes cover the rest.
### Clinical significance
The laboratory answers two questions: what is it, and what will treat it. The cause of an infection is often confirmed by isolating and culturing the microorganism, either in artificial media or in a living host. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing is a laboratory procedure that identifies which antimicrobial regimen is effective for an individual patient.

Control works on the transmission step instead. Sterilization is the chemical or physical destruction or removal of all pathogenic microorganisms and their spores from a surface. That is what closes the route equipment and surfaces provide.
### Common misconceptions
Reading the four groups as a classification exercise. The group an organism belongs to determines which drug classes can possibly work against it, which is why it is the first thing established and not a preliminary.
## hold_these
Bacteria are organisms that have only one cell.
Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites containing either an RNA or a DNA genome surrounded by a protein coat.
Colonization is the presence of an organism on a non-sterile body surface, such as skin or mucosa, in the absence of disease.
Virulence is the measure of the pathogenicity of an organism.
The cause of an infection is often confirmed by isolating and culturing the microorganism, either in artificial media or in a living host.
Sterilization is the chemical or physical destruction or removal of all pathogenic microorganisms and their spores from a surface.
## lose_the_mark
Reading the four groups as taxonomy. The group decides which drug classes can work at all.
Treating presence of an organism as disease. Colonisation is presence without disease, and it is the normal state at many sites.
