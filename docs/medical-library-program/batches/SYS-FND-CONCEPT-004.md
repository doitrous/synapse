## label
Mendelian inheritance
## id
med.concept.mendelian-inheritance
## canonical_key
mendelian-inheritance
## aliases
Single-gene inheritance | Monogenic inheritance
## arabic_label
الوراثة المندلية
## arabic_aliases
وراثة الجين الواحد
## definition
The transmission of a trait determined by a single gene, following the dominant and recessive patterns Mendel described.
## explicit_objective
Given a pedigree, say which Mendelian pattern it fits, and state the recurrence risk that pattern predicts for the next child.
## pitfalls
Reading an unaffected parent as a non-carrier. In a recessive condition both parents are usually unaffected carriers, and the risk to each pregnancy is one in four.

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A condition caused by a change in the number or structure of chromosomes rather than by a variant within a single gene.
## explicit_objective
Distinguish a numerical from a structural chromosomal abnormality, and state why aneuploidy usually affects many body systems at once.
## pitfalls
Expecting a single-gene inheritance pattern. Most aneuploidies arise from a new non-disjunction event and do not recur in a Mendelian pattern.

## aliases

## arabic_label
الوراثة الميتوكوندرية
## arabic_aliases

## definition
Transmission of variants in the mitochondrial genome, which passes from mother to all her children and from father to none.
## explicit_objective
Recognise the pattern in a pedigree and explain why it follows from where the embryo gets its mitochondria.
## pitfalls
Applying it to a father. An affected man transmits the variant to none of his children, which is the feature that separates this pattern from every autosomal one.
