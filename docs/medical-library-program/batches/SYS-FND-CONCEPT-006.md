Dose–response relationship
## id
med.concept.dose-response
## canonical_key
dose-response
## aliases

## arabic_label
العلاقة بين الجرعة والاستجابة
## arabic_aliases
منحنى الجرعة والاستجابة
## definition
The relationship between the amount of drug given and the size of the effect produced, and the window between the least effective and the toxic dose.
## explicit_objective
Read potency and efficacy off a dose–response curve, and say which of the two a competitive antagonist changes.
## pitfalls
Reading a more potent drug as a better one. Potency is where the curve sits on the dose axis; efficacy is how high it reaches, and efficacy is what usually matters clinically.

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A quantitative measure of a drug’s relative safety, comparing the dose that produces the wanted effect with the dose that produces toxicity.
## explicit_objective
Say what a narrow therapeutic index implies for monitoring, and name what else must be considered before calling a drug safe.
## pitfalls
Reading a high index as safe in every patient. The index is a population measure and says nothing about an individual whose handling of the drug is altered.

## arabic_aliases

## definition
A noxious and unintended response to a drug occurring at doses normally used for prophylaxis, diagnosis or therapy.
## explicit_objective
Distinguish a dose-related reaction from an idiosyncratic one, and say which is predictable from the drug’s known action.

Treating every unwanted effect as an allergy. Most adverse reactions are exaggerations of the drug’s known action and are dose-related, not immunological.

A reaction between a drug and another drug, a food, a beverage, a supplement or a disease, which changes what the drug does.
