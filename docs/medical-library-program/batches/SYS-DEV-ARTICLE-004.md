## summary
The same logic as in childhood, applied to people who feel well and have decades of repeat testing ahead of them.
## sections
### Definition
A health screening test is a medical test or procedure performed on members of an asymptomatic population or population subgroup to assess their likelihood of having a particular disease. Nothing in that definition is specific to age; what changes in adults is which diseases have a usable asymptomatic period.

One important mathematical distinction is that a screen results in a risk value, as opposed to a diagnostic test that reports a value in comparison to a reference range.
### Mechanism
A screening programme sorts a large well population into a small group needing a confirmatory test. Because the group screened is mostly free of the disease, most positives are false ones — an arithmetic property of screening rather than a fault in the test.

That is why the confirmatory step belongs to the programme rather than following it as an afterthought.
### Key determinants
The disease must have an asymptomatic period during which treatment reduces morbidity and mortality significantly more than waiting until symptoms develop. In adults this is the condition deciding which cancers and chronic diseases are worth screening for.

Where earlier detection does not change what happens, screening converts well people into patients and achieves nothing else.
### Clinical significance
Adults are screened over decades, so harms accumulate alongside benefits: repeat testing, false positives, and the investigation each one triggers.

That is why screening is organised as a programme with an age range and an interval, rather than offered to whoever asks.
### Common misconceptions
Reading a positive screen as a diagnosis. It is a risk value and the confirmatory test still follows.

Assuming more screening is better. The condition on the asymptomatic period is not met by every disease.

## summary
The clearest case of primary prevention, and the only one whose benefit reaches people who never received it.
## sections
### Definition
Immunization is the process of becoming protected against a disease. Vaccination is how that protection is usually produced deliberately.

It is primary prevention in its cleanest form. Primary prevention aims to reduce the risk of disease before it begins by targeting modifiable risk factors in individuals who are still healthy. Here the risk factor removed is susceptibility itself.
### Mechanism
A vaccine produces immunity without the disease that would otherwise produce it, so the individual is protected on exposure.

Beyond the individual, the effect is collective. Herd immunity, also known as population immunity, is the indirect protection from an infectious disease that happens when a population is immune either through vaccination or immunity developed through previous infection. Enough immune people interrupt transmission, and the interruption protects everyone.
### Key determinants
Indirect protection depends on coverage across a population rather than on any one person's status, which is why a programme is measured by uptake and not by doses available.

It also decides who benefits most: those who cannot be vaccinated are protected only by the immunity of everyone around them.
### Clinical significance
Because the benefit is partly collective, a conversation about vaccination is also a public health act, and hesitancy has consequences beyond the person hesitating.

And because this is primary prevention, its success is invisible: what it produces is disease that does not happen.
### Common misconceptions
Judging vaccination only by the individual protected. The indirect protection reaches people who were never vaccinated.

Reading falling disease rates as evidence that vaccination is no longer needed. The rates are low because of it.

## summary
A structured conversation short enough to happen at a routine visit — which is the only reason it happens at all.
## sections
### Definition
Counselling in preventive care is a structured conversation aimed at changing a behaviour that raises the risk of disease. It is prevention delivered by talking. Primary prevention aims to reduce the risk of disease before it begins by targeting modifiable risk factors in individuals who are still healthy.

Structure is what separates it from advice. The five major steps of the intervention are as follows: Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, and Arrange.
### Mechanism
Each step does something the next depends on. Ask establishes the behaviour. Advise states a clear recommendation. Assess finds willingness. Assist supports the attempt. Arrange sets follow-up.

The sequence is what keeps it brief: without Assess, effort goes into helping someone who is not ready, and without Arrange the encounter has no second half.
### Key determinants
Whether the behaviour is raised at all is the largest determinant, and it usually falls to the clinician, because a patient who is not asked rarely volunteers.

Willingness, established at Assess, decides the shape of everything after it.
### Clinical significance
The intervention is designed to be short, which lets it happen at an ordinary appointment rather than requiring referral. Its value comes from frequency rather than depth.

Because it targets modifiable risk factors in people who are still well, it is prevention in the same sense as immunisation, by a different route.
### Common misconceptions
Waiting for the patient to raise the behaviour. Ask is the first step for a reason.

Skipping to Assist. Without Assess, the help offered may not be help the person can use.
## hold_these

Jumping to Assist without Assess. Willingness decides what help is worth offering.

## summary
What to do for someone with nothing wrong: find disease that is silent, act before disease arises, and make the encounter one they can use.
## sections
### Definition
Care of the well patient is the work done for someone who has not presented with a complaint. Its logic is prevention. Primary prevention aims to reduce the risk of disease before it begins by targeting modifiable risk factors in individuals who are still healthy.

It has three strands, and the articles beneath this hub follow them: finding disease that is present but silent, acting before disease arises, and arranging the encounter so the person can take part in it.
### Mechanism
Finding silent disease is screening. A health screening test is a medical test or procedure performed on members of an asymptomatic population or population subgroup to assess their likelihood of having a particular disease.

Acting before disease is immunisation and counselling. Immunization is the process of becoming protected against a disease. The five major steps of the intervention are as follows: Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, and Arrange.
### Key determinants
What any of this achieves depends on whether the person can use it. Health literacy represents the personal knowledge and competencies that accumulate through daily activities, social interactions and across generations. A plan not understood is not a plan.

Where the barrier is disability the duty is specific. Reasonable accommodation means necessary and appropriate modification and adjustments not imposing a disproportionate or undue burden, where needed in a particular case, to ensure to persons with disabilities the enjoyment or exercise on an equal basis with others of all human rights and fundamental freedoms.
### Clinical significance
The well patient consultation is where most preventive medicine actually happens, and it is the only encounter with people who are not yet ill.

Its effects are invisible by construction. Screening that finds nothing, a vaccine that prevents an infection and a behaviour that changes all produce non-events, which is why the work is easy to undervalue and easy to omit.
### Common misconceptions
Treating a well patient's visit as having no clinical content. It carries the whole of primary and secondary prevention.

Reading prevention as advice. Immunisation, screening and adjustments to access are all prevention and none is a conversation.
