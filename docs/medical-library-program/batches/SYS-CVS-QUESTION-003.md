# Item

## title
The first branches of the aorta

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
The ascending aorta is traced from the aortic valve to the beginning of the arch.

## question
Which vessels arise from it?

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
The brachiocephalic trunk and the left common carotid artery

## explanation_a
These arise from the arch, not the ascending aorta. Students choose this because they are the first branches they meet when learning the arch, and the two segments are easy to merge into one.

## answer_b
The right and left coronary arteries

## explanation_b
The ascending aorta gives only the two coronary arteries. That they are the very first branches of the systemic circulation is the point worth holding: the heart is perfused before any other organ.

## answer_c
The coronary arteries and the bronchial arteries

## explanation_c
The bronchial arteries arise much further downstream, from the descending thoracic aorta. This catches students who group all the small thoracic branches together without separating them by aortic segment.

## answer_d
No branches — the ascending aorta is a conducting segment only

## explanation_d
This is a reasonable-sounding generalisation from the arch and descending aorta being where the named branches are, and it is wrong in the one place it matters most.

# Item

## title
When the left ventricle is actually perfused

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
The coronary arteries arise immediately above the aortic valve, where pressure is highest during ventricular ejection. Yet left ventricular myocardial perfusion is greatest at a different moment.

## question
When does most left ventricular coronary flow occur, and why?

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
During systole, because aortic pressure is at its peak and drives flow into the ostia

## explanation_a
Driving pressure is highest in systole, which is exactly why this is the intuitive answer. It ignores the other side of the equation: the vessels running through contracting muscle are squeezed shut, and resistance rises faster than pressure does.

## answer_b
During systole, because the aortic valve cusps are pushed away from the ostia

## explanation_b
The cusps do move clear of the ostia in systole, so this half is sound. But unobstructed ostia do not help when the intramyocardial vessels beyond them are compressed. Students choose it by reasoning about the entrance rather than the bed.

## answer_c
Evenly through the cycle, because the aortic root pressure never falls to zero

## explanation_c
Aortic pressure indeed stays well above zero throughout, but coronary flow is markedly phasic — far from even. This is chosen by students who treat a continuous driving pressure as implying continuous flow.

## answer_d
During diastole, because the myocardium relaxes and stops compressing its own vessels

## explanation_d
Left ventricular coronary flow is largely diastolic. In systole the contracting wall compresses the vessels running through it and flow falls sharply; in diastole the muscle relaxes, resistance drops, and the still-high aortic pressure drives perfusion. This is why anything that shortens diastole disproportionately threatens the left ventricle.

## title
Course of the anterior interventricular artery

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
A diagram of the sternocostal surface shows a vessel descending in the groove between the two ventricles, accompanied by a vein, and continuing around the inferior border a short distance to the right of the apex.

## question
Which vessel is that, and where does it arise?

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The anterior interventricular artery, from the left coronary artery

## explanation_a
The left coronary artery divides into the anterior interventricular artery and the circumflex artery. The anterior interventricular branch descends in the anterior interventricular groove with the great cardiac vein and rounds the inferior border just to the right of the apex, which is why it supplies territory on both sides of the apex.

## answer_b
The anterior interventricular artery, from the right coronary artery

## explanation_b
The vessel is right and the parent is wrong. This is chosen by students who see the artery running down the front of the heart, note that the right chambers are anterior, and infer a right coronary origin from position rather than from the branching pattern.

## answer_c
The right marginal artery, from the right coronary artery

## explanation_c
The right marginal artery runs along the inferior border towards the apex, not down the interventricular groove. It is a genuine right coronary branch in roughly the right region, which makes it the strongest distractor.

## answer_d
The circumflex artery, from the left coronary artery

## explanation_d
The circumflex is the other division of the left coronary but runs in the coronary sulcus around the left border, not in the interventricular groove. Choosing it means the parent vessel is right and the groove is wrong.

## title
Territory of the anterior interventricular artery

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
Occlusion of the anterior interventricular artery close to its origin is the single most damaging coronary lesion in most hearts.

## question
Which combination of territories explains that?

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The right ventricle and the posterior third of the interventricular septum

## explanation_a
This is the territory of the right coronary artery and its posterior interventricular branch. Students choose it by attaching "interventricular" to whichever interventricular artery they thought of first.

## answer_b
The left atrium and the posterior wall of the left ventricle

## explanation_b
This is circumflex territory. It is chosen by students who know the lesion is left-sided but have not separated the two divisions of the left coronary artery.

## answer_c
The anterior wall of the left ventricle, the anterior part of the interventricular septum, and the apex

## explanation_c
The anterior interventricular artery supplies the anterior left ventricular wall, most of the anterior interventricular septum, and the apex — a large mass of the chamber that does the systemic work, plus the septum that carries the bundle branches. Losing all three at once is why a proximal occlusion here is so consequential.

The sinoatrial node and the right atrium

## explanation_d
The sinoatrial nodal artery arises from the right coronary artery in most hearts, and the right atrium is right coronary territory. This catches students who associate any severe cardiac event with the conducting system rather than with contractile mass.

Course of the circumflex artery

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
One division of the left coronary artery leaves the anterior surface almost at once and disappears round the left border of the heart.

## question
Where does it run and how does it end?

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
In the anterior interventricular groove, ending at the apex

## explanation_a
This is the anterior interventricular artery's course. Students choose it by taking the more familiar of the two left coronary divisions.

## answer_b
In the coronary sulcus round the left border to the posterior surface, ending by anastomosing with the right coronary artery

## explanation_b
The circumflex artery travels in the coronary sulcus — anteriorly, then round the left border, then posteriorly — and ends by anastomosing with the right coronary artery in the posterior part of the same sulcus. That end-to-end meeting in one continuous groove is what makes the coronary sulcus a ring around the heart.

## answer_c
In the coronary sulcus round the left border, ending by becoming the anterior interventricular artery

## explanation_c
This makes the two divisions of the left coronary artery into one continuous vessel, when they diverge from a common short stem. It is chosen by students who have not registered that the left coronary artery bifurcates almost immediately.

## answer_d
Down the posterior interventricular groove, ending at the crux of the heart

## explanation_d
The posterior interventricular groove carries the posterior interventricular artery, which in most hearts is a right coronary branch. Selecting it confuses a sulcus that encircles with one that descends.

# Item

## title
What the coronary sulcus contains

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
The coronary sulcus encircles the heart between the atria and the ventricles, and is occupied along its whole length by vessels.

## question
What does the sulcus mark, and what runs in it?

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
The atrioventricular junction, carrying the circumflex and right coronary arteries and the coronary sinus

## explanation_a
The sulcus is the surface expression of the atrioventricular junction — the plane of the fibrous skeleton. The circumflex artery occupies its left part, the right coronary artery its right part, and the coronary sinus its posterior part. Vessels lie in the groove precisely because nothing contractile crosses that plane.

## answer_b
The boundary between the two ventricles, carrying the interventricular arteries

## explanation_b
That is the interventricular groove, anterior and posterior. This is the commonest confusion, and the way to keep them apart is by direction: one encircles, the other descends.

## answer_c
The line of the interatrial septum, carrying the atrial branches

## explanation_c
The interatrial septum is internal and has no corresponding surface groove carrying named vessels. Students choose this by pattern-matching "septum to groove" without asking which septa show on the surface.

## answer_d
The attachment of the fibrous pericardium, carrying the phrenic nerves

## explanation_d
The phrenic nerves run on the lateral surface of the pericardium, outside the heart altogether, and the fibrous pericardium attaches at the great vessels and the diaphragm. This catches students who have not separated structures on the heart from structures on its covering.

# Item

## title
A balanced coronary pattern

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
In some hearts the posterior interventricular groove is supplied by a branch from each coronary artery rather than by one alone.

## question
What is this arrangement called?

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Right dominance

## explanation_a
Right dominance means the posterior interventricular artery comes from the right coronary artery alone, which is the commonest single pattern. Students choose it because it is the arrangement they learnt as standard.

## answer_b
Left dominance

## explanation_b
Left dominance means the posterior interventricular artery arises from the circumflex. It is the least common of the three and is chosen here by students who know dominance has a left variant without holding what balanced means.

## answer_c
A balanced pattern

## explanation_c
A balanced pattern has two posterior interventricular branches, one from each coronary artery. The territory that is usually the preserve of one vessel is shared, which is why the term sits alongside right and left dominance rather than inside either.

## answer_d
Collateral circulation

## explanation_d
Collaterals are anastomotic channels that enlarge in response to disease, not a normal branching variant. This is the most instructive error: it confuses a developmental pattern present from the start with an acquired response to narrowing.

# Item

## title
What "dominance" actually names

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
Coronary dominance is described in every report of a coronary angiogram, yet it says nothing about which artery is larger or carries more blood.

## question
What does the term define?

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Which coronary artery has the larger calibre at its origin

## explanation_a
The left coronary artery is usually the larger vessel and supplies the greater mass of myocardium, in most hearts that are nonetheless called right dominant. That mismatch is exactly why calibre cannot be the definition, and why the word misleads.

## answer_b
Which coronary artery gives rise to the posterior interventricular artery

## explanation_b
Dominance is defined solely by the origin of the posterior interventricular artery — right coronary in most hearts, circumflex in a minority, and one from each in a balanced pattern. It is a statement about a single branch, not about size or supply.

## answer_c
Which coronary artery supplies the sinoatrial node

## explanation_c
The sinoatrial nodal artery does arise from the right coronary artery in most hearts, so this pairs a real variation with the wrong term. Students choose it by attaching "dominance" to whichever coronary variation they remember best.

## answer_d
Which coronary artery supplies the greater mass of left ventricle

## explanation_d
This is what the word sounds like it should mean, and it is the reason the term is confusing. The left coronary artery supplies most of the left ventricle in nearly every heart, which would make almost all hearts left dominant if this were the definition.

# Item

## title
Artery and vein sharing the coronary sulcus

## subject
cvs

## status
Draft

## vignette
On the posterior surface of the heart the circumflex artery and the coronary sinus lie together in the coronary sulcus.

## question
How do they relate as they run?

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
They run in opposite directions, as artery and vein normally do

## explanation_a
Elsewhere in the body an artery and its companion vein do carry blood in opposite directions, which is exactly why this feels right. In the coronary sulcus the arrangement is different, and noticing that is the point of the item.

## answer_b
They cross at right angles at the crux of the heart

## explanation_b
Nothing crosses at right angles here; both structures follow the same curved groove. Students choose this by importing the idea of a crossing point from the crux, which is a meeting of grooves rather than of these two vessels.

## answer_c
The coronary sinus lies inside the circumflex artery's sheath

## explanation_c
No such shared sheath exists. This is chosen by students who over-apply the neurovascular bundle model to a region that does not use it.

## answer_d
They run together left to right, with blood flowing in the same general direction

## explanation_d
The coronary sinus runs left to right in the posterior coronary sulcus alongside the circumflex artery, and blood in both moves in the same general direction. It is worth registering as an exception, because it breaks the habit of assuming companion vessels always run counter to one another.

## vignette
Many arteries in the body can be occluded with little consequence because neighbouring vessels take over. Coronary occlusion usually cannot be tolerated this way.

## question

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
The coronary arteries carry blood at a much higher pressure than other arteries of similar size

## explanation_a
Coronary perfusion pressure is aortic pressure, which is high, but so is pressure in every other systemic artery of similar calibre. Pressure does not distinguish the coronary bed.

## answer_b
Cardiac muscle cannot use anaerobic metabolism at all

## explanation_d
The myocardium is densely capillarised, and diffusion from the chamber supplies only the thinnest inner layer. This catches students who over-apply the venae cordis minimae into a whole alternative supply.
