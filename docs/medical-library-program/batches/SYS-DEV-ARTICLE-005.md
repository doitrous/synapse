## summary
Four behaviours, acting over decades, behind most of the disease that kills adults. All four are modifiable and none is purely a personal choice.
## sections
### Definition
Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs), also known as chronic diseases, tend to be of long duration and are the result of a combination of genetic, physiological, environmental and behavioural factors.

Behavioural is the term that makes this a clinical topic rather than an epidemiological one: it names the part that can be changed.
### Mechanism
The most important behavioural risk factors of heart disease and stroke are unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, tobacco use and harmful use of alcohol. The same four recur across the major chronic diseases, which is why they are treated as one topic rather than four.

They act slowly and by accumulation. A single day of any of them does nothing measurable; two decades of them produce the disease burden below.
### Key determinants
Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) killed at least 43 million people in 2021, equivalent to 75% of non-pandemic-related deaths globally. That scale is what makes these behaviours a clinical priority rather than a lifestyle aside.

The effect sizes are large for individuals too. People who are insufficiently active have a 20% to 30% increased risk of death compared to people who are sufficiently active.
### Clinical significance
Exposure is not confined to the person choosing it. Second-hand smoke causes serious cardiovascular and respiratory diseases, including coronary heart disease and lung cancer, and kills more than 1.6 million people prematurely every year.

That is the clearest case of a general point: these behaviours are shaped by price, availability, advertising and the built environment, so they are addressed by policy as well as in the consultation. Treating them as pure personal choice misplaces both the cause and the remedy.
### Common misconceptions
Treating lifestyle as individual choice alone. Diet and activity are shaped by what is available and affordable.

Expecting a short intervention to show a short-term result. These factors act by accumulation, and the benefit is a disease that does not arrive.

## summary
Add the risk factors up before deciding. A modest abnormality in someone with several can matter more than a marked one in someone with none.
## sections
### Definition
Risk assessment estimates a person's probability of a future event from all their risk factors together, rather than judging each one against a threshold on its own.

The principle is stated plainly in WHO's guideline. Decisions about whether to initiate specific preventive action, and with what degree of intensity, should be guided by estimation of the risk of any such vascular event.
### Mechanism
Risk factors do not add — they multiply. Someone with three modest abnormalities can carry a higher probability of an event than someone with one marked abnormality and nothing else.

That is why a single measurement cannot decide treatment. The same blood pressure means different things in a young non-smoker and an older smoker with diabetes, and the decision follows the total rather than the number.
### Key determinants
Which factors enter the estimate is fixed by whichever tool is used, and the tools differ in what they require. The most important behavioural risk factors of heart disease and stroke are unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, tobacco use and harmful use of alcohol. Those, with age, sex and the measured variables, are what most instruments combine.

The output is a probability over a stated period, which means it is a statement about a population of similar people rather than a prediction about this one.
### Clinical significance
Estimating risk converts a set of numbers into a decision about whether to act and how hard. Preventive treatment carries its own harms, so matching intensity to probability is what keeps the balance favourable.

It also identifies people who would be missed by treating each factor separately: the patient whose every individual value is unremarkable and whose combination is not. It is important to detect cardiovascular disease as early as possible so that management with counselling and medicines can begin.
### Common misconceptions
Treating each risk factor separately. The decision rests on total risk.

Reading a ten-year risk as a forecast for this patient. It is a frequency in people like them, and the individual either has the event or does not.
## hold_these

## summary
The cheapest risk assessment there is, and the only one that also tells you who will be looking after the patient.
## sections
### Definition
A family history consists of the collection of information about the patient and other family members devoted to an understanding of heritable illness, current family health status, psychosocial disorders, and interactional and relationship data.

Four elements, and only the first is genetic. That is what separates a family history from a pedigree.
### Mechanism
Heritable illness in relatives raises the prior probability of the same illness in the patient, which is the part that functions as risk assessment.

The other three elements do something different. Current family health, psychosocial disorders and relationship data describe the environment the patient lives in and the people who will or will not support a treatment plan.
### Key determinants
How informative the genetic part is depends on how close the relatives are, how many are affected, and how young they were — the same reasoning that decides whether a pattern is worth investigating.

The non-genetic parts depend on nothing but asking. They are missed by omission rather than by difficulty.
### Clinical significance
Taking a family history costs time and no money, and it changes two things: what the patient is at risk of, and whether a plan made in clinic can survive at home.

It also reaches beyond the patient. A heritable condition identified in one person identifies relatives who may benefit from screening or early intervention, which is a preventive act performed on people who are not in the room.
### Common misconceptions
Reducing it to a list of diseases in relatives. The cited definition includes current family health, psychosocial disorders and relationships.

Taking it only when a genetic condition is suspected. Most of what it yields is context rather than inheritance.
## hold_these
A family history consists of the collection of information about the patient and other family members devoted to an understanding of heritable illness, current family health status, psychosocial disorders, and interactional and relationship data.
Only the first of the four elements is genetic; the rest describe the environment the patient lives in.
A heritable condition found in one person identifies relatives who may benefit from screening.
## lose_the_mark

Taking it only when inheritance is suspected. Most of its value is context.

## reading_time

## summary
Where habits form, and where a whole population can be reached at once. Health and education outcomes depend on each other.
## sections
### Definition
School health treats the school as a setting for health rather than only a place of teaching — its environment, curriculum, services and policies together.

Schools play a vital role in the well-being of students, families and their communities, and the link between education and health has never been more evident.
### Mechanism
Schools reach children daily, over years, at the age when habits form and before most chronic disease risk has accumulated. That combination is unavailable anywhere else in the health system.

The relationship also runs backwards. No education system is effective unless it promotes the health and well-being of its students, staff and community. A child who is unwell, hungry or unable to see the board does not learn, so health work in schools is educational work as well.
### Key determinants
A setting approach depends on the whole school rather than on a visiting service: what is sold at the gate, whether there is clean water, how physical activity is timetabled, and what the curriculum says all act on the same children.

That is why the WHO and UNESCO standards are addressed to governments and school systems rather than to health workers alone.
### Clinical significance
For a clinician the practical consequence is that a child's school is part of the history and part of the plan. Attendance, what happens at lunch, and whether medication can be given during the day all decide whether a plan works.

It is also the most efficient preventive setting available: the population is defined, present, and reachable without anyone having to attend a clinic.
### Common misconceptions
Reading school health as running clinics on school premises. The setting approach is about environment, curriculum and services together.

Treating health and education as separate goals. The cited standard states that no education system is effective without promoting health.
## hold_these
Schools play a vital role in the well-being of students, families and their communities, and the link between education and health has never been more evident.
No education system is effective unless it promotes the health and well-being of its students, staff and community.
A setting approach acts through the whole school, not through a visiting service.
## lose_the_mark
Reading school health as clinics held at school. The setting is the intervention.
Treating health and education as separate aims. The cited standard ties them together.
