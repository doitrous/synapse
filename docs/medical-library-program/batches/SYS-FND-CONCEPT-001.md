Plasma membrane
## id

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The lipid bilayer enclosing every cell, which separates the cytosol from the extracellular fluid and controls what crosses between them.
## explicit_objective
State what the plasma membrane separates and name the two properties — a hydrophobic core and embedded transport proteins — that make it selectively permeable.
## pitfalls
Describing the membrane as a passive barrier. Its selectivity comes from the proteins embedded in it, so a substance that cannot cross the lipid core may still enter freely through a channel.

## label
Selective permeability
## id
med.concept.selective-permeability
## canonical_key
selective-permeability
## aliases
Semipermeability | Differential permeability
## arabic_label
النفاذية الانتقائية
## arabic_aliases
النفاذية الاختيارية
## definition
The property of a biological membrane that allows some substances to cross while restricting others, determined jointly by the lipid core and the transport proteins present.
## explicit_objective
Predict whether a given solute crosses a membrane by passive diffusion or requires a transport protein, from its size, charge and lipid solubility.
## pitfalls
Assuming small size alone predicts passage. A small ion such as Na⁺ is charged and cannot cross the lipid core unaided, while a much larger steroid dissolves through it readily.

Cytoskeleton
## id

## canonical_key
cytoskeleton
## aliases
Cytoskeletal network
## arabic_label
الهيكل الخلوي
## arabic_aliases

## definition
The protein filament network that gives a cell its shape, anchors its organelles, and provides the tracks along which intracellular transport and cell movement occur.
## explicit_objective
Name the three filament classes — microfilaments, intermediate filaments and microtubules — and state the distinct role each plays in shape, strength or transport.
## pitfalls
Treating the cytoskeleton as a static scaffold. Microfilaments and microtubules are continuously assembled and disassembled, which is exactly what allows cell division and migration.

## arabic_label

## arabic_aliases

## definition
The membrane-bound organelle that holds the cell's chromosomal DNA and is the site of DNA replication and transcription.
## explicit_objective
State what the nuclear envelope separates, and explain why separating transcription from translation allows a eukaryotic cell to process its messenger RNA before it is read.
## pitfalls
Calling the nucleus the site of protein synthesis. Transcription happens in the nucleus; translation happens on ribosomes in the cytoplasm, and the nuclear envelope is what keeps the two apart.

Mitochondrion
## id

## canonical_key
mitochondrion
## aliases
Mitochondria
## arabic_label
الميتوكوندريا
## arabic_aliases

## definition
The double-membraned organelle that carries out oxidative phosphorylation, generating most of a cell's ATP, and which holds its own small circular genome.
## explicit_objective
State where in the mitochondrion the electron transport chain sits, and explain why a cell with high energy demand carries more mitochondria than one without.
## pitfalls
Saying the mitochondrion "makes energy". It transfers energy already held in reduced carriers into ATP; it does not create it, and the distinction is what oxidative phosphorylation actually describes.
