## summary
What a person can do, and what they actually take part in. The gap between those two is where most of the clinical work is.
## sections
### Definition
In ICF, the term functioning refers to all body functions, activities and participation, while disability is similarly an umbrella term for impairments, activity limitations and participation restrictions. Three levels, and disability can arise at any of them.

WHO's current public framing says something different in emphasis. Disability results from the interaction between health conditions such as dementia, blindness or spinal cord injury, and a range of environmental and personal factors.
### Mechanism
Activity is the execution of a task or action by an individual. Participation is involvement in a life situation. The first is capability, the second is life.

The mirror terms follow. Activity limitations are difficulties an individual may have in executing activities. Participation restrictions are problems an individual may have in involvement in life situations. A person can have the second without the first — able to do something, and still not taking part.
### Key determinants
What separates ability from participation is usually not the body. It is transport, buildings, attitudes, cost and whether anyone expected the person to be there.

That is what WHO's interactional framing makes explicit and the ICF triad leaves implicit: the same impairment disables differently in different environments, so the environment is a legitimate target of treatment.
### Applied physiology
Function is assessed in practice through daily activities. Activities of daily living refer to the basic skills necessary for individuals to independently care for themselves, such as eating, bathing, and mobility.

Instrumental ADLs include more complex activities related to living independently in the community. These activities include managing finances and medications, preparing food, performing housekeeping tasks, and doing laundry. The instrumental ones fail first, which is why asking only about washing and dressing finds the problem late.
### Clinical significance
Recording function rather than diagnosis changes what is planned. Two people with the same condition and different participation restrictions need different things, and neither need is visible from the diagnosis.

It also identifies what can be changed. An impairment may be fixed; a participation restriction often is not, because it is partly made of the environment.
### Common misconceptions
Locating disability in the body alone. Both cited definitions place it partly outside the person.

Collapsing activity into participation. A person can be able to do something and still not take part, and the interventions differ.
## hold_these

## aliases
Health literacy | Accessible communication
## reading_time

## summary
Understanding is not a property of the patient alone. It is built over a lifetime, and services either work with it or against it.
## sections
### Definition
Health literacy represents the personal knowledge and competencies that accumulate through daily activities, social interactions and across generations.

Accumulate is the word that matters. Health literacy is not delivered in a consultation; it is what a person arrives with, built from everything before.
### Mechanism
Because it accumulates through daily life and across generations, it tracks schooling, language, and what a family has had reason to learn — not intelligence, and not attention in the room.

The WHO definition continues past the part quoted here to make organisations responsible too: what people can access, understand, appraise and use depends on how services are arranged, not only on what people bring.
### Key determinants
The gap between what was said and what was understood widens with unfamiliar vocabulary, with speed, and with anything that has to be held in memory. None of those is a property of the patient.

What closes it is checking rather than asking. "Do you understand?" reliably returns yes; asking someone to say back what they will do at home does not.
### Clinical significance
A treatment plan that is not understood is not a treatment plan. Most of what goes wrong afterwards — a dose taken wrongly, a follow-up missed — is a communication outcome rather than an adherence failure.

And because health literacy is unevenly distributed, communication that only works for the confident widens a gap the service exists to narrow.
### Common misconceptions
Treating health literacy as a property of the patient to be worked around. The WHO definition also makes it a function of how services are organised.

Confirming understanding by asking for it. Checking means having the person say back what they will do.
## hold_these
Health literacy represents the personal knowledge and competencies that accumulate through daily activities, social interactions and across generations.
Health literacy is what a person arrives with, not what a consultation delivers.
Checking understanding means asking the person to say back what they will do.
## lose_the_mark

Asking "do you understand?". It reliably returns yes and confirms nothing.

## aliases
Reasonable accommodation
## reading_time
5
## summary
Not a favour. A modification owed so that someone can exercise the same rights as everyone else, limited only by proportionality.
## sections
### Definition
Reasonable accommodation means necessary and appropriate modification and adjustments not imposing a disproportionate or undue burden, where needed in a particular case, to ensure to persons with disabilities the enjoyment or exercise on an equal basis with others of all human rights and fundamental freedoms.

Every clause in that sentence does work: necessary and appropriate, not disproportionate, in a particular case, and on an equal basis with others.
### Mechanism
The adjustment is decided case by case, which is why it is not a checklist. What one person needs to attend an appointment is not what another needs, even with the same diagnosis.

The standard is equal exercise of rights, not best effort. The question is whether the person can now do the thing others can do, not whether something was tried.
### Key determinants
Proportionality is the only limit named, and it is a limit on burden rather than on entitlement. An adjustment is not unreasonable because it is unfamiliar or because nobody has asked before.

Because the need arises in a particular case, the trigger is usually the person telling you — which means asking is part of providing them.
### Clinical significance
In a clinical service the adjustments are mostly small and procedural: a longer appointment, a different room, information in another format, a companion present. What makes them adjustments is that without them the person gets less care, not less comfort.

Framed this way, they belong to the same reasoning as any other part of access: they exist so that the participation restriction does not decide the outcome.
### Common misconceptions
Treating adjustments as goodwill. The cited definition frames them as what is required for equal exercise of rights.

Applying a standard list. The definition says "in a particular case", and the need is individual.
## hold_these
Reasonable accommodation means necessary and appropriate modification and adjustments not imposing a disproportionate or undue burden, where needed in a particular case, to ensure to persons with disabilities the enjoyment or exercise on an equal basis with others of all human rights and fundamental freedoms.
The standard is equal exercise of rights, not best effort.
Proportionality limits the burden, not the entitlement.
## lose_the_mark
Treating an adjustment as a favour. It is what equal exercise of rights requires.
Working from a standard list. The definition says "in a particular case".

## aliases
Informal caregiver | Carer | Caregiver stress
## reading_time
5
## summary
Most long-term care is given unpaid by someone with a relationship to the patient — and that person carries a strain of their own.
## sections
### Definition
An informal caregiver, often a family member, provides care, typically unpaid, to someone with whom they have a personal relationship.

Three features define the role: the relationship comes first, the care is usually unpaid, and it is not the person's profession. That is what separates it from a care worker doing the same tasks.
### Mechanism
Because the relationship precedes the caring, the caregiver cannot easily set limits that a professional would. Hours are not fixed, the role does not end at a shift, and refusing is a family act rather than a contractual one.

Caregiver stress is the stress that comes from the emotional and physical strain of caregiving. It is a recognised consequence of the role rather than a sign that a particular person is not coping.
### Key determinants
How much strain a caregiver carries tracks what the patient needs, how long it has run, and whether anyone else is sharing it — the instrumental and basic activities the patient can no longer manage translate directly into hours of somebody's day.

What reduces it is practical: shared load, respite, and the caregiver's own health being attended to rather than assumed.
### Clinical significance
The caregiver is present in most consultations about a dependent patient, and their capacity is part of whether any plan will work. A discharge that depends on care nobody has capacity to give will fail.

They are also a patient. A caregiver whose health fails removes the care as well as becoming unwell, which is why asking after them is clinical rather than courteous.
### Common misconceptions
Treating the caregiver as a resource rather than a person. The strain is documented, and a caregiver who becomes unwell removes the care too.

Assuming unpaid means informal in the sense of casual. The hours are often greater than a professional's.
## hold_these

A caregiver whose health fails removes the care as well as becoming unwell.
## lose_the_mark

Planning care that depends on capacity nobody has confirmed exists.

## arabic_title

## aliases
Work and health | Occupational disease
## reading_time
5
## summary
Work causes disease, and the claim that a particular disease is occupational has to meet two tests rather than one.
## sections
### Definition
Occupational health concerns the effect of work on health. The task of specialized occupational health services is to assess these risks and develop recommendations for prevention of occupational and work-related diseases.

The term occupational disease covers any disease contracted as a result of an exposure to risk factors arising from work activity.
### Mechanism
Exposure at work differs from exposure elsewhere in being sustained, repeated and often at higher concentration — the same agent, met daily for years. That is what turns a tolerable exposure into a cause.

It also makes the exposure identifiable, because it is shared by a defined group of people doing the same job, which is what the second criterion below rests on.
### Key determinants
Two elements identify an occupational disease: the causal relationship between exposure in a specific working environment or work activity and a specific disease, and the fact that the disease occurs among a group of exposed persons with a frequency above the average morbidity of the rest of the population.

Both are required. A worker with a common disease does not have an occupational disease, and an unusual disease with no plausible exposure route is not one either.
### Clinical significance
Occupational disease is preventable by definition — the exposure is known, located and controllable — which is why the services described are preventive rather than curative.

For a clinician the practical consequence is that occupation belongs in the history. A disease that is common in one trade and rare elsewhere will not be recognised unless the trade is asked about.
### Common misconceptions
Calling a disease occupational because a worker has it. The definition requires a causal relationship and a frequency above that of the rest of the population.

Treating occupational health as a workplace administrative function. The definition is about disease causation and prevention.
## hold_these
The term occupational disease covers any disease contracted as a result of an exposure to risk factors arising from work activity.
Two elements identify an occupational disease: the causal relationship between exposure in a specific working environment or work activity and a specific disease, and the fact that the disease occurs among a group of exposed persons with a frequency above the average morbidity of the rest of the population.
The task of specialized occupational health services is to assess these risks and develop recommendations for prevention of occupational and work-related diseases.
Occupation belongs in the history.
## lose_the_mark
Calling a disease occupational because the patient works. Both criteria have to be met.
Omitting occupation from the history. A disease common in one trade is invisible without it.
