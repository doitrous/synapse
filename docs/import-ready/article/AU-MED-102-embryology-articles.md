## summary
Before a sperm can fertilise an oocyte it must be capacitated — stripped of the surface
coat that keeps it from binding the zona pellucida. Once one sperm fuses with the oocyte,
the zona itself changes chemically so that no second sperm can enter, and it later keeps
the dividing blastomeres from sticking to the uterine tube while the morula travels to the
uterine cavity. Two separate mechanisms, one on the sperm and one on the oocyte's own coat,
both make fertilisation a single, controlled event rather than an uncontrolled one.

## sections
### Definition
Capacitation is the removal of the glycoprotein coat and seminal plasma proteins from the
plasma membrane over the sperm's acrosomal region, which happens in the female genital
tract. The zona pellucida is the acellular glycoprotein coat surrounding the oocyte; its
own reaction after fertilisation is the block to polyspermy.

### Mechanism
Only a capacitated sperm can bind zona pellucida receptors and undergo the acrosome
reaction — acrosomal exocytosis releasing acrosin and hyaluronidase, which let the sperm
penetrate the corona radiata and then the zona.
Once the first sperm fuses with the oocyte's own plasma membrane, cortical granules release
their contents and change the zona's chemical structure. This zona reaction is what blocks
any further sperm from penetrating it, and it is a timed chemical change, not simple
mechanical crowding by the first sperm to arrive.

### Key determinants
Capacitation takes place only inside the female genital tract; sperm collected for assisted
reproduction must be capacitated artificially in vitro before use. The zona's block to
polyspermy depends on the same cortical granule release that also hardens the zona and
supports the dividing morula: the intact zona keeps the blastomeres from adhering to the
uterine tube mucosa as the mitotically dividing zygote is carried to the uterine cavity over
about three days.

### Clinical significance
Fertilisation failure and polyspermy are both clinically relevant to assisted reproduction:
sperm that have not undergone capacitation cannot fertilise an oocyte in vitro either, and a
zona that fails its reaction after the first sperm entry risks polyspermic fertilisation,
which produces a non-viable triploid zygote.

## hold_these

## summary
Amniotic fluid volume is not just "more" or "less" — the exam tests a number.
Oligohydramnios is amniotic fluid under 400 mL at term, against a normal term volume of
roughly 800–1500 mL, and because most of that fluid from mid-pregnancy onward is fetal
urine, the finding usually points to a fetal renal or urinary-outflow problem, or to
uteroplacental insufficiency reducing fetal urine output.

## sections
### Definition
Oligohydramnios is an abnormally reduced amniotic fluid volume, conventionally defined as
less than 400 mL at term.

### Mechanism
From mid-pregnancy, fetal urine is the dominant contributor to amniotic fluid volume, so a
reduction in fetal urine output — from renal agenesis, a urinary-outflow obstruction such as
posterior urethral valves, or chronic uteroplacental insufficiency reducing renal perfusion —
reduces the total volume below the normal range.

### Key determinants
Normal term amniotic fluid volume is roughly 800–1500 mL. Because so much of it is fetal
urine from mid-pregnancy onward, oligohydramnios and the fetal urinary tract are linked by
mechanism, not by coincidence, which is why a single ultrasound finding of reduced fluid
prompts a fetal renal survey.

### Clinical significance
Severe, early oligohydramnios restricts fetal movement and lung expansion, and is
associated with pulmonary hypoplasia and limb deformities (the sequence historically named
after Potter). The volume threshold itself — under 400 mL — is what an exam question tests
directly.

## summary
The urachus is what the allantoic duct becomes once it obliterates before birth — a
fibrous cord from the bladder apex to the umbilicus, the median umbilical ligament in the
adult. When it fails to obliterate, urine tracks along it and presents as a urachal
fistula: a discharge of urine from the umbilicus, distinct from a vitelline (yolk-stalk)
fistula, which discharges bowel content instead because it connects to the ileum.

## sections
### Definition
The urachus is the fibrous remnant of the allantoic duct, running from the bladder apex to
the umbilicus. It normally obliterates before birth to become the median umbilical
ligament. A patent (persistent) urachus is one whose lumen fails to close.

### Mechanism
When the urachal lumen persists, urine can leak along its length. If the whole tract stays
open from bladder to umbilicus, urine discharges continuously from the umbilicus — a
urachal fistula. Partial patency instead produces a urachal sinus (open at the umbilical
end only), a urachal cyst (a closed off pocket partway along the tract), or a vesico-
urachal diverticulum (open at the bladder end only), depending on which part of the duct
fails to close.

### Key determinants
The distinguishing feature is what the tract connects to. The urachus connects the bladder
to the umbilicus, so a urachal fistula discharges urine. A vitelline (omphalomesenteric)
duct fistula instead connects the ileum to the umbilicus, so it discharges faecal or mucoid
bowel content. The nature of the discharge is the sign an exam question is built to test,
not merely the presence of a discharge.

### Clinical significance
A urachal fistula presents in a neonate as continuous or intermittent urine leakage from
the umbilicus, sometimes with excoriation of the surrounding skin, and is confirmed by
imaging or contrast studies of the tract before surgical excision.

## published_summary


## published_sections


## hold_these
A patent urachus discharges urine because it connects the bladder to the umbilicus; a
patent vitelline duct discharges bowel content because it connects the ileum to the
umbilicus. The discharge tells you the connection.
The urachus's normal fate is the median umbilical ligament; the yolk stalk's normal fate
is complete disappearance — two different structures with two different endpoints, and an
exam distractor set is built to test whether a student mixes them up.

## lose_the_mark
Treating any umbilical discharge in a neonate as the same finding regardless of what it
contains, rather than using the discharge itself (urine vs faecal/mucoid) to identify which
embryonic remnant failed to obliterate.
