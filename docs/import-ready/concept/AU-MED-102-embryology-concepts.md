## definition
In the female genital tract, the sperm undergoes capacitation: the removal of the
glycoprotein coat and seminal plasma proteins from the plasma membrane overlying the
acrosomal region. Capacitation is a prerequisite for the acrosome reaction — only a
capacitated sperm can bind zona pellucida receptors and undergo acrosomal exocytosis to
penetrate the corona radiata and zona pellucida.

## explicit_objective
State what capacitation removes from the sperm surface and explain why it must happen
before the acrosome reaction can occur.

## pitfalls
Confusing capacitation with the acrosome reaction itself. Capacitation only strips the
sperm's surface coat; it is the acrosome reaction, triggered afterward by the sperm binding
zona pellucida glycoproteins, that actually releases acrosin and hyaluronidase to penetrate
the corona radiata and zona.

## arabic_label

## definition
The zona pellucida is the acellular glycoprotein coat around the oocyte. Once the first
sperm fuses with the oocyte membrane, cortical granule release changes the zona's chemical
structure — the zona reaction — which blocks any further sperm from penetrating it and so
prevents polyspermy. Before implantation, the intact zona also keeps the dividing
blastomeres from adhering to the uterine tube mucosa while the morula is carried to the
uterine cavity.

## explicit_objective
State the two functions of the zona pellucida during fertilisation and cleavage, and name
the mechanism (the zona reaction) that blocks polyspermy.

## pitfalls
Treating the zona pellucida as inert packaging around the ovum. It performs an active,
timed chemical change — the zona reaction — immediately after the first sperm fuses, and
that change is what blocks polyspermy, not simple mechanical crowding by the first sperm.

## definition
Oligohydramnios is an abnormally reduced amniotic fluid volume, conventionally defined as
less than 400 mL at term, against a normal term volume of roughly 800–1500 mL. Because
most of the fluid from mid-pregnancy onward is fetal urine, oligohydramnios classically
points to a fetal renal or urinary-outflow problem (for example renal agenesis or urethral
obstruction) or to chronic uteroplacental insufficiency reducing fetal urine output.

## explicit_objective
State the volume threshold that defines oligohydramnios and the mechanism (reduced fetal
urine output) that most often explains it.

## pitfalls
Treating "reduced amniotic fluid" as a vague descriptor rather than a number with a
mechanism. The threshold (under 400 mL) is what the exam tests, and tying it to reduced
fetal urine output is what makes the finding clinically actionable rather than a label.

## label
A patent urachus discharges urine from the umbilicus

## canonical_key
urachus.patent.fistula-urine-discharge

## aliases
Urachal fistula
Patent urachus

## arabic_label
ناسور القناة البولية السرية (الأوراكس)

## arabic_aliases
بقاء القناة البولية السرية مفتوحة

## definition
The urachus is the fibrous remnant of the allantoic duct running from the bladder apex to
the umbilicus; it normally obliterates before birth to become the median umbilical
ligament. When its lumen fails to close — a patent or persistent urachus — urine leaks
along it and presents as a urachal fistula: a discharge of urine from the umbilicus. This
is distinct from an omphalomesenteric (vitelline duct) fistula, which connects to the ileum
and discharges bowel content instead.

## explicit_objective
Name the embryonic origin of a urachal fistula and state what distinguishes its discharge
(urine) from a vitelline-duct fistula (faecal or mucoid).

## pitfalls
Confusing a urachal fistula with a vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct fistula. Both present
as an umbilical discharge in a neonate, but the urachus connects to the bladder — so the
discharge is urine — while the vitelline duct connects to the ileum, so the discharge is
faecal or mucoid. The nature of the discharge, not the presence of a discharge, is the
diagnostic sign.
