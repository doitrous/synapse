## title
Loss of DIP flexion in the ring and little fingers

## question
A 25 years old female with loss of flexion of distal interphalangeal joint of the little finger and ring finger, what is the muscle might be affected?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Pronator Quadratus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Pronator quadratus pronates the forearm and has no action on the fingers.

## answer_b
Flexor digitorum Superficialis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Flexor digitorum superficialis inserts on the middle phalanx and cannot flex the distal interphalangeal joint at all.

## answer_c
Flexor Pollicis Longus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Flexor pollicis longus flexes the thumb's interphalangeal joint, not the fingers'.

## answer_d
Flexor digitorum profundus

## explanation_d
Correct. Flexion at the distal interphalangeal joints is produced only by flexor digitorum profundus (flexor digitorum superficialis inserts more proximally and cannot flex this joint), and its medial (ulnar) half supplies exactly the ring and little fingers — so loss confined to these two fingers' DIP flexion points to the ulnar-supplied half of flexor digitorum profundus specifically. Pronator quadratus pronates the forearm, flexor digitorum superficialis cannot flex the DIP joint at all, and flexor pollicis longus acts on the thumb, not the fingers.

## title
A true statement about the median nerve

## question
Regarding the affected nerve (the median nerve, continuing the carpal tunnel syndrome case), which of the following is true?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
gives off muscular branches in the arm

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The median nerve gives no branches at all in the arm; its first branches arise in the forearm.

## answer_b
passes on the lateral side of brachial artery in cubital fossa

## explanation_b
Incorrect. In the cubital fossa the median nerve lies medial to the brachial artery, not lateral.

## answer_c
gives a branch the supplies deep flexor muscles of forearm

## explanation_c
Correct. The median nerve gives off the anterior interosseous nerve, which supplies the deep flexor muscles of the forearm — flexor pollicis longus, the lateral half of flexor digitorum profundus, and pronator quadratus — making this the true statement. The median nerve gives NO branches in the arm at all (only in the forearm and hand); by the cubital fossa it lies medial, not lateral, to the brachial artery (having crossed over from its earlier lateral position higher up); and its palmar cutaneous branch crosses superficial to, not deep to, the flexor retinaculum, which is exactly why palmar sensation is spared in carpal tunnel syndrome.

## answer_d
its palmar cutaneous branch passes deep to flexor retinaculum

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve passes superficial to, not deep to, the flexor retinaculum.
