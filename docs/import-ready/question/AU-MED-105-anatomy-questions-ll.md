## title
Fracture site affecting peroneus brevis insertion

## question
Fracture of which of the following bones will affect the insertion of the peroneus brevis muscle?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
D

## answer_a
Calcaneus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The calcaneus receives the tendo calcaneus and long plantar ligament, not peroneus brevis.

## answer_b
Navicular

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The navicular receives part of tibialis posterior's insertion, not peroneus brevis.

## answer_c
Base of 1st metatarsal bone

## explanation_c
Incorrect. The base of the first metatarsal (with the medial cuneiform) is where peroneus longus, not brevis, inserts.

## answer_d
Base of 5th metatarsal bone

## explanation_d
Correct. Peroneus brevis inserts on the tuberosity at the base of the fifth metatarsal, so a fracture there (a "Jones fracture" in clinical shorthand) directly threatens this tendon's insertion. No other tarsal or metatarsal named in this stem carries peroneus brevis's insertion, which is what makes the fifth metatarsal base the single correct site.

## title
The incorrect statement about leg muscles

## question
Regarding the muscles of the leg, the INCORRECT statement is:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Peroneus longus muscle is inserted into 5th metatarsal bone

## explanation_a
Correct — this is the incorrect (false) statement. Peroneus longus inserts on the base of the first metatarsal and medial cuneiform, not the fifth metatarsal; the fifth metatarsal is peroneus brevis's insertion. Recognising this reversed pairing — longus to the first metatarsal, brevis to the fifth — is the exact discrimination this stem tests.

## answer_b
The tibialis anterior is inserted into the medial cuneiform bone

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this statement IS true). Tibialis anterior does insert on the medial cuneiform (and base of the first metatarsal).

## answer_c
The gastrocnemius is inserted into the back of calcaneus

## explanation_c
Incorrect (this statement IS true). Gastrocnemius, via the tendo calcaneus, does insert on the back of the calcaneus.

## answer_d
The tibialis posterior is inserted into the tuberosity of the navicular bone

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this statement IS true). Tibialis posterior's chief insertion is the tuberosity of the navicular, with slips to neighbouring tarsals.

Nerve damaged by a fibular neck fracture

## question
A 20-year-old college student receives a severe blow to the inferolateral side of the left knee joint while playing football. Radiographic examination reveals a fracture of the head and neck of the fibula. Which of the following nerves is damaged?

## subject
msk

## vignette

## answer_a

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The sciatic nerve has already divided well above the knee in most people, so a fibular-neck fracture affects only its common peroneal branch, not the intact sciatic trunk.

## answer_b
Tibial

## explanation_b
Incorrect. The tibial nerve runs deep in the posterior compartment, well away from the subcutaneous fibular neck.

## answer_c
Common peroneal

## explanation_c
Correct. The common peroneal nerve winds directly around the fibular neck, making it the nerve classically injured by a fracture at exactly this site. This mechanism — a direct blow or fracture compressing the nerve against the bone at its most exposed point — is the classic teaching example of a peripheral nerve injury from local trauma.

## explanation_d
Incorrect. The deep peroneal nerve is only the common peroneal nerve's distal branch, given off after it has already passed the fibular neck — the injury at the neck itself affects the whole common peroneal trunk, which is the more precise answer here.

As a result of this nerve injury, the patient may complain of severe impairment in:

## subject
msk

Plantar flexion of foot

Incorrect. Plantarflexion is a tibial-nerve (posterior compartment) action, unaffected by a common peroneal nerve injury.

Plantar flexion and inversion

Incorrect. Knee flexion is a hamstring (mostly tibial-division) action, largely spared by an isolated common peroneal nerve injury at the fibular neck.

## title
Where the common peroneal nerve terminates

## question
The common peroneal nerve terminates inside ...... muscle.

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
A

## answer_a
Peroneus longus

## explanation_a
Correct. The common peroneal nerve divides into its superficial and deep peroneal branches within the substance of peroneus longus, after winding around the fibular neck and piercing that muscle's origin. This detail matters clinically because a laceration or compression at peroneus longus itself, not only at the fibular neck, can produce the same distal nerve deficits.

## answer_b
Peroneus brevis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Peroneus brevis lies distal to this division point and does not contain the nerve's bifurcation.

## answer_c
Extensor digitorum longus

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Extensor digitorum longus is a deep-peroneal-nerve-supplied anterior compartment muscle, not the site of the common peroneal nerve's own division.

## answer_d
Popliteus

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Popliteus is a tibial-nerve-supplied posterior compartment muscle, unrelated to the common peroneal nerve's course.

## title
The false statement about the superficial peroneal nerve

## question
All of the following statements concerning the musculocutaneous nerve of the leg (superficial peroneal nerve) are true EXCEPT:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
It supplies the peroneus longus muscle

## explanation_a
Incorrect (this IS true). The superficial peroneal nerve does supply peroneus longus (and brevis).

## answer_b
It participates in the cutaneous supply of the dorsum of the foot

## explanation_b
Incorrect (this IS true). It does contribute most of the dorsum of the foot's cutaneous supply.

## answer_c
It passes deep to the extensor retinaculum of the foot

## explanation_c
Correct — this is the false statement. The superficial peroneal nerve becomes cutaneous by piercing the deep fascia above the ankle and runs superficial to (not deep to) the extensor retinaculum; it is the deep peroneal nerve that passes deep to it. This distinction matters clinically: a superficial peroneal nerve injury near the ankle produces sensory loss over the dorsum without a foot-drop-type motor deficit, since the deep peroneal nerve (which does run deep to the retinaculum) is unaffected.

## answer_d
It is one of the 2 terminal branches of the common peroneal nerve

## explanation_d
Incorrect (this IS true). It is one of the common peroneal nerve's two terminal branches, the other being the deep peroneal nerve.

A motorcyclist falls from his bike and gets a deep gash that severs the superficial peroneal nerve near its origin. Which of the following muscles is paralyzed?

A

Peroneus longus

## explanation_a
Correct. Peroneus longus, a lateral compartment muscle, is supplied by the superficial peroneal nerve, so it is paralysed by this injury. Because peroneus brevis shares the same nerve, this injury also weakens eversion generally, not just the single named muscle.

Fibularis tertius

Incorrect. Fibularis (peroneus) tertius is anatomically part of the anterior compartment and deep-peroneal-supplied despite its name, spared.

Extensor digitorum brevis

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Extensor digitorum brevis, the one intrinsic dorsal foot muscle, is deep-peroneal-supplied, spared.

## title
Groove on the cuboid bone

## question
The inferior surface of the cuboid bone is marked by a groove for the passage of:

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
B

## answer_a
Posterior tibial nerve

## explanation_a
Incorrect. The posterior tibial nerve terminates well proximal to this level, dividing into the plantar nerves near the medial malleolus, not at the cuboid.

## answer_b
Peroneus longus muscle

## explanation_b
Correct. Peroneus longus's tendon grooves the inferior surface of the cuboid as it turns medially to cross the sole toward its insertion on the base of the first metatarsal and medial cuneiform. Because the groove is a fixed bony landmark, it is used surgically and radiographically to trace the tendon's path even when it cannot be directly visualised.

## answer_c
Tibialis posterior muscle

## explanation_c
Incorrect. Tibialis posterior's tendon runs behind the medial malleolus and inserts chiefly on the navicular, not grooving the cuboid.

## answer_d
Dorsalis pedis artery

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Dorsalis pedis crosses the dorsum of the foot before diving into the sole near the first metatarsal space, not grooving the cuboid's inferior surface.

Tendon damaged by a destroyed cuboid groove

## question
A 21-year-old man was involved in a motorcycle accident, resulting in destruction of the groove in the lower surface of the cuboid bone. Which of the following muscle tendons is most likely damaged?

## subject
msk

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## correct_answer
C

## answer_a
Flexor hallucis longus

## explanation_a
Incorrect. Flexor hallucis longus runs medially behind the ankle and along the sole close to the great toe, not grooving the cuboid.

## answer_b
Peroneus brevis

## explanation_b
Incorrect. Peroneus brevis inserts directly on the base of the fifth metatarsal, without crossing the cuboid's inferior groove the way peroneus longus does.

Peroneus longus

## explanation_c
Correct. Peroneus longus's tendon runs in the groove on the cuboid's inferior surface as it crosses the sole, so destruction of that groove threatens this tendon specifically. Losing this groove's guiding contour would let the tendon's line of pull shift, weakening the foot's eversion and its dynamic support of the transverse arch.

Tibialis anterior

## explanation_d
Incorrect. Tibialis anterior's tendon runs on the front of the ankle/dorsum, nowhere near the cuboid's plantar surface.

## answer_e
Tibialis posterior

## explanation_e
Incorrect. Tibialis posterior's tendon inserts chiefly on the navicular, medial to the cuboid groove.
