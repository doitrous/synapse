## format

## written_parts
### (a) 5 marks
Describe the ionic basis of the depolarisation phase of the action potential.
Expects: Depolarisation is a regenerative process — a positive feedback loop driven by sodium inflow
Expects: Slow phase, from -90 mV to -65 mV — the stimulus causes an initial depolarisation which opens some of the activation gates of the sodium channels
Expects: Slow phase — sodium enters along its electrochemical gradient, which depolarises the membrane further, which opens more gates, until the firing level (threshold) of -65 mV is reached
Expects: Rapid phase, from -65 mV to +35 mV — all the sodium channels open and sodium rushes in
Expects: Rapid inactivation of the sodium channels then begins
Expects: The inactivated channels stay inactive for a few milliseconds before returning to the resting state
Concept: CON-NEU-7A30FECF042995 | CON-NEU-157E05FAF3B100

## question
Compare the absolute and the relative refractory periods, by excitability, by what stimulus can fire a second action potential, by when each period runs, and by the state of the ion channels.

## format

## written_parts
### (a) 6 marks
Compare the absolute and the relative refractory periods, by excitability, by what stimulus can fire a second action potential, by when each period runs, and by the state of the ion channels.
Expects: In the absolute refractory period excitability is zero
Expects: In the relative refractory period excitability is below normal
Expects: In the absolute refractory period no second action potential can be produced, whatever the strength of the stimulus
Expects: In the relative refractory period a second action potential can be produced, but only by a stronger stimulus above threshold
Expects: The absolute refractory period runs from the firing level to early repolarisation
Expects: The relative refractory period runs from the end of the absolute refractory period to the end of the action potential
Expects: In the absolute refractory period the sodium channels are open and are then rapidly inactivated by closure of the inner gate
Expects: In the relative refractory period some sodium channels have returned to the resting state, and the potassium channels opened during repolarisation carry an outward current that opposes sodium entry
Concept: CON-NEU-2235199E9F4373 | CON-NEU-F119674A8DFD8D

How tension is generated during skeletal muscle contraction

## subject

## status

## vignette

## question
Explain the steps of the generation of tension during skeletal muscle contraction.

## format
structured_written

## written_parts
### (a) 4 marks
Explain the steps of the generation of tension during skeletal muscle contraction.
Expects: Tension is the force developed when the muscle contracts, and it is generated by cross-bridge cycling, in four steps
Expects: Binding — actin and myosin bind spontaneously
Expects: Bending and sliding — the cross-bridges bend and actin slides across myosin, the energy coming from the hydrolysis of ATP that phosphorylated the cross-bridge
Expects: Detachment — ADP and phosphate must leave the cross-bridge and a fresh ATP must attach, which lowers the cross-bridge's affinity for the active site
Expects: With no ATP there is no separation, and the muscle goes into contracture
Expects: Return — the cross-bridge returns to its original upright position, ready to take part in another cycle
Expects: Cycling continues as long as calcium is bound to troponin and ATP is available
Expects: The tension developed is transmitted through actin, to the Z line, to the sarcolemma, to the tendon, to the bone
Concept: CON-MSK-B2B106C1D81C30 | CON-MSK-AC42FE7AB41DF2

## question
Summarise the factors affecting smooth muscle contraction.

## format

## written_parts
### (a) 4 marks
Summarise the factors affecting smooth muscle contraction.
Expects: Stretch increases contraction — a hollow organ contracts and evacuates automatically when it is distended
Expects: Local factors that relax it — acids, a rise in carbon dioxide, and a fall in oxygen
Expects: Local factors that contract it — alkalis, and a rise in potassium
Expects: Cold increases contraction
Expects: Humoral factors — a ligand binding an excitatory receptor raises cytoplasmic calcium and causes contraction
Expects: Humoral factors — a ligand binding an inhibitory receptor lowers cytoplasmic calcium and causes relaxation, by opening potassium channels which inhibits calcium influx, and by increasing the active transport of calcium
Expects: Nerve supply — a dual supply from the autonomic nervous system, which does not initiate activity but modifies the spontaneous activity and its sensitivity to chemicals
