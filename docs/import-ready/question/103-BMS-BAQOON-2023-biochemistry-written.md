## title
Explain: ammonia neurotoxicity, ketogenesis/ketolysis sites, cyanide, vitamin D and insulin, Von Gierke's hypoglycaemia, and vitamin C anaemia

## subject
fnd

## status
Draft

## owner
Claude

## vignette

## question
Explain each of the following on its biochemical basis.

## format
multipart_written

## written_parts
### (a) 2 marks
Ammonia has neurotoxic effects on CNS.
Expects: Ammonia shifts α-ketoglutarate towards glutamate formation, depleting α-ketoglutarate and decreasing ATP production, which can lead to coma and death
Expects: Convulsions follow from a deficiency of γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the inhibitory neurotransmitter formed from glutamate by decarboxylation, since glutamate is being consumed to fix ammonia instead
Expects: Brain oedema follows from osmotic imbalance due to high levels of ammonia and glutamine in astrocytes
Concept: CON-FND-880D165894A5EC

### (b) 2 marks
Ketogenesis is hepatic while ketolysis is extrahepatic.
Expects: Ketogenesis (ketone body synthesis) builds ketone bodies from acetyl-CoA generated by β-oxidation of fatty acids and ketogenic amino acids, in the mitochondria of the liver, which has HMG-CoA synthase and HMG-CoA lyase
Expects: Ketolysis (ketone body utilisation) oxidises ketone bodies to CO2 and H2O in the mitochondria of extrahepatic tissues, which have high thiophorase (succinyl-CoA acetoacetate CoA-transferase) activity — an enzyme the liver lacks, which is why the liver cannot use the ketone bodies it makes
Concept: CON-END-2E748A37DA660A

### (c) 2 marks
Cyanide is toxic to brain.
Expects: Cyanide, together with carbon monoxide and hydrogen sulfide, is a powerful poison that inhibits cytochrome c oxidase (complex IV of the respiratory chain) and so inhibits ATP production
Expects: Low amounts cause dizziness, breathlessness, numbness and headache; high levels cause convulsions, coma and death — the brain is affected first because it depends almost completely on oxidative ATP production
Concept: CON-FND-A3BC299ED2C7C9

### (d) 2 marks
Vitamin D supplements improve type II diabetes.
Expects: Vitamin D has a non-calcaemic (non-skeletal) function in regulating insulin secretion and sensitivity
Expects: It increases pancreatic insulin secretion and reduces insulin resistance, acting via its effects on intracellular calcium
Concept: CON-FND-46B9F239340ED9

### (e) 2 marks
Fasting hypoglycaemia is one of the principal effects of Von Gierke's disease.
Expects: Von Gierke's disease (type I glycogen storage disease) is a defect of glucose-6-phosphatase in the liver
Expects: Without this enzyme, glucose 6-phosphate released by glycogenolysis, and made by gluconeogenesis, cannot be dephosphorylated to free glucose that can leave the liver cell, so fasting hypoglycaemia results despite normal or raised liver glycogen
Concept: CON-FND-1BE461A57AB76D

### (f) 2 marks
Causes of anaemia in vitamin C deficiency.
Expects: Haemorrhagic blood loss, giving a normocytic anaemia, from the fragile capillaries of defective collagen
Expects: Defective iron absorption, giving a microcytic anaemia, because vitamin C keeps dietary iron reduced to the ferrous state the gut absorbs
Expects: Decreased activity of dihydrofolate reductase, giving a macrocytic anaemia, because vitamin C keeps the enzyme active
Concept: CON-FND-C9E5128193029E

Compare: phenylketonuria, gout and obstructive jaundice

## vignette

## question
Compare between phenylketonuria, gout and obstructive jaundice.

## format
multipart_written

## written_parts
### (a) 1 marks
Phenylketonuria.
Expects: Deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase, or in 1–2% of cases of its coenzyme tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4)
Expects: Phenylalanine and its metabolites — phenylpyruvate and phenylacetate — accumulate and interfere with tyrosine and tryptophan transport to the brain, causing mental retardation, and competitively inhibit tyrosinase, causing hypopigmentation of hair, skin and iris
Concept: CON-FND-D7BB8C3AFB54CC

### (b) 1 marks
Gout.
Expects: Hyperuricaemia from either overproduction of uric acid or decreased renal excretion of it
Expects: Deposits urate crystals as tophi in soft tissue around joints, especially the big toe, causing arthritis, and may deposit urate stones in the urinary tract
Concept: CON-REN-31708150F8B722

### (c) 1 marks
Obstructive jaundice.
Expects: Obstruction of the biliary passages, for example by gallstones or a tumour of the pancreatic head, so conjugated (direct) bilirubin cannot reach the intestine and regurgitates into the blood
Expects: Stercobilin disappears from the stool, giving a clay-coloured stool, and conjugated bilirubin is excreted by the kidney, giving dark urine; serum alkaline phosphatase is raised
Concept: CON-GIT-A265DD7A7CC8EF

## title
Enumerate: tryptophan derivatives, irreversible glycolytic enzymes, urea cycle enzyme factors, hypercholesterolaemia causes, apolipoprotein functions, vitamin A deficiency causes

## subject

## status

## owner

## vignette

## question
Enumerate each of the following.

## format
multipart_written

## written_parts
### (a) 2 marks
Derivatives of tryptophan.
Expects: Serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine), formed by BH4-dependent hydroxylation then decarboxylation
Expects: Melatonin, formed from serotonin in the pineal body
Expects: Nicotinic acid (niacin, vitamin B3), formed from tryptophan only in the presence of vitamin B6
Expects: Indole and skatole, formed by bacterial putrefaction of tryptophan in the large intestine and excreted in stool
Concept: CON-NEU-6C4A6BDA725F0E

### (b) 2 marks
Irreversible enzymes of glycolysis.
Expects: Glucokinase (in liver and β-cells) or hexokinase (everywhere else)
Expects: Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)
Expects: Pyruvate kinase (PK)
Concept: CON-FND-853096A349FFBD

### (c) 2 marks
Factors affecting the level of urea cycle enzymes.
Expects: A high protein diet increases urea cycle enzyme levels 10 to 20 fold
Expects: Protein catabolic hormones increase the concentration of urea cycle enzymes
Expects: Starvation elevates urea cycle enzyme levels to cope with the increased ammonia that accompanies enhanced protein degradation
Concept: CON-FND-6A2CCA2892E78C

### (d) 2 marks
Causes of hypercholesterolaemia.
Expects: Familial hyperlipoproteinaemias
Expects: Hypothyroidism, since thyroid hormone normally increases oxidation of cholesterol and its conversion to bile acids
Expects: Obesity
Expects: Dietary causes — a diet rich in saturated fat, carbohydrate and cholesterol
Expects: Obstructive jaundice, which blocks the pathway for excretion of cholesterol and bile acids
Expects: Diabetes mellitus, through increased lipolysis and fatty acid oxidation raising acetyl-CoA and hepatic cholesterol synthesis, increased hepatic VLDL and cholesterol output, and decreased plasma lipoprotein lipase activity (normally induced by insulin) that slows lipoprotein clearance
Concept: CON-GIT-E6AEB25F31B529

### (e) 2 marks
Function of apolipoproteins.
Expects: Structural element in the lipoprotein particles
Expects: Activators for enzymes — apo C-II is an activator for lipoprotein lipase
Expects: Interact with cell-surface receptors to direct lipoproteins to target organs — apo B-100 in LDL is specific for uptake of LDL by the LDL receptor
Expects: Transfer lipid between lipoproteins — apo D transfers triacylglycerol in exchange for cholesterol esters between different lipoproteins
Concept: CON-GIT-99EF5E989B0C65

### (f) 2 marks
Causes of vitamin A deficiency.
Expects: Decreased intake
Expects: Malabsorption, as in obstructive jaundice and steatorrhoea
Expects: Liver cirrhosis, through reduced synthesis of retinol-binding protein (RBP)
Expects: Chronic nephrosis, where RBP is lost by excretion in the urine
Concept: CON-FND-46B9F239340ED9
