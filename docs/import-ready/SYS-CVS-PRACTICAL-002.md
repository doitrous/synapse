## title
Measuring blood pressure by auscultation
## subject
cvs
## type
Skills checklist
## status
Draft
## owner
Clinical skills team
## duration
6
## marks
20
## difficulty
Easy
## candidate_instructions
Measure this patient's blood pressure by auscultation and state the reading. Talk the examiner through what you are doing and why.
## mark_scheme
Preparation (5): Washes hands, introduces self, confirms identity and gains consent
Preparation (5): Asks about caffeine, smoking and exertion within the last thirty minutes
Preparation (5): Seats the patient with the back supported and the legs uncrossed
Preparation (5): Rests the patient for several minutes before the first reading
Cuff and positioning (5): Selects a cuff whose bladder encircles most of the upper arm
Cuff and positioning (5): Applies the cuff to the bare upper arm, snugly, with the bladder over the brachial artery
Cuff and positioning (5): Supports the arm so the cuff sits at the level of the heart
Cuff and positioning (5): Checks that clothing above the cuff is not acting as a tourniquet
Estimating and measuring (7): Palpates the brachial or radial pulse and inflates until it disappears, to estimate systolic pressure
Estimating and measuring (7): Deflates fully, then re-inflates 20 to 30 mmHg above the estimated systolic pressure
Estimating and measuring (7): Places the stethoscope over the brachial artery below the cuff without tucking it under the cuff
Estimating and measuring (7): Deflates at approximately 2 to 3 mmHg per second
Estimating and measuring (7): Identifies the first appearance of sounds as systolic pressure
Estimating and measuring (7): Identifies the disappearance of sounds as diastolic pressure
Reporting (3): States the reading to the nearest 2 mmHg and names the arm used
Reporting (3): States that an abnormal reading should be repeated rather than acted on alone
Reporting (3): Explains that silence above systolic pressure means the artery is occluded by the cuff
## main_concept
CON-CVS-BE94FBA0B205EF
## concept_ids
CON-CVS-85F9DE6C65F2F7
CON-CVS-22A9DCDEA41021
## learning_objective
Perform an auscultatory blood pressure with the cuff size, arm position and deflation rate that make the number defensible, and say what silence above systolic pressure means.

## candidate_instructions
Record a 12-lead electrocardiogram on this manikin. State where each electrode goes and why the recording works. Do not interpret the trace.
## mark_scheme
Preparation (4): Washes hands, introduces self, confirms identity and explains the procedure in lay terms
Preparation (4): States clearly to the patient that the machine records and does not deliver a current
Preparation (4): Offers a chaperone and maintains privacy during chest exposure
Skin and contact (5): Prepares the skin, wiping and if necessary shaving to reduce contact impedance
Skin and contact (5): Explains that body fluids conduct the heart's electrical changes to the skin, which is why surface electrodes can record them
Skin and contact (5): Checks that electrodes are within their expiry and the gel is not dried out
Limb electrodes (4): Places one electrode on each of the four limbs, over bone rather than large muscle bellies
Limb electrodes (4): States that ten electrodes in total produce the twelve leads
Chest electrodes (7): Places V1 in the fourth intercostal space at the right sternal edge
Chest electrodes (7): Places V2 in the fourth intercostal space at the left sternal edge
Chest electrodes (7): Places V4 in the fifth intercostal space at the midclavicular line
Chest electrodes (7): Places V3 midway between V2 and V4
Chest electrodes (7): Places V5 and V6 in the same horizontal plane as V4, at the anterior axillary and midaxillary lines
Recording (2): Asks the patient to lie still and breathe normally before recording
Recording (2): Labels the trace with the patient's identity, the date and the time, and checks it for artefact before removing the electrodes

## learning_objective
Place ten electrodes accurately and justify the recording from the fact that body fluids carry cardiac potentials to the skin.

## candidate_instructions
Measure this patient's jugular venous pressure and state the height. Explain to the examiner how you know you are looking at the venous and not the arterial pulsation.
## mark_scheme
Preparation (4): Washes hands, introduces self, confirms identity and gains consent
Preparation (4): Reclines the patient at approximately 45 degrees
Preparation (4): Turns the patient's head slightly away and ensures the sternocleidomastoid is relaxed
Preparation (4): Ensures the neck is adequately exposed and lit from a tangential angle
Finding the pulsation (8): Looks for the internal jugular pulsation between the two heads of sternocleidomastoid
Finding the pulsation (8): States that the venous pulsation is impalpable, whereas the carotid is palpable
Finding the pulsation (8): States that the venous pulsation has two peaks per cardiac cycle, whereas the carotid has one
Finding the pulsation (8): States that the venous pulsation is obliterated by light pressure at the base of the neck
Finding the pulsation (8): States that the venous pulsation varies with position and with respiration
Measuring (5): Measures the vertical height of the top of the pulsation above the sternal angle
Measuring (5): Reports the height in centimetres and states whether it is raised
Measuring (5): Explains that the absence of valves between the right atrium and the venae cavae is what makes the neck vein report right-atrial pressure
Completion (3): Performs or offers the abdominojugular test and states what a sustained rise means
Completion (3): Offers to examine for peripheral and sacral oedema

## candidate_instructions
Auscultate this patient's heart. State where you are listening, with which part of the stethoscope, and what you are listening for at each site.
## mark_scheme
Preparation (3): Washes hands, introduces self, confirms identity, gains consent and offers a chaperone
Preparation (3): Positions the patient at 45 degrees with the chest adequately exposed
Systematic listening (8): Auscultates the mitral area at the apex
Systematic listening (8): Auscultates the tricuspid area at the lower left sternal edge
Systematic listening (8): Auscultates the pulmonary area in the second left intercostal space
Systematic listening (8): Auscultates the aortic area in the second right intercostal space
Systematic listening (8): Uses the diaphragm for high-frequency sounds and the bell for low-frequency sounds
Timing (6): Palpates a central pulse simultaneously to time any additional sound against systole or diastole
Timing (6): Identifies the first and second heart sounds before commenting on anything else
Timing (6): Describes any murmur by its timing, site of maximal intensity and radiation
Manoeuvres (2): Repositions the patient at least once, such as rolling to the left or sitting forward in expiration
Completion (1): States that the listening areas reflect the direction in which blood carries the sound, not the surface position of the valves

Assess this patient's peripheral perfusion and check for oedema. Present what you find and what each finding tells you.
## mark_scheme

Preparation (3): Exposes the hands, lower legs and ankles adequately
Peripheries (6): Assesses the temperature of the hands and feet with the back of the hand, comparing sides

Oedema (6): Ascends the leg to establish the upper level of any oedema
Oedema (6): Establishes whether the swelling is bilateral or one-sided
Completion (1): Offers to examine the sacrum in a patient who has been lying down
Completion (1): States that cool peripheries with delayed refill indicate reduced perfusion, and that bilateral pitting oedema points to a raised systemic venous pressure

## title
Measuring a lying and standing blood pressure
## subject
cvs
## type
Skills checklist
## status
Draft
## owner

## duration
8
## marks
20
## difficulty
Challenging
## candidate_instructions
Measure this patient's lying and standing blood pressure. State the timings you use, what you would call a significant fall, and what the test is actually testing.
## mark_scheme
Preparation (4): Washes hands, introduces self, confirms identity, gains consent and explains that the patient may feel light-headed
Preparation (4): Checks that it is safe for the patient to stand and arranges a chair within reach
Preparation (4): Ensures the patient has been lying down for at least five minutes before the first reading
Lying reading (4): Takes the supine reading with the arm supported at heart level
Lying reading (4): Records the heart rate as well as the pressure
Standing readings (7): Asks the patient to stand, keeping the arm supported at heart level throughout
Standing readings (7): Takes a reading within the first minute of standing
Standing readings (7): Repeats the reading at three minutes of standing
Standing readings (7): Asks about symptoms at each reading and records them alongside the numbers
Standing readings (7): Stops and sits the patient down if symptoms become severe
Interpretation (5): States that a sustained fall of 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic is significant
Interpretation (5): Interprets the heart rate response alongside the pressure rather than the pressure alone
Interpretation (5): Explains that standing shifts blood into the compliant systemic veins, and that the arterial baroreceptors normally restore pressure within seconds
Interpretation (5): States that symptoms occurring without a significant fall, or a fall without symptoms, both change what the result means

Perform a postural blood pressure with the timings that make it interpretable, and read the pressure and the heart-rate response together as a test of the baroreflex.
